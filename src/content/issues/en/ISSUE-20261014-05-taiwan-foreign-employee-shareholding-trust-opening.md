# Taiwan plans to open employee shareholding trusts to foreign staff

[CNA reported on October 13, 2026](https://www.cna.com.tw/news/afe/202610130293.aspx) that Taiwan plans to admit eligible foreign employees to employee shareholding trusts, with implementation expected in late December. The [Financial Supervisory Commission's release](https://www.fsc.gov.tw/ch/home.jsp?dataserno=202610130002&dtable=News&id=96&mcustomize=news_view.jsp&parentpath=0,2) describes a forthcoming interpretation and says implementation is expected in the fourth quarter of 2026. Exchange rules and supporting systems still need work. These are expected dates, not confirmation that a particular employer's plan is open for enrollment today.

For an international hire weighing a benefits offer, the announcement does not establish eligibility, what the employee would pay, what the employer would add or what happens when employment ends.

## The opening concerns qualifying listed groups

The FSC identifies foreign employees of companies listed on the Taiwan Stock Exchange or Taipei Exchange, including foreign issuers with primary listings, and affiliates linked by controlling or subordinate relationships. The intended investments are shares of domestic listed companies for which the employees work or which belong to their corporate group. This does not announce general access for every foreign employee of every Taiwan business.

Under the announced mechanism, the trust enterprise would register the relevant onshore or offshore foreign-employee trust investment account with the Taiwan Stock Exchange. A multinational group's existing overseas stock-award program is a separate arrangement; the announcement does not convert it into this Taiwan trust mechanism.

[Article 10 of the securities investment regulations for foreign nationals](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0400011&flno=10) already generally requires exchange registration, subject to the exceptions stated there. The FSC cites that provision as the basis for its forthcoming interpretation. The existing article alone does not supply the detailed new participation rules.

## What the written terms would need to explain

[Trust Enterprise Act Article 19](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0310027&flno=19) requires a written trust agreement. Its prescribed contents include the trust's duration, management, calculation and distribution of gains, who receives the property and how it is delivered when the trust ends, fees, and grounds for changing or terminating the agreement. The trust enterprise must also provide periodic accounting reports under the agreement and regulatory requirements.

For an employee considering participation, those provisions make the exit terms especially relevant. Employee-funded amounts, employer-funded amounts and shares already purchased may raise different questions. A resignation could prompt a dispute about whether the employee keeps an employer contribution, when the property is delivered, or whether payment is in shares or cash. The answer needs the actual documents and applicable law; the news does not establish that any forfeiture term is valid.

An English benefits letter and a Chinese trust agreement may also describe different parts of the arrangement. The company's contribution commitment belongs alongside the trustee's administrative terms when the employee assesses the offer. An employer's promised contribution formula cannot safely be inferred from a regulator's description of how the account will operate.

If the documents use different departure dates or payment conditions, the discrepancy needs resolving in the terms on which the employee will participate. A general statement that foreign staff will receive the “same benefit” leaves unanswered which amounts, conditions and timing are being promised.

## A trust is not a guaranteed return

[Article 22(1)](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0310027&flno=22) requires the trust enterprise to exercise prudent care and loyalty in administering the trust. That duty does not amount to a guarantee of the shares' value. [Article 31](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0310027&flno=31) expressly prohibits the trust enterprise from promising principal protection or a minimum return.

For a hire comparing compensation offers, the employer's contribution and the eventual value of the investment are therefore different figures. Neither an anticipated regulatory opening nor the use of a trustee fixes what the shares will be worth when the employee receives them.
