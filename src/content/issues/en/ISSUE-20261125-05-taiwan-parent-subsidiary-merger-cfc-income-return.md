# Taiwan parent-subsidiary mergers: the surviving parent reports CFC income

A Taiwan subsidiary absorbed by its Taiwan parent can omit CFC investment income from its final-period tax return under a [Ministry of Finance interpretation announced November 20](https://www.mof.gov.tw/singlehtml/384fb3077bb349ea973e7fc6f13b6974?cntId=b6ab1c172198439286c5cb025151cb7e). The surviving parent takes over that calculation. It reports the income in its annual return for the year containing the merger reference date.

For a US-owned group, the relevant parent is the Taiwan company that survives the merger. The [operative order](https://law-out.mof.gov.tw/LawContent.aspx?id=GL011786) addresses that company absorbing its Taiwan subsidiary and acquiring the subsidiary's shares or capital interest in a related enterprise in a low-tax foreign jurisdiction. A US ultimate parent sitting above those entities does not become the filing company under this rule.

## Two returns remain

The subsidiary still has a filing deadline. Under [Income Tax Act Article 75](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0340003&flno=75), a business that dissolves through a merger must close its accounts through the relevant date and report its final-period income and tax within 45 days. Any tax due must be paid before filing. This is the final-period return; the same article separately regulates income arising during liquidation.

Why move the CFC calculation? The ministry explains that CFC tests and income calculations use the offshore affiliate's full-year information. A subsidiary disappearing partway through a year may not yet have those figures when its short filing window arrives. The interpretation allows the surviving parent to handle that calculation with its merger-year annual return under the [Article 71 reporting framework](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0340003&flno=71).

For the group's finance team, the two filings therefore need a shared ownership history. Closing the subsidiary's books establishes its final period. The annual calculation also needs the offshore interest that passed to the surviving parent and the dates for which each entity held it.

## The CFC conditions still apply

Taiwan's corporate controlled foreign company rules appear in [Article 43-3](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0340003&flno=43-3). The control test concerns a business and its related persons together holding at least half of a related enterprise in a low-tax foreign jurisdiction, directly or indirectly, or having significant influence over it. An overseas affiliate does not qualify simply because it is overseas.

Statutory exceptions cover substantive operations and earnings below a prescribed threshold, with an aggregation rule for the latter. Where income must be recognized, annual earnings are attributed using the relevant ownership percentage and holding period. Both affect the calculation. A closing-date organization chart alone does not show how long an interest was held earlier in the year.

Under the new order, the surviving Taiwan parent combines its own and the absorbed subsidiary's holding percentages and periods when applying those rules. The calculation therefore reaches the subsidiary's pre-merger holding as well as the parent's holding. The ministry has changed which return contains the calculation, while retaining recognition under Article 43-3.

## Earlier assessments may qualify

Publication brought the order into effect on November 20. Its second paragraph also permits application to cases whose tax assessments were not final on that date. For an earlier merger, that makes the assessment's procedural status relevant alongside the transaction structure.

The surviving parent's records need to connect the subsidiary's closing accounts with the offshore affiliate's annual information, including the ownership percentages and periods used in the CFC calculation. A share-transfer date alone cannot supply that history.
