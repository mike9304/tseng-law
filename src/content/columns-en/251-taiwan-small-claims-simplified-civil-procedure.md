# Owed NT$100,000 or Less in Taiwan: Small Claims Costs, Mediation, and Appeals

A claim for NT$100,000 or less in money goes to Taiwan's small-claims procedure (小額程序) automatically; the plaintiff does not have to ask for it. As of October 2026 the first-instance court fee is NT$1,500, and as a rule the court finishes oral argument in a single hearing. In exchange, the case normally begins with court mediation, and the judgment can be appealed only on the ground that it violates the law.

## Which claims qualify

Under [Article 436-8 of the Code of Civil Procedure](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=436-8), paragraph 1, the small-claims procedure applies to an action for payment of money, other fungible things or securities where the amount or value claimed is NT$100,000 or less. In an invented example, an American freelance designer in Taipei has finished a branding project for a local company. The last NT$80,000 of the fee is still unpaid, so the claim fits. A claim to get a specific item back, or to have someone vacate an apartment, does not, however small its value.

The parties may agree in writing to use the small-claims procedure for a claim of up to NT$500,000 (paragraph 4), and the court may, of its own motion, switch a case to the summary procedure (簡易程序) if it considers the small-claims track unsuitable (paragraph 2).

Summary procedure covers property claims of up to NT$500,000 and certain case types whatever the amount, such as road traffic accident claims ([Article 427](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=427)). Unlike small claims, its appeals are not limited to points of law ([Article 436-1](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=436-1)).

## The NT$1,500 fee, and what a larger claim costs

[Article 77-13](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=77-13) sets the court fee at NT$1,000 for the part of a claim up to NT$100,000, plus NT$100 for each NT$10,000 of the part between NT$100,000 and NT$1 million. Fractions round up to another NT$10,000.

A surcharge comes on top. [Article 77-27](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=77-27) lets each High Court add up to 50 percent with the Judicial Yuan's approval, and since January 1, 2025 the High Courts have added 50 percent on the first NT$100,000 and 30 percent on the part from NT$100,000 to NT$10 million ([Judicial Yuan announcement of December 30, 2024](https://www.judicial.gov.tw/tw/cp-1887-1228003-6d778-1.html)). The [Judicial Yuan fee table](https://www.judicial.gov.tw/tw/np-167-1.html), checked on October 6, 2026, shows NT$1,500 for a first-instance claim of NT$100,000 or less. Each further NT$10,000 adds NT$130.

| Claim | First-instance fee | How it is worked out |
|---|---|---|
| NT$80,000 | NT$1,500 | Flat fee |
| NT$125,000 | NT$1,890 | 1,500 + 3 × 130; the 25,000 excess counts as 30,000 |
| NT$300,000 | NT$4,100 | 1,500 + 20 × 130 |

Losing parties bear the litigation costs ([Article 78](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=78)), and in a small-claims case the court fixes the amount in the judgment itself ([Article 436-19](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=436-19)). Lawyer's fees are different: the provisions of the Code that treat a lawyer's remuneration as part of litigation costs concern lawyers appointed by the court ([Article 77-25](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=77-25)) and counsel in a third-instance appeal ([Article 466-3](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=466-3)). It is safer not to count on recovering the fee of a lawyer you hire yourself for a small claim.

## Where to file and what to bring

For jurisdiction, [Article 1](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=1) places the claim in the district court for the defendant’s domicile; for a company, that is the place of its principal office or principal place of business ([Article 2](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=2)). If the contract fixes a place of performance, the court there may also hear the case ([Article 12](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=12)). In a small claim, however, a place-of-performance or forum clause in the standard terms of a legal entity or merchant does not determine jurisdiction, unless both parties are legal entities or merchants ([Article 436-9](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=436-9)).

For filing, a claimant can use the Judicial Yuan's [tabular small-claims form](https://www.judicial.gov.tw/tw/cp-1361-4051-f9e37-1.html) ([Article 436-10](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=436-10)) or make an oral complaint under [Article 428](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=428), applied through [Article 436-23](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=436-23). Prepare the defendant's name, date of birth and address, plus originals and copies of the contract, receipts and other evidence ([Judicial Yuan small-claims guide](https://www.judicial.gov.tw/tw/cp-1654-4873-0557c-1.html)). Hearings are conducted in Mandarin, and an interpreter translates for a party who does not understand it ([Court Organization Act, Article 97](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=A0010053&flno=97) and [Article 98](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=A0010053&flno=98)).

## When mediation comes first

Property disputes worth NT$500,000 or less must ordinarily go through court mediation (調解) before an action is brought under [Article 403](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=403), paragraph 1, item 11; every small claim falls within that amount. If you file suit without applying for mediation, the filing is treated as a mediation application ([Article 424](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=424)). If mediation fails, [Article 419](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=419), paragraph 4, requires the court to order oral argument at once, unless a party asks for another date. A successful mediation has the effect of a court settlement under [Article 416](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=416); [Article 380](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=380) gives that settlement the effect of a final judgment.

Mediation is not required on the grounds listed in [Article 406](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=406), paragraph 1, such as an earlier failed mediation before another statutory mediation body, or service on the other side abroad or by public notice.

In a small claim, if a party duly notified at least five days ahead fails to appear at mediation without good reason, the court may, at the other party's request, order oral argument at once, and may give judgment on the appearing party's argument alone ([Article 436-12](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=436-12)).

## One hearing, and a short judgment

One hearing is the norm under [Article 433-1](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=433-1). Parties are told to bring evidence and witnesses in the hearing notice ([Article 430](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=430)). If one side does not appear, the court may, of its own motion, give judgment on the appearing side's argument alone ([Article 433-3](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=433-3)). All three rules apply through Article 436-23.

[Article 436-11](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=436-11) permits evening, Sunday and other holiday hearings unless a party objects. The court may skip evidence-taking and find the facts in light of all the circumstances if both parties agree, or if the time and cost would be clearly out of proportion to the claim ([Article 436-14](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=436-14)). Under [Article 436-18](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=436-18), the written judgment may state only the operative part, with a summary of reasons on disputed points where needed. When the defendant loses, the court declares the judgment provisionally enforceable of its own motion ([Article 436-20](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=436-20)).

## An appeal only on a point of law

Small-claims judgments can be appealed to a collegial panel of the district court, but only on the ground that the judgment violates the law ([Article 436-24](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=436-24)). Under [Article 436-25](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=436-25), the appeal must identify the law violated and its specific content, and the specific facts in the record that show the violation. The basic period is 20 days from service ([Article 440](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=440), applied through [Article 436-32](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=436-32)). For a party living outside the court locality, [Article 162](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=162) provides a statutory travel allowance in calculating the period. That allowance does not apply if a litigation agent lives in the court locality and can perform the required act within the period.

On appeal a party may not raise new arguments or evidence unless the first court's own violation of law prevented it ([Article 436-28](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=436-28)), and the second-instance decision cannot be appealed further ([Article 436-30](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=436-30)).

Failing to apply a legal provision, or applying it wrongly, violates the law under [Article 468](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=468). Disagreeing with which side the judge believed is unlikely to be enough, so the facts and the evidence need to be before the court at the first hearing.

## If you have already left Taiwan: security for costs

Where the plaintiff has no domicile, office or place of business in Taiwan, the court must, on the defendant's application, order the plaintiff to provide security for litigation costs ([Article 96](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=96), paragraph 1). Nationality is not the test. The rule does not apply to the part of the claim the defendant does not dispute, or where the plaintiff has assets in Taiwan sufficient to cover the costs (paragraph 2).

Under [Article 97](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=97), a defendant who has argued the merits can no longer request security unless the grounds for requiring it became known only later. The court sets the amount by reference to the total costs the defendant would incur at each level of court ([Article 99](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=99)). [Article 101](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=101) requires dismissal if security is not provided within the period set, unless the plaintiff supplies it before the dismissal ruling.

Court representation is generally reserved to lawyers under [Article 68](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=68); a non-lawyer needs the presiding judge’s permission. Preparing a power of attorney from overseas is covered in [Instructing a Taiwan Lawyer From Outside Taiwan](/en/columns/hire-taiwan-lawyer-from-abroad).

## Collecting, and the payment-order alternative

Judgment provides a basis for enforcement. It does not itself transfer the money. If the defendant does not pay voluntarily, the creditor has to apply to the enforcement court ([Compulsory Enforcement Act, Article 4](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010004&flno=4) and [Article 5](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010004&flno=5)).

Where the debtor is unlikely to contest the debt, a payment order (支付命令) is worth considering before a lawsuit. [Article 508](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=508) permits an application for a fixed money claim. The fee is NT$500 ([Article 77-19](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=77-19)). The basic objection period is 20 days from service; its end date also depends on the statutory calculation rules and [Article 162](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=162)’s travel allowance and local-agent exception. If the debtor does not file a valid objection within the statutory period, the order becomes a basis for enforcement ([Article 516](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=516) and [Article 521](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=521)); if the debtor objects, the application is treated as the filing of an action or a mediation application ([Article 519](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=519)). [Article 509](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=509) bars this procedure where service must take place abroad or by public notice.
