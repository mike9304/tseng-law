# You sold your company's shares and bought them back within six months. Do you owe the profit to the company?

Selling company shares in March and buying them back in July can trigger Taiwan's profit-turnover rule for insiders. If you are a covered managerial officer (經理人) and the statutory matching method shows a profit, the company may claim that amount even though your account lost money overall. No use of inside information appears among the elements stated in [Article 157, paragraph 1 of the Securities and Exchange Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0400001&flno=157).

That result comes from a specific calculation. It does not turn every trading loss into a gain, and it does not settle whether a vice president is legally a managerial officer. Status, dates, quantities, prices, and deductions each need their own review.

## Why an overall loss may still produce a claim

As of November 18, 2026, Article 157 covers two directions of trading in the company's listed stock (上市股票): an acquisition followed by a sale within six months, or a sale followed by a purchase within six months. When a covered person obtains a profit, the company must request that the profit be turned over to it. A March sale followed by a July market purchase fits the second sequence if the actual execution dates fall within the six-month window.

Profit is not calculated by ordinary account netting. For purchases and sales of the same type of security, [Article 11, paragraph 2 of the Enforcement Rules](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0400002&flno=11) pairs the highest sale price with the lowest purchase price, then the next-highest sale with the next-lowest purchase, continuing in order. Loss portions are left out. That is why an overall portfolio loss does not answer the company's demand.

Dividends allotted on the shares included in the price-difference calculation are added to the profit. From the last included trade, or from receipt of a cash dividend, the rule adds 5% annual statutory interest until payment to the company. Brokerage fees and securities transaction tax paid on the matched trades may be deducted.

Does a lack of inside information end the issue? Not under the text of Article 157. That provision does not state intent, actual knowledge, or possession of material nonpublic information as conditions. [Article 157-1](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0400001&flno=157-1) is the separate insider-trading rule and expressly addresses actual knowledge of material information during its restricted period.

## Who falls within the rule

Article 157, paragraph 1 of the Securities and Exchange Act (證券交易法) names directors (董事), supervisors (監察人), managerial officers, and shareholders holding more than 10% of the company's shares, and it applies to listed stock. [Article 62, paragraph 3](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0400001&flno=62) applies Article 157 to a securities broker or dealer buying and selling securities, for a customer or for its own account, at its business premises.

Article 157 names managerial officers (經理人) without defining which titles qualify. Whether a vice president falls within that category therefore needs separate confirmation, and the company's insider filings show whom it reported rather than settling the question.

Reporting records are still useful. [Article 25](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0400001&flno=25) requires a public company to report and announce the holdings of its directors, supervisors, managerial officers, and shareholders holding more than 10%. Those holders must report the prior month's changes to the company by the fifth day of each month, and the company must submit the consolidated report to the competent authority by the fifteenth.

The trading review can extend beyond shares held in the executive's own name. Through paragraph 5, [Article 157](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0400001&flno=157) imports the rule covering a spouse's holdings, minor children's holdings, and holdings in another person's name. For the last category, [Article 2 of the Enforcement Rules](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0400002&flno=2) requires all three listed features: directly or indirectly providing the shares to the other person, or providing funds for that person to buy them; a right to manage, use, or dispose of those shares; and attribution of all or part of the profit or loss to the person concerned.

Stock is not the outer limit. [Article 157, paragraph 6](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0400001&flno=157) extends the rule to other securities issued by the company that have an equity character (具有股權性質之其他有價證券). [Article 11, paragraph 1 of the Enforcement Rules](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0400002&flno=11) lists instruments such as convertible bonds, bonds with warrants, warrants, Taiwan depositary receipts, and other equity-like securities. Where the matched securities are of different kinds, common shares use their trade price and share count, and the other securities use that day's common-stock closing price and the number of common shares obtainable on exercise or conversion; the same matching order then applies. No conclusion about RSU vesting follows from this open-market sale and buyback; tax issues for employee options and RSUs are discussed in [Equity Awards from a US Parent](/en/columns/taiwan-stock-options-rsu-foreign-parent-employee-tax).

## What follows a company or shareholder demand

The claim belongs to the company. If its board of directors or a supervisor does not exercise it, a shareholder may demand that the directors or a supervisor exercise it within 30 days. Once that period passes without action, the demanding shareholder may exercise the claim for the company under [Article 157, paragraph 2](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0400001&flno=157). Under paragraph 3, directors or supervisors whose failure to exercise the claim causes the company a loss are jointly and severally liable to the company for that loss.

Time runs from the profit, not from a later demand letter. [Article 157, paragraph 4](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0400001&flno=157) extinguishes the company's paragraph 1 claim if it is not exercised within two years from the date the profit was obtained. The actual trade set therefore matters to both the amount and the deadline.

## Trade sheets, dividends and the matched figure

Start with the documents that can change the answer: insider-status filings, the corporate appointment and authority records, complete trade confirmations, dividend records, fee statements, and securities transaction tax entries. The trade ledger should include every potentially matched transaction within the relevant period, including covered family or nominee holdings. Those records also show whether the demand used the right quantities and deductions. Partial records invite a wrong match.
