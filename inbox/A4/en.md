# When a Foreign Professional's Taiwan Job Ends: The Family's Dependent ARCs and the Spouse's Work Permit

A dependent ARC looks like the family's own permission to live in Taiwan. Legally, it hangs on someone else's. When the professional's residence rests on an employer-sponsored work permit and that job ends, the spouse's and children's cards are part of the same problem.

What happens to the professional's own permit and ARC is covered in [our column on being told a Taiwan job is ending](/en/columns/foreign-professional-dismissed-taiwan).

## Why the family's cards can depend on an employer-sponsored job

Dependent residence for a foreign spouse or a child under 18 exists because a family member holds residence in Taiwan ([Immigration Act Article 23(1)(i)–(ii)](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0080132&flno=23)). For professionals, the Act for the Recruitment and Employment of Foreign Professionals adds a direct route: the spouse, minor children and adult children who cannot live independently because of a disability may apply for residence after entering visa-free or on a visitor visa ([Article 16(2)](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=A0030295&flno=16)).

Then the sponsored job ends. The employer must notify the labor authority, the National Immigration Agency and the police in writing within three days ([Employment Service Act Article 56](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0090001&flno=56)), and the end of employment is a ground on which the authorities revoke the work permit ([Article 73(iii)](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0090001&flno=73)). Under [Immigration Act Article 31(4)](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0080132&flno=31), when the reason for a foreign national's residence disappears during the residence period, the Agency revokes the residence and cancels the ARC, unless one of nine listed exceptions applies.

None of those exceptions is written for the family of a professional between jobs. Two concern the worker: a labor dispute with a Taiwan employer that is in litigation, and an occupational injury still being treated. Others cover, for example, the death of the family member a dependent relied on.

That chain assumes the professional's residence was granted for the job that has ended. A valid Employment Gold Card works differently. It combines a work permit, resident visa, ARC and re-entry permit in one card ([Foreign Professionals Act Article 9](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=A0030295&flno=9)), and the Taiwan Employment Gold Card Office's [official FAQ](https://goldcard.nat.gov.tw/en/tags/work/) says the card's validity is not affected by changes in employment and that employers need not apply for a separate work permit for a cardholder. The card itself is covered in [our Gold Card column](/en/columns/taiwan-employment-gold-card). The rest of this column concerns families whose residence rests on an employer-sponsored permit.

## An extension available to qualifying family members

Under [Article 17](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=A0030295&flno=17) of the Foreign Professionals Act, as fully revised with effect from January 1, 2026, a professional who has worked in Taiwan may apply before residence expires if residence is still needed. The spouse, minor children and adult children unable to live independently because of a disability who already held residence permission may apply as well. An approved extension runs six months from the day after the original residence expires. It can be extended once more. The total cap is one year. [Article 10 of the residence regulations](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0080129&flno=10) follows the same pattern.

Three practical points come from the National Immigration Agency's [filing guide](https://www.immigration.gov.tw/5385/7244/7250/7317/%E5%B1%85%E7%95%99/httpswwwimmigrationgovtw5385724472507317%E5%B1%85%E7%95%99/) (posted January 4, 2024, and still citing the pre-revision article number 13). An employment separation certificate issued within the past month is among the documents. During the extension, nobody may work without a Ministry of Labor permit unless another rule allows it. And extensions of relatives' ARCs should be filed within three months before they expire.

Timing is the awkward part. The statute speaks of applying before residence expires and counts the extension from the original expiry date. A job can end long before that date, and the employer must notify the relevant authorities, the Agency among them, in writing within three days after employment ends. Which application the Agency will accept for the family in that gap is a question for the local service station, ideally answered in writing.

## The spouse's own work permit

Is the spouse working too? The answer depends on the permit. [Article 15(1)](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=A0030295&flno=15) lets the spouse of a foreign special professional or senior professional who holds dependent residence apply directly to the Ministry of Labor for a work permit. That permit cannot run past the dependent residence.

A spouse with an employer-sponsored work permit of their own is governed by the same Employment Service Act rules as any other foreign employee, including the employer's duty to give written notice within three days when that job ends.

## If the family leaves and comes back

Someone who leaves Taiwan to regroup has another option. [Article 13](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=A0030295&flno=13) allows a foreign professional who needs to search for work long term to apply at a Taiwan mission abroad for a multiple-entry visitor visa, valid three months, with stays of up to six months and a total stay of six months. It cannot be used again for three years after that total stay ends. It is a visa for the job-seeker; the Article does not mention family members.

Four dates tell the family where it stands: the day the employment ended, the issue date of the employment separation certificate, the date the employer notified the authorities, and the expiry printed on each ARC in the household.
