## Verdict
naturalness (1 = machine, 5 = native professional): n/a for guidance pack (part a scope), 4/5 for columns
variety used: standard Hungarian as written in Hungary, consistent; no regional split, no foreign-language leftovers. Formal register throughout, Ön-address where addressed. Quotation marks are correctly Hungarian („…”, 22/22 balanced, no English or German pairs), the decimal comma is right (012: "0,5 méter"), and dates are correct Hungarian ("2023. január 1-jétől", "2022. január 24-én"). The thousands separator is the one presentation defect: every file groups with a period ("1.579.589 TWD"), which in Hungarian marks an ordinal, not a thousand.
systemic patterns (max 6, each one line, with 1 example quote):
- Thousands are grouped with a period in all 9 files, and the currency code sits before the amount in 011 and 017 while the same files also put it after: "gyógyhatás állítása esetén TWD 600.000–TWD 5.000.000".
- "közönséges" renders English "general/ordinary" 7 times in 017 and 018, where Hungarian reads it as "common, vulgar": "A közönséges gépjárműves árufuvarozásra elvben legalább TWD 25.000.000".
- The English imperative checklists come back as third-person plural indicative, so they describe instead of instruct (all 16 items of 014 §9, all 6 of 016 §9): "Először a törvényi feltételt pontosítják".
- Colloquial "néz" does the work of vizsgál/mérlegel 15 times in 014 and 016: "Együtt nézik, kap-e a munkavállaló ténylegesen többletelőnyt".
- Digit telegrams replace ordinary prose where the English spells the number out: "Igazgató 1-től 3-ig állítható, a tagok közül választva."; "azaz annak 1-szereséig".
- U+200B survives in 013 (lines 34, 58, 72, 86, 108) and 015 (lines 28, 36, 52), on lines whose only character is that space.

012 is native professional and needs nothing. 015 and 013 are a natural FAQ voice apart from the zero-width spaces and the items below. 010, 011, 014, 016, 017 and 018 are professional legal Hungarian with the defects listed. Product facts hold: Wei Tseng is feminine everywhere she appears (ügyvédnő ×5, "ügyvédnőjeként" in 010) with no masculine form or title anywhere, so there is no F finding. The consultation lock holds in all five places that state it — only angol, kínai (中文), japán and koreai, with 018 using "kizárólag" and the rest "nem magyarul, hanem" — so there is no E finding; a grep for win rates, guarantees, "legjobb", free meetings, interpreters and reply times returns nothing. No Korean, Japanese, Simplified-Chinese or stray English text is present, so there is no G finding. The Traditional-Chinese gloss convention is kept throughout. 018 skipping heading 6 matches the English source and is not a Hungarian defect.
