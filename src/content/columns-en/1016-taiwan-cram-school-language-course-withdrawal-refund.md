# Quitting a Taiwan Cram School or Language Class: How Much of the Fee Comes Back

NT$1,000. That is the most a registered Taiwan cram school may keep when a student quits before the actual start date. Under [article 24](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=H0080087&flno=24)(1)(i) of the Ministry of Education's Regulations for the Establishment and Administration of Short-Term Cram Schools (短期補習班設立及管理準則), the school refunds 90% of the term's agreed fees, and any part of the retained 10% above NT$1,000 must also go back.

At the other end, the refund can be zero. Once a third of the term or of the total teaching hours is reached, the school may keep the whole term's fee (item iii). From the start date until then, half comes back (item ii).

## Who sets the scale

Maths classes, exam prep and adult conversation classes are all run by short-term cram schools (短期補習班). [Article 9](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=H0080002&flno=9)(1)(iv) of the Supplementary and Continuing Education Act leaves licensing to city and county education authorities, has the Ministry of Education set refund standards by regulation, and lets each local government add its own rules.

The Regulations cover fee-charging institutions teaching five or more students at a fixed site (art. 2). A home tutor with one pupil falls outside.

More generous local rules prevail (art. 24(8)), as item 12 of the Ministry's [mandatory and prohibited terms for cram school service contracts](https://edu.law.moe.gov.tw/LawContent.aspx?id=FL023114) (partly amended March 16, 2020, order No. 1090029343B; "the contract terms") also says.

## The national scale and Taipei's

Taipei's [Short-Term Cram School Administration Rules](https://laws.gov.taipei/Law/LawSearch/LawArticleContent/FL020608), revised April 30, 2019, split the scale into five steps (art. 9).

| When the student applies | National Regulations, art. 24 | Taipei Rules, art. 9 |
|---|---|---|
| 60 or more days before the start date | 90% (retained 10% above NT$1,000 also refunded) | 95% (retained 5% above NT$1,000 also refunded) |
| From day 59 to the day before the start date | 90% (same) | 90% (retained 10% above NT$1,000 also refunded) |
| Start date, before the first class begins | Half | 90% (same) |
| After the first day's class, before the second | Half (if under one-third) | 70% |
| After the second class, before one-third is reached | Half | Half |
| One-third or later | Nothing owed | Nothing owed |

Take a three-month class costing NT$24,000 (an invented example). Quit by the day before the start date and either scale returns NT$23,000: NT$21,600 (90%) plus the NT$1,400 by which the retained NT$2,400 exceeds NT$1,000. Quit on the first morning? The scales part: NT$12,000 nationally, still NT$23,000 in Taipei. After one class: NT$12,000 against NT$16,800.

Registration fees and seat deposits (訂位金) cannot be carved out as "never refundable": article 24(3) bars deducting them from the total. Agency fees (代辦費) the school collected on the student's behalf come back in full, or as the goods if already bought (art. 24(4)). A student who paid only a deposit and left before classes cannot be asked to top up the amount the school could have kept (art. 24(7)).

## Counting one-third

Hours, sessions or periods are counted as the contract says; if it says nothing, by how the course actually ran (art. 24(6)). Twice a week for twelve weeks is 24 sessions, so the line falls at session eight. Joined late? The next class after enrolment counts as the start, and the remaining period as the whole course (art. 24(5)).

Lump-sum payments bring in [article 7](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=H0080087&flno=7): courses over six months are split into terms of at most six months (two up to a year, three beyond), with fees collected term by term. A school that collected beyond the current term applies the scale to that term only and returns the rest in full (art. 24(2)).

A student pays NT$60,000 for a year and quits after three months (an invented example). Half the first term is gone, so its fee stays with the school. If the contract puts the second term at NT$30,000, all NT$30,000 comes back.

Sign-up gifts, capped at 20% of fees, cannot be reclaimed or deducted from a refund (contract terms, item 11).

## When the school changes or stops the course

Under [article 25](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=H0080087&flno=25), a class that cannot open through the school's fault must be announced before the planned start and refunded in full within seven days of that date. Classes halted for the school's reasons are refunded by the share of days not taught. For typhoons and other causes outside the school's control, the untaught share is refunded within ten days from the day after the event, unless the student accepts make-up classes, recordings or similar. In all three cases, attending more than three classes after the event counts as agreement (art. 25(3)).

The contract terms go further. Under item 14, if the school changes the course, subjects, hours or location without good reason, or replaces more than a third of the teachers, the student may terminate and receive the amount paid plus at least 30%. Item 9 adds at least 10% to a pro-rata refund for oversized or merged classes without consent, and requires a full refund if the student rejects a merger or the start is postponed. The contract's blanks set the exact figures. Refunds must be completed within one month of the termination formalities (item 12).

## Contracts, guarantees and course loans

Written contracts must follow the Ministry's model and contract terms ([art. 30](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=H0080087&flno=30)), with a review period of at least five days that cannot be waived (item 2; prohibited item 1). [Consumer Protection Act art. 11-1](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0170001&flno=11-1) also requires a reasonable review period of up to 30 days; without it, the clauses do not become part of the contract.

Schools taking fees in advance must offer a performance guarantee, shown on the contract's front: a trust account or bank guarantee for 30% of total fees, a joint guarantee scheme, guarantee insurance of at least NT$2 million, or another Ministry-approved method (item 11). Monthly billing is exempt.

When fees are financed through a lender the school introduced, item 11 also lets the student cancel the loan in writing, without reasons, within seven days of approval. If the school closes, the student may terminate the loan and ask the lender to stop paying out the balance for untaught classes, attaching a certified letter (存證信函) or other proof, unless the student agreed to rely on a guarantee instead.

Ambiguous standard-form terms are read in the consumer's favour ([art. 11](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0170001&flno=11)(2)), and obviously unfair ones are void ([art. 12](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0170001&flno=12)(1)). For minors, enrolment needs a legal representative's consent, and refunds are paid to that representative ([Regulations art. 22](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=H0080087&flno=22)(4)).

## Unregistered schools

An unlicensed cram school faces an immediate shutdown order and a fine of NT$50,000 to NT$250,000 on the person in charge ([Act art. 24](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=H0080002&flno=24)(1)). The article says nothing about refunds. Recovery then rests on the contract, the Civil Code and, for a business operator, the Consumer Protection Act ([art. 2](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0170001&flno=2)(2)). Authorities must publish each registered school's name, address, subjects and licence number online ([Regulations art. 11-1](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=H0080087&flno=11-1)), and brochures must state the refund rules and licence number ([art. 16](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=H0080087&flno=16)).

## Foreign students and university language centers

Foreign students at a cram school get the same scale ([Regulations Governing Short-Term Cram Schools' Recruitment of Foreigners, art. 3](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=H0080071&flno=3)). For students recruited from abroad to study Mandarin, the school must report withdrawal, or absences reaching a quarter of the term's hours, to the Bureau of Consular Affairs and the local immigration service station within seven days ([art. 12](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=H0080071&flno=12)(3)). These regulations do not say what then happens to the student's stay.

University extension courses (推廣教育) follow [article 17](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=H0030027&flno=17)(2) of the Regulations for Extension Education at Junior Colleges and Above: 90% back before classes start, half until one-third is exceeded, nothing once it is, agency fees in full, and everything if the class never opens. There is no NT$1,000 cap.

Is a university Chinese language center course extension education? Extension courses must carry the label 推廣教育 ([art. 14](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=H0030027&flno=14)), and the school must explain its refund standard at enrolment and get a signature (art. 9). For unlabelled courses this column found no nationwide refund article, so the school's rules and the enrolment papers decide.

## When the school refuses

Complain first to the business, a consumer protection group or a consumer service center; the business must deal with it within 15 days of the complaint ([Consumer Protection Act art. 43](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0170001&flno=43)). Next comes the local consumer ombudsman, then the consumer dispute mediation committee ([art. 44](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0170001&flno=44)). Education authorities may also inspect how a school handles refunds (Act art. 9(5)). Small sums can go to [small claims court](/en/columns/taiwan-small-claims-simplified-civil-procedure); see also [the consumer dispute column](/en/columns/taiwan-online-consumer-dispute-foreigners).
