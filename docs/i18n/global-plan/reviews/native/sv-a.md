## Verdict
naturalness (1 = machine, 5 = native professional): 3/5 for guidance pack, 2/5 for columns
variety used: Sweden Swedish (rikssvenska), consistent; no Finland-Swedish mix. Number punctuation follows German (`.` as thousands, English `1.57M`), not Swedish (`500 000` / `1,57 miljoner`). Register splits: guidance + 001/002/004/007 are stiff German-pivoted legal prose with capital **Ni**; 003 Q16–Q20, 005, 006, 008, 009 are first-person blog copy from the Korean originals.
systemic patterns (max 6, each one line, with 1 example quote):
- German pivot, not Swedish legal style: "anvisningar", "arbetsgrupper", "farbar kommunikationsväg", "Vi följer klienter" (from *Hinweise / Tätigkeitsgruppen / gangbar / begleiten*).
- Capital **Ni** throughout (German *Sie*); 2026 Swedish professional sites use **du**, or at most lowercase **ni**.
- Korean-reader leftovers on Swedish pages: Taiwan–Korea tax treaty as the 10 % rate, "rådfråga er huvudbank i Korea", "advokat som kan koreanska", links to `/ko/…`.
- English/Hangul left in the Swedish surface: `OUR TEAM`, `OFFICES`, `(English)`, `Q&A`, Hangul slugs in 005/008/009.
- `läge/lägen` used for German *Lage* (“situation”); in Swedish **lägen** is apartments.
- Legal-term calques: 拘役 → "häkte" (Swedish remand, not a sentence), 侵權/unerlaubte Handlung → "otillåten handling", 行使或負擔 copied word for word.

Gender: Wei Tseng is correctly **hon** / **jag** / ungendered **advokat**. No masculine form for her. "han gav B ett A4-ark" in 008 is the male manager, not the attorney.
