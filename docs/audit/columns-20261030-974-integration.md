- seoTitle: Taiwan Resale Home Loan Shortfall: Standard Contract Options, Deposit Rules and the 15% Forfeiture Cap → Taiwan Resale Home Loan Shortfall
- summary: When a Taiwan bank approves less than the loan written into a resale home (成屋) purchase, the seller's side may ask the buyer for a promissory note or other security covering the difference. Point 7 of the Ministry of the Interior's mandatory terms allows that where the amount in the buyer's disbursement authorization falls short of the handover payment, and the seller may enforce the note if the buyer does not pay the price as agreed and still refuses after a demand. Whether the gap is owed in cash at all is a different question, answered by point 6 and the contract. Point 6 sorts a shortfall by cause, and only the no-fault and seller-fault rows carry boxes to tick. A buyer-caused gap must be paid within the agreed period, at least ten bank business days from notice, unless the seller agrees to instalments; if it is not, the seller may cancel. Keeping money already paid needs a further written demand under point 12 and is capped at 15% of the total price for land and building. The mandatory terms become part of a business seller's standard-form contract with a consumer even where they were left out (Consumer Protection Act art. 17(5)); in a private sale the Ministry's model contract is only a reference. → When a Taiwan bank approves less than the loan in a resale home contract, mandatory terms decide whether the gap is owed in cash and how forfeiture is capped.
