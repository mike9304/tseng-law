# Taiwan board vacancies: the 60-day meeting duty and foreign issuers

Taiwan's Financial Supervisory Commission fined the responsible person at Yeong Guan Energy Technology Group Co., Ltd. NT$240,000 for failing to hold a required shareholders' meeting to elect replacement directors. [E.SUN Securities' October 21 report](https://m.esunsec.com.tw/news/instant-detail.aspx?id=%7B90A26515-6C3E-4CCB-95BB-4424ECD811B3%7D) reproduced the regulator's announcement from the previous day: the foreign issuer had failed to hold an extraordinary shareholders' meeting for replacement elections within the required 60 days.

Nine directors were elected at the company's May 28, 2025 annual meeting, according to the decision. By June 11, 2026, all independent directors had left office and board vacancies had reached one-third of the elected seats. The regulator cited two separate vacancy provisions. For overseas counsel arranging a replacement election, both the independent-director roster and the total board count can affect the timetable.

## Which foreign issuers are covered

[Article 165-1 of the Securities and Exchange Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0400001&flno=165-1) applies specified Taiwan securities-law provisions to a defined category of foreign issuer. It concerns a foreign company's shares that were not traded on a foreign securities exchange when first approved for Taiwan exchange listing, over-the-counter trading or registration on the emerging-stock market. The rule is subject to contrary provisions by the regulator.

The incorporated provisions include the independent-director replacement rule in Article 14-2(6) and the board-vacancy rule in Article 26-3. Incorporation abroad therefore does not, by itself, put a qualifying issuer outside these requirements. Conversely, a foreign parent does not enter this category merely because it owns a Taiwan subsidiary.

## Two vacancy counts, two possible triggers

Under [Article 14-2(6)](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0400001&flno=14-2), if independent directors leave and the remaining number falls below the statutory or charter requirement, replacements must be elected at the next shareholders' meeting. If all independent directors have left, the company must hold an extraordinary shareholders' meeting to elect replacements within 60 days of that event.

[Article 26-3(7)](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0400001&flno=26-3) separately addresses the whole board. If departures leave fewer than five directors, replacements must be elected at the next shareholders' meeting. But when vacancies reach one-third of the seats specified in the articles of incorporation, the company must hold an extraordinary shareholders' meeting for replacements within 60 days of that event.

These conditions can arise separately. All independent directors may leave before total vacancies reach one-third. Or the board may lose one-third of its charter seats while an independent director remains. Counting only independent directors can miss the second trigger; counting only vacant seats can miss the first.

For the one-third test, the denominator comes from the articles of incorporation. How many board seats do they specify? The number of directors still in office does not change that denominator. For a hypothetical nine-seat charter, three vacancies reach one-third.

## The deadline is for holding the meeting

Both provisions require a meeting within the statutory period. A board discussion about candidates or a notice sent to shareholders does not establish that the replacement meeting took place. Nor do the provisions start the clock when an overseas parent approves its preferred nominee.

Which departure started the deadline? Resignation notices and other departure records can establish the effective dates. Alongside the charter and independent-director roster, they show whether one trigger arose before the other. A calendar prepared solely around the next annual meeting can overlook the earlier duty.

In the October decision, the penalty fell on the responsible person identified by the regulator. It does not establish automatic liability for every director. A qualifying foreign issuer's Taiwan replacement timetable depends on its board composition and departure dates; the meeting arrangements also need to satisfy the issuer's incorporation-law requirements.
