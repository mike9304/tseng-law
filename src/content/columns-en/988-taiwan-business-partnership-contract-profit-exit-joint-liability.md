# Opening a Shop With a Friend in Taiwan Without a Company: Who Pays the Debts?

NT$900,000. That is what a small Taipei café owed its fit-out contractor and bean supplier at closing (an invented example).

American A put in NT$600,000; B, a Taiwanese friend, put in NT$200,000. Liquidating the shop raises NT$300,000, leaving NT$600,000 unpaid. If the supplier bills A for all of it, can A pay only three quarters? Article 681 of Taiwan's [Civil Code](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=681) says no. When partnership property cannot cover partnership debts, each partner is jointly liable for the shortfall.

## A partnership needs no written contract

[Article 667](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=667)(1) defines a partnership (合夥) as a contract by which two or more persons agree to contribute to a common business. It sets no written form.

Contributions need not be cash. Money or other property rights may be contributed, or labor, credit or another benefit may stand in for them (para. 2). A non-cash contribution is valued at an agreed figure; if none was set, it counts as the average of the other partners' contributions (para. 3). With two partners, unvalued barista skills count as whatever the other paid in.

Nobody has to top up. Absent a special agreement, partners need not contribute beyond what they promised or replenish capital lost to losses ([art. 669](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=669)). Creditors are another matter (art. 681).

## Who decides, and who sees the books

Unless the contract or a resolution says otherwise, all partners run the business together ([art. 671](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=671), para. 1). If certain partners are chosen to manage, they act jointly (para. 2). Any managing partner may handle ordinary matters, such as ordering beans, alone, but must stop if another objects (para. 3).

Resolutions default to unanimity. [Article 670](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=670) lets the contract choose majority voting, but changing the contract or the type of business still needs two thirds of all partners. [Article 673](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=673) presumes one vote for each partner who has a voting right, regardless of contribution, so A's triple stake adds no weight.

A supply contract B signs while A is abroad can bind A. A partner managing by agreement or resolution represents the others toward third parties within that scope ([art. 679](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=679)). A partner without management rights may still inspect the business, assets and books at any time, whatever the contract says ([art. 675](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=675)).

## When and how profits are split

Accounts are settled and profits distributed at the end of each business year unless the contract says otherwise ([art. 676](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=676)). Monthly payouts need an agreement.

[Article 677](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=677) supplies the ratio. Without an agreed ratio, shares follow contributions, so A and B split 3 to 1. A ratio agreed only for profits, or only for losses, applies to both. A partner who contributed only labor bears no share of losses unless the contract provides otherwise.

B works the counter every day. Is B owed a salary? Not by default. [Article 678](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=678) lets partners recover partnership expenses but bars pay for managing unless the contract allows it. Pay also raises the standard of care: an unpaid managing partner owes the care used in their own affairs, a paid one the care of a prudent manager ([art. 672](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=672)).

## Whose espresso machine is it?

All partnership property, contributions included, is jointly owned by the partners (公同共有, [art. 668](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=668)). A machine bought with A's money is not A's alone. No partner may demand division before liquidation, and a debtor of the partnership cannot set off a claim against an individual partner ([art. 682](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=682)).

Shares do not move freely. Transferring one to an outsider takes the consent of all other partners; transfers to fellow partners do not ([art. 683](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=683)). Admitting a new partner also takes everyone's consent, and newcomers answer for existing debts too ([art. 691](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=691)).

Personal debts can reach the shop too. While the partnership lasts, a partner's creditors cannot exercise the partner's rights against the partnership, profit distributions excepted ([art. 684](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=684)). They can attach the share; unless the partner pays or gives adequate security within two months, the partner is treated as withdrawn from the time of attachment ([art. 685](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=685)).

## When debts exceed the shop's assets

Back to the café. Partnership property pays first; article 681 reaches only the remaining NT$600,000.

A creditor of joint debtors may demand all or part from any one, several or all, at once or in turn ([art. 273](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=273), para. 1). The supplier may claim the full NT$600,000 from A alone. If A has left Taiwan, it may claim all of it from B. A labor-only partner is still one of the "partners" article 681 names.

Settling up between partners comes later. Joint debtors bear equal shares unless a statute or contract provides otherwise ([art. 280](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=280)). One who pays and frees the others may recover each one's share plus interest from that date ([art. 281](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=281), para. 1). Between partners, A's recovery from B starts from the loss ratio in article 677 or the contract. A claim against a partner with no assets collects little.

## When one partner wants out

With no fixed term, or a term set as one partner's lifetime, any partner may withdraw on two months' notice, but not at a time harmful to the business ([art. 686](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=686), paras. 1–2). With a fixed term, a partner may still withdraw, free of those limits, for a serious reason not attributable to them (para. 3). Trading on past an agreed term makes it open-ended ([art. 693](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=693)).

Some exits are involuntary. [Article 687](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=687) lists death (unless the contract lets heirs succeed), a declaration of bankruptcy or guardianship, and expulsion. Expulsion is hard. It requires just cause and the consent of all other partners, and the expelled partner must be notified ([art. 688](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=688)).

The leaver is then paid out. Under [article 689](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=689), the settlement rests on partnership property as it stood at withdrawal, and the partnership may pay the share in money whatever the contribution was. A partner who contributed the machine cannot insist on taking it. Unfinished business is accounted for, and its result shared, once concluded.

Debts follow the leaver. [Article 690](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=690) keeps a withdrawing partner liable for debts incurred before withdrawal. A record of the withdrawal date and debts then outstanding helps settle later disputes.

## Where the money goes at closing

A partnership dissolves when its term expires, all partners agree, or its business is completed or becomes impossible ([art. 692](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=692)). All partners, or liquidators chosen by a majority of all partners, then wind it up ([art. 694](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=694)); several liquidators decide by majority ([art. 695](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=695)).

[Article 697](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=697) sets the order. Debts are paid first, with reserves for debts not yet due or in litigation. Money and property contributions come back next, property at its value when contributed, and assets are sold as needed. If too little remains, contributions are returned pro rata ([art. 698](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=698)); any surplus is divided by profit shares ([art. 699](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=699)). Article 697(2) names only money and property contributions; it is silent on labor.

## Registration does not create a company

The Business Registration Act treats a for-profit sole proprietorship or partnership as a business ([art. 3](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0080004&flno=3)) that cannot be established without local registration ([art. 4](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0080004&flno=4)), except small operations listed in [article 5](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0080004&flno=5), such as street vendors and those with monthly sales below the business tax threshold. Registration covers each partner's name, address, ID number and contribution plus a contract copy ([art. 9](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0080004&flno=9), para. 1, subpara. 7); the managing partner is the responsible person ([art. 10](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0080004&flno=10), para. 1). Trading unregistered draws an order to register by a deadline, then repeatable fines of NT$10,000 to NT$50,000 ([art. 31](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0080004&flno=31)).

Registering does not change the form. Under the Company Act, limited company shareholders answer only up to their capital contribution ([art. 2](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0080001&flno=2), para. 1, subpara. 2; [art. 99](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0080001&flno=99), para. 1), though a shareholder whose abuse of the company's separate status causes it to bear a specific debt that is clearly difficult to repay must pay that debt where the circumstances are serious and it is necessary (art. 99, para. 2). Formation steps are in [our company setup guide](/en/columns/taiwan-company-establishment-basics).

For foreign partners, the Act for Investment by Foreign Nationals counts setting up a sole proprietorship or partnership in Taiwan as an investment ([art. 4](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0040002&flno=4), subpara. 2). Investors under the Act apply to the Ministry of Economic Affairs ([art. 2](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0040002&flno=2)) for approval ([art. 8](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0040002&flno=8), para. 1). Whether a venture needs approval, and how that affects residence and work status, is a separate question.
