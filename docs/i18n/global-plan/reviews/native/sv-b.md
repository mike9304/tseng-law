## Verdict
naturalness (1 = machine, 5 = native professional): n/a for guidance pack (part a: 3/5), 2/5 for columns
variety used: Sweden Swedish (rikssvenska), consistent; no Finland-Swedish mix. Number punctuation is German (`TWD 25.000.000`), not Swedish (`25 000 000 TWD`). Register splits: 011/014/016/017 are stiff German-pivoted legal prose with capital **Ni**; 013/015 are first-person Korean blog copy (emoji, `Ex.)`, line-break poetry); 010 mixes case-study first person with the same stiff Ni-close; 012 is the one file a native could almost publish.
systemic patterns (max 6, each one line, with 1 example quote):
- Capital **Ni** throughout (German *Sie*); 2026 Swedish professional sites use **du**. "Om ni har fått en gymskada är det viktigt att först få den nödvändiga vården"
- German thousands `.` and digit-style legalese (`1 instans`, `3:e man`, `2 barn`) instead of spelled ordinals and space-grouped amounts.
- FAQ answers pasted as body openings: sections start with "Nej." / "Det är inte alltid så." with no question above them (011, 014, 016, 017).
- *Ersättning* used both for 合理補償 (compensation) and for 人力替補 (replaceability), so one sentence in 014 sets "ersättningssvårigheten" next to "den ersättning arbetstagaren mottagit".
- *Facklig* for 專業: a Swedish reader hears trade-union training, not professional skills training. Throughout 014.
- 013/015 still read as a Korean blog (😊, "lämna en kommentar", `Råd：`, `Q.`), while 011/014/016/017 read as a German statute commentary.

Gender: Wei Tseng is correctly **jag** (010, 013) / ungendered **advokat**. No masculine form, pronoun, or title for her. The Korean student in 010 is the male plaintiff, not the attorney.

012 (overtaking) is genuinely the strongest file in this batch: clear structure, working anti-guarantee close, no Korea-reader leftover, no `/ko/` links. 014 is the weakest: several statutory terms would mislead a Swedish labour lawyer.
