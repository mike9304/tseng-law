# Taiwan's proposed NT$10,000: which residence cards are in the plan?

An ARC in your wallet does not yet answer whether you would receive Taiwan's proposed NT$10,000 payment. [Focus Taiwan reported on October 27, 2026](https://focustaiwan.tw/politics/202610270020) that the 2027 general budget containing the payment had moved to Legislative Yuan committee review. The [Executive Yuan's October 8 plan](https://www.ey.gov.tw/Page/448DE008087A1971/331daa62-8dc4-4de4-8379-ab7f9545b4d5) sets out proposed categories. The proposed 2027 payment has not begun.

In the Executive Yuan's October 8 list, a foreign resident who holds an APRC and a foreign national with a residence permit whose spouse is an ROC national each have a stated route; an employee's ordinary work ARC alone is absent.

## Permanent residence is a named category

Under the October 8 plan, foreign nationals who have obtained permanent residence permits are expressly listed. [An APRC is evidence of that permanent status](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0080132&flno=25). The [National Immigration Agency's permanent-residence guidance](https://www.immigration.gov.tw/5475/5478/141465/141808/411648/cp_news) describes an application and a decision; time spent living in Taiwan or an application in progress is not itself an issued permanent residence permit.

NIA guidance gives a general route involving five consecutive years of lawful residence with at least 183 days in each year, while it sets out separate routes for certain foreign professionals. Applicants use the service station for their place of residence. The NIA also lists required documents. Meeting a residence period may support an application. It does not grant an APRC. Only an issued permit appears in the Executive Yuan's list.

For someone who already holds an APRC, the October 8 plan gives a concrete reason to follow the budget. It is still a plan. It does not make the holder an approved recipient in October 2026, and the payment process has not begun. The card's actual status, not a colloquial label such as “long-term ARC,” is the relevant starting point.

## A work ARC is not separately named

Taiwanese citizens with household registration and nationals without household registration who hold residence permits are also listed. Those are nationality categories. For foreign nationals, the plan names permanent residents and one specified spouse group. What if you have an employment ARC? That card alone is not separately listed.

Being left off a proposed list is not the same as being excluded from the final program. The Legislature can change the budget or accompanying rules, and the administrative details remain to be settled. As of October 29, an American employee with only an ordinary work ARC has no stated eligibility route in the Executive Yuan list on that basis alone. Employment, years of residence and tax payment are not substitutes for a category in the current proposal.

## The spouse category turns on the relationship too

The Executive Yuan's proposed spouse category covers foreign nationals who hold residence permits and are married to ROC nationals. The same clause also mentions spouses from China, Hong Kong and Macau under their respective descriptions. For a U.S. citizen married to a Taiwanese national, the proposed wording turns on the marriage to that national and the residence permit. No APRC is specified for this spouse category.

Marriage alone is insufficient under this wording. Someone who has applied for a permit but has not received one cannot treat that pending application as a permit already obtained under the October 8 wording. Nor does the plan say what date will eventually control a person's eligibility. A permit issued later might matter under rules still to be enacted, but the current proposal does not answer that question. The marriage record and the actual residence decision would have to be read together.

A residence permit issued because someone is married to another foreign resident is a different situation. The Executive Yuan's proposed clause covers a foreign national who holds a residence permit and is married to an ROC national. If the permit type or the nationality and residence status of the spouse is uncertain, the underlying immigration record matters more than the short label on a card.

## The budget still has to pass

The [Legislative Yuan's October 27 sitting record](https://ppg.ly.gov.tw/ppg/sittings/2026102170/details?meetingDate=115%2F10%2F27) lists the premier's report on the 2027 general budget. [Focus Taiwan reported](https://focustaiwan.tw/politics/202610270020) that the budget was referred to committee review that day. The Executive Yuan says the payment timetable depends on the budget completing legislative review and being promulgated. Its proposed methods for delivering money are preparations, not an open application channel. A rival proposal mentioned by Focus Taiwan has different terms; neither proposal is a final instruction to residents.

Under the [Executive Yuan's conditional schedule](https://www.ey.gov.tw/Page/448DE008087A1971/331daa62-8dc4-4de4-8379-ab7f9545b4d5), payment could begin before Lunar New Year if lawmakers pass the 2027 budget by early January and the president promulgates it by mid January. It also envisages roughly six months for ordinary recipients to claim a payment. These are planning assumptions from October 8, not an announced start date or a final claim window. A resident whose permit application is pending cannot use that tentative calendar to infer a payment right.
