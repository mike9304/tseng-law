# After a foreign fraud conviction, can Taiwan prosecute the same conduct?

A foreign judgment is not automatically the end of a Taiwanese case. Three questions follow. Between an overseas arrest and a Taiwanese indictment lie these issues: does Taiwanese criminal law apply, what did the individual do, and how should time already served abroad be treated?

[Bernama reported on 31 October 2026](https://www.bernama.com/tv/news.php?id=2628320) that Malaysian police had detained 18 Taiwanese nationals on suspicion of impersonating Hong Kong police in a phone scam. Those were allegations. The report did not establish guilt or a final foreign judgment.

## When Taiwan law applies to conduct abroad

[Criminal Code Article 5](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0000001&flno=5)(11) extends Taiwanese criminal law to aggravated fraud under Article 339-4 committed outside Taiwan. An overseas location does not by itself exclude Taiwanese law.

The prosecution must first establish basic fraud under [Article 339](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0000001&flno=339): an intent to unlawfully appropriate property for oneself or a third person, by deception causing the victim or another person to deliver property, or thereby obtaining an unlawful pecuniary benefit for oneself or a third person. [Article 339-4](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0000001&flno=339-4) then requires an aggravating circumstance, such as impersonating a government agency or official, acting jointly with three or more people, or disseminating to the public through media or the internet.

Eighteen arrests do not prove that all eighteen participated in the same deception. Contact with victims, control of accounts and other employment tasks affect individual responsibility. Whether posing as Hong Kong police satisfies paragraph 1, subparagraph 1, also depends on the actual conduct and statutory interpretation, not merely the seized uniforms.

## A final foreign judgment has a specific effect under Article 9

[Article 9](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0000001&flno=9) allows the same conduct to be dealt with under Taiwanese criminal law despite a final foreign judgment. If all or part of a sentence has already been executed abroad, the court may exempt execution of all or part of the Taiwanese sentence.

“May” is not “must.” Days served abroad do not turn into a mechanical deduction formula, and the provision does not compel prosecutors to indict every case. Evidence and prosecutorial assessment remain necessary.

This is also different from the preclusive effect of a final Taiwanese judgment. Article 9 addresses a foreign judgment, so the domestic rules governing final local judgments should not simply be substituted.

## Families should keep documents separated by jurisdiction

Foreign detention notices, indictments, judgments, proof of sentence enforcement and deportation papers serve different purposes. Any later Taiwanese summons, search record or detention order needs its own file. News reports about group size and seized items cannot establish a relative's offense.

While a foreign investigation is pending, local counsel and procedure come first. Once a final judgment exists, the complete judgment and enforcement record are needed to assess Article 9. Similar offense labels in two jurisdictions do not mean identical elements.
