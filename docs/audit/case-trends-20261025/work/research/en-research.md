## 914 / rank 1 — Offshore stock bonuses, Taiwan employer, US executive role

### Reader, trigger and search intent

Immediate trigger: the bonus is stated in BVI holding-company shares, the shareholder record arrives later, or an exit payment is disputed. The February 2026 reciprocal-trade fact sheet records a January 15 AIT–TECRO investment MOU intended to support two-way investment. Do not import the fact sheet's tariff treatment or call the agreement in force without separate current verification.

- https://ustr.gov/about/policy-offices/press-office/fact-sheets/2026/february/fact-sheet-us-taiwan-agreement-reciprocal-trade

### Verified case and exact proposition
Taiwan High Court, 112年度重勞上字第14號, judgment 2025-12-30. Official full judgment read, including reasons VII–IX and disposition:
https://judgment.judicial.gov.tw/FJUD/printData.aspx?id=TPHV%2C112%2C%E9%87%8D%E5%8B%9E%E4%B8%8A%2C14%2C20251230%2C1

A Canadian executive contracted with a Taiwan electronics company and was assigned as CEO of its US subsidiary. The compensation included BVI holding-company shares. The court rejected the Taiwan employer's argument that the offshore issuer alone owed the performance bonus: the contract, prior bonus arrangement and consistent reading of “issued” made the Taiwan company responsible. Nevertheless, the executive failed on the damages claim. The record supported beneficial ownership before formal registration; he did not establish inability to exercise the rights, a compensable loss caused by late registration, or that the employer caused the alleged fall in share value. A low purchase offer was not proof of net share value. The appeal and additional claim were dismissed.

Disposition: claim for US$338,310 share-value loss plus US$120,000 contractual departure payment failed. All these figures are USD. The departure provision required contract-specific written notice and opportunity to cure; the court found those conditions unproved before the agreement expired. The case did not establish that offshore shares need no registration under current BVI law. Its account of BVI rights is evidence evaluated in that litigation, not independent foreign-law advice.

### Drafting spine / new reader decision
Start with the separation between the company owing the bonus and the evidence of loss. Then explain the actual contract and why the Taiwan employer could not shift its promise to the issuer. The important turn is that winning this interpretation did not establish damages. End with the documents needed to evaluate a real claim: signed award terms and employer identity; allocation/beneficial ownership records; dividend/capital-return records; evidence of an attempted sale or blocked right; relevant-date valuation; breach notice and cure correspondence. This is advice to preserve evidence, not a promise of recovery.
Alternative title: “Offshore Stock Bonuses: Who Owes the Shares, and What Was Lost?”

- Civil Code Article 216, actual loss/lost profit, was also opened/read: https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=216. Its case-specific application is in reasons VII(4).

- An appeal notice appears in the judgment. Subsequent appellate disposition/finality has not been established. Say “the High Court held,” never “finally settled.”
- Keep the plaintiff's Canadian nationality correct if nationality is mentioned. US audience relevance comes from the US subsidiary role and group compensation structure.

## 915 / rank 2 — Public contract mediation and the specific claim sent to arbitration

### Reader, trigger and search intent
Reader: US engineering/construction or technical-services contractor, project claims manager or in-house lawyer pursuing a Taiwan public-project variation, extension or payment claim. Trigger: mediation has failed, but the claim changed from time relief to money or vice versa.

These are forecasts/planned tenders, not completed awards. A supply-only contract does not automatically qualify for the statutory construction/technical-services arbitration route.

Avoid quoting its approximate dollar GPA thresholds as current 2026 thresholds.
Search phrase hypotheses: “Taiwan public construction arbitration mediation,” “Taiwan government contract extension of time arbitration,” “Government Procurement Act 85-1 same dispute.”

### Verified case and exact proposition
Supreme Court, 106年度台上字第723號, judgment 2017-07-12. Primary evidence actually read: official Supreme Court explanation, not the entire underlying case file:
https://jirs.judicial.gov.tw/GNNWS/NNWSS002.asp?id=274693
Official gazette judgment URL also located:
https://www.judicial.gov.tw/tw/communique/dl-4302-efd0d9e996c44e6db9f30999b8ebe050.html
Do not describe the gazette PDF as fully read unless the capture confirms it.

A hospital construction contractor sought money, changed the mediation request to an extension of time, then returned to monetary relief. The public entity had rejected a mediation proposal concerning the extension. No equivalent recommendation/proposal on the later money claim preceded arbitration. An award of NT$44,417,150 was annulled. The Supreme Court dismissed the contractor's appeal, maintaining the annulment; the court's press release expressly calls this case final.

The explanation distinguishes the same concrete performance dispute from a general history of failed mediation under the same project. The court also corrected the lower court's analysis of temporal application of the 2007 amendment while preserving the result. Do not say it adopted all lower-court reasoning.

### Drafting spine / new reader decision
Use the changed relief as the narrative. Explain what had been mediated, what was proposed, what the entity rejected and what reached arbitration. The next action is to compare the actual mediation request, amendments, recommendation/proposal, entity objection and proposed arbitration claim before filing. A failure certificate alone cannot be treated as a universal substitute for an arbitration agreement.

### Current law and cautions
Government Procurement Act Article 85-1 now addresses construction works and technical services. Current official English law opened/read; Act last amended 2019-05-22:
https://law.moj.gov.tw/ENG/LawClass/LawAll.aspx?pcode=A0030057
Article 85-1's relevant amendment was promulgated 2016-01-06; older relevant amendment 2007-07-04. Official history opened:
https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawHistory.aspx?pcode=A0030057
The current rule requires failed mediation because the entity did not agree to the CRBGP recommendation/proposal; it is not automatic compulsory arbitration for every procurement dispute. The 2017 case concerns the older construction context; technical services must be identified as current statutory scope, not invented case facts.
Arbitration Act Article 40(1)(2) is the annulment ground identified by the court explanation. Current Act page: https://law.moj.gov.tw/ENG/LawClass/LawAll.aspx?pcode=I0020001 (last amended 2015-12-02). Do not add a generic annulment deadline without source checking.
Do not advise that changing legal arguments or a money amount always creates a different claim. The concrete facts and relief need comparison. Distinguish absence of consensual arbitration agreement in this case from parties that independently agreed to arbitrate.

## 916 / rank 3 — RCA, environmental knowledge and the parent-company shield

### Reader, trigger and search intent
Reader: US acquisition counsel, Taiwan operating-company investor, CFO or parent-company board considering an acquisition, remediation plan, capital reduction or exit remittance. Trigger: environmental diligence reveals historical worker exposure or contamination, while the group proposes to reduce the Taiwan company's assets.
The official 2026 US–Taiwan investment MOU announcement and USTR bilateral trade context support the transaction setting. They do not prove a surge in acquisitions or environmental claims. ITA's 2026 carbon-fee market intelligence is available as separate environmental-commercial context, but carbon fees and historic toxic-exposure damages are legally different; omit a forced connection.
Search phrase hypotheses: “Taiwan parent liability environmental due diligence RCA,” “RCA Taiwan corporate veil asset transfer,” “Taiwan acquisition contamination parent company.”

### Verified case and exact proposition
Supreme Court, 107年度台上字第267號, judgment 2018-08-16. Primary evidence actually read: the Supreme Court's official explanation:
https://jirs.judicial.gov.tw/GNNWS/NNWSS002.asp?id=359523

The court described long-term chemical exposure, parental operational control, environmental baseline investigations before acquisition, knowledge of pollution, failure to inform/remedy, capital reduction and offshore transfers that depleted the Taiwan company's assets. It upheld the veil-piercing reasoning for the relevant liability portions. This was a cumulative factual pattern, not liability imposed merely because a foreign company owned a Taiwan subsidiary. The explanation describes 不真正連帶責任; avoid casually converting that into a universal US-style joint-and-several rule.

Disposition must remain split: 246 claimants' portions were vacated/remanded; appeals concerning 262 were dismissed. Do not call the entire RCA litigation concluded, all claimants victorious or all remanded claims rejected. The remand included problems with the assessment of unmanifested injury and proof/standing, not a blanket finding that exposure without visible disease can never support a claim.

### Drafting spine / new reader decision
An environmental report can establish the group's knowledge as well as reveal a deal risk. Walk from knowledge to control to response and asset movements in the actual explanation. Then ask what the deal record should show: who received the report, how worker/exposure concerns were handled, whose decision authorized transfers, and whether the Taiwan entity retained resources to meet foreseeable obligations. These are review topics, not a claim that ring-fencing, a parent guarantee or an indemnity alone cures past wrongdoing.
Title alternative: “RCA's Taiwan Case: Why the Parent Companies Faced Liability.”

### Current-law boundary
The article can explain the court's 2018 veil-piercing reasoning without asserting a new statutory rule. Current Company Act Article 154(2) was separately opened/read: https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0080001&flno=154. It requires abuse of corporate personality, a specific debt, evident difficulty paying, and sufficiently serious circumstances making shareholder responsibility necessary. The official history records its amendment on 2013-01-30: https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawHistory.aspx?pcode=J0080001. Its later codification must not be retroactively labeled the sole statutory basis for these historical facts. Current Act overall amendment date is 2025-12-26; that is not the commencement date of every provision.
The court explanation also discusses adjusted causation proof and abuse of a limitation defense. Keep these subordinate to the parent-liability angle; do not generalize them to all tort cases or say limitation periods disappear. Later RCA proceedings exist; article scope is this identified 2018 ruling and the portions it decided, not a present total compensation tally.

### Overlap / fit
EN004 gives ordinary subsidiary/branch liability distinctions. EN643 concerns statutory supervisor stop notices and a director's dissent. EN515 concerns employees after acquisition. This article adds an actual parent-liability fact pattern linking environmental diligence, conduct and asset extraction. It should link one existing formation/governance column rather than repeat it. Firm fit: Taiwan corporate/dispute advice, environmental-risk issue spotting within transaction counsel; do not claim a dedicated environmental specialty or representation in RCA.

## 917 / rank 4 — Named film licensee, admissions and a US declaratory judgment

### Reader, trigger and search intent
Reader: US film/content rights seller, distributor, platform business team or counsel dealing with a Taiwan media group and offshore acquisition vehicle. Trigger: a catalog invoice is paid, affiliated companies later screen the work, and a damages dispute arises over which company actually obtained which rights.
Current dated trigger: TAICCA's 2026-09-22 TCCF MARKET announcement anticipates 107 exhibitors from seven countries and international film/TV/IP rights transactions in November; the event is announced for November 6–9, 2026. The listed exhibitor countries do not include the US. US audience relevance is a licensing-buyer hypothesis and the actual judgment's US/overseas licensing record, not proof of US attendance.
https://en.taicca.tw/news/detail/222
Search phrase hypotheses: “Taiwan film license company rights proof,” “Taiwan US declaratory judgment admission license,” “Taiwan film catalog licensing dispute.”

### Verified case and exact proposition
Taiwan High Court, 113年度重上更三字第14號, judgment 2025-03-25, after a third remand from the Supreme Court. Official full judgment read:
https://judgment.judicial.gov.tw/FJUD/printData.aspx?id=TPHV%2C113%2C%E9%87%8D%E4%B8%8A%E6%9B%B4%E4%B8%89%2C14%2C20250325%2C1

This was a former general manager's loan-repayment action with assigned film-licensing damages claims raised as setoff. It was not a standalone copyright-infringement action or an action to recognize/enforce a US judgment. The operative loan balance was reduced to US$21,200 plus specified interest after US$105,000 and US$269,300 setoffs against US$395,500.

The court distinguished a signed US$1.5 million arrangement from a competing US$800,000 document lacking the relevant party's signature. The Taiwanese company's earlier admission about eight films bound it. A US declaratory judgment describing an agency relationship did not prove that earlier admission false. For two other films, the court found the offshore licensee had not obtained the rights; later availability through a different affiliate's CATCHPLAY+ platform did not establish that entity's earlier license. Because the existence of loss was proved but precise amount was difficult, the court estimated part of the loss under CPC222.

### Drafting spine / new reader decision
Focus on the exact company and rights at the relevant date. A helpful rights file links the signed grant and appendices to the named entity, titles, territory, media, dates, sublicensing path, payments/allocations and actual delivery. When litigation already exists, earlier factual admissions and the scope of a foreign decision must be assessed before adopting a new pleading. Do not offer strategic concealment; preserve accurate consistent records.
Alternative title: “The Taiwan Film Deal: Payment, Platform Access and Proof of a License.”

Current Chinese CPC279(1)–(3), opened/read: https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=279 — judicial admissions need no proof; withdrawal ordinarily requires proof of inconsistency with fact or opponent consent.

Appeal rights appear in the judgment. Subsequent finality is unconfirmed. Do not call it final or the newest word on all related litigation.
