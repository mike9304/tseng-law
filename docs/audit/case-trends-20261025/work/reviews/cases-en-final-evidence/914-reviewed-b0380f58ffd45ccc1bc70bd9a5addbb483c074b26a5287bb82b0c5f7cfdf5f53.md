The executive established that his Taiwan employer owed the stock bonus. His damages claim still failed. In [Taiwan High Court judgment 112年度重勞上字第14號](https://judgment.judicial.gov.tw/FJUD/printData.aspx?id=TPHV%2C112%2C%E9%87%8D%E5%8B%9E%E4%B8%8A%2C14%2C20251230%2C1), identifying the company responsible for payment did not resolve whether the executive had suffered a compensable loss.

Decided on December 30, 2025, the case concerned a Canadian executive assigned to lead a US subsidiary under an agreement with a Taiwan electronics company. His compensation included shares in a British Virgin Islands holding company. That arrangement put the employer, the workplace and the share issuer in three jurisdictions.

[USTR's February 2026 investment announcement](https://ustr.gov/about/policy-offices/press-office/fact-sheets/2026/february/fact-sheet-us-taiwan-agreement-reciprocal-trade) describes plans for Taiwanese companies to expand US investment under the January AIT–TECRO memorandum.

## Holding-company shares, the employer's promise

The Taiwan company argued that the offshore issuer owed the performance bonus. Reading the agreement alongside the earlier annual share-bonus arrangement, the High Court found that the employer remained responsible. Its inconsistent interpretations of the same English word, “issued,” also weakened its position. The issuer's involvement concerned how the employer would perform the promise. See the [judgment's bonus analysis](https://judgment.judicial.gov.tw/FJUD/printData.aspx?id=TPHV%2C112%2C%E9%87%8D%E5%8B%9E%E4%B8%8A%2C14%2C20251230%2C1).

An award document should therefore identify who owes the shares and what counts as delivery. HR's payroll entity, the company signing the employment agreement and the share issuer may each have a different role. Naming those roles leaves less room for a payment dispute to become an argument about which group company made the promise.

## A late record did not establish a loss

Ownership evidence changed the analysis. The court accepted that the executive had beneficial ownership before formal registration, supported by confirmations and payments. He did not prove that he had been unable to exercise the relevant rights or that the employer caused the alleged fall in value. A low purchase offer did not establish the shares' net value. The High Court dismissed his appeal and additional claim. These are findings about the [evidence in this case](https://judgment.judicial.gov.tw/FJUD/printData.aspx?id=TPHV%2C112%2C%E9%87%8D%E5%8B%9E%E4%B8%8A%2C14%2C20251230%2C1), not guidance on present BVI registration requirements.

[Civil Code Article 231(1)](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=231) permits recovery of damage caused by delayed performance. Under [Article 216](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=216), compensation ordinarily covers actual loss and lost profit, unless a law or contract provides otherwise; expected profit must rest on ordinary circumstances, an established plan or other specified circumstances. A changed registration date alone does not supply that evidence.

What transaction or shareholder right did the delay prevent? That question produces a more useful evidence request than a comparison of two dates on a register. A proposed sale, the response to a transfer request, dividend records and a valuation tied to the relevant period can help establish what happened. None guarantees recovery.

For an unlisted share award, an offer from an interested buyer and an independent assessment of value answer different questions. Preserve both, with the underlying financial information. The reason for any change in value also needs to be examined: a damages calculation must connect the claimed loss to the conduct for which the employer is said to be responsible.

## Departure payments had separate conditions

A contractual departure-payment claim also failed. The court found the required breach notice, opportunity to cure and qualifying termination unproved before the agreement expired. The [departure-clause analysis](https://judgment.judicial.gov.tw/FJUD/printData.aspx?id=TPHV%2C112%2C%E9%87%8D%E5%8B%9E%E4%B8%8A%2C14%2C20251230%2C1) concerned that agreement's terms. They were not statutory deadlines available to every departing executive.

Notice has its own timetable. Someone considering departure should have the actual clause assessed while the agreement remains in force, including the required recipients and how a cure period interacts with termination. An ordinary compensation complaint may not perform every function that the clause assigns to a written notice.

An initial [consultation inquiry](/en/contact) for a Taiwan employment-contract review can identify the contracting companies, the disputed award and any approaching notice date. Confidential compensation records can be arranged separately. US employment obligations and BVI corporate requirements may need advice from counsel qualified in those jurisdictions.

Together, the signed promise, evidence of when rights became available, the claimed financial consequence and the notice correspondence form the file for assessment. In this dispute, the employer's obligation was only the beginning of that inquiry.
