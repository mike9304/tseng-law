# Finding a Late Parent's Taiwan Accounts From Abroad: The Tax Office's Single-Window Search

If an heir asks, Taiwan's tax authorities will send a deceased parent's details to banks, insurers, securities firms and the other participating institutions and report what was held on the day of death. A child living in Seattle or Manchester may have a key ring, two passbooks and no idea what else is out there (an invented example). One application can cover inquiries to multiple institutions.

## What one request covers

The rules sit in a Ministry of Finance directive on single-window inquiries into a decedent's financial estate, amended on December 26, 2023 and in force from January 1, 2024. It lists twelve categories: deposits; investment funds; listed, OTC and emerging-board securities; short-term bills; personal insurance (人身保險); futures; safe deposit boxes; bank loans and credit card debt; electronic payment accounts; registered stored-value cards; investment accounts; and credit cooperative member shares. Each is reported as of the date of death. The answers come from the bodies the directive names, among them the bankers' association, the life insurance association, the central securities depository and the Joint Credit Information Center (JCIC), along with their member institutions.

Insurance replies cover a narrow category of policies. Under [Insurance Act Article 13](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0390002&flno=13), personal insurance covers life, health, accident and annuity policies. The Ministry's process sheet, however, says the life insurance association reports only policies where the deceased was the policyholder on the date of death and someone else was the insured, together with each policy's reserve value on that date. A policy on the parent's own life will not appear in that reply.

## Debts arrive by a separate letter

Loans and card debts are covered, but the information travels differently. According to the same process sheet, JCIC mails the deceased's credit report, with bank loan and credit card information, directly to the applicant by registered post, about five working days after it picks up the weekly batch. The sheet says the point is to let heirs decide promptly whether to renounce. The tax office's financial estate reference list records only the date JCIC mailed the report, not its contents.

Renunciation has to be filed in writing with a court within three months of learning that one is an heir ([Civil Code Article 1174](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=1174)). The steps for doing that from overseas are in [our column on renouncing a Taiwanese parent's debts from Japan](/en/columns/taiwan-parent-debt-renunciation-heir-in-japan).

## Applying from overseas

Heirs who have not renounced, executors and estate administrators may apply. The person who died must be a Taiwan national with a national ID number.

Online applications need a Taiwanese digital certificate, a registered National Health Insurance card, mobile phone authentication or another credential the Ministry of Finance accepts. Without one, the counter is the practical route. Without a Taiwan ID card, the applicant shows an original passport. Documents issued abroad must be authenticated by a Taiwan overseas mission, such as a Taipei Economic and Cultural Office, and an authorization signed abroad for an agent in Taiwan needs the same treatment. Add a death certificate or the deregistered household record, and proof of the relationship; if the death has already been registered with a Taiwan household registration office, the death certificate can be skipped.

## The rest of the results

Every Wednesday, the Ministry's Fiscal Information Agency collects the week's applications and passes them to the participating bodies, which reply within ten working days. Apart from the JCIC report, results come by whichever route the applicant chose: registered mail from the tax office for the decedent's household registration; download from the Ministry's e-filing site during a 90-day window that opens 30 days after the application; or the estate tax filing software, from 30 days after the application until nine months after the death.

It is not the whole estate. The directive calls the list a reference for the estate tax return, says other property must still be reported, and warns that under-reporting can be penalized. It does save paperwork: items the taxpayer confirms as matching the position on the date of death need no separate proof.

What about the parent's account in the U.S. or UK? If the parent counted as habitually residing in Taiwan (經常居住中華民國境內) under the Estate and Gift Tax Act, property inside and outside Taiwan is taxed ([Article 1](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0340072&flno=1)). [Article 4](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0340072&flno=4) sets the test: a domicile in Taiwan within the two years before death, or, without a domicile, a residence there and more than 365 days in Taiwan in total during those two years. The second limb excludes people working in Taiwan at the government's invitation for a fixed term. Where the test is met, a foreign account will not appear in this search but may still belong on the return.

## Six months, then the bank

The estate tax return is normally due within six months starting from the day after death, at the tax office for the decedent's household registration ([Article 23](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0340072&flno=23)). Where a court appointed an estate administrator on the tax office's application, the period runs from the day after the appointment. For a Taiwan national who did not meet that test, a return for property in Taiwan goes to the tax office where the central government sits. The taxpayers are the heirs and legatees, including those living overseas, and their liability is limited to the estate ([Article 6(1)](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0340072&flno=6)). With good reason, a written request made before the deadline can extend it, normally by up to three months; where force majeure or other special circumstances apply, the tax office may set the period case by case ([Article 26](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0340072&flno=26)).

Then comes the bank. Article 8 says that until the estate tax is paid, the estate may not be divided, bequests may not be delivered, and transfers may not be registered. The bar lifts once the tax office has issued a consent-to-transfer certificate in advance, a tax exemption certificate, or a certificate of exclusion from the gross estate. Enterprises registering a transfer of title must first see one of those certificates or a tax payment certificate (Articles 41 and 42). A passbook in hand does not unlock the account.

Small balances are treated differently. Under the 1995 Ministry ruling (台財稅第841611225號), when the deposit being transferred or withdrawn has a balance of NT$200,000 or less, the heirs need not attach a tax payment or exemption certificate. Only the paperwork is waived; that deposit still has to be reported as part of the gross estate.

Six months is not long from abroad. Counting the time an overseas mission needs to authenticate documents, an early search request leaves more room both to decide on renunciation and to prepare the return.
