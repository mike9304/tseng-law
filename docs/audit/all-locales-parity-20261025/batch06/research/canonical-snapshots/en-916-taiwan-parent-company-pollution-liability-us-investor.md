Environmental investigations before an acquisition became part of the evidence against parent companies in a Taiwan factory-pollution case. The Supreme Court connected knowledge of contamination with operational control, the response to the danger and transfers that depleted the Taiwan company's assets. Its [official explanation of judgment 107年度台上字第267號](https://jirs.judicial.gov.tw/GNNWS/NNWSS002.asp?id=359523) shows why an acquisition review cannot end when the consultant delivers a report.

Issued on August 16, 2018, the decision concerned workers' chemical exposure at an electronics manufacturer and the responsibility of companies controlling that business. It did not impose liability merely because a foreign investor owned a Taiwan subsidiary.

## Knowledge, control and the movement of assets

According to the [Supreme Court's explanation](https://jirs.judicial.gov.tw/GNNWS/NNWSS002.asp?id=359523), the record included environmental surveys identifying contamination, knowledge within the controlling groups, failure to inform workers or remedy the problem, and capital reductions and offshore remittances intended to evade liabilities. The court upheld the reasoning that allowed the relevant claims to reach the controlling companies despite separate corporate identities.

That sequence matters to acquisition counsel. A report's distribution list can identify who knew about the problem. Board papers and operational instructions can show who could decide how to respond. Later remittance approvals may need to be read against both. Treating those records as unrelated workstreams makes the group's own decisions harder to reconstruct.

The report is a starting point. A useful review asks who investigated the exposure concern, what further work was commissioned and how the findings affected decisions about the operating company's resources. It should record unanswered questions as unanswered. An optimistic closing presentation should not erase the underlying technical findings or the people responsible for following them up.

## The ruling had a divided outcome

For 246 claimants, the Supreme Court vacated the relevant portions and sent them back for further proceedings. Appeals concerning 262 claimants were dismissed. The remand included shortcomings in the analysis of injury, evidence and authority to pursue claims. Those figures and limits appear in the [court's disposition summary](https://jirs.judicial.gov.tw/GNNWS/NNWSS002.asp?id=359523).

The split prevents a broad reading of the case. Remand did not decide that all affected people lacked a claim, and the dismissed appeals did not resolve every part of the wider litigation. A transaction team assessing historical exposure needs to identify the particular proceedings and their status before estimating an obligation. A headline about a single court date cannot supply that assessment.

## Limited liability and its conditions

Taiwan's [Company Act Article 154](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0080001&flno=154) generally limits a shareholder's responsibility to payment for its shares. Paragraph 2 provides an exception where a shareholder abuses the company's legal personality, causes it to incur a specific debt that is clearly difficult to repay, and the circumstances are serious enough to make shareholder liability necessary.

Each condition matters. A shareholder relationship, an unpaid debt or an environmental finding alone does not state the whole statutory test. Nor should the statute be treated as the sole explanation for the court's treatment of historical conduct in the 2018 case. That decision examined the controlling companies' conduct and the basis for looking beyond separate legal identity.

Investors comparing entity structures can find the ordinary starting point in the [subsidiary and branch article](/en/columns/taiwan-company-subsidiary-vs-branch). The pollution dispute adds a question that incorporation documents cannot answer: how did the group actually use its control when it learned about the risk?

## A current investment plan needs a response record

[USTR's February 2026 fact sheet](https://ustr.gov/about/policy-offices/press-office/fact-sheets/2026/february/fact-sheet-us-taiwan-agreement-reciprocal-trade) describes the two sides' intention to facilitate cross-border investment and possible US support for private investment financing in Taiwan, subject to applicable law.

Before approving a distribution, capital reduction or exit transfer, the relevant decision-makers should have an assessment of identified exposure, unresolved claims and the resources that will remain in the Taiwan company. The legal review must address the proposed transaction itself. No document label establishes that a transfer is lawful, and a report sitting unread in a data room does not establish a response to its findings.

For a [Taiwan corporate or disputes inquiry](/en/contact), the initial description can identify the transaction, the Taiwan entity and any scheduled transfer or closing date. Technical reports and sensitive worker information can be handled through an agreed delivery process. The immediate records to preserve are the original findings, the decisions they prompted and the approvals for any subsequent movement of assets.
