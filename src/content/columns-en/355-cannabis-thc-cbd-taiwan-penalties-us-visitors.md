# Cannabis, THC and CBD in Taiwan: possession, transport and medicine imports

Cannabis bought lawfully in a US state does not become lawful to bring into Taiwan. [Article 2 of the Narcotics Hazard Prevention Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0000008&flno=2) places cannabis in Category 2. Possession, use, transport and supplying another person are different legal questions, with different penalties and procedures.

Product identity matters. A label saying “CBD,” “hemp” or “personal use” does not establish the actual ingredients or Taiwan approval status. Before travel or an overseas order, check the product’s composition and intended import route. A prescribed medicine requires a different inquiry from a gummy sold as a supplement.

## Cannabis and the limits of the 10 ppm shorthand

The current [Category 2 schedule](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawGetFile.ashx?FileId=0000318788&lan=C) lists cannabis, resin, extracts, tinctures and THC. Its cannabis entry excludes specified mature-stem products, other than resin, and non-germinating seed products. The THC entry includes isomers and stereoisomers and sets a 10 microgram-per-gram, or 10 ppm, limit for products made from mature stems and seeds.

Those are specific provisions. They do not grant a general exemption for every product marketed as hemp or for every CBD item claiming less than 10 ppm THC. Composition, the relevant schedule entry and the product’s pharmaceutical, food or cosmetic status must all be checked. Article 2 also expressly leaves medical and scientific narcotics management to separate law.

## Possession, transport and sharing have different elements

The principal Category 2 provisions distinguish the following conduct. These are statutory ranges, not predictions of a sentence:

| Conduct | Provision and ordinary statutory range |
|---|---|
| Possession under [Article 11(2)](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0000008&flno=11) | Up to two years’ imprisonment, short-term detention or a criminal fine up to NT$200,000 |
| Possession of at least twenty grams pure net weight | Article 11(4): six months to five years; a fine up to NT$700,000 may be added |
| Manufacturing, transporting or selling | [Article 4(2)](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0000008&flno=4): life imprisonment or at least ten years; a fine up to NT$15 million may be added |
| Possession with intent to sell | [Article 5(2)](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0000008&flno=5): at least five years; a fine up to NT$5 million may be added |
| Transfer to another person | [Article 8(2)](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0000008&flno=8): six months to five years; a fine up to NT$700,000 may be added |

For these thresholds, “pure net weight” is not simply the total weight of a package. Article 8 also provides increased punishment at specified transfer quantities, and Articles 4 and 8 punish attempts.

Transport is not limited to crossing an international border. Conduct inside Taiwan can engage Article 4 too; conversely, whether particular movement constitutes transport requires examination of the conduct and intent. Mailing an item, accepting a parcel or sharing a vape should not be assigned a charge solely from that description.

Under [Criminal Code Articles 12](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0000001&flno=12) and [13](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0000001&flno=13), culpability and intent matter. Preserve the order, product information, shipping instructions, messages and records showing what you knew and did. Keep originals. A name on a parcel does not replace an investigation of those facts, and an assertion of ignorance does not settle them either.

Can personal use reduce a transport sentence? [Article 17(3)](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0000008&flno=17) allows a court to reduce it where the transport was for the defendant’s own use and the circumstances were minor. Personal use is not immunity. Other paragraphs provide reductions or remission for specified offenses when identifying the source leads to detection of another principal or accomplice, and reduction for Article 4–8 offenses confessed to during investigation and every trial stage. Each route has its own conditions; discuss them with counsel without inventing or tailoring evidence.

## A criminal fine differs from an administrative penalty

Ordinary Category 2 possession has a criminal-fine alternative in Article 11(2). A fine imposed by a criminal court is different from an administrative ticket, and conversion of a prison sentence to payment is another question with its own requirements.

[Article 11-1](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0000008&flno=11-1) sets administrative fines of NT$10,000–50,000 and four to eight hours of education for unjustified possession or use of Category 3 or 4 substances, with separate juvenile rules. That does not make every Category 3 or 4 possession case administrative. At five grams or more pure net weight, Article 11(5) provides up to two years’ imprisonment and a possible NT$200,000 fine for Category 3; paragraph 6 provides up to one year and a possible NT$100,000 fine for Category 4. Transport, sale and other conduct have separate criminal provisions regardless of that possession threshold.

## Use offenses and treatment procedures

Using a Category 2 narcotic carries up to three years’ imprisonment under [Article 10](https://mojlaw.moj.gov.tw/LawContentExtent.aspx?LSID=FL001431&LawNo=10&media=print). The response cannot be inferred from that maximum alone.

For Article 10 cases within its procedure, [Article 20](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0000008&flno=20) provides court-ordered observation and detoxification for no more than two months. A finding of no tendency toward continued use leads to release and non-prosecution for an adult. If that tendency remains, court-ordered compulsory rehabilitation lasts at least six months and no more than one year, ending when further compulsory treatment is unnecessary.

Prior treatment dates matter. Article 20 applies its procedure again to use after the statutory three-year interval following release from completed observation or rehabilitation; [Article 23](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0000008&flno=23) directs prosecution for use within three years, subject to the separate route in Article 24. Article 23 also provides release and non-prosecution after completion of compulsory rehabilitation.

[Article 24](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0000008&flno=24) permits a prosecutor’s prior conditional deferred-prosecution disposition to displace the specified observation or repeat-use prosecution procedures. It is not limited to addiction treatment: the provision identifies several eligible conditions. Where addiction treatment is used, medical input is required before the disposition. Revocation leads back to continued investigation or prosecution. The [deferred-prosecution guide](/en/columns/taiwan-deferred-prosecution-conditions-revocation) explains compliance and challenge deadlines.

## CBD medicine is different from CBD food or cosmetics

Taiwan has approved licenses for CBD medicines, according to [TFDA’s personal-use import FAQ, updated September 9, 2026, Question 16](https://www.fda.gov.tw/tc/includes/GetFile.ashx?id=f639245467983366472&iid=11974&type=3).

The same answer says CBD may not be added to food or cosmetics. For a CBD medicine, it describes additional import documentation: origin-country marketing authorization, a certificate of analysis, a medical diagnosis and a sufficiently detailed physician’s prescription. Its personal-use medicine application does not provide a route for CBD foods or cosmetics.

[TFDA’s January 2026 cosmetics notice](https://www.fda.gov.tw/tc/csmnewsContent.aspx?id=7574&mid=271) likewise distinguishes permitted hemp-seed-oil ingredients subject to the stated THC residue limit from CBD added to cosmetics, which is prohibited under the cited rules. A product’s “cosmetic” label does not turn CBD into an allowed ingredient.

Under [Pharmaceutical Affairs Act Article 22](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=L0030001&flno=22), unauthorized imported medicines can be prohibited drugs, but the statute expressly excepts qualifying personal-use medicine carried by travelers or transport crew, subject to announced quantity limits. Postal orders and carried medicines are not interchangeable. Neither that exception nor an overseas prescription supplies blanket permission for cannabis or every CBD product. Confirm the particular medicine, ingredients, quantity and applicable approval requirements with TFDA and customs before importing it.

[Article 82](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=L0030001&flno=82) addresses manufacturing or importing counterfeit or prohibited drugs, with up to ten years’ imprisonment and a possible fine up to NT$100 million for its intentional offense. [Article 83](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=L0030001&flno=83) addresses specified knowing dealings, including supply or transfer, with up to seven years and a possible fine up to NT$50 million. Both contain separate negligent-offense provisions. Classification and culpability must be established; a foreign product label alone does not decide criminal liability.

## If customs or police question you

Ask what status you are being questioned in and which offense is suspected. Before suspect questioning, [Code of Criminal Procedure Article 95](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0010001&flno=95) requires notice of the suspected offense, silence and counsel rights, and the ability to request investigation of favorable evidence. [Article 99](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0010001&flno=99) requires interpretation where language is a barrier. The [police-questioning guide](/en/columns/taiwan-police-questioning-foreigner-rights) explains the record and the different stages.

Request counsel and interpretation before making a statement you cannot understand. Preserve the seizure receipt, any laboratory result, product packaging and actual prescription or approval documents. Do not create a replacement label or delete correspondence. The [first-twenty-four-hours article](/en/columns/taken-taiwan-police-station-first-24-hours-not-release) explains why an investigation does not imply one automatic detention or release outcome.

## Immigration is a separate decision

[Immigration Act Article 18](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0080132&flno=18) permits the National Immigration Agency to deny entry on listed grounds, including prohibited items or a criminal record. [Article 36](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0080132&flno=36) permits removal or an order to depart on specified post-entry grounds and contains notice, participation and review requirements. A pending criminal proceeding is not a universal bar to removal, although the provision treats detention and exit restrictions separately.

A court may also order deportation of a foreigner sentenced to imprisonment or a more severe punishment after execution or pardon under [Criminal Code Article 95](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0000001&flno=95). None of these provisions justifies saying every allegation causes automatic deportation or a lifetime entry ban. Bring the current residence permission, expiry date and criminal papers to the same legal consultation so each applicable route can be assessed.
