# A Broken Engagement in Taiwan: Betrothal Money, Gifts, Wedding Costs and Two Years

Whoever calls off the engagement loses the betrothal money (聘金) and the ring, many people believe. Taiwan's Civil Code says nothing of the kind. [Article 979-1](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=979-1) provides only that where a gift was made because of an engagement, and the engagement is void, rescinded or revoked, "either party" may demand return of the gift from the other.

Fault matters for damages, which the Code handles in articles separate from gift returns. Both carry a two-year limit.

## How an engagement is formed

The man and woman must make the engagement themselves ([article 972](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=972)), and nobody under 17 may become engaged ([article 973](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=973)). A minor needs a legal representative's consent ([article 974](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=974)), and adulthood begins at 18 ([article 12](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=12)). A 17-year-old therefore needs that consent.

No article requires a ceremony or a document. Marriage is different: [article 982](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=982) requires writing, two witnesses' signatures and registration with the household registration office. An engagement leaves no register entry, so in a dispute it must be proved with party photos, guest lists and family messages.

Nor can anyone be made to marry. "An engagement may not be specifically enforced," says [article 975](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=975). What remains is money.

## Seven grounds for rescission

Under [article 976](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=976)(1), one party may rescind if the other:

1. became engaged to or married someone else after the engagement;
2. intentionally broke the agreed wedding date;
3. has been missing, alive or dead unknown, for a full year;
4. has a serious incurable illness;
5. had consensual sex with someone else after the engagement;
6. was sentenced to imprisonment after the engagement; or
7. is subject to some other serious ground.

Does one postponed wedding date justify rescission? Ground 2 requires intent, so a missed date alone does not fit; why it was missed matters. Grounds 1, 5 and 6 cover only post-engagement events, and the court decides what is "serious" under ground 7.

If rescission cannot in fact be communicated, no declaration is needed, and the party is released from the moment rescission becomes possible (paragraph 2).

## Who broke it decides the damages route

| | Rescission on an article 976 ground ([art. 977](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=977)) | Breach without such a ground ([art. 978](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=978), [979](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=979)) |
|---|---|---|
| Claimant | Party not at fault | The other party |
| Against | Party at fault | The party in breach |
| Financial loss | Loss resulting from it | Loss resulting from it |
| Non-financial loss | A reasonable sum (art. 977(2)) | A reasonable sum, only if the victim is not at fault (art. 979(1)) |

Claims for non-financial loss cannot be assigned or inherited unless already promised by contract or brought to court (articles 977(3), 979(2)). If the claimant dies before suing and without such a promise, the heirs cannot take that claim over.

What about wedding deposits and photo shoots? The Code says only "loss resulting from it". Which costs qualify, and how much, is for the court, looking at when the money was spent and whether it could be refunded.

Articles 977 and 978 speak of the other party's loss, so if parents signed and paid themselves, who can claim comes first.

## Betrothal money and gifts under article 979-1

What comes back? Gifts made "because of the engagement". Betrothal money, an engagement ring or gold jewellery may stand apart from a birthday present, so evidence of when and why each was given matters.

Who claims? The article names the parties to the engagement. If the parents exchanged the betrothal money, who gave and who received it comes first.

Which endings count? The article lists void, rescinded and revoked engagements. It does not say directly whether a breach without grounds under article 978, or an ending by mutual agreement, is a "rescission". Couples parting by agreement should put the gifts in writing; any written agreement made at the engagement is read first.

If article 979-1 does not fit, unjust enrichment remains. [Article 179](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=179) requires a person who benefits without legal ground to return the benefit, including where a ground existed but later ceased. Whether an ended engagement is such a ground is also for the court.

## Promised but not yet paid

A gift is a contract to give one's property for nothing, accepted by the other side ([article 406](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=406)). Until the right passes, the giver may revoke it, or revoke the part not yet transferred; notarised gifts and gifts fulfilling a moral duty are excluded ([article 408](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=408)). Promise NT$600,000 and hand over half at the ceremony, and only the unpaid half can be revoked.

## Two years from when?

Claims under articles 977 to 979-1 lapse after two years unexercised ([article 979-2](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=979-2)). Unlike the tort rule in [article 197](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=197), which counts two years from knowledge of the loss and the liable person, with a ten-year outer limit, article 979-2 states no starting point.

So the general rule in [article 128](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=128) applies: prescription runs from when the claim can be exercised. Whether that is the day a rescission notice arrived or the day the breach became clear can be disputed, so plan from the earliest date.

A Korean man and a Taiwanese woman living in Taipei become engaged in March 2026 (an invented example). His side gives NT$300,000 in betrothal money and gold jewellery; she pays NT$110,000 for photos and a banquet deposit. In September he says, without any particular reason, that he will not marry.

If Taiwan law applies, she can claim her costs under article 978 and, if she is not at fault, non-financial compensation under article 979. If he wants the betrothal money back, the first fight is whether a breach without grounds is a "rescission" under article 979-1. If his mother handed over the jewellery herself, who may claim it also needs sorting out. Her claims carry the two-year limit; his has it only if article 979-1 applies.

Set-off is also possible. Under [article 334](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=334), two people owing each other debts of the same kind, both due, may set them off, unless the nature of the debt or an agreement excludes it.

## Things bought while living together

The engagement articles say nothing about furniture or a car bought together. Co-owned items follow the co-ownership rules: unclear shares are presumed equal ([article 817](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=817)), any co-owner may in principle demand partition at any time ([article 823](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=823)), and without agreement the court may divide in kind, order a sale or award one side the item with money compensation to the other ([article 824](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=824)). Items bought in one name with the other's money need a different starting point: was it a loan or a gift?

## Court means mediation first

Disputes over a void, rescinded or revoked engagement, damages for breach and return of engagement gifts are Category C family matters ([Family Act art. 3](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010048&flno=3)(3)(i)), heard by a juvenile and family court or, where none exists, a district court's family division ([art. 2](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010048&flno=2)).

Court mediation must come before a request for judgment, and a suit filed directly is treated as a mediation application, except where a party must be served by public notice or abroad ([art. 23](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010048&flno=23)(1)–(2)). Money claims can be settled; a settlement recorded in the mediation transcript has the force of a final judgment, and two-thirds of the court fee can be refunded on application within three months of the settlement ([art. 30](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010048&flno=30)).

Keep transfer records or red-envelope photos, vendor contracts, and dated break-up messages.

## Different nationalities

[Article 45 of the Act Governing the Choice of Law in Civil Matters Involving Foreign Elements](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000007&flno=45) governs. Formation follows each party's national law (for multiple nationalities, the closest one, [art. 2](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000007&flno=2)), and the form is valid if it meets one party's national law or the law of the place of engagement. Effects follow the common national law, then the common domicile, then the place most closely connected.

So the Korean party's age and consent rules come from Korean law. A Korean–Taiwanese couple in Taipei has no common national law; common domicile is next. The article does not say whether gift-return or damages claims are "effects". Foreign law is not applied where the result offends Taiwan's public order or good morals ([art. 8](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000007&flno=8)); marital property after the wedding falls under [art. 48](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000007&flno=48).
