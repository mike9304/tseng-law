For a purchase dispute with a Taiwan business, [Article 43 of the Consumer Protection Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawAll.aspx?pcode=J0170001) lets the buyer complain to the business, a consumer organisation or a local consumer service centre. The provision does not set a Taiwan-citizenship test. Identify the contracting seller from the receipt or order record: a platform, an overseas merchant and a Taiwan retailer may call for different routes.

## The contracting seller and purchase records

Save the listing as shown when you ordered, seller details, checkout terms, receipt or invoice, payment record, delivery record, product photos and the messages in which you sought a remedy. State the order number, what was promised, what arrived or failed, and whether you seek delivery, repair, cancellation or a specified refund. For an in-store purchase, keep the shop receipt and any written return policy. The legal basis for a defective product is not the same as a change-of-mind request.

For qualifying distance purchases, [Article 19 and the Executive Yuan's consumer guidance](https://cpc.ey.gov.tw/Page/4432D6D5FA6677B9/2cd52250-9cc5-4c34-99ee-a350985d0b37) provide a seven-day cancellation route after receipt of goods or services, subject to statutory exceptions. The Executive Yuan's [online-shopping explanation](https://cpc.ey.gov.tw/Page/BC16ACF0BBB9CCC2/f8e15c56-0cbe-49ac-a7ba-2f41e1d9ca55) says an exception must meet the applicable rules and be disclosed. The seven-day rule is not a general no-reason return policy for purchases made in a physical shop. Check the transaction type and any exception before asserting it.

## Local complaint, mediation and court

Under [Articles 42–44 of the Consumer Protection Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawAll.aspx?pcode=J0170001), local governments have consumer service centres. A consumer may complain to the business, a consumer organisation or a centre. The business must properly handle the complaint within **15 days**. If it does not, the consumer may complain to the local consumer protection officer (消保官), then seek mediation through the local consumer-dispute mediation committee. The [Executive Yuan's procedure page](https://cpc.ey.gov.tw/Page/71E988F034956960) also points to its online complaint system and says court proceedings do not require a prior complaint or mediation. Its online form offers email verification, useful when a visitor no longer has a Taiwan mobile number; the form's current fields and eligibility should still be checked when filing.

If the claim is for a refund of money, [the Judicial Yuan describes](https://www.judicial.gov.tw/tw/cp-1654-4873-0557c-1.html) a small-claims procedure generally used for payment claims of up to **NT$100,000**. A [payment order](https://www.judicial.gov.tw/tw/cp-1654-2721-42bcf-1.html) may fit a claim for a specified monetary amount, but it is unavailable where service must be made abroad or by public notice, among other conditions. The respondent can object within 20 days of service, so an order is not a guaranteed shortcut to recovery. Compare cost, evidence, service and the seller's address before choosing a court route.

You may separately report a misleading listing or unsafe product to the platform and ask it to preserve the listing and seller records. Ask for a written case number or response. A platform report is not itself a court order, a promise that the listing will be removed, or a substitute for a claim against the correct seller.
