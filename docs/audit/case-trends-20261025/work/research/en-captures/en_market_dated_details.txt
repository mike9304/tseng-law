L43:   *   *   *   *   *   *   *   *   *   *   * # TCCF MARKET to bring together the global content industry Spotlight Screenings target international buyers to propel Taiwan content onto the world stage
L44: 
L45: September 22, 2026
L46: The highly anticipated 2026 Taiwan Creative Content Fest (TCCF) is set to take place this November. At the heart of industry transactions is TCCF MARKET, which this year will bring together 107 exhibitors from seven countries—Taiwan, South Korea, Thailand, Japan, Singapore, France, and the Philippines. Focusing on rights transactions for international film and television content and promising IPs, the event continues to serve as a key platform connecting Taiwan with the global content industry.
L47: In particular, the number of exhibitors in IP categories such as publishing, comics, and characters has increased by 13% from last year. This reflects the continued development of global IPs with established fan bases into screen adaptations and diverse licensing opportunities, an increasingly prominent focus in MARKET exhibitors’ strategies.
L48: 
L49: 
L50: 【14†Image†taicca.tw】
L51: TAICCA Chairperson Sue WANG said that MARKET has always served as TCCF’s central hub for connecting the global film and television industry with Taiwan’s wealth of original content. This year’s inaugural Spotlight Screenings offer international buyers and industry partners an early opportunity to experience the quality of the finished works firsthand ahead of the business meetings, with the aim of further facilitating meaningful cross-border transactions and collaborations.
L52: She expressed hope that TCCF would not just be an industry event, but also a steadfast partner supporting Taiwan’s cultural content industry as it moves forward. By bringing together different fields and technologies, TCCF hopes to continue creating diverse opportunities for international cooperation and help Taiwan establish an indispensable strategic position in the global film and television production chain.
L53: The biggest new initiative at this year’s MARKET is TCCF’s first collaboration with Vieshow Cinemas at Mitsui Shopping Park LaLaport Nangang to host the Spotlight Screenings, offering international buyers, overseas media, and other key industry professionals an exclusive preview of two selected high-profile titles, with the aim of boosting overseas sales opportunities and rights transactions for Taiwanese film and television content.
L54: On November 7, the first screening will feature April, which follows a migrant family caregiver working in a Hakka community in Pingtung, Taiwan, who upon returning home faces estrangement and challenges within her own family and embarks on a journey to mend fractured relationships.
L55: Starring LIU Kuan-ting, Angel AQUINO, Esther HUANG, Kaiser CHUANG, YU Tzu-yu , and SHIH Ming-shuai, the film was selected for the 2025 Tokyo International Film Festival and received multiple nominations at the 2026 Taipei Film Awards, including Best Actress.
L56: 
L57: 
L58: The second screening, on November 8, will feature the series See You At 11pm, a fantasy romance spanning Taiwan and Singapore that transcends the realms of the living and the dead. Co-produced by Taiwan’s Three-Giant Production and Singapore’s Mediacorp, the series stars Taiwanese actors LIU Hsiu-fu, Chloe XIANG, WU Nien-hsuan, Peter HO, Kimi HSIA, and Morni CHANG, as well as Singaporean actors Mark Kok Huang LEE , Gavin TEO Chong Ze, and Hong Ling.
L59: A total of 38 international companies and organizations will participate in TCCF MARKET this year, including many first-time exhibitors spanning television, animation, distribution, and other sectors.
L60: These include Nippon TV, Japan’s first commercial television broadcaster; GMA Network, one of the Philippines’ largest commercial television networks; French children’s animation companies Madgic Distribution and Zephyr Animation; BASH, developer of the South Korean healing character IP WASHZ; and Thailand’s Chada Entertainment, founded by Taiwan-based veteran producer Shee Heng KUEK.
L61: 
L62: 
L63: 【15†Image†taicca.tw】
L64: Several South Korean local government agencies and organizations, including Bucheon City, Gwangju Information & Content Agency (GICON), and Daegu Digital Innovation Promotion Agency (DIP), will also lead exhibitors to TCCF MARKET for the first time.
L65: Thailand, meanwhile, will set up a national pavilion jointly organized by the Creative Economy Agency (CEA), Thai Animation and Computer Graphics Association (TACGA), and first-time exhibitor Thai Association of Boys Love Content (TBLC).The Korea Creative Content Agency (KOCCA), returning for its third consecutive year, will be bringing animation IP company Sunwoo & Company and JTBC subsidiary SLL Joongang for the first time, alongside returnees KBS Media and SBS subsidiary Studio S.
L66: Other returning exhibitors include Singapore’s Mediacorp and Japan's TV Tokyo.
L67: On the domestic front, 69 Taiwanese exhibitors will participate this year, spanning a diverse range of sectors including television, animation, publishing, gaming, and content marketing. These include Sanlih E-Television, Videoland, the Chinese Animation and Comic Publishers Association (CCPA), and channels under the Taiwan Broadcasting System such as Public Television Service and Hakka TV.
L68: First-time exhibitors include STR Network, Joint Journey Creative, So-net Entertainment Taiwan, Gamania, and Hope Content Marketing, reflecting the breadth of Taiwan’s content industry ecosystem.
L69: 
L70: 
L71: TCCF MARKET will continue to focus on five major aspects—Copyright Transaction Zone, Production Resource Zone, Animation & Visual Technologies Zone, National Pavilions, and Industry Press Conferences—bringing together production, distribution, and investment professionals across sectors such as television, animation, gaming, and publishing to promote IP adaptation and international collaboration.
L72: The 2026 TCCF will take place from November 6 to 9 at Taipei Nangang Exhibition Center, Hall 2, 7F. For the full list of exhibitors, detailed information on TCCF MARKET events, and how to register for matchmaking activities, please stay tuned to the TCCF official website and social media channels.

L9: GROUNDBREAKING TERMS: Key terms of the Agreement on Reciprocal Trade include:
L10:   * Breaking Down Tariff Barriers for U.S. Industrial and Agricultural Exports: The Taiwan side will eliminate or reduce 99 percent of tariff barriers.  The Taiwan side will provide preferential market access for U.S. industrial exports, including autos and auto parts, chemicals, seafood, machinery, health products, electrical products, metals, and minerals.  It will also provide preferential market access for U.S.
L11: agricultural exports, including horticultural products, wheat, beef and beef products, dairy products, pork and pork products, lamb and sheep meat, tree nuts, dog and cat food, ketchup, and peanuts.
L12:   * Breaking Down Non-Tariff Barriers for U.S. Industrial Exports: The Taiwan side has committed to resolve longstanding non-tariff barriers affecting U.S. exports of motor vehicles, medical devices, and pharmaceuticals.  For U.S. vehicles, Taiwan will remove quantitative restrictions and accept U.S. vehicles built to U.S. Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standards, and emissions standards without additional requirements to enter Taiwan. Taiwan will also accept U.S.
L13: FDA marketing authorizations for medical devices and pharmaceuticals manufactured in the United States without additional requirements for entry into Taiwan’s market. Taiwan will also ensure transparency and fairness regarding reimbursement and listing of medical devices and pharmaceuticals under its health care system.
L14:   * Breaking Down Non-Tariff Barriers for U.S. Agricultural Exports: The Taiwan side has committed to resolve and prevent non-tariff barriers to U.S. agricultural exports, including beef, pork, poultry, and processing potatoes.  It will also work with the U.S. side to complete the regulatory process to allow bison meat into Taiwan.
L15:   * Geographical Indications (GIs) and Market Access: The Taiwan side has committed to groundbreaking provisions that will preserve current and future U.S. market access for U.S. cheese and meat producers who rely on the use of common names.  This includes ensuring that market access will not be restricted due to the mere use of certain cheese and meat terms.  The Taiwan side has also committed to robust standards for transparency and fairness regarding the protection of GIs and to ensure that U.S.
L16: products can continue using terms that have been unfairly protected as GIs.
L17:   * Protecting and Enforcing Intellectual Property: The Taiwan side has made commitments that will benefit American innovators and creators by enhancing intellectual property protection and prioritizing enforcement against intellectual property theft.  This includes aligning with the standards in key international intellectual property treaties.
L18:   * Improving Labor Standards: The two sides recognize the importance of protecting internationally recognized labor rights.  The Taiwan side will prohibit the importation of goods produced with forced labor, and improve protection of labor rights on commercial fishing vessels, address the charging of recruitment fees in certain sectors, prohibit retention of workers’ identity documents, and strengthen enforcement of labor laws.
L19:  The two sides will continue to advance additional changes to enhance protection of workers’ rights to freedom of association and collective bargaining in Taiwan.
L20:   * Strengthening Environmental Protections: The Taiwan side has committed to continue to adopt and maintain high levels of environmental protection and to effectively enforce its environmental laws, including by taking measures to promote a more resource efficient economy as well as to address illegal logging, fisheries subsidies, illegal fishing, and illegal wildlife trade.
L21:   * Confronting Public Enterprises and Subsidies: The Taiwan side has committed to refrain from providing non-commercial assistance or otherwise subsidizing its good-producing public enterprises, except where the actions fulfil a legitimate public services objective, benefit small businesses, or protect its essential security interest.
L22:   * Advancing Trade Facilitation and Good Regulatory Practices: The two sides share common values and are committed to similar approaches to good regulatory practices, customs and trade facilitation, standards and technical regulations, and services and digital trade.
L23:   * Facilitating Mutually Beneficial Investment: The two sides intend to create the best possible environment to promote and facilitate cross-border investments.  The U.S. side will, as appropriate, consider supporting investment financing in critical sectors in Taiwan in collaboration with U.S. private sector partners, consistent with applicable law.
L24:  The two sides also take note of the Memorandum of Understanding signed by AIT and TECRO on January 15, 2026, in which the Taiwan side promises to work with the United States to establish industrial parks and industry clusters that will help the United States substantially increase its domestic production capacity in key high-tech sectors.
L25:  The MOU contemplates that Taiwan companies will significantly increase their investments in the semiconductors supply chain, electronics manufacturing services (including AI applications), energy, and other sectors in the United States, with financial support from the Taiwan side.  The MOU also confirms that the U.S.
L26: side will provide Taiwan preferential treatment with regard to tariffs and other remedial measures in the Section 232 investigation of semiconductors and semiconductor manufacturing equipment, taking into account Taiwan’s economic and national security alignment and high-tech strategic partnership with the United States, as well as in certain other Section 232 investigations.  A link to the Fact Sheet describing the MOU can be found here.
L27:   * Promoting Supply Chain Resilience: The two sides are committed to strengthening cooperation toward their shared goal of enhancing supply chain resilience and will continue to work to finalize commitments on combatting duty evasion.
L28:   * Strengthening Economic Security Alignment: The two sides have committed to strengthen economic and national security cooperation to enhance supply chain resilience.  These efforts will include ensuring that international procurement obligations benefit only those countries that have taken on the same commitments.
L29:   * Increasing Purchases of U.S. Products: The two sides commit to cooperate on ensuring secure, reliable, and diversified sources of supply.  The Taiwan side plans to facilitate a long-term increase in its purchases of important U.S.
L30: goods, including $44.4 billion worth of liquefied natural gas and crude oil, $15.2 billion worth of civil aircraft and engines, and $25.2 billion worth of power equipment, power grids, materials, generators, storage facilities, marine equipment, steel-making equipment, and other equipment from 2025 through 2029.
L31: THE PROSPEROUS PATH FORWARD:  In advance of the Agreement on Reciprocal Trade entering into force, the two sides will undertake domestic formalities, which will include the Taiwan side submitting the Agreement on Reciprocal Trade to its legislature for review.
L32:   * Given the Taiwan side’s commitment to take significant steps to advance a stronger and more reciprocal trade relationship, the U.S. side has committed to reduce its tariffs on originating goods from Taiwan.  It will apply the higher of either the U.S. Most Favored Nation (MFN) tariff rate or a tariff rate of 15 percent, comprised of the MFN tariff and a reciprocal tariff within the meaning of Executive Order 14257 of April 2, 2025, as amended.
L33:   * The U.S. side has also identified certain products from the goods contained in the “Potential Tariff Adjustments for Aligned Partners” Annex to Executive Order 14346 of September 5, 2025 (Modifying the Scope of Reciprocal Tariffs and Establishing Procedures for Implementing Trade and Security Agreements), as amended, that will not be subject to a reciprocal tariff.
L34:   * Two-way trade in goods and services with Taiwan amounted to more than $185 billion in 2024.
L35: LIBERATING AMERICA FROM UNFAIR TRADE PRACTICES: Since Day One, President Trump challenged the assumption that American workers and businesses must tolerate unfair trade practices that have disadvantaged them for decades and contributed to our historic trade deficit.
L36:   * On April 2, President Trump declared a national emergency in response to the unprecedented threat caused to the United States by the large and persistent trade deficit caused by a lack of reciprocity in our bilateral trade relationships, disparate tariff rates and non-tariff barriers, and U.S. trading partners’ economic policies that suppress domestic wages and consumption.
L37:   * President Trump continues to help U.S. farmers, ranchers, fishermen, small businesses, and manufacturers to increase U.S. exports to and expand business opportunities with our trading partners.
L38:   * Today’s announcement provides a tangible path forward with Taiwan that underscores the President’s dedication to bringing balanced, reciprocal trade with an important trading partner.

L589: Article 101
L590: Where a procuring entity finds that a supplier has any of the following circumstances, the entity shall notify the supplier of the facts, reasons related thereto, and the period pursuant to paragraph 1 of Article 103, indicate with a note in the notification that it will be published on the Government Procurement Gazette if the supplier does not file a protest:
L591: 1. where the supplier allows any others to borrow its name or certificate to participate in a tender;
L592: 2. where the supplier borrows or assumes any other’s name or certificate to participate in a tender;
L593: 3. where the supplier has substantially reduced the work or materials without obtaining a prior approval, particularly serious thereof;
L594: 4. where the supplier uses false or untrue documents to tendering, contracting, or performing a contract, particularly serious thereof ;
L595: 5. where the supplier participates in tendering during the period when its business operation has been suspended by a disciplinary action;
L596: 6. where the supplier has committed any of the offenses prescribed in Articles 87 to 92 hereof, and has been sentenced by a court of the first instance;
L597: 7. where the supplier refuses to execute a contract without due cause after award;
L598: 8. where an inspection indicates any particularly serious non-conformity with the contractual requirements;
L599: 9. where the supplier does not fulfill its obligation of guarantee after inspection and acceptance, particularly serious thereof;
L600: 10. where the time-limit for contract performance is particularly seriously delayed due to causes attributable to the supplier;
L601: 11. where the supplier is in breach of the requirement of Article 65 by assigning a contract to others;
L602: 12. where a contract is rescinded or terminated for causes attributable to the supplier, particularly serious thereof;
L603: 13. where the supplier is under the procedure of bankruptcy;
L604: 14. where the supplier discriminates gender, indigenous peoples , people with disabilities, or personnel of disadvantaged groups, particularly serious thereof; or
L605: 15. where the supplier offered, promised, or delivered improper benefits to the personnel in relation to procurement;
L606: Where there is a supplier who shares the liability with the contractor jointly and severally and the entity notifies the supplier to fulfill its liability, the preceding paragraph shall also apply.
L607: Prior to conducting the notification pursuant to paragraph 1, an entity shall provide the supplier an opportunity to give a verbal or written statement, and the entity shall also establish a working and review group to determine whether the supplier has satisfied with any circumstance referred to in paragraph 1.
L608: For the circumstances of particularly serious prescribed in paragraph 1, an entity shall evaluate the extent of damage suffered by the entity, the extent of responsibilities attributable to the supplier, and the actual remedies or compensation measures from the supplier, etc.
L609: Article 102
L610: A supplier who deems that the notification made by an entity according to the preceding Article is in breach of this Act or untrue may file a written protest to the entity within twenty days from the date following the date of receipt of the notification.
L611: The supplier may file a written complaint with the competent CRBGP within fifteen days from the date following the date of receipt of the disposition of the protest referred to in the preceding paragraph if the supplier objects to the disposition, or from the expiry of a period of fifteen days starting from the date following the date the entity receives the protest if the entity fails to dispose the case within such period, irrespective of whether the procurement is of a value reaching the threshold for publication or not.
L612: Where an entity has notified a supplier pursuant to the preceding Article and the supplier does not file a protest or complaint within the prescribed time-limit, or the complaint filed is not accepted, or the review decision indicates that the notification is not in breach of this Act or untrue, the entity shall immediately publish the name of the supplier and the relevant circumstance on the Government Procurement Gazette.
L613: The provisions prescribed in Chapter VI hereof shall apply mutatis mutandis to the handling of protests and complaints provided for in paragraphs 1 and 2.
L614: Article 103
L615: A supplier whose name has been published on the Government Procurement Gazette pursuant to paragraph 3 of the preceding Article is prohibited from participating in tendering, or being awarded or sub-contracted within the following periods:
L616: 1. three years from the next day to the date of publication arising from circumstances of subparagraphs 1 through 5 ,15 of paragraph 1 of Article 101 hereof, or a sentence of imprisonment under subparagraph 6 of paragraph 1 of the same Article; provided that the publication shall be cancelled where the original disposition has been revoked or that a final judgment of acquittal has been entered;
L617: 2. one year from the next day to the date of publication arising from circumstances of subparagraphs 13, 14 of paragraph 1 of Article 101 hereof; or sentence of short-term imprisonment, fine, or probation under subparagraph 6 of the same Article; provided that the publication shall be cancelled where the original disposition has been revoked or that a final“judgment of acquittal” has been entered; or
L618: 3.
L619: arising from circumstances of subparagraphs 7 through 12 of paragraph 1 of Article 101 hereof, three months from the next day to the date of publication, provided that the supplier’s name has never been published by any entity within the past five years not later than the date of notification; six months from the next day to the date of publication, provided that the supplier’s name has been published once by any entity within the same five years; one year from the next day to the date of publication, provided that the supplier’s name has been published twice or more by any entity accumulatively within the same five years.
L620: The publication shall be cancelled where the original disposition has been revoked.
L621: Where there is a special need for an entity to conduct a procurement and is necessary to procure from the supplier referred to in the preceding paragraph, without following the requirement of the preceding paragraph, the entity may do so with an approval from its superior entity.
L622: Before effective of the articles revised on April 30, 2019, where a disposition notification has been made pursuant to paragraph 1 of Article 101, but the disposition has not yet become final, the provisions of the revised articles shall apply.
L623: Article 104
L624: Procurement conducted by military entities shall follow the provisions of this Act except where procurement is for weapons, ammunition, war supplies, or related to national security or national defense and has one of the following situations:
L625: 1. where this nation is confronted with a war, in mobilization for a war, or in a war, this Act may not apply;
L626: 2. where the procurement is a confidential or strictly confidential one, Articles 27, 45 and 61 hereof may not apply;
L627: 3. where there is an emergency which may jeopardize an important military mission, Articles 26, 28, and 36 may not apply; or
L628: 4. where there is only one supplier invited for tendering, the first sentence of paragraph 3 of Articles 26 may not apply;
L629: The coverage of procurement referred to in the preceding paragraph and the regulations thereof shall be prescribed jointly by the responsible entity and the Ministry of National Defense, and be sent to the Legislative Yuan for reviewing.
L630: Article 105
L631: The provisions of invitation to tender and award of contracts of this Act may not apply to the following procurement:
L632: 1. where the procurement is an emergent one in response to wars, natural disasters, epidemics, or economic or financial crises confronted by this nation;
L633: 2. where the procurement is an emergent one in response to imminent danger to the life, body, health, or property of the people;
L634: 3. where the procurement is an acquisition of property or service between governmental entities and approved by an immediate superior entity; or
L635: 4. where the procurement from an international organization, a foreign government, or any of their authorized institutions is conducted in accordance with a treaty or agreement to which this nation is a party and which has specific provisions for the invitation to tender and award of contracts.
L636: Where it is necessary to prescribe regulations for procurement referred to in the preceding paragraph, the regulations shall be prescribed by the responsible entity.
L637: Article 106
L638: Except otherwise prohibited by the treaties or agreements to which this nation is a party, a procurement conducted by an entity stationed abroad either for itself or as agent may be exempt from the provisions of the following subparagraphs in response to local circumstances or the constraint of local practices, provided that the ways to handle the matters referred to in the following subparagraphs 2 through 4 shall be specified in the tender documentation.

L640: 2. The bid bond and guarantee bond under Article 30 hereof;
L641: 3. The granting of priority in price reduction, and price reduction and comparison under paragraph 1 of Article 53 and Article 54 hereof; and
L642: 4. The protest and complaint set forth in Chapter VI hereof.
L643: Where a procurement referred to in the preceding paragraph is of a value reaching the threshold for supervision, a report explaining the reasons shall be submitted to the superior entity together with relevant documents for file afterwards.

L652: Article 109
L653: 
L654: For procurement conducted by an entity, an auditing entity may audit it at any time.
L655: 
L656: Article 110
L657: The Comptroller of the Directorate-General of Budget, Accounting and Statistics, the Assistant Auditor-General of the Ministry of Audit, or the Prosecutor of the Ministry of Justice may bring an action, join the parties of an action, or appeal on behalf of an entity for a procurement case.
L658: 
L659: Article 111
L660: An entity shall report annually to the responsible entity the service condition and the effectiveness analysis of a subject procured in a large procurement during the service period, and the responsible entity may send its personnel to inspect for verification.
L661: The responsible entity shall have an annual effectiveness analysis for the important procurement that have been done, and the outcomes shall be published on the Government Procurement Gazette except those shall be confidential.
L662: 
L663: Article 112
L664: The responsible entity shall prescribe ethics regulations for the procurement personnel.
L665: 
L666: Article 113
L667: 
L668: The enforcement rules of this Act shall be prescribed by the responsible entity.
L669: 
L670: Article 114
L671:
