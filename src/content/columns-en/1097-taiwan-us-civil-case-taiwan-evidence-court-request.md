A Taiwan court asked to obtain evidence for a U.S. civil case investigates under Taiwan's procedural rules. [Article 6 of the Law in Supporting Foreign Courts on Consigned Cases](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawAll.aspx?pcode=B0010014) requires the request to identify the parties, the type of evidence, the person to be examined and the matters to investigate. A request for “everything relevant” leaves that preparation unfinished.

For an American business seeking testimony from a Taiwan supplier's former employee, the initial file should establish who the witness is, where the person can be located and what the person is expected to know. Those facts do not establish that the witness must comply with every request sent by U.S. counsel, or that a Taiwan court has already agreed to act.

## The request comes from a court

[Articles 1–4](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawAll.aspx?pcode=B0010014) set the framework. The law applies to assistance entrusted by foreign courts in civil or criminal matters unless a treaty or another law provides otherwise. Assistance must not conflict with Taiwan law. Transmission must be in writing through diplomatic authorities.

Reciprocity is another condition. Article 4 requires the requesting court's country to state that it will provide equivalent assistance when a Taiwan court needs help in the same or a similar matter. A private company's promise is not that statement. U.S. and Taiwan counsel should address the requirement while preparing the court request.

The [State Department's Taiwan guidance](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/legal/Judicial-Assistance-Country-Information/Taiwan.html), marked last updated November 15, 2013, identifies letters rogatory as a route to obtain documents or testimony from an unwilling witness. Counsel should confirm the current transmission and hearing arrangements before relying on that operational guidance. That is a judicial-assistance process, not an automatic conversion of an American discovery demand into a Taiwan order. The U.S. court's requirements and any independent U.S.-law obligations still need their own assessment.

## Identify the person and the investigation

Article 6 calls for more than a witness's job title. Its required particulars include the person's name, nationality and domicile, residence, office or business address, together with the parties' names, evidence type and matters to be investigated. An old corporate email address alone may not establish where a former employee can be reached.

The factual questions need similar care. If the disputed issue is whether a factory accepted a specification change, identify the relevant order, dates and communications, then connect each proposed question to the witness's involvement. This is a preparation method. The statute does not prescribe the same questions for every supply dispute.

According to the State Department's guidance for the letters-rogatory route, the court uses written questions and does not permit attorneys to conduct the examination. This is the procedure described in that dated country guide; it is not a general statutory ban on attorney questioning in every Taiwan proceeding. It also advises expressly requesting the full Chinese transcript. Request the transcript expressly when using that procedure. A plan built only around counsel's live follow-up questioning may not supply what the receiving court needs.

## The Chinese version carries the substance of the request

[Article 7](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawAll.aspx?pcode=B0010014) requires a Chinese translation of a foreign-language request and related documents, with a statement that it accurately corresponds to the original. The State Department's guidance also calls for certified Mandarin Chinese translations. The accuracy statement belongs with the translation.

Names, company identities and defined technical terms should remain consistent between the questions and attachments. If a question refers to an exhibit, the translated reference must lead to the same document. Otherwise, the witness and court may be working from different versions. Counsel can check that correspondence before transmission, rather than relying on a later interpreter to repair an unclear written request.

Costs need attention too. [Article 8](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawAll.aspx?pcode=B0010014) applies Taiwan's relevant fee rules to service or investigation in civil matters. The State Department separately identifies charges for the assistance route. The cited provisions and guidance set no guaranteed turnaround for this request. Counsel needs to account for transmission, translation and the requested investigation when planning the American litigation schedule.

## A willing witness raises a different planning question

Voluntary depositions are addressed separately in the same dated guide, which describes contacting the American Institute in Taiwan (AIT) Taipei to arrange one with a willing witness. It leaves private litigants responsible for arrangements such as reporting and interpretation. Confirm current arrangements directly with AIT. This article does not confirm appointment availability or a particular remote-deposition format.

Where the underlying lawsuit is in Taiwan instead, a request for the opponent's records falls under a different procedure, discussed in [document production in Taiwan civil litigation](/en/columns/taiwan-civil-us-company-opponent-document-production). Identifying which court is handling the main case prevents the two applications from being mixed together.
