## Verdict
naturalness (1 = machine, 5 = native professional): 3/5 for guidance pack, 3/5 for columns
variety used: standard Estonian (ühiskeel), consistent; no Võro and no Finnish mix. Formal **teie** is the right register for a law firm, and capital **Teie** in a heading is normal Estonian courtesy. Decimal comma is right in “0,5 kuu”; thousands still follow German dots.
systemic patterns (max 6, each one line, with 1 example quote):
- Dates stay in the nominative after a verb that needs a case: “jõustus 27. detsember 2023” (also “muudeti 29. mai 2026”, “allkirjastatud 17. november 2021”).
- German thousands and an English million: “TWD 500.000” and “TWD 1.57M”.
- Digit-memo style: “põhimõtteliselt 1 järgmistest kriteeriumidest”, “enne 2. astme suulise arutelu”, “registreerimine 1 nimele”.
- Calques a Tallinn firm would not publish: “mitteavalikus objektisalves”, “Teie kirjutamiskeel”, “Kuva keel”, “Eelnevalt öeldud number”.
- English left on the team page: “Paralegal”, “Senior Paralegal, Boyin Law Firm”, “Institute of Finance, National Taiwan University”.
- Blog voice in 003 Q16–Q20, 005, 006, 008 and 009: one sentence per line, “Olen Wei Tseng”, “Kas teie olete kogenud Taiwani varaste aastate”.

Gender: no masculine title, pronoun or agreement for Wei Tseng. Estonian **ta** / **advokaat** are epicene; the bio correctly says “Ta esindas”; first-person columns use “Olen”. “Härra B” and “härra Lin” are male third parties. Consultation-language lock and the negative no-Estonian / no-interpreter / no-reply-time / no-free-first-meeting lines are intact. 005 labels the Korea bank step and asks about hiring foreigners, not Koreans. 003 Q17–Q20 already soften the English sentence predictions and drop “maximum compensation”. 001–002 and 004 are dense but editable legal Estonian once the case endings and the two 004 mistranslations are fixed. 006, 008 and 009 read as a translated blog. Frontmatter `url` in 008 and 009 is Hangul because those are the original post URLs (same as English); leave them.
