# Injured at a Taiwan Amusement Park: Does the 'No Liability' Line on Your Ticket Hold?

Visitors often assume a sign reading "the park accepts no liability for any accident" ends the matter. At a Taiwan park licensed as a tourist amusement enterprise (觀光遊樂業), that sentence alone does not block a claim. The Ministry of Transportation and Communications has published terms forbidding such wording, and the Consumer Protection Act voids terms that break them. Voiding the clause does not itself pay damages.

## The ticket, the board and the website are all contract terms

Under the Consumer Protection Act, a standard-form term is one a business prepares in advance for use with many consumers. Posted notices, signboards (牌示) and online text count too ([art. 2](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0170001&flno=2)(7)).

On April 1, 1999, the Ministry first announced mandatory and prohibited terms for amusement service contracts (觀光遊樂業遊樂服務契約應記載及不得記載事項). The December 8, 2009 amendment (Jiao-Lu (1) No. 0980011343) is the latest text on the Tourism Administration's site as of November 3, 2026. Four kinds of terms are barred:

1. blanket exemptions for force majeure, such as "no refunds for rain or power cuts";
2. a "no liability at all" (概不負責) line excluding liability for intent or gross negligence;
3. a term denying all compensation merely because a visitor broke the usage rules, warning signs or staff instructions;
4. a ban on re-entry later the same day.

[Article 17](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0170001&flno=17) of the Act underpins such announcements: violating terms are void (para. 4), and mandatory terms bind even when left out (para. 5).

Unlisted wording can fail: standard-form terms that breach good faith and are obviously unfair to the consumer are void ([art. 12](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0170001&flno=12)(1)). Liability for intent or gross negligence cannot be waived in advance ([Civil Code art. 222](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=222)). Pre-drafted terms excluding or reducing the drafter's liability are void where obviously unfair ([art. 247-1](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=247-1)(1)).

What about a clause covering only ordinary negligence? The announcement is silent. Courts apply arts. 12 and 247-1.

It covers businesses approved to run tourist amusement facilities ([Act for the Development of Tourism art. 2](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=K0110001&flno=2)(11)). They need a licence before opening ([art. 35](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=K0110001&flno=35)(1)) and must display a licence emblem at the entrance ([Regulations for the Management of Tourist Amusement Enterprise art. 18](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=K0110018&flno=18)(1)). At an unlicensed hotel-pool slide the announcement may not apply directly, but the three articles above still do.

## Safety is the operator's to prove

[Consumer Protection Act art. 7](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0170001&flno=7) requires service providers to ensure the safety reasonably expected under the technology or professional standards of the time, with conspicuous warnings and emergency instructions where a service could harm life, body, health or property. Breach means joint liability; proving no fault only lets the court reduce the award. An operator claiming its service met that standard must prove it ([art. 7-1](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0170001&flno=7-1)(1)).

Rules of use must be posted at the ticket office, entrance and any website ([Regulations art. 19](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=K0110018&flno=19)(1)), and each ride needs warning and restriction signs ([art. 33](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=K0110018&flno=33)(2)). Height limits qualify.

## Civil Code routes

Intentional or negligent wrongful injury creates liability, as does breaching a statute that protects others unless no fault is proven ([Civil Code art. 184](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=184)). Employers answer jointly for staff acting on duty unless they show due care in selection and supervision, or that the damage would have occurred anyway ([art. 188](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=188)(1)).

For defective structures, [art. 191](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=191) makes the owner of a building or other work on land liable. Escape requires showing no defect in installation or maintenance, no causal link to one, or due care to prevent the damage. The Building Act lists mechanical amusement facilities as miscellaneous works ([art. 7](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0070109&flno=7)). Owner, not operator. Facilities may be leased or run under management contracts ([Regulations art. 23](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=K0110018&flno=23)), so the ticket seller and the owner can differ.

Buying a ticket also forms a contract. Unsafe service attributable to the operator, staff fault included unless otherwise agreed ([art. 224](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=224)), is incomplete performance ([art. 227](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=227)); where it injures the person, [art. 227-1](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=227-1) applies the tort damages rules in arts. 192–195 and 197. Recoverable loss covers damage suffered and lost profits ([art. 216](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=216)(1)), lost or reduced earning capacity and added living needs ([art. 193](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=193)(1)), and solatium for non-pecuniary harm ([art. 195](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=195)(1)).

## A child below the height limit

A 110 cm child queues for a water slide marked "120 cm and above". The attendant does not measure. The child hits their head in the splash pool (an invented example).

Can the park point to the sign and walk away? Item 3 of the prohibited terms says no. The breach counts. Where the injured person's fault contributed to the damage, a court may reduce or deny compensation, and the same applies to the fault of their representative ([Civil Code art. 217](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=217)(1), (3)), so a parent's carelessness may count. Parks must also assign ride operators and qualified lifeguards with rescue gear ([Regulations art. 34](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=K0110018&flno=34)(1)).

## Records the park keeps

Under item 5 of the mandatory terms, operators must help with rescue or hospital transfer when a visitor falls ill or is hurt, and are liable for damage caused by failing to.

Mechanical, water, land and aerial facilities need safety inspections, with passing documents displayed at each ride ([Regulations arts. 32](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=K0110018&flno=32)(1), [33](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=K0110018&flno=33)(1)). Operators file their own inspection records before the 5th of January, April, July and October ([art. 36](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=K0110018&flno=36)(1)). Local authorities inspect by the end of April and of October; a non-compliant or dangerous facility stays closed until it passes re-inspection ([art. 37](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=K0110018&flno=37)(2), (3)). Mechanical rides also need a use permit and two or more yearly safety inspections ([Building Act art. 77-3](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0070109&flno=77-3)), due by May 31 and November 30 unless otherwise set ([Regulations for Installation and Inspection of Mechanical Amusement Facilities art. 9](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0070019&flno=9)(1)), with a copy of the certificate posted at each ride ([art. 12](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0070019&flno=12)(1)).

## Insurance minimums and direct claims

Licensed operators must insure against liability ([Act for the Development of Tourism art. 31](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=K0110001&flno=31)(1)).

| Minimum cover (NT$) | Tourist amusement enterprise ([Regulations art. 20](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=K0110018&flno=20)(1)) | Mechanical amusement facility ([art. 7](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0070019&flno=7)) |
|---|---|---|
| Bodily injury or death, per person | 3 million | 2 million |
| Bodily injury or death, per accident | 30 million | 10 million |
| Property loss, per accident | 2 million | 2 million |
| Total for the policy period | 64 million | 24 million |

These are floors; nothing in these articles caps liability at the insured sum. Uninsured operators face a fine of NT$30,000 to NT$500,000, must be ordered to insure by a deadline, and risk licence revocation ([art. 57](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=K0110001&flno=57)(3)). A March 2026 Executive Yuan check of six parks passed all six on building safety and public liability insurance; four had deficient service contracts (press release, May 14, 2026).

Art. 31(4)'s no-fault direct claim covers travel agency itineraries only. For parks, [Insurance Act art. 94](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0390002&flno=94)(2) allows a direct claim against the insurer, in the proportion due within the insured amount, once the operator's liability is established; the insurer may pay directly earlier on the operator's notice ([art. 95](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0390002&flno=95)).

## After you fly home

Complaints go to the operator, a consumer group or a consumer service centre; if the operator fails to handle one properly within 15 days, the local consumer protection officer is next ([Consumer Protection Act art. 43](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0170001&flno=43)), then a mediation committee ([art. 44](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0170001&flno=44)). Suit may be filed where the consumer relationship arose ([art. 47](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0170001&flno=47)). Suits under the Act allow punitive damages up to five times the loss for intent, three times for gross negligence and once for negligence ([art. 51](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0170001&flno=51)). Claims lapse two years after learning of the damage and the liable party, or ten years after the act ([Civil Code art. 197](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=197)(1)).
