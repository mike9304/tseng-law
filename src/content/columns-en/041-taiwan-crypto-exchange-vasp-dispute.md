# When a Taiwan Crypto Platform Freezes Your Withdrawal

You try to withdraw coins or cash from a platform serving customers in Taiwan. The request stays pending. Support stops replying, asks for another payment, closes the account, or announces that the business has collapsed. You need to know who holds the assets, what the platform promised, and what can still be preserved.

Save what you can see now. An account balance is useful evidence, but it does not by itself identify a wallet under your control or prove that the operator still holds the displayed assets. The route in Taiwan depends on the contracting entity, its assets, the account terms, and whether the facts indicate a civil breach, suspected crime, or both.

## Is the operator on Taiwan's current VASP list?

Start at the [Securities and Futures Bureau's VASP page](https://www.sfb.gov.tw/ch/home.jsp?id=1053&parentpath=0%2C8), marked **updated September 29, 2026**. Compare the platform's contracting company's full legal name and Taiwan unified business number (統一編號), not merely its app or trading brand. Open the linked PDF for the categories of virtual-asset service the company has registered to provide. The page separately lists operators that completed AML registration, one that applied to cancel its own registration, and operators from the old compliance-declaration system that did not complete registration and can no longer provide virtual-asset services. Preserve a dated copy of the relevant entry and the platform's own legal-entity disclosure.

This list is a check of **anti-money-laundering registration**, not a promise that your balance is recoverable or that every act of the company is lawful. [Article 3 of the FSC registration regulation](https://law.fsc.gov.tw/LawContent.aspx?id=GL004000) requires registration by business category and expressly says a VASP still needs any permission required under other laws. Even for registered firms, the FSC's [official bulletin](https://www.fsc.gov.tw/uploaddowndoc?file=chdownload%2F202511241727400.pdf&filedisplay=%E9%87%91%E8%9E%8D%E5%B1%95%E6%9C%9B%E6%9C%88%E5%88%8A%E4%B8%AD%E6%96%87_11411.pdf&flag=doc) warns that “虛擬資產無漲跌幅限制容易暴漲暴跌，交易風險高” (virtual assets have no daily price limit and trading risk is high). A missing name is a serious point to investigate, but it does not alone resolve every question about a platform's cross-border conduct or your contractual counterparty.

## Which VASP rules apply today?

[Article 6 of the Money Laundering Control Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0380131&flno=6) says a VASP must complete the required AML registration before providing virtual-asset services. For an overseas operator providing those services **in Taiwan**, it also requires a Taiwan company or branch registration and the relevant AML registration. Its fourth paragraph makes provision of services without the required registration, or continued provision after revocation, a criminal offence punishable by up to two years' imprisonment, detention, or a fine of up to NT$5 million, alone or combined. This describes the statutory rule, not a finding that a particular exchange has committed an offence.

The FSC's [VASP registration regulation](https://law.fsc.gov.tw/LawContent.aspx?id=GL004000) implements that scheme. Its Article 13 requires registered VASPs to establish a complaint process and handle customer disputes fairly and promptly; Articles 14 and 15 address disclosures and records. Ask a registered operator for its complaint procedure and a written explanation of the hold, citing the exact withdrawal and account identifiers. Do not assume those duties create an immediate right to bypass a documented, lawful account restriction; the reason for the restriction must be checked.

The Legislative Yuan [passed](https://www.fsc.gov.tw/ch/home.jsp?id=96&parentpath=0&mcustomize=news_view.jsp&dataserno=202606300002&dtable=News) the **Virtual Asset Service Act** on June 30, 2026, and it was promulgated on July 22. Yet the [FSC's official law page](https://law.fsc.gov.tw/LawContent.aspx?id=GL004301) still marks its provisions as not implemented and Article 56 leaves the effective date to the Executive Yuan. **As of September 30, 2026, do not treat that Act's future licensing and civil-liability provisions as already in force.** The current AML registration rules above remain the starting point for this check.

## What should I preserve before the account changes again?

Download your account history, deposits, trades, withdrawal requests, rejection messages, fiat bank transfers, identity-verification submissions, and the displayed balance with timestamps and currency units. Keep the transaction hashes and public-chain explorer links for transfers, together with wallet addresses; a hash proves a chain event, while ownership and the platform's obligation need further evidence. Save the terms of service and risk notices that applied when you opened the account **and** any later version, plus emails, support tickets and chats. Record the exact legal name on each receipt, bank payee, invoice and website footer.

Make a simple timeline: when you paid, what was credited, when you requested withdrawal, what the platform replied, and any new demand for a “release” payment. Keep original files and headers where possible, not only cropped screenshots. If records are at risk of disappearing, [Code of Civil Procedure Article 368](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=368) permits an application to preserve evidence where it may be lost or become hard to use; it also covers certain examinations or preservation of documents when there is a legal interest and need. A Taiwan lawyer can assess whether the evidence and court requirements fit your case.

## Is a civil claim or asset preservation possible?

First identify the party that promised custody, exchange, redemption or withdrawal. The contract, account rules and the operator's actual performance determine a possible claim for delivery or payment; a displayed token quantity may require valuation and proof of ownership. [Civil Code Article 229](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=229) addresses delay where performance is due, including the role of a demand when no fixed date was set; [Article 227](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=227) addresses attributable incomplete performance. Which remedy fits a frozen withdrawal depends on the promised performance and the reason for the hold. Send a precise written demand for the withdrawal or a reasoned account of the hold, and retain its delivery record. If the platform has shut down, promptly map the legal entity and any reachable Taiwan assets. A judgment against the wrong company is of little practical use.

For a monetary claim, [Article 522](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=522) allows a request for provisional attachment to preserve later enforcement. [Article 523](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=523) requires a risk that enforcement will become impossible or very difficult. Under [Article 526](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=526), the creditor must make a prima facie showing of the claim and the reason for attachment; the court may require security. A platform's silence is evidence to evaluate, not an automatic attachment order. If the counterparty or assets are abroad, jurisdiction and enforcement need separate analysis. For a foreign judgment that may later need to be enforced against Taiwan assets, see [enforcing a foreign judgment in Taiwan](/en/columns/enforce-foreign-judgment-in-taiwan).

## When should suspected crime be reported?

If the facts point to deception, misappropriation, forged records, or unregistered Taiwan operations, preserve the same evidence and consider a report to Taiwan police or prosecutors. Explain events and identify wallets, people and companies without calling every failed withdrawal fraud. The AML offence in Article 6 is distinct from any offence involving your assets. A criminal investigation can obtain evidence or trace transfers, but filing a report does not itself return the assets or replace a civil claim. If someone demands another transfer to “unlock” a withdrawal, save the demand and check it before paying: the FSC's bulletin specifically warns about supposed unfreezing fees, deposits and taxes used in scams.
