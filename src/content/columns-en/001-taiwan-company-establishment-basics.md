> First, you need to decide the type of entity.

There are three types.

I will explain the advantages and disadvantages of each type.

![](../images/001-taiwan-company-establishment-basics/img-02.jpg)



**1. Subsidiary (company limited by shares, limited company):**

It has an independent legal personality and enjoys the same rights and obligations as a Taiwanese company. If the scale is large enough, it can be listed, but it must pay 20% profit-seeking enterprise income tax, and when dividends are paid to foreign shareholders, 21% non-resident income tax must be paid.

**- However, under the Arrangement between Korea and Taiwan for the Avoidance of Double Taxation and the Prevention of Fiscal Evasion, which took effect on December 2, 2023, double taxation is prevented: if there is no permanent establishment (and the service period is less than 183 days within 12 months), business profits are exempt, and the maximum tax rate on dividends is 10%.**

**2. Branch:**

It has no legal personality and only business capacity. It cannot be listed. After paying 20% profit-seeking enterprise income tax, profits may be remitted to the foreign head office. It must be 100% owned by the Korean company, and other Taiwanese persons cannot participate as shareholders.

**3. Representative office:**

It has neither legal personality nor business capacity, and may only perform legal acts such as signing contracts, issuing quotations, and making purchases.



![](../images/001-taiwan-company-establishment-basics/img-03.jpg)

> Next, there are 10 steps to set up a company in Taiwan.



1. Search and reserve the company’s Chinese name

2. Authenticate and notarize the power of attorney

3. Submit the investment application to the Ministry of Economic Affairs

4. Open a preparatory company bank account

5. Remit operating capital

6. Capital audit

7. Company registration

8. Tax registration

9. Convert the temporary account into a formal account

10. Apply for import/export certificates (if needed) / apply for a work permit & ARC (if needed)

Many documents must be submitted at each stage.

For example, the most important investment application requires preparation and submission of an investment plan. By preparing a power of attorney for a Taiwan attorney,

it is safer and more convenient to have a Taiwan attorney or accountant help prepare and submit the materials.

> Common issues foreigners face when setting up a company in Taiwan



**1. I am a student. Can I set up a company?**

- Yes, you can. Whether a foreigner is a student or a working adult, they may set up a company in Taiwan.

**2. Are all industries allowed?**

- Almost all industries are possible. However, certain business items—such as medical devices, alcohol imports, travel agencies, and construction—require government approval in Taiwan. In addition, immigration companies, real estate brokerage, accounting firms, law firms, and similar businesses require relevant professional licenses.

**3. Can I obtain a Taiwan visa if I set up a company?**

- Yes, you can. If the investor needs to manage and operate the company in Taiwan, they may apply to Taiwan’s Ministry of Labor for a foreign work permit. After obtaining the work permit, they may apply to the National Immigration Agency for an ARC. The foreign work permit is issued for 1 to 3 years, and the ARC and work permit share the same validity period.

**4. After obtaining a visa, can I invite my family?**

- Yes, you can. A spouse and minor children may reside in Taiwan as family members.

**5. Is there a minimum capital requirement?**

- There is no minimum capital requirement for company setup in Taiwan. You can form a company with as little as TWD 1, but if capital is too low, it may raise questions from the Investment Commission of the Ministry of Economic Affairs, and it may be difficult to explain the investment plan. However, a minimum capital amount is required to obtain a work permit and ARC. A sole shareholder must invest at least TWD 500,000; if there is a Taiwanese partner, at least one-third of the minimum capital—about TWD 170,000—must be invested. In addition, the company’s annual revenue must exceed TWD 3 million.

**7. If I obtain a visa, can I obtain permanent residency?**

- Yes, you can. If you hold a work permit and ARC for five consecutive years and reside in Taiwan for 183 days or more each year, you may apply for permanent residency.

**8. Is an address registration required when setting up a company?**

- Yes. Company setup requires both “company registration” and “tax registration.” Therefore, the company must register a lawful commercial address.

**9. How does taxation work in Taiwan?**

- First, companies must pay business tax (VAT) at a rate of 5%, filed every two months.

- Each May, companies must file profit-seeking enterprise income tax for the previous year, at a rate of 20%.

- When the company distributes profits to foreign shareholders, foreign shareholders pay 21% non-resident income tax. (However, if a foreigner resides in Taiwan for 183 days or more, individual income tax rates start from 5%, the same as for Taiwanese residents.)

Recently — under the Arrangement between Korea and Taiwan for the Avoidance of Double Taxation and the Prevention of Fiscal Evasion, which took effect on December 2, 2023 — if there is no permanent establishment, business profits are exempt, and the maximum tax rate on dividends is 10%.
