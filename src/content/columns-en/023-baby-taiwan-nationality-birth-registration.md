# A Baby in a U.S.–Taiwan Family: Citizenship, Birth Records and Taiwan Household Registration

A hospital birth certificate, a U.S. Consular Report of Birth Abroad, a Taiwan household transcript and a passport answer different questions. Families can avoid missed steps by identifying what each document proves before planning the baby's first journey.

The starting facts are where the child was born, each parent's nationality at that time, the legal parent-child relationship and the Taiwanese parent's household-registration status. Having one American parent and one Taiwanese parent does not make every filing path identical.

## Check nationality at birth before choosing documents

Under [Article 2 of Taiwan's Nationality Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0030001&flno=2), a person whose father or mother was a Republic of China national at birth falls within a statutory nationality category. The provision also covers other specified situations. It does not confer nationality on every child merely because the child was born in Taiwan. Evidence of the parent's nationality and legally established parentage is important, particularly where the parents are unmarried or the records disagree.

For a baby born outside the United States, consult the [State Department's citizenship-transmission guidance](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/legal/travel-legal-considerations/us-citizenship/Acquisition-US-Citizenship-Child-Born-Abroad.html). The applicable rules depend on the parents' citizenship and marital circumstances, the legal relationship to the child and required U.S. physical presence. Do not apply a single residence formula to every family. Gather the U.S. parent's relevant history and ask AIT which evidence is needed for this child.

## A Taiwan birth: keep the hospital and government records distinct

Check the baby's name, date and place of birth, and the parents' identifying details on the hospital record. Then ask the household registration office which Taiwan birth-registration route applies. The [Department of Household Registration's guidance](https://www.ris.gov.tw/documents/html/2/3/4/702.html) distinguishes domestic birth registration from initial household registration in other situations. The parent's actual household status matters; nationality alone does not mean the baby already has a household record or identity card.

If the child acquired U.S. citizenship at birth abroad, a Consular Report of Birth Abroad records that acquisition. The [State Department's birth-abroad guidance](https://travel.state.gov/en/international-travel/living-abroad/birth.html) explains this function. A CRBA is not a procedure that grants citizenship to an otherwise ineligible child, and the child's U.S. passport is a separate document. Confirm the current Taiwan appointment and application arrangements with AIT rather than treating a general worldwide checklist as a local filing instruction.

## A U.S. birth: identify the Taiwan settlement category

A U.S. birth record does not automatically enter the baby into the Taiwanese parent's household. Taiwan's [National Immigration Agency settlement guidance for nationals without household registration](https://www.immigration.gov.tw/5385/7244/7250/7281/定居/36430/) sets out relevant application categories, including certain children born abroad. Eligibility depends on facts such as the parent's Taiwan household registration at the time of birth, the child's age and the documents or passport used for entry.

For an eligible category, settlement permission can lead to initial household registration. Ask NIA to identify the exact category before assuming either immediate eligibility or a mandatory period of residence for every child. The household office then handles its part of the registration. If the Taiwanese parent also lacks household registration, or parentage and parental consent need clarification, those facts must be raised first.

Ask the Taiwan representative office responsible for the U.S. document's jurisdiction and the receiving Taiwan authority about verification and Chinese translation. Have them review the document type and issuing authority before ordering extra copies. Settlement, initial household registration and passport issuance are separate procedures. An eligible overseas-born child may obtain an ROC passport before settlement and household registration; see [BOCA’s overseas-born first-passport guidance](https://www.boca.gov.tw/fp-27-22-83434-1.html). After initial household registration through the cited NIA route, however, the first departure must use an ROC passport bearing the assigned national identification number, as NIA’s notice (7) explains.

## Plan travel around the baby's actual status

Prepare a list of the child's established nationalities and issued documents. Confirm the passport and entry requirements with the relevant U.S. and Taiwan authorities before booking travel. The [State Department’s passport guidance for children under 16](https://travel.state.gov/en/passports/apply/child/under-16.html) explains citizenship evidence, parental consent and the child’s application. A parent's passport, the hospital certificate or a pending CRBA does not stand in for the child's required travel document. For Taiwan passport questions, check the BOCA overseas-born guidance above; [BOCA’s household-office identity verification guidance](https://www.boca.gov.tw/cp-35-6012-14fbe-1.html) concerns eligible applicants who already have Taiwan household registration.

If the family's marriage or parentage record needs attention first, see [the U.S.–Taiwan marriage registration checklist](/en/columns/marrying-taiwanese-national-registration-checklist). For a consultation, provide the child's birthplace, each parent's nationality and household status at birth, the parents' marriage or acknowledgment records, any existing registrations and the intended travel date.
