## Issues requiring correction

### M1 — An unestablished sequence is stated as certain

원문/Original (`drafts/C8/en.md:56`): “A clip ending at the first confrontation would leave out evidence of how the dispute affected passing traffic.”

→ Problem & reason: This makes a definite claim that relevant passing-traffic evidence occurred after the first confrontation. The indictment gives the parking interval, describes getting out and knocking on the window, and records 27 passing cars slowing, including four activating hazard lights. It does not timestamp those passing cars relative to the first confrontation or establish what a clip ending at that moment would contain. The evidence list identifies footage and an inspection record; it does not reproduce the sequence needed to support this precise assertion. Calling the paragraph an evidential lesson does not substantiate the factual premise. Under the requested facts-only standard, this unsupported sequence is a major issue.

Source: `cases/jud/19.txt`, attached indictment, 犯罪事實一 (“自當日19時47分10秒至19時50分6秒間”) and 證據並所犯法條一㈣ (“共計27輛汽車行經A車、B車右側車道時被迫減速，其中4輛更點亮警示燈”). Neither passage supplies individual passing times relative to the window confrontation. The fact sheet itself labels the sentence an editorial inference and acknowledges that exact passing times are unavailable.

→ Fix (required; not applied): Replace the definite sequence claim with an explicitly conditional recommendation, for example: “A clip limited to the confrontation could omit evidence of how the stop affected passing traffic.” Retain the adjacent explanation that this is an evidential lesson, not a recording requirement imposed by the court. Alternatively, remove the disputed sentence; the preceding sentences already explain the value of preserving the recording.

→ Facts/conditions preserved: The 27 cars, four hazard-light activations, stopping interval, recording's evidential role and absence of a court-imposed recording requirement all remain unchanged. No order or timestamp is invented for individual passing vehicles.

### M2 — Gendered pronouns are unsupported by the supplied record

원문/Original (`drafts/C8/en.md:2`, repeated at line 18): “He stopped in Route 66's fast lane to confront the driver behind”. Additional examples at lines 24, 26 and 48 include “His explanation”, “he said”, “let him overtake”, “his position”, “He confessed” and “his personal circumstances”.

→ Problem & reason: The column assigns male pronouns to both the defendant and the following driver. The supplied judgment and indictment identify their procedural and driving roles but do not expressly establish either driver's gender. Names, generic Chinese references and contextual assumptions are not sufficient verification. The fact sheet supplies no primary-source support for these gender assignments. This does not establish that the pronouns are wrong; it establishes that the supplied material does not substantiate them. Under the instruction that unsupported factual details are major, this requires correction before publication.

Source: `cases/jud/19.txt`, judgment and attached indictment in full. The defendant and complainant are referred to through role labels and anonymized identifiers; no express gender identification supports the English pronouns.

→ Fix (required; not applied): Use role descriptions or singular “they/their” consistently for both drivers. For example, the title and H1 can read “A driver stopped in Route 66's fast lane to confront the motorist behind”; “let him overtake” can become “let the following driver overtake”; the sentencing paragraph can use “the defendant's admission of wrongdoing and remorse” and “the defendant's motive, the degree of danger created and personal circumstances”. Avoid obtaining or adding unnecessary personal information merely to retain gendered wording.

→ Facts/conditions preserved: Keep the defendant distinct from the complainant, preserve attribution of each driver's account, and preserve the defendant's admission, remorse and sentencing factors. No change is required to the conduct, legal findings, sentence, dates, amounts or citations.
