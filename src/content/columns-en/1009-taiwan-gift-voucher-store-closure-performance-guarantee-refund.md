# Your Taiwan Gift Voucher Shop Closed or Claims It Expired: Who Pays Now?

A paid gift voucher (禮券) sold in Taiwan has to say who protects its face value for at least one year from the date of sale, such as a bank guarantee or a trust account. So say the voucher rules (商品(服務)禮券定型化契約應記載及不得記載事項), announced by the Ministry of Economic Affairs and eight other authorities on April 10, 2020 and in force since January 1, 2021.

Picture a NT$3,000 cake voucher bought at a Taipei bakery in December 2025, and a closure notice on its door in October 2026 (an invented example). The back of the voucher names a bank and a guarantee period from December 20, 2025 to December 31, 2026. Two months are left.

## Which vouchers are covered

The preamble covers certificates, magnetic-stripe cards, chip cards and similar instruments issued for a set amount, which the holder presents to the issuer or its nominee for goods or services of that value. Free coupons and discount vouchers are excluded, as are electronic tickets (電子票證) under the 電子票證發行管理條例.

Payment is the dividing line. A 2009 reply from the Executive Yuan's consumer protection commission (消保法字第09800097482號, October 23) put a "spend NT$5,000, get NT$500" department store coupon outside the rules because the shopper paid nothing; it may carry a fully disclosed expiry date. The reply predates consolidation, but the preamble keeps the same exclusion.

Before 2021 each trade had its own voucher rules; the retail version took effect on April 1, 2007 (Ministry of Economic Affairs letter 經商字第09600629350號, September 17, 2007). Today one text applies, and its annex spreads 19 trades across nine authorities: retail to Economic Affairs, restaurants and bakeries to Health and Welfare, tourist hotels to Transportation, cinemas to Culture.

## Two things to find on the voucher: the sale date and the guarantee

Point 1 lists six items: issuer details, face value, serial number and date of sale, how to use it, a complaints channel, and the performance guarantee, which must state that the issuer stays liable after the guarantee period ends.

When does the year start? On the date of sale. Even when a travel agency resells the vouchers, cover must run at least a year from the consumer's purchase, according to the Executive Yuan's Department of Consumer Protection (院臺消保字第1050009980號, March 7, 2016). A paper voucher with no sale date is already out of line. Card or other electronic vouchers that cannot show everything may print only the issuer, the guarantee and the complaints line, but must disclose the rest reasonably and offer a way to check transactions at any time (point 6).

Gaps are common. On March 11, 2026, the department reported 15 of 20 restaurant voucher issuers in breach. Six left out the sale date and seven a guarantee period of at least a year. In three, the guarantee itself failed: a trust shorter than the printed period, or no money deposited. Buyers can verify cover through the method the issuer must provide, such as a bank's voucher trust webpage (point 2(4)).

## The guarantee decides where you claim

Issuers choose one of five methods listed in point 2(1).

| Method printed on the voucher | What the rules say | Who to contact after closure |
|---|---|---|
| Full guarantee by a financial institution (item 1) | The institution guarantees the full face value | The named guarantor |
| Trust account (item 2) | Face value deposited in advance in a dedicated trust account | The bank holding the account |
| Escrow (item 3) | A bank or electronic payment institution holds the money in a dedicated account | The named escrow provider |
| Joint guarantee by a peer company (item 4) | A same-trade company guarantees; the holder may claim goods or services up to face value | The named peer company |
| Other method approved by the authority (item 5) | Details and approval number printed prominently | As the approval provides |

Item 4 is the most specific: the guarantor may not object, discriminate, or charge anything. If a retail issuer uses this method, a Ministry of Economic Affairs announcement of April 21, 2021 (經商字第11002409100號) applies. Grade A means paid-in capital of at least NT$30 million and net operating revenue of at least NT$100 million; meeting one test is grade B. Revenue for both grades is the prior year's figure in CPA-audited statements. A grade-A issuer needs a grade-A guarantor in the same trade, a grade-B issuer one of grade B or above, and both companies' articles must allow such guarantees.

For bank guarantees and trusts, the rules stop at "guaranteed" or "deposited in trust." Documents and payment timing depend on the issuer's agreement with the institution, so take the voucher and receipt there and ask. With the bakery voucher, asking before the printed period ends on December 31, 2026 is the safer course; whether the institution pays after that depends on its agreement.

Third-party issuers face a higher bar. An issuer that does not supply the goods or services itself needs paid-in capital or operating funds in Taiwan of at least NT$30 million, a figure the authority may adjust by announcement. An issuer below it may still issue with the authority's approval, printing the year, number and term of that approval on the voucher. Either way, only items 1 to 3 are allowed: a bank guarantee, a trust or escrow (point 5).

## "Sorry, it has expired"

No expiry date: that is the whole of point 1 of the prohibited terms. Point 2 bars clauses that forfeit an unused balance, and point 8 bars terms less favourable than paying cash. The 2026 inspection flagged holiday exclusions and refunds net of postage or transfer fees.

What if an expiry date is printed anyway? It has no effect. A standard-form term breaching an announced rule is void, the rest of the contract is assessed under [art. 16](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0170001&flno=16), and mandatory terms apply even when left out ([Consumer Protection Act art. 17](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0170001&flno=17)(4), (5)).

Often the printed date is simply the end of the bank's or trust's cover. The issuer's own duty continues; point 1(6) makes it say so on the voucher.

Issuers ordered to correct a breach who fail to do so face fines of NT$30,000 to NT$300,000, then NT$50,000 to NT$500,000, repeatable each time ([Consumer Protection Act art. 56-1](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0170001&flno=56-1)). The voucher money still has to be claimed separately.

## Refund fees and replacements

The voucher must state the refund procedure and amount (point 4). A fee of up to 3% is allowed, but none when the refund stems from something not attributable to the consumer. A shop closure is hard to place on the customer's side.

Damaged but legible vouchers can be exchanged, and lost registered ones reissued, for no more than NT$50 per paper voucher or NT$100 per card (point 3).

## No guarantee, or the guarantee has run out

If there was never any cover, or the institution declines under its agreement once the period ends, the issuer is left. Complain to the business, a consumer protection group or a consumer service centre; the business must respond properly within 15 days. Next come the city or county consumer ombudsman and then the local consumer dispute mediation committee ([Consumer Protection Act arts. 43](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0170001&flno=43) and [44](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0170001&flno=44)). Claims of NT$100,000 or less go to the [small-claims track](/en/columns/taiwan-small-claims-simplified-civil-procedure).

A liquidator of a company limited by shares must publish at least three notices asking creditors to file within three months; late claims are left out unless already known ([Company Act art. 327](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0080001&flno=327)). Late creditors are covered in our [liquidation claims article](/en/columns/taiwan-company-liquidation-creditor-claim-notice).

In a bankruptcy, the voucher becomes a bankruptcy claim, enforceable only through the proceedings ([Bankruptcy Act arts. 98](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010006&flno=98) and [99](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010006&flno=99)). The court sets a filing period of 15 days to three months from the declaration and publishes it; creditors who miss it cannot be paid from the estate ([art. 64](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010006&flno=64)(1), [art. 65](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010006&flno=65)(1)(5)). Priority claims are paid first and the rest is shared in proportion ([arts. 112](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010006&flno=112) and [139](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010006&flno=139)), so there is no assurance of recovering the full face value.

Guarantee still running and the issuer bankrupt? Do both. The bank or trust claim follows the voucher and its agreement, while a missed filing period shuts you out of the estate.
