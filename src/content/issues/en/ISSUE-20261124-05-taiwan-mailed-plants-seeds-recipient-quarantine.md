# Seeds in a parcel to Taiwan: the recipient's quarantine obligation

Seeds, bulbs and wood are among the items Taichung Customs says it has found in international parcels without plant quarantine. Its [November 18 warning](https://web.customs.gov.tw/taichung/singlehtml/274?cntId=97bf9627f3c3446c8c5263a5fb6bd717), [reposted on the Ministry of Finance website by its Customs Administration on November 23](https://www.mof.gov.tw/singlehtml/384fb3077bb349ea973e7fc6f13b6974?cntId=d1a3ab4ad0764afa81686c2332d01f05), addresses overseas purchases and packages sent by friends or relatives. The concern is the pests those contents may carry.

A resident whose relatives mail seeds from abroad may still have a step to take after the parcel is delivered. If it arrives without quarantine clearance, the recipient can be required to apply immediately to Taiwan's plant quarantine authority.

## Two routes through the postal restriction

Under [Article 13-1 of Taiwan's Plant Protection and Quarantine Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=M0140001&flno=13-1), the central authority may designate plants, plant products and other items posing a pest-transmission risk for import quarantine. These are regulated articles. Import status can still differ from one wooden or processed plant item to the next. Unlisted items can also be subject to treatment, return shipment, destruction or other necessary measures if the quarantine authority finds a pest-transmission risk.

[Article 17, paragraph 3](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=M0140001&flno=17) generally prohibits importing regulated articles by mail and requires their return shipment or destruction. It provides two alternative exceptions: the quarantine authority has announced an exemption from presenting a phytosanitary certificate, or the recipient has applied for and obtained advance import approval.

Calling the contents a gift does not create another exception in Article 17, and an overseas seller's willingness to ship says nothing about whether either statutory route applies.

## Permission, a certificate and clearance serve different purposes

The exporting country's plant quarantine authority issues the phytosanitary certificate. [Article 16](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=M0140001&flno=16) generally requires the importer or agent to present it, subject to announced exemptions. Where a required certificate is missing or fails to meet the quarantine conditions, the authority must take one of the specified measures: requiring the certificate documents to be supplemented within a set period, treatment, return shipment or destruction. The importer bears the expense.

Mailing through an exception does not end the process. Article 17, paragraph 4 requires the package to identify its contents clearly. The postal service must cooperate with the quarantine authority in notifying the recipient to apply.

What happens if the parcel arrives without proof that it passed quarantine? The recipient must apply immediately. The relevant authority is Taiwan's Animal and Plant Health Inspection Agency and its subordinate offices. An exemption from presenting an exporting-country certificate does not remove this recipient obligation.

## The fine attaches to a specified failure

A recipient who fails to apply under Article 17, paragraph 4 faces the fine specified in [Article 24, paragraph 1, subparagraph 6](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=M0140001&flno=24). The range is NT$30,000–150,000. The provision also covers an importer or agent who fails to apply at arrival, opens the packaging before quarantine is complete, or moves the articles without authorization.

Receipt alone is not that violation. [Article 25-2](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=M0140001&flno=25-2) also permits reduction or waiver for minor failure-to-apply cases, subject to the competent authority's criteria. That provision offers no general promise that a small family parcel will escape a penalty.

For a regulated parcel already delivered, the useful facts are its exact contents, the applicable postal exception and the quarantine documents accompanying it. If the recipient has no proof of clearance, the immediate application duty still applies.
