# Taiwan's 2027 wage fines will use a common three-year repeat window

From January 1, 2027, Taiwan's revised labor-penalty principles will use a common three-year period to count repeat violations of the same provision. [Focus Taiwan reported the Ministry of Labor's announcement on November 9](https://focustaiwan.tw/society/202611090016). For a foreign-invested employer preparing next year's payroll, earlier Taiwan penalty decisions are part of that preparation.

The [ministry's release](https://www.mol.gov.tw/1607/1632/1633/100074/) also says local governments must publish their penalty schedules, including amounts that increase with repeated violations. Its revised common principles take effect in January. The minimum-wage obligation already applies.

## An older violation can remain relevant

According to the ministry, breaches of the Labor Standards Act's basic-wage provisions and of the Minimum Wage Act within the three-year period will count as violations of the same provision. The switch between those statutory bases therefore does not, by itself, separate the violations for this calculation.

That history can be easy to miss. In an international reporting system, a payroll manager may see a corrected rate while the earlier penalty decision remains in a separate legal file, perhaps indexed under a statutory provision different from the one used in a later case. The records serve different purposes. What is being paid now, and which earlier violations may affect a later penalty?

The ministry's announcement provides a common counting period, while local authorities publish the amounts used in their schedules. It gives no universal second-offense or third-offense figure. An estimate for a particular Taiwan employer therefore needs the applicable local schedule and the employer's own record, rather than an assumed nationwide escalation formula.

## The existing wage duty and sanctions

Coverage comes first. Under [Article 3 of the Minimum Wage Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0030028&flno=3), the Act applies to workers covered by the Labor Standards Act. The discussion does not extend automatically to every person providing services to a business.

[Article 5](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0030028&flno=5) prohibits an agreed wage below the statutory minimum and replaces a lower agreed figure with that minimum. An employee's acceptance of the lower amount does not establish the lawful wage. This duty is already operative; the Act took effect in January 2024, as its [legislative history](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawHistory.aspx?pcode=N0030028) records.

For a violation, [Article 17](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0030028&flno=17) sets an ordinary fine range of NT$20,000 to NT$1 million. It also permits an increase above the statutory maximum by up to one-half based on business scale, the number of affected workers or the circumstances. Accumulated violations are already among the factors the statute permits authorities to consider.

Publication is also mandatory. Article 17 requires authorities to publish specified details of the penalty and order improvement; failure to improve by the deadline brings further penalties. For an employer, the decision can therefore have a public record as well as a financial consequence.

## The employer behind the payroll

MOL's announced NT$50,000 starting floor covers specified categories, including listed and over-the-counter companies, companies with paid-in capital exceeding NT$100 million, and medical centers. This is a category-specific starting amount. The announcement does not establish that a Taiwan subsidiary belongs to that category merely because its overseas parent is publicly listed.

For headquarters and local HR, the relevant entity's identity belongs alongside its capital information, previous penalty decisions and local schedule. A group-wide payroll report may be useful, but it may not show which business received a particular Taiwan penalty. The earlier decision's named employer, cited provision and date give the company a concrete basis for reviewing how its own record fits the announced three-year approach.
