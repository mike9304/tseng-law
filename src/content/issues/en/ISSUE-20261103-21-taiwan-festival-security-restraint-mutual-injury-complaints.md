# Grabbed by festival security in Taiwan: the Nuit Blanche scuffle and its two injury complaints

Two men, two sets of head and upper-body abrasions, and two promised complaints. That is how [United Daily News described the end of a scuffle](https://udn.com/news/story/7320/9850092) on the night of October 31 near the Yuanshan section of Taipei Expo Park, where Taipei's Nuit Blanche festival overlapped with Halloween crowds.

According to the police account in that report, a 52-year-old American believed a 24-year-old security worker had deliberately pushed him in the crowd and hit the worker on the head. The worker moved to restrain him, and the two fought. Both went to hospital on their own and were not seriously hurt. The worker gave a statement. The American was to be summoned. Police said both would be referred to prosecutors on suspicion of causing injury. None of this has been tested by a prosecutor or a court.

## Who may restrain whom

Event security staff are not police. Taiwan's criminal procedure law still gives everyone a narrow power: [Article 88 of the Code of Criminal Procedure](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0010001&flno=88) lets any person arrest someone caught in the act of an offence or immediately after it. The power comes with a duty. Under [Article 92](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0010001&flno=92), a person without investigative authority who makes such an arrest must hand the arrested person over at once to a prosecutor or the police.

Self-defence works in both directions. [Article 23 of the Criminal Code](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0000001&flno=23) excuses acts that defend oneself or another against a present unlawful infringement, while excessive defence may still be punished, at a reduced level or not at all. In a crowd, everything turns on sequence. Was there a deliberate push? Did the blow come first? Did the restraint continue after the threat had ended?

## Two complaints, one incident

Simple injury under [Article 277(1)](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0000001&flno=277) carries up to five years' imprisonment, short-term detention or a fine of up to NT$500,000. [Article 287](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0000001&flno=287) makes it an offence prosecuted only on complaint, unless a public official commits it while on duty. A festival security worker is not normally a public official.

Each man can therefore be a complainant in one matter and a suspect in the other. The deadline is six months from the time the complainant learns who the offender is ([Code of Criminal Procedure, Article 237](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0010001&flno=237)). Settlement and withdrawal follow their own rules, set out in our columns on [who can withdraw an injury complaint and until when](/en/columns/taiwan-complaint-only-offense-settlement-withdrawal-deadline) and on [settling before the money arrives](/en/columns/taiwan-criminal-settlement-withdraw-complaint).

## A fine that does not depend on a complaint

There is a second track. The [Social Order Maintenance Act, Article 87](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0080067&flno=87), sets a fine of up to NT$18,000 for assaulting a person or for mutual fighting. Police decide fine-only cases themselves after questioning ([Article 43](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0080067&flno=43)). When the same conduct is also referred as a suspected crime, [Article 38](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0080067&flno=38) sends the criminal part to prosecutors but keeps the fine within the Act. Withdrawing a criminal complaint does not, on the face of these provisions, erase that administrative track.

## Where a residence permit comes in

The [Immigration Act, Article 32(iii)](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0080132&flno=32), requires the National Immigration Agency to revoke a residence permit after a final sentence of one year's imprisonment or more, except for negligent offences or where the sentence is suspended. Revocation on that ground can in turn lead to deportation under [Article 36](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0080132&flno=36). A complaint, a police interview or a prosecutor's referral is not a final sentence. Visitors on short stays have no residence permit to lose, though a pending case can affect travel; see [how Taiwan courts handle exit bans on foreign defendants](/en/columns/taiwan-criminal-exit-ban-foreign-defendant-temporary-travel).

For now, the reported case has reached only the first stage: one statement taken, one summons to come. What the crowd, the cameras and the medical notes show about the first push and the first blow will shape both complaints.
