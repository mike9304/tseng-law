# Can your Taiwan Gold Card cover work outside its approved field?

Yes. An American who received a Taiwan Employment Gold Card (就業金卡) through the digital field may take professional work in another industry. Taiwan's official Gold Card FAQ calls the card an open work permit and says it allows “any professional work without industry restrictions.” The limiting word is professional.

Changing fields can still change the tax result. The same FAQ says that when the profession does not match the field indicated on the card, the holder is ineligible for the Gold Card tax benefits. Work authorization and the income-tax concession therefore require two separate readings.

## The approved field qualifies the holder, not the employer

[Article 4 of the Act for the Recruitment and Employment of Foreign Professionals](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=A0030295&flno=4) (外國專業人才延攬及僱用法) supplies three definitions that matter here. A foreign professional (外國專業人才) is a foreign national who may engage in professional work in Taiwan. A foreign specific professional (外國特定專業人才) is a foreign professional with special expertise that Taiwan needs in one of the fields the statute lists, such as technology, digital or economics, as announced by the central authority for that field, or whose special expertise the competent authority recognizes after consulting the relevant central authority. “Professional work” (專業工作) is then tied to listed statutory categories. It does not mean every paid activity.

The Gold Card itself comes from [Article 9](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=A0030295&flno=9). A foreign specific professional who plans to do professional work in Taiwan may apply directly to the National Immigration Agency (內政部移民署) for a single card combining a work permit, resident visa, Alien Resident Certificate (外僑居留證), and re-entry permit (重入國許可). Its validity is one to three years. The Agency reviews the application together with the Ministry of Labor (勞動部) and the Ministry of Foreign Affairs (外交部).

That route differs from employer sponsorship. Under [Article 8](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=A0030295&flno=8), an employer hiring a foreign specific professional for professional work may receive an employment permit lasting up to five years, with extensions of up to five years each. Article 9 instead puts the work permit on the card itself. The [official work FAQ](https://goldcard.nat.gov.tw/en/tags/work) calls that permit open and says employers do not need to apply for a separate one.

## Marketing, university teaching, and freelance work

A Taiwan company's marketing offer is not barred merely because the card was approved in the digital field. The [field-mismatch FAQ](https://goldcard.nat.gov.tw/en/faq/can-technology-or-economic-companies-hire-gold-card-holders-who-applied-under-the-field-of-education-or-culture-arts) answers that technology or economic companies may hire holders approved under education or culture and the arts, because the card allows professional work without industry restrictions. Yet the work must remain professional. [Article 46 of the Employment Service Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0090001&flno=46) (就業服務法), which Article 4 incorporates in part, includes specialized or technical work (專門性或技術性之工作). A marketing title, standing alone, does not show whether the duties meet that category.

University teaching has a more specific statutory home. Article 46 includes teachers at public or registered private colleges and universities, and Article 4 brings that category into “professional work.” A part-time lecture appointment may therefore fall within the Gold Card's work permission when the institution and role fit the provision. The same Article 4 also includes employment in lecturing or academic research at a public or registered private university, where that work is recognized by the Ministry of Education, through [Article 48](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0090001&flno=48) of the Employment Service Act. A one-off talk, an unregistered provider, or duties outside a teaching appointment cannot be treated as covered without checking the actual arrangement.

Freelancing is also possible. The official work FAQ says a Gold Card holder may be self-employed or start a company. A separate remote-work answer describes the open permit as freedom to engage in professional work in Taiwan. So a holder can serve clients without asking each client to sponsor a permit, but the open permit does not erase Article 4's boundary. Is the proposed service professional work as the Act defines it? That question belongs in the contract and scope of services, not in the label “freelance.”

## The tax concession follows related professional work

As of November 17, 2026, [Article 22 of the Foreign Professionals Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=A0030295&flno=22) provides a five-year tax window with several conditions. It begins with the first tax year in which the eligible person both resides in Taiwan for at least 183 days and earns more than NT$3 million in salary income. For each qualifying year with at least 183 days of residence, half of the salary income above NT$3 million is excluded from consolidated income for tax purposes. Article 22 leaves the detailed conditions to a Ministry of Finance regulation, so possession of the card is not enough.

The [income-tax regulation's Article 3](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0340150&flno=3) connects those conditions to the approved expertise. A Gold Card holder must have first received residence approval because of work, must do professional work related to the recognized special expertise, and must have had neither Taiwan household registration nor Taiwan income-tax resident status during the five years before the card was issued. During the card's validity, the person must be employed in professional work. Two exceptions sit in paragraph 5. If an earlier residence permit was granted for a reason other than professional work, the first-approval rule does not bar the holder. And for someone who studied in Taiwan and earned an associate degree or higher, the years of study and any post-graduation extension under Article 12 of the Act are left out of the day count used to decide tax residence.

What income receives the reduction? [Article 4 of the regulation](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0340150&flno=4) defines the relevant salary income as taxable salary received from a Taiwan or foreign employer for the related professional work. Changing employers or jobs during the five-year window does not end the concession when the new work still meets that related-work condition. By contrast, the official FAQ states that work outside the field shown on the Gold Card is ineligible for the Gold Card tax benefits.

Actual duties matter more than a job title. Digital marketing may use the holder's recognized digital expertise, while another marketing role may not. [Article 5 of the regulation](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0340150&flno=5) sets the filing rules. The claim goes in with that year's income-tax return, in the prescribed form, before the filing deadline; missing it means that year cannot use the concession. A Gold Card holder attaches proof of the first work-based residence approval, a copy of the card, and a contract or other proof that the work relates to the recognized expertise. If the circumstances in Article 3(4), or another reason not attributable to the holder, prevent receipt of the card before the filing deadline, the holder must apply within one month after receiving it. Where the tax office doubts that connection, it may ask the relevant central authority for an opinion. Freelance receipts also should not be assumed to receive the salary concession: Article 22 and the regulation describe qualifying salary income, not every form of business revenue.

## Why the ordinary side-gig rule is not the answer

[Article 43 of the Employment Service Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0090001&flno=43) sets the ordinary baseline: unless that Act provides otherwise, a foreign national must not work in Taiwan without a permit applied for by an employer. [Article 2 of the Foreign Professionals Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=A0030295&flno=2), however, makes that Act the first source of rules for foreign professionals doing professional work, and Article 9 places the work permit in the Gold Card. That is why a Gold Card employer does not file a second permit.

Articles [73](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0090001&flno=73) and [74](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0090001&flno=74) should not be stretched into a field restriction that their text does not contain. Article 73 addresses an employer-hired foreign worker's employment permit, including work for a different employer or self-directed work outside that permit; Article 74 addresses expiry or revocation of such a permit. The official FAQ describes the Gold Card's permit as open. For the employer-specific regime, see [the separate discussion of side gigs and another employer](/en/columns/taiwan-work-permit-side-gig-other-employer); the [general Gold Card guide](/en/columns/taiwan-employment-gold-card) covers the card's application structure.

One unresolved edge is work outside Article 4's definition. Neither the statute nor the official FAQ says that the card authorizes every kind of work. Before accepting an offer that may not be professional work, the holder should not rely on the card's industry flexibility alone.
