# Injured on a Taiwan sidewalk: a US visitor’s state-compensation claim after returning home

Taiwan's State Compensation Law has a nationality-related condition that a foreign visitor can miss. Under [Article 15](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=I0020004&flno=15), a foreign victim comes within the law only where a treaty, the home country's law or its practice gives Taiwan nationals rights equivalent to those enjoyed by that country's own nationals. A US passport or Taiwan residence card does not by itself answer that question.

## Public-facility liability needs a causal connection

[Article 3(1)](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=I0020004&flno=3) imposes state liability where a defect in the installation or management of a public facility causes harm to life, body, personal liberty or property. The location alone is not enough. The particular defect and its connection to the injury remain matters of proof.

Photos showing both the defect and the surrounding route can help identify the location. Lighting, barriers, warnings and witness contact details may matter to how the fall happened. Taiwan's [pre-lawsuit evidence-preservation procedure](/en/columns/taiwan-civil-evidence-preservation-before-lawsuit) may be relevant where records could disappear.

## The city’s maintenance contractor is not the state-compensation claim recipient

Management contracted out to a private business does not remove state liability for a management defect: Article 3(2) expressly addresses that situation. [Article 9(2)](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=I0020004&flno=9) identifies the installing or managing agency as the responsible agency, and the commissioning agency where management was entrusted to a private party.

What if two departments each refer you elsewhere? Article 9(4) allows the superior agency to be asked to determine responsibility when it cannot be established or is disputed. If that superior agency fails to make the determination more than twenty days after being asked, it may itself be treated as the responsible agency. A dated request and proof of receipt are therefore useful; informal calls do not necessarily establish that this statutory process was invoked.

## A written claim starts the compensation procedure

A claimant must first request compensation in writing from the responsible agency under [Article 10](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=I0020004&flno=10). [Article 17 of the Enforcement Rules](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=I0020005&flno=17) requires prescribed identifying information, the facts, grounds and evidence, the amount or restoration requested, and the agency, among other particulars. The claimant or agent signs or seals the request.

The agency must enter negotiations. An agreement must be recorded in writing. That document can support enforcement. A message saying the department will investigate the broken pavement has not yet achieved that result.

A refusal to compensate ends the need to await a negotiated agreement before suing. If the agency has not refused, [Article 11(1)](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=I0020004&flno=11) also permits an action once more than thirty days have elapsed from the request without negotiations starting, or more than sixty days from their start without agreement. The representative handling the Taiwan correspondence needs the request receipt and any refusal, not merely updates that the agency is still investigating.

The damages action follows civil procedure except where the compensation law provides otherwise ([Article 12](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=I0020004&flno=12)). Article 11(1) bars bringing the claim again if compensation for the same underlying facts has already been sought as an ancillary claim in administrative litigation.

## Two limitation periods, neither starting at the airport

[Article 8(1)](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=I0020004&flno=8) provides two years from knowledge of the damage and an outside period of five years from the occurrence of the damage. Discovering the correct department is not the stated starting point. Returning to the US does not reset either period.

Demanding payment does not preserve the claim indefinitely. The Civil Code applies through [Article 5](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=I0020004&flno=5); its [Article 129](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=129) recognizes interruption by demand, but [Article 130](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=130) treats that interruption as ineffective if no action is brought within six months after the demand. Negotiation dates and any litigation deadline need to be planned together.

## Treatment in the US and the amount claimed

Continuing treatment after departure does not make every later medical bill recoverable. Records must connect the Taiwan injury to the treatment and show why it was needed and what it cost; lost earnings and care needs require evidence of their own. Relevant Civil Code provisions include [Article 193](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=193) on lost or reduced working capacity and increased living needs, and [Article 195](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=195) on nonpecuniary injury.

Pedestrian fault can affect the result. [Civil Code Article 217](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=217) permits a court to reduce or remove compensation where the victim's fault contributed to the damage or its increase. That is why photographs of the usable walking route and any warnings may be relevant to more than the facility's condition.

## What a representative can do while you are abroad

From the US, a claimant can authorize a Taiwan representative to conduct negotiations. The authority document must be submitted at the first negotiating act under [Article 7 of the Enforcement Rules](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=I0020005&flno=7). Its scope matters if negotiations produce an offer: abandoning or withdrawing the claim and receiving compensation require special authority under [Article 8](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=I0020005&flno=8). A later lawsuit requires submission of litigation authority under [Code of Civil Procedure Article 69](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=69); the negotiation appointment should not be assumed to settle that separate requirement.
