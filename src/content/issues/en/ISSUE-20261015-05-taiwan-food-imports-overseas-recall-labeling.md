# Nissin noodle recall in Australia: the Taiwan label and local duties

Taiwan imports of a Nissin noodle product carried Chinese labels declaring milk, the Taiwan Food and Drug Administration (TFDA) said in a CNA report published by [Focus Taiwan on October 14, 2026](https://focustaiwan.tw/society/202610140013). The report said an October 9 Australian recall concerned an undeclared milk allergen. TFDA said it had confirmed the Taiwan labeling with the importer, so the same labeling problem did not arise for those shipments. That account addresses the reported allergen-label discrepancy; it is not a general assurance about every aspect of the product's safety.

For a US or other overseas food supplier selling through a Taiwan importer, a recall notice raises a specific operational question: does the stock here share the reported defect? The brand name alone cannot answer it. Product specifications, the Taiwan packaging and shipment records allow the importer to assess whether the overseas finding applies locally.

## Taiwan packaging has its own requirements

[Article 22 of the Act Governing Food Safety and Sanitation](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=L0040001&flno=22) requires the containers or outer packaging of food and food raw materials to display prescribed information conspicuously in Chinese and common symbols. That information includes ingredients, the manufacturer or responsible domestic business, origin and expiration date. The article also authorizes the central authority to prescribe additional labeling items. A label prepared for another market does not establish compliance with these Taiwan requirements.

The [Ministry of Health and Welfare's allergen-labeling rule](https://www.mohw.gov.tw/cp-16-43376-1.html), effective July 1, 2020, covers cow's milk, goat's milk and their products, except lactitol derived from them. It permits specified allergen statements or a product name that fully identifies the allergenic ingredients. The legal question therefore concerns the contents and permitted labeling method, not simply whether the package contains a translated ingredient list.

Supplier records also matter when two markets receive different packaging. [Article 9](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=L0040001&flno=9) requires food businesses to retain documents identifying the sources of ingredients, semi-finished products and finished products. A formal system tracing sources and destinations is a separate requirement for categories and scales of business designated by the central authority. The provision does not impose an identical electronic system on every small importer.

In practice, the relevant comparison may involve the affected lot identifiers, ingredient specification and label actually attached to Taiwan stock. A packaging design approved for a future order does not show what was on goods already delivered to retailers. Records connecting those goods to their supplier help explain why an alert does or does not affect them.

## A discovered risk calls for immediate action

[Article 7(5)](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=L0040001&flno=7) requires a food business that discovers its product may pose a risk to sanitation and safety to act immediately: stop manufacturing, processing and selling it, arrange a recall, and notify the competent city or county authority. The duty is triggered by discovery of a risk. The business does not have to wait for an actual injury or a separate Taiwan recall order before that duty arises.

An overseas alert may supply evidence of such a risk. It may instead identify a defect limited to packaging used elsewhere, as TFDA described in this report. Which conclusion is justified depends on the product and records. A distributor's general reassurance cannot substitute for evidence about the affected shipments.

There is also a separate regulatory route for defective labeling. Under [Article 52(1)(3)](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=L0040001&flno=52), when statutory inspection or testing establishes specified labeling violations, the local authority must require recall and correction within a deadline. The goods cannot continue to be sold before correction. That mechanism does not make relabeling an adequate answer to every safety defect, nor does it postpone the business's own immediate duty under Article 7 when the risk threshold is met.
