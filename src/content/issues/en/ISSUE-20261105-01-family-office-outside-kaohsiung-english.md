# What should a Taiwan bank’s family-office agreement cover?

A bank's family-office proposal may place your will, shares and family records in the hands of several professionals. Who is doing each job? Taiwan's [rules for banks providing family-office advisory services](https://law.fsc.gov.tw/LawContent.aspx?id=GL004340&media=print) require disclosure of the bank's relationship with third parties and each provider's services and responsibilities.

Issued on 8 October 2026, the rules permit applications from banks already approved for and conducting high-asset business that have run an approved family-office pilot in an asset-management zone for at least a year. Point 10 requires application documents, including a business plan. Eligibility is only the starting point. [CNA reported on 1 November](https://www.cna.com.tw/news/afe/202611010020.aspx) that at least seven banks intended to apply to operate outside the zone; that report does not establish a particular branch's current approval.

## Follow the fee and the information

Point 7 requires the bank to disclose when fees arise, how they are calculated and how they are collected. It prohibits the bank and a third-party provider from charging twice for the same service. Fee sharing or transfers to a provider must also be disclosed, alongside complaint channels and dispute procedures.

Consent comes first. Under point 8, the bank needs the customer's prior written consent before disclosing, referring or jointly using customer information with a cooperating third party. Keep that written consent. Point 6 requires checks on that provider's qualifications and arrangements for responsibility, disputes and data protection.

Point 3 also addresses professional work: where a task legally belongs to another professional institution or requires specific regulatory approval, the bank must cooperate with the appropriate institution within the legal framework. Ask which contract covers drafting the will, making a filing and responding if the documents are defective.

## Check what has actually been completed

An advice agreement is not evidence that every legal formality has been met. For a will governed by Taiwan law, [Civil Code article 1187](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=1187) preserves restrictions protecting compulsory shares. Families with different nationalities and assets in several countries also need a governing-law analysis.

[Trust Act article 4](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=I0020024&flno=4) requires trust registration for registrable property rights before the trust can be asserted against third parties. For shares or corporate bonds, notice to the issuing company is required to assert the trust against that company. A family charter does not itself show those steps were completed.
