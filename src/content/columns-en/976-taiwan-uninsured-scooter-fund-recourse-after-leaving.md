# An uninsured scooter, a rider who left Taiwan, and a court that still granted the fund all NT$643,759

On August 10, 2016, the rider left Taiwan and never came back. The court knew this from a letter by Taiwan's National Immigration Agency. On October 20, 2017, the Taiwan High Court ordered the rider to pay a further NT$600,000 plus interest, on top of the NT$43,759 the first-instance court had already granted. Together, the two courts granted the Fund's entire NT$643,759 claim ([Taiwan High Court civil judgment 106 Shang-Yi No. 548](https://www.mvacf.org.tw/ckfinder/connector?command=Proxy&type=Attach&currentFolder=/tblResearches/&fileName=%E8%87%BA%E7%81%A3%E9%AB%98%E7%AD%89%E5%9C%B0%E6%96%B9%E6%B3%95%E9%99%A2%E6%B0%91%E4%BA%8B%E5%88%A4%E6%B1%BA106%E5%B9%B4%E5%BA%A6%E4%B8%8A%E6%98%93%E5%AD%97%E7%AC%AC548%E8%99%9F.pdf&c=7&p=e52ce99c79b747929d729912c03f3f9a&l=3)). No appeal was allowed.

It was not the person injured in the crash who sued. It was the Special Compensation Fund for motor vehicle accidents (財團法人汽車交通事故特別補償基金), the foundation that pays victims when the vehicle involved is uninsured or cannot be traced.

## A collision at an alley entrance in Sanchong

The facts in the judgment are brief. On the morning of January 15, 2014, the rider, a Hong Kong resident, hit the victim at an alley entrance on Sanhe Road in Sanchong, New Taipei. The victim suffered fractures to the cheekbone and both hands, a facial laceration, optic nerve damage and a subdural haematoma. The motorcycle carried no compulsory automobile liability insurance.

Motorcycles are covered by that scheme ([Compulsory Automobile Liability Insurance Act, Article 5](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0390060&flno=5)). The owner is in principle the person who must buy the policy, and must renew it before it ends ([Article 6](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0390060&flno=6)). With no insurer to claim from, a victim can instead ask the Special Compensation Fund to pay, up to the statutory insurance amounts ([Article 40(1)(ii)](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0390060&flno=40)).

That is what happened here. The Fund paid NT$43,759 in medical benefits and NT$600,000 as a disability benefit.

## Who the Fund goes after

[Article 42(2)](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0390060&flno=42) lets the Fund, once it has paid, exercise the victim's own claim against the person liable for the damage, capped at what it paid. Here that person was the rider. Under [Article 191-2 of the Civil Code](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=191-2), a driver whose motor vehicle damages another person while in use must pay, unless the driver took reasonable care to prevent the damage.

Insurance changes this picture sharply. When a policy is in force, the insurer pays the victim and can recover from its own insured only in the cases listed in [Article 29](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0390060&flno=29), such as drink-driving, drug-driving, intentional acts, criminal conduct or riding without a licence. An uninsured vehicle has no such shield. One exception remains: if the person liable is the victim's spouse, head of household, household member, or a close blood relative or in-law within the degrees set out in [Article 42(4)](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0390060&flno=42), the Fund has no subrogated claim unless an Article 29(1) circumstance applies. The judgment names the rider as the defendant. Whether the rider also owned the scooter, it does not say.

## Why Taiwanese law governed a Hong Kong resident

Under [Article 38 of the Act Governing Relations with Hong Kong and Macau](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=Q0010003&flno=38), civil matters involving Hong Kong apply Taiwan's Act Governing the Choice of Law in Civil Matters Involving Foreign Elements by analogy. That Act points tort claims to the law of the place where the tort occurred, unless another law is more closely connected ([Article 25](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000007&flno=25)). The crash was in New Taipei, and the court applied Taiwanese law.

For a rider from the US, the UK or Europe, the Hong Kong statute drops out and the choice-of-law Act applies directly. The crash location is the starting point, though the closer-connection exception remains.

## Serving a defendant who has gone

What happens to a lawsuit when the defendant is abroad? The Fund placed a newspaper notice on December 16, 2016. [Article 152 of the Code of Civil Procedure](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=152) says service by public notice on someone who must be served abroad takes effect after 60 days. The court treated service as complete on February 15, 2017 and ran 5% annual interest from the next day.

At the hearing, the rider neither appeared nor filed anything. The court found none of the grounds in [Article 386](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=386) that would have blocked a one-sided hearing, such as a party not being properly notified. It then decided the case on the Fund's request after hearing only the Fund.

## NT$43,759 at first instance, NT$600,000 more on appeal

At first instance, the New Taipei District Court (105 Su No. 3239) allowed only the medical portion. It refused the NT$600,000 disability benefit. The High Court reversed that part.

The difficulty was a mismatch of labels. The victim had sued the rider separately. In that case (Taiwan High Court 105 Shang No. 971), the victim's losses were fixed at NT$873,310: medical costs, nursing care, transport, medicine, a pair of glasses and NT$700,000 for pain and suffering. Nothing on that list was called "disability". The High Court said this did not matter. Compulsory insurance reimburses actual medical costs and pays fixed sums for disability regardless of the individual case, so under the purpose of [Article 42(1)](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0390060&flno=42) the payments are set off against the victim's total damages. The total, about NT$873,000, exceeded what the Fund had paid. The Fund could recover every dollar.

In the victim's own judgment, the NT$643,759 had already been deducted. Put the two judgments together and the rider owed NT$873,310 before interest: the whole loss the court had found.

## For anyone riding a scooter in Taiwan

Buying a used scooter, or taking one over from a friend who is leaving? Check when the compulsory policy ends. Failing to insure is itself fined, and the fine falls on the person obliged to insure: NT$1,500 to NT$3,000 for a motorcycle, and NT$9,000 to NT$32,000 if an uninsured vehicle causes an accident ([Article 49](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0390060&flno=49)). The award in this case was more than twenty times the top fine.

There is a deadline on the Fund's side too. Its subrogated claim lapses if not exercised within two years from the date it paid compensation ([Article 42(3)](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0390060&flno=42)). Leaving the country does not start that clock. How a final Taiwanese judgment might be enforced against assets outside Taiwan is a question this judgment did not reach.

In the summer of 2016 the rider left Taiwan. The judgment runs interest from February 16, 2017.
