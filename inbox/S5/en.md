# Widowed or Divorced in Taiwan: The Three-Year Route to Naturalization That Stays Open

If your Taiwanese spouse dies, are you back to the general five-year wait to become a Taiwan national? Not necessarily. The Nationality Act keeps a three-year route open for three groups of former spouses and foreign parents.

## Who keeps the three-year route

[Article 4(1) of the Nationality Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0030001&flno=4) lets certain foreigners apply after three continuous years of lawful residence, with at least 183 days in Taiwan in each year, provided they have a domicile here. Being a current spouse is one ground. Two others reach people whose marriage has ended.

Item 2 covers two situations. A former spouse of a Taiwan national who divorced because of domestic violence qualifies if they have not remarried. A widow or widower qualifies if they have not remarried and there are facts showing they remain in contact with the late spouse's relatives; that contact is not required where the marriage lasted at least two years.

Item 3 covers a foreigner who supports, exercises parental rights and duties over, or has contact with a Taiwan-national child who has no or limited legal capacity. A foreign parent raising or visiting a child after a divorce can fall within it.

The list may look familiar. [Article 31(4) of the Immigration Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0080132&flno=31), which allows continued residence after the reason for it ends, opens with nearly the same three cases: the spouse's death, a domestic-violence divorce without remarriage, and caring for or visiting a Taiwanese minor child after divorce. The Nationality Act keeps the shorter route for roughly the same people.

The other requirements are those of ordinary naturalization: legal capacity under both Taiwan law and your own country's law, no bad conduct, no criminal case on a police criminal record certificate (警察刑事紀錄證明), and basic language ability and civic knowledge ([Article 3(1)(2)–(5)](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0030001&flno=3)).

## Showing you can support yourself

A current spouse is excused from the self-support requirement. Items 2 and 3 are not, but they get easier proof. Under [Article 7 of the Enforcement Rules](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0030022&flno=7), these applicants may submit any one of: Taiwan income, tax, movable or immovable property records; an employment certificate, or their own written account of their work and earnings; a professional, technical or skills certificate issued by a Taiwan government agency; or other evidence of self-support. The Ministry of the Interior decides whether it is enough. Holders of a permanent resident certificate may leave this proof out ([Article 9(3)](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0030022&flno=9)). Applicants on routes covered by Article 7(1)(2), such as the general five-year route, must instead meet one of its tests, for example an average monthly income in Taiwan over the most recent year of more than twice the basic wage announced by the Ministry of Labor, or movable and immovable property in Taiwan valued at more than NT$5 million.

## The extra proof

[Article 8 of the Enforcement Rules](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0030022&flno=8) lists the common documents: a valid residence certificate, a police record from your home country, proof of self-support, and proof of language ability and civic knowledge, among others. Item 2 applicants add proof of domestic violence and no remarriage, or proof of contact with the late spouse's relatives and no remarriage. The contact proof is dropped if the marriage lasted two years or more. Item 3 applicants add proof of support or contact, unless parental rights are already recorded in the household register. The application is filed at the household registration office for your domicile and passes through the city or county government to the Ministry of the Interior ([Article 2](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0030022&flno=2)). On the way, the city or county government reviews the documents and the household registration office checks residence, travel, criminal-case and household records (Article 8).

## Counting three years

Count backward from the application date. The three years must be continuous, with at least 183 days of lawful residence in each year ([Article 6](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0030022&flno=6)). An overstay of under 30 days does not break continuity, but those days do not count toward the 183.

Some residence never counts. Article 5 of the Enforcement Rules excludes time spent on residence granted for a labor dispute lawsuit, treatment of an occupational injury, being a crime victim or witness, or an exit ban. Continued residence after a spouse's death, after a domestic-violence divorce or for a child is not on that list. Domicile itself means living in Taiwan intending to stay, with a valid resident or permanent resident certificate ([Article 4](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0030022&flno=4)). The certificate therefore has to be valid when you apply.

How to keep residence after a death or divorce is covered in [our column on residence as the foreign spouse of a Taiwanese national](/en/columns/taiwan-foreign-spouse-residence); what happens to your original citizenship is in [our naturalization column](/en/columns/taiwan-naturalization-keep-original-nationality). For someone widowed less than two years into a marriage, the record of staying in touch with the spouse's family is the first piece of evidence the three-year route asks for.
