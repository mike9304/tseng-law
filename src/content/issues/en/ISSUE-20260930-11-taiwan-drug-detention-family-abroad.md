# A Relative Detained in Taiwan Over a Suitcase: What Families Abroad Can and Cannot Do

The news usually arrives second-hand: someone you love was stopped after landing in Taiwan and is being held. Then, often, silence. You cannot visit, letters go unanswered, and you do not know how they are coping.

That is the situation described in a [LADbible report of September 29, 2026](https://www.ladbible.com/news/uk-news/mum-heartbreak-son-detained-taiwan-police-crime-travel-uk-631523-20260929). It reported that a young British man has been held in Taiwan since shortly after arriving in June, after it was alleged that he carried a "substance" in a suitcase, and that no further details have been confirmed. His family say he has consistently maintained that he did not know about it and that the luggage belonged to a friend. His mother told LADbible she has had no contact with him since his arrest, and that his lawyer had said he might have to plead guilty or face "eight years plus." A local MP described him as isolated by a "communication restriction order," and the Foreign Office said it was supporting the family and in contact with the local authorities.

Those are the family's and the report's accounts. We have not seen the case file and do not comment on what happened. Below is how Taiwan law answers the questions such a case raises for families abroad.

## Why can't the family visit or write?

Pre-trial detention (羈押) is ordered by a judge on statutory grounds, set out in [Article 101 of the Code of Criminal Procedure](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0010001&flno=101) (刑事訴訟法; articles below are from this Code unless stated otherwise), such as a risk of flight or of tampering with evidence or witnesses. Under [Article 108](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0010001&flno=108), detention during an investigation lasts up to two months and can be extended once, by up to two months; trial-stage detention has its own limits.

A detained person may normally receive visitors and letters, subject to monitoring. [Article 105](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0010001&flno=105) lets the court, on the prosecutor's application or its own motion, prohibit visits, correspondence and the passing of items where they could lead to escape, tampering with evidence, or collusion with co-offenders or witnesses. During the investigation the prosecutor sets who and what is covered and for how long; at trial, the judge does. This order is often called 禁見, and the report's "communication restriction order" appears to describe it. It may not restrict the detainee's right to a proper defense.

Defense counsel is in a different position. Under [Article 34](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0010001&flno=34), counsel may visit a detained client and exchange letters, and that can be restricted only where there is evidence of a risk of tampering or collusion. The UK government's [information pack for British people detained in Taiwan](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/taiwan-prisoner-pack/information-pack-for-british-prisoners-in-taiwan) says that under such an order suspects can still communicate with their appointed lawyer and the British consular team, and are normally allowed to receive clothes, funds and other permitted items.

These orders can be challenged. A court ruling on a contact ban can be appealed by 抗告 ([Article 404](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0010001&flno=404)), generally within ten days of service ([Article 406](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0010001&flno=406)). A prosecutor's decision on its scope can be taken to court within ten days under [Article 416](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0010001&flno=416).

## Who can hire a lawyer, and is there an interpreter?

The family need not wait. Under [Article 27](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0010001&flno=27), a suspect's statutory agent, spouse, lineal relatives, collateral relatives within the third degree, and head of household or household members can each appoint defense counsel on their own. A parent in the UK can therefore instruct a Taiwan lawyer directly. Our column on [hiring a Taiwan lawyer from abroad](/en/columns/hire-taiwan-lawyer-from-abroad) covers the practical steps.

Some cases get a lawyer by law. If no lawyer has been retained, the court must appoint one for a detention hearing during the investigation, unless the appointed lawyer has not arrived after four hours and the suspect asks to go ahead ([Article 31-1](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0010001&flno=31-1)). It must also appoint one at trial for offenses carrying a minimum sentence of three years or more ([Article 31](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0010001&flno=31)).

Language is a right, not a favor. [Article 99](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0010001&flno=99) says a defendant who does not understand the language must be assisted by an interpreter, with written questions and answers where needed. Our column on [police questioning and a foreigner's rights](/en/columns/taiwan-police-questioning-foreigner-rights) covers the warnings, the first 24 hours and the transcript.

## What penalties does Taiwan's narcotics law set?

The [Narcotics Hazard Prevention Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0000008&flno=2) (毒品危害防制條例) divides controlled drugs into four schedules. Its Article 2 lists heroin, morphine, opium and cocaine in Schedule 1, and cannabis and amphetamine among the drugs in Schedule 2.

For manufacturing, transporting or selling, [Article 4](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0000008&flno=4) sets:

- Schedule 1: death or life imprisonment
- Schedule 2: life imprisonment or at least 10 years
- Schedule 3: at least 7 years
- Schedule 4: 5 to 12 years

A fine can be added to a prison sentence, and attempts are punishable. Possession is dealt with separately under [Article 11](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0000008&flno=11), with lower penalties that depend on the schedule and, for larger quantities, on the pure net weight.

The report does not say what the substance was, so no one can say from it which of these, if any, would apply. The prosecutor's indictment (起訴書) states the offense and article charged.

## What does "pleading guilty" mean in Taiwan?

The narcotics law does not use the language of a guilty plea. It speaks of confession (自白) and cooperation, in [Article 17](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0000008&flno=17):

- A person who commits an offense under Articles 4 to 8 and **confesses during the investigation and at every trial stage** must receive a reduced sentence.
- A person who commits an offense under Articles 4 to 8, 10 or 11 and **reveals the source of the drugs, leading to the capture of other principal offenders or accomplices**, receives a reduced sentence or is exempted from punishment.
- Where the Article 4 transport offense was committed for the person's own use and the circumstances are minor, the sentence may be reduced.

"Reduced" has a set meaning in the Criminal Code. For fixed-term imprisonment, the general reduction is up to one half; where the applicable provision also permits exemption from punishment, the reduction may reach two thirds ([Article 66](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0000001&flno=66)). That exception matters for the source-identification provision in Article 17(1) above. Life imprisonment becomes 15 to 20 years ([Article 65](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0000001&flno=65)); death becomes life imprisonment ([Article 64](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0000001&flno=64)). Taiwan also has a negotiated judgment procedure, but [Article 455-2](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0010001&flno=455-2) excludes offenses punishable by death, life imprisonment or a minimum of three years or more.

A confession has lasting effects. The decision belongs to the accused, taken with a lawyer who has seen the evidence and an interpreter they trust. Families can support it, but cannot make it from abroad.

## Could a sentence be served back home?

Only after a sentence is final. Taiwan's [Act on the Transfer of Sentenced Persons](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=I0030044&flno=18) (跨國移交受刑人法) applies a treaty or arrangement first (Article 2). To send a foreign prisoner home, Article 18 requires both sides to agree, the prisoner to hold that country's nationality (and not also hold Taiwan nationality with household registration in Taiwan), written consent, at least one year of the sentence remaining when the request is made (unless both sides agree otherwise), a written assurance of reciprocity, and no other criminal case pending in Taiwan. The consent is checked, and once confirmed it cannot be withdrawn (Article 19).

For the UK there is an official basis. The [Arrangement between the justice authorities of Taiwan and the UK on the Transfer of Sentenced Persons](https://law.moj.gov.tw/ENG/LawClass/LawAll.aspx?pcode=Y0030149) entered into force on May 3, 2016. Its conditions include close ties to the UK, the prisoner's consent, a final judgment with no other proceedings pending, generally at least 12 months left to serve, the act being an offense in the UK too, and approval by both authorities. Taiwan's [Ministry of Justice reported](https://www.moj.gov.tw/2832/2833/2834/2835/263399/post) that it transferred a third British prisoner under the arrangement on December 8, 2025. Each transfer is decided case by case.

A prison sentence can also bring deportation afterwards, which our [police questioning column](/en/columns/taiwan-police-questioning-foreigner-rights) touches on. If a relative is released on bail, a ban on leaving Taiwan can be attached; see our guide to [exit bans on foreigners](/en/columns/taiwan-exit-ban-foreigners).

## What can the family do from abroad now?

- **Get the paperwork through the lawyer.** Ask for the case number, the prosecutors office, the date of the detention ruling, and the scope and end date of any contact ban.
- **Use the consular route.** Under [Article 59 of the Detention Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=I0040005&flno=59) (羈押法), the detention center must, at the detainee's request, help them meet and correspond with diplomatic or consular officers or representatives of their country. The UK pack explains what the British Office Taipei can and cannot do, including providing lists of lawyers and interpreters and, in some circumstances, helping with a transfer application.
- **Send what is allowed.** Ask the lawyer or the consular team what items and funds can be passed on while contact is banned.
- **Think before posting.** Anyone can read crowdfunding pages and interviews. Agree public statements with the lawyer.
