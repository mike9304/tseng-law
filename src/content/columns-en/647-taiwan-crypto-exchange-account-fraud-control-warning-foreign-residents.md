# Frozen on a Taiwan Crypto Exchange Under the Anti-Fraud Rules: The 20-Day and 48-Hour Clocks, and Why Damages Are Hard

One sentence in Taiwan's anti-fraud statute shapes disputes over frozen exchange accounts. When an exchange causes damage to customers by implementing the Act's fraud-prevention measures, or by cooperating with the police, it is "exempted from the compensation responsibility." That is the official English translation of Article 12(2) of the [Fraud Crime Hazard Prevention Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0080226&flno=12).

So the practical questions are which measure froze the account, who can lift it, and how fast. A claim for the price drop comes a distant second.

## The exchange's own control

Article 8(1) of the [Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0080226&flno=8) requires exchanges to strengthen customer verification on accounts suspected of fraud links. It lets them suspend deposits and withdrawals, block transfers of money or virtual assets out, suspend some or all trading, or refuse service.

What counts as suspicious is set out in thirteen items in Article 12 of the [Regulations Governing Fraud Crime Hazard Prevention by Financial Institutions and Businesses or Personnel Providing Virtual Asset Services](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0380271&flno=12), last amended on July 20, 2026. Repeated small transfers that look like testing are on the list. So is a dormant account that suddenly trades.

Item 9 is written for foreigners. It covers a non-citizen whose residence certificate, checked when the account was opened, was cancelled before its expiry date, who has overstayed, or who was reported missing. Leaving a job can lead there. When the reason for residence disappears, the National Immigration Agency revokes the residence permit and cancels the certificate, with exceptions such as a pending labor-dispute lawsuit ([Immigration Act Article 31](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0080132&flno=31)(4)).

## Twenty days, and then what?

The exchange may report the account to the police ([Regulations Article 25](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0380271&flno=25)). Police then have 20 days to notify it, by official document, whether to keep or lift the control. Silence does not free the account. If no notice comes, the exchange may continue the control ([Article 26](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0380271&flno=26)(1)).

Police can also act first. To investigate fraud, they may notify an exchange to list an account as a warning account and stop all transactions ([Act Article 9](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0080226&flno=9)(4)). The exchange then halts every function and traces where the reported assets went ([Regulations Article 45](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0380271&flno=45)). No reply deadline like the 20 days or 48 hours applies to a warning designation. [Article 66](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0380271&flno=66)(2) of the Regulations mentions only release by notice and expiry of the warning period.

## The 48-hour hold

A narrower restriction comes through the banks' and exchanges' joint reporting chain. When the previous bank or exchange reports that scam proceeds moved into your account, the exchange holds that amount. Police must, within 48 hours of the hold, say whether the account becomes a warning account. If no designation arrives, the exchange releases the hold on its own ([Article 48](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0380271&flno=48)).

Whether the whole account stopped or only a set amount is held shows which of these rules applies and whom to ask.

## Where to push

For a control imposed on police instructions, you may ask the exchange and, where needed, the police unit that issued the instruction (Article 26(3)). Objections to a hold go to the police unit that designated the original warning account the funds were traced from, with the exchange assisting when necessary ([Article 51](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0380271&flno=51)). Useful material includes the transaction history, the source of the deposit in question, and the chat or contract showing what it paid for.

If the police refuse, the fight moves to administrative court. On September 17, 2026, the Supreme Administrative Court upheld a lower-court view in a company's bank warning-account case: when a request to the police to lift a warning is refused, the account holder can file an administrative appeal, a revocation suit, or a general performance suit ([114 Shang No. 388](https://judgment.judicial.gov.tw/FJUD/data.aspx?ty=JD&id=TPAA%2c114%2c%e4%b8%8a%2c388%2c20260917%2c1)). A suit to bar future warnings in advance lacked the required need for protection.

Assets of a victim still sitting in a warning account may be returned to that victim once the police send a written notice, with a copy to the account holder ([Article 64](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0380271&flno=64)). Where the exchange sees a likely trading dispute or a complicated case, that return procedure does not apply and the matter goes to the courts ([Article 69](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0380271&flno=69)).

## If you are the one under suspicion

[Article 22 of the Money Laundering Control Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0380131&flno=22) bars handing an exchange account to someone else to use, unless it fits ordinary business practice, trust between relatives or friends, or another legitimate reason. Doing it for promised or received payment, or with three or more accounts in total, can mean up to three years' imprisonment, among other penalties. A warning designation is an investigative step, not a finding of guilt. Anyone under investigation is presumed innocent, and a first-instance judgment can be appealed.

Whether the exchange itself was allowed to serve you is a separate check, covered in [When a Taiwan Crypto Platform Blocks Your Withdrawal](/en/columns/taiwan-crypto-exchange-vasp-dispute). Frozen bank accounts are discussed in [When a Taiwan bank account is listed as a warning account](/en/columns/taiwan-warning-account-foreigners).
