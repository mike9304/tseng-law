## 텍스트 첨부 · 4379자 · 링크 3개

```text
I've left out the renter's name, which the ruling gives. What the post calls a deposit is 定金 in the ruling: earnest money, not a security deposit (押金).

Nov 28, 2025: a would-be renter went to see an apartment in Taipei, and someone from the management company showed them around. At 2:51 that afternoon they wired NT$21,800 to the company's account, exactly one month's rent.

After paying, they sent the company a LINE message. The ruling quotes it: 「您好，我已匯款＄21,800元作為○○承租訂金」 (I've masked the property name). Roughly: "Hi, I've transferred $21,800 as the rental deposit for [the unit]." Later they gave this LINE chat to the court as evidence.

By their account, it only hit them after paying that their stuff wouldn't fit. So around 5 p.m., less than three hours after the showing, they messaged the company on LINE, backing out and asking for the money back. The company said no.

Nothing was signed, the renter told the court, and they'd never settled the lease length, the security deposit (押金), the start date or the move-in date. They said the money was just to show they wanted the place and to keep the option open, so the company had no right to hold on to it (unjust enrichment). They asked for the NT$21,800 back plus 5% a year, counted from the day after the complaint reached the company.

The company didn't dispute that nothing was signed. The company's version: the NT$21,800, one month's rent, was earnest money (定金) holding the unit until a lease was signed within 7 to 14 days, and the renter was told backing out meant losing it. They'd sent bank details, house rules and the equipment list before the payment, they said, and the renter went through them first. The listing had a one-year minimum, and the rent was settled that day. They also say three more groups were booked to see the place after the renter, and they canceled all three once the money came in.

The judge found that a lease had already been formed verbally at the showing on Nov 28. The essentials were agreed: which unit, NT$21,800 a month, the house rules, the equipment list, a one-year minimum. With those terms and the renter's own "承租訂金" message, the NT$21,800 counted as earnest money. The two sides had never agreed on what kind, but since the key terms were already agreed, the court called it 證約定金, earnest money that proves a contract exists. Once a written lease was signed, it would have become the first month's rent or the security deposit.

The judge looked at the refund claim under Art. 249. To get the money back under item 3, the renter would have had to show the company's fault made the lease impossible to carry out, and they never offered any evidence of that. The reason they gave was that the place felt too small once they'd paid. The court put that on them. The claim and the interest were both dismissed, and the first-instance court costs of NT$1,500 fall on the renter.

In the LINE it's 訂金. The ruling itself uses 定金 and doesn't get into the difference.

Here are the two articles, if you want them.
Civil Code Art. 248: when one party to a contract receives earnest money from the other, the contract is presumed to have been formed. Presumed, so evidence the other way can still beat it.
https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=248
Art. 249, unless the parties agreed otherwise. Item 1: when the contract is carried out, the earnest money is returned or counted toward payment. Item 2: if the payer's fault makes performance impossible, the payer can't ask for it back. Item 3: if it's the recipient's fault, the recipient pays back double what it received. Item 4: if neither side is at fault, it's returned. Items 2 and 3 are the ones that matter here.
https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=249

The "0" and "2x" on the cards only compare how the earnest money itself comes back, not total damages.

Source: Taiwan Shilin District Court, Shilin Summary Court, Case 115 Shi-Xiao-Zi No. 864, a small-claims first-instance ruling dated June 30, 2026. I don't know if it was appealed or has become final.
https://judgment.judicial.gov.tw/FJUD/printData.aspx?id=SLEV%2C115%2C%E5%A3%AB%E5%B0%8F%2C864%2C20260630%2C1

The LINE wording on cards 4 and 5 is quoted from the ruling and translated, not a screenshot. The speech bubbles and drawings on the other cards are made-up scenes based on the ruling.
```
