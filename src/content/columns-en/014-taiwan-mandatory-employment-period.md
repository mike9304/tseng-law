# Taiwan Mandatory Minimum Service Period Clauses

![Featured image](../images/014-taiwan-mandatory-employment-period/featured-01.jpg)

![](../images/014-taiwan-mandatory-employment-period/img-01.jpg)

Last time, we discussed how difficult it can be to receive severance pay in Taiwan.

Today, I want to talk about minimum service period clauses that sometimes appear in Taiwan employment contracts.

​

Korea's minimum wage has gone up again.

The minimum wage plan for next year is 10,030 won per hour,

which, based on 40 hours per week and 209 hours per month, converts to a monthly amount of 2,096,270 won.

Taiwan, like Korea, raises its minimum wage every year.

From 2024, Taiwan's hourly minimum wage is NT$183, and

the monthly minimum wage is NT$27,470.

​

When labor costs are high, if an employee works only a few months and then resigns,

the employer incurs substantial costs for handover.

Some Taiwan employers, concerned that employees will resign too quickly,

insert a "minimum service period clause" into the employment contract.

​

For example, a clause stating that the employee must work for at least one year before resigning, and

otherwise must pay compensation equal to six months' salary.

Such a clause is clearly illegal in Korea.

Under Article 20 of Korea's Labor Standards Act,

an employer may not enter into an employment contract that claims a penalty or damages for breach of the employment contract.

​

Recently, a Korean person asked me:

Is a clause like this legal in Taiwan?

**​**

**The answer: it is almost always illegal.**

​

​

Taiwan employment contracts may stipulate a "minimum service period" or "mandatory service period," but

all three of the following requirements must be met.

**1. The employer must provide 'professional training' and bear the training costs.**

**2. The employer must pay 'reasonable compensation' for the minimum service period.**

**3. The arrangement must be reasonable and necessary.**

​

If even one of these is not met,

the employer cannot restrict the employee's minimum service period.

​

In addition, Taiwan courts apply these three requirements very strictly.

​

Professional training must involve expenditure of a certain level of cost and

must teach professional skills for specific work.

Mere ordinary job-handover processes—

for example, corporate-culture training, familiarization with the work environment, or explanation of work rules—

do not constitute professional skills training.

​

Reasonable compensation is recognized only when there is additional compensation provided for the minimum service period requirement.

Ordinary overtime pay, travel expenses, and the like are not recognized.

​

Finally, the court judges the reasonableness of the contract.

Even with professional training and compensation, the contract cannot be forced without limit.

So the standard is very strict.

​

A representative lawful example is **airline pilots**.

In recent years, Taiwan civil courts have frequently seen cases in which pilots who breached a minimum service period clause were sued by airlines.

Because an airline spends about NT$5 million in training costs

to train one qualified pilot from the ground up,

it stipulates a 20-year service period with the trained pilot.

​

So when you take a job in Taiwan, if the employment contract states a minimum service period, do not worry too much.

Unless the job is one for which the employer must spend substantial costs and a long period of training,

it is highly likely to be illegal.

If you are unsure, consulting a lawyer is also a good approach.

​

​

• Taiwan Labor Standards Act (勞動基準法) Article 15-1:

Unless the following requirements are met, an employer may not agree with a worker on a minimum service period:

1. The employer has provided professional technical training to the worker and has borne the cost of that training.

2. The employer has provided the worker with reasonable compensation for complying with the minimum service period.

​

A minimum service period agreement must comprehensively consider the following matters and must not exceed a reasonable scope:

1. The duration and cost of the professional technical training provided by the employer to the worker.

2. The replaceability of workers in the same or similar positions.

3. The scope and amount of the compensation provided by the employer to the worker.

4. Other matters affecting the reasonableness of the minimum service period.

An agreement that violates the preceding provisions is void.
