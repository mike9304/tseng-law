# Taiwan Real Estate Agents: Deposits, Written Offers, the 6% Fee Cap and Liability

Six percent. That is the most a Taiwan brokerage may collect on a sale, buyer and seller combined. For a lease, the ceiling is one and a half months' rent from landlord and tenant combined.

## Six percent is a combined ceiling

Article 19(1) of the [Real Estate Brokerage Management Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0060066&flno=19) (不動產經紀業管理條例) bars brokerages (經紀業) and agents (經紀人員) from taking a price difference (差價) or other remuneration; fees must follow the Ministry's standard, based on the actual price or rent. [Article 4](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0060066&flno=4), item 9, defines the price difference as the gap between the actual sale price and the listing price. Reporting NT$19 million to the seller while collecting NT$20 million leaves a prohibited NT$1 million price difference (an invented example).

Taipei City's [land administration page](https://land.gov.taipei/News_Content.aspx?n=836A6D9F6E91D3F3&s=44C446CA352466CD) sets out the Ministry's fee standard (不動產經紀業報酬計收標準). Total fees from one or both sides may not exceed 6% of the actual transaction price or 1.5 months' rent. It is a maximum, not a fixed rate; each brokerage sets its own schedule, without collusion, deception or obvious unfairness. The fee and each side's share must be written in the agency agreement or written offer, and [Article 20](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0060066&flno=20) requires the schedule and collection method to be posted at the office.

On a NT$20 million flat, the ceiling is NT$1.2 million in total; on a NT$30,000-a-month lease, NT$45,000 (an invented example). Combined is the word that matters. If the seller already signed for 4%, at most 2% is left for the buyer; a landlord paying one month leaves half a month for the tenant. The other side's share belongs in the paperwork, so ask to see it.

Under Article 19(2), any price difference or other remuneration already taken in breach, such as the NT$1 million gap or a fee over the ceiling, must have interest added and then be repaid to the payer at double that sum. Regulators can fine the brokerage NT$60,000 to NT$300,000 under [Article 29](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0060066&flno=29), paragraph 1, item 1, and suspend the agent for six months to three years under [Article 31](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0060066&flno=31), paragraph 1, item 2.

## Is a deposit needed to make an offer?

No. Agents sometimes ask for a negotiation deposit (斡旋金) first. Point 2 of the [Fair Trade Commission's guidance on real estate brokerages](https://law.ftc.gov.tw/law/LawContent.aspx?id=FL011931) defines it as money a buyer hands over so the brokerage can negotiate with the seller. A written offer (要約書) states the price and terms for the agent to present, and the buyer pays nothing in advance.

Point 9 requires a brokerage, before collecting a deposit, to tell the buyer in writing how a deposit agreement differs from the Ministry's written offer, that one can replace the other, and that the buyer may choose either. Omitting this is deception that violates [Article 25 of the Fair Trade Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0150002&flno=25) where it is able to affect trading order.

The Commission's sample notice says the written offer needs no deposit and no other payment before the seller accepts. With a deposit agreement, the buyer is told to read its terms on breach, so refunds depend on the brokerage's own contract. The buyer signs the chosen option, and that sheet is evidence later.

## What the seller's signature creates

The written offer form is Annex 2 of the Ministry's [model listing agreement, amended June 2003 (Tainan City Government copy)](https://w3fs.tainan.gov.tw/Download.ashx?u=LzAwMS9VcGxvYWQvMjA3L3JlbGZpbGUvMjI0ODYvNzgxMDgwNC83OTU4OWNkNS01YTJiLTQ1ZmYtYjdlMi02YzU1MTYwZTQ3NWEucGRm&n=MjAxNjAzMTUxNTE1MzUzNDI3ODQucGRm&icon=.pdf). Under clause 3, once the seller writes the acceptance time and signs, and the form reaches the buyer together with its attachments, both sides must sign the full contract, unless they cannot agree on its terms (signing note 3). A seller who changes the terms rejects the offer and makes a new one, binding only once the buyer's acceptance is delivered back, as in [Civil Code Article 160](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=160), paragraph 2.

Before signing, the buyer takes the form and its attachments home for at least three days. The signing notes call it a preliminary contract (預約), a promise to sign the sale contract. Clause 5 then requires both sides to negotiate taxes, the land registration agent (地政士), payment, the loan and handover, and sign within the stated number of days after acceptance arrives.

An offeror is normally bound under [Civil Code Article 154](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=154), paragraph 1, so the model adds a right to withdraw. Clause 4 allows withdrawal within the offer period unless the agent has already delivered the seller's acceptance to the buyer. Withdraw by certified-contents letter (存證信函), by written notice handed to the seller, or at the brokerage address the seller authorised on the form. Under [Article 95](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=95), a withdrawal arriving first or simultaneously cancels the offer; under [Article 158](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=158), an offer lapses if not accepted by its deadline.

Where seller and brokerage signed on this model, clause 7, item 5, makes the brokerage pass a signed offer to the seller within 24 hours unless the seller's circumstances prevent delivery; item 8 does the same for earnest money.

## Backing out after acceptance

Refusing to sign when the terms could be agreed breaches that duty; the contract sets the consequences. An agreed penalty counts as total damages unless agreed otherwise ([Article 250](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=250), paragraph 2), and a court may cut an excessive one ([Article 252](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=252)).

Other rules apply if a deposit agreement says the money becomes earnest money (定金) once the seller accepts. Receiving earnest money raises a presumption that a contract exists ([Article 248](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=248)). Absent other terms, if the contract cannot be performed because of the buyer, the buyer cannot recover the earnest money; if because of the seller, the seller returns double ([Article 249](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=249), items 2 and 3). See our [holding deposit](/en/columns/taiwan-rental-holding-deposit-dingjin) and [loan shortfall](/en/columns/taiwan-resale-home-loan-shortfall-cash-difference-rescission) pieces.

And the fee? A broker (居間人) may claim it only when the contract was concluded through the broker's report or brokerage, and not before a condition precedent is met ([Article 568](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=568)). On the payer's request, a court may cut a disproportionate fee, but not one already paid ([Article 572](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=572)).

## The disclosure statement becomes part of the contract

In a brokered deal, the agency agreement, written offer, earnest money receipt, advertising copy, property disclosure statement (不動產說明書) and final contract must be signed and sealed by a licensed broker (經紀人) the brokerage assigns ([Article 22](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0060066&flno=22), paragraph 1); the Ministry sets the statement's required and banned content (paragraph 3). Agents explain the property using a statement the owner has signed ([Article 23](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0060066&flno=23)). At signing, the licensed broker hands it to the other party to sign, and it is deemed part of the contract ([Article 24](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0060066&flno=24)).

Dual agency requires both sides' written consent ([Article 24-2](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0060066&flno=24-2)). The brokerage must then treat both sides fairly on comparable prices and contract terms, inform the buyer or tenant, disclose defects professional diligence should have found, and help with inspections. Anyone brokering as a business must also investigate the contract matters, the parties' ability to perform, and their capacity to conclude that contract ([Civil Code Article 567](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=567), paragraph 2). Advertising that does not match the facts also brings liability for damages ([Article 21](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0060066&flno=21)).

In a brokered deal, the written offer, earnest money receipt and disclosure statement should all bear a licensed broker's signature. Without it, the brokerage faces a fine and the agent a reprimand (申誡) under Articles 29 and 31, each at paragraph 1, item 1.

## Who pays when the information was wrong

When an agent's intentional or negligent act in brokering or sales-agency (代銷) work causes loss to a party to the deal, the brokerage and the agent are jointly liable ([Article 26](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0060066&flno=26), paragraph 2). So a buyer can claim against a brokerage the seller hired.

A fund remains if the brokerage closes: each lodges a business guarantee deposit after registration ([Article 7](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0060066&flno=7), paragraph 3), held by the national federation in a dedicated fund account ([Article 8](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0060066&flno=8), paragraph 1). It cannot be assigned, attached, set off or encumbered over the debts or claims of the brokerage or its agents ([Article 9](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0060066&flno=9), paragraph 1).

Asking the federation to pay counts as applying to the committee for mediation, which must start at once (Article 26, paragraph 3). Payment needs a final judgment or other enforceable title, an arbitration award, or a committee decision to pay, and is capped at the deposit and security that brokerage lodged (Article 26, paragraph 4).

How large is that? [Article 3 of the deposit rules](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0060071&flno=3) sets NT$250,000 per office for up to five offices, NT$100,000 per further office and NT$30,000 per licensed broker beyond five at an office, capped at NT$10 million. A one-office brokerage with five or fewer licensed brokers that lodged only the minimum is good for NT$250,000; voluntary extra deposits raise that (Article 7, paragraph 5).
