For a Singapore buyer relying on a supplier's Taiwan assets for recovery, an award made in Singapore becomes a Taiwan enforcement title only after a Taiwan court recognises it under [Article 47 of the Arbitration Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=I0020001&flno=47).

Before accepting the clause, identify the company that owes the delivery or refund obligation and the assets it owns. The Taiwan factory named in a quotation may belong to a different group company from the supplier signing the agreement. Do not assume an award against that supplier will reach the factory owner's property merely because both companies use the same brand.

## Identify the contracting supplier and its assets

Ask for the supplier's registered legal name and company number, and reconcile them with the signature block, invoices and payment instructions. An English trading name can be useful in correspondence, but the contract should leave no doubt about the legal entity accepting the obligation.

Assess what a claim against that entity could recover. Relevant enquiries may concern its Taiwan receivables, equipment ownership or other identifiable assets; access to information and competing claims need separate assessment. Registration and paid-in capital alone do not establish what will remain available to a creditor.

If the commercial team expects a parent company to support repayment, negotiate the undertaking expressly. Have counsel check its legal effect, authority and dispute clause. A parent guarantee, purchase agreement and individual order can create different claims against different parties. Giving each document a different forum can fragment one failed supply relationship into several proceedings.

## Decide what “Singapore” means in the contract

A clause choosing Singapore law does not, by itself, select Singapore courts or arbitration. From the Taiwan conflicts perspective, [Article 20 of the Choice of Law Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000007&flno=20) addresses the law governing contractual obligations; the dispute-resolution mechanism needs its own wording.

For arbitration, the drafting decisions include:

| Decision | What the agreement should identify |
|---|---|
| Seat | Singapore as the legal seat, if that is the bargain; distinguish it from hearing arrangements |
| Institution and rules | For example, administration by the Singapore International Arbitration Centre (SIAC) under the chosen SIAC Rules |
| Tribunal and language | The agreed number of arbitrators and language of the proceedings |
| Applicable laws | The law governing the supply contract and the law governing the arbitration agreement |

If choosing SIAC, use its [published model clause](https://siac.org.sg/siac-model-clause) and make each selection expressly. Have Singapore counsel review the Singapore-law choices and any changes to the institution's wording.

The separate choice for the arbitration agreement matters at the Taiwan end too. [Arbitration Act Article 50](https://mojlaw.moj.gov.tw/ENG/LawContentE.aspx?LSID=FL010105) includes invalidity under the law chosen for that agreement among the possible grounds for resisting recognition; absent a choice, it refers to the law of the place where the award was made.

Review incorporated purchase terms as well as the signed master agreement. Resolve any clash between an arbitration clause and a purchase-order term assigning disputes to a court. State which document prevails. If negotiation must precede arbitration, specify how it starts and when the parties may proceed, and address access to urgent protective measures.

## Compare the Taiwan routes before choosing a forum

Singapore is a New York Convention contracting state; Taiwan is not. A Singapore award therefore does not acquire an automatic Convention route into Taiwan. The relevant route is Taiwan's Arbitration Act. [UNCITRAL status table](https://uncitral.un.org/en/texts/arbitration/conventions/foreign_arbitral_awards/status2); [Chinese Arbitration Association guidance](https://www.arbitration.org.tw/qa08.php).

Recognition still has conditions. [Article 49](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=I0020001&flno=49) requires refusal for public-order or good-morals conflicts and disputes that Taiwan law does not allow to be arbitrated. It permits refusal where the award's country of origin, or the country whose arbitration law applies, does not recognise Taiwan awards. No clause can promise a favourable reciprocity decision. Article 50 also permits objections concerning matters such as notice, procedure and the agreement's scope.

A final Singapore court judgment follows a different route: [Compulsory Enforcement Act Article 4-1](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010004&flno=4-1) requires a Taiwan judgment permitting enforcement, with no refusal ground under [Code of Civil Procedure Article 402](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=402). Those grounds concern jurisdiction, safeguards for a defendant who did not appear, public order or good morals, and reciprocity. An uncontested Singapore case should not be treated as eliminating the Taiwan service question.

Compare the likely claim value, arbitration costs, parties and enforcement location before selecting the forum. Neither route guarantees collection. ## Allow for a Taiwan application before the award

If there is evidence that recovery may be frustrated, assess Taiwan protective relief promptly. [Code of Civil Procedure Articles 522 and 523](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawAll.aspx?pcode=B0010001) provide for provisional attachment of monetary claims, subject to a risk that later enforcement will be impossible or very difficult. Under [Article 526](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=526), the applicant must make a preliminary showing of the claim and grounds for attachment; the court may require security. A missed invoice alone does not establish every requirement.

Taiwan's [Arbitration Act Article 39](https://mojlaw.moj.gov.tw/LawContent.aspx?LSID=FL010105) addresses protective applications by parties to an arbitration agreement. If arbitration has not begun, the court must, on the other party's request, set a period for commencing it; where litigation is legally available, it may order that instead. Counsel should coordinate any Taiwan application with the Singapore proceeding. Neither commencing arbitration nor inserting an urgent-relief clause itself freezes Taiwan assets.
