When a Taiwan subsidiary files a patent at TIPO for a process tweak developed on site, the first registration question is often who may be named as applicant. Salary and a signature line on a handbook do not answer it by themselves.

Payroll routed through a US parent while the engineer works daily in Taiwan does not settle ownership either. Under paragraph 2 of Article 42 of the Act Governing the Choice of Law in Civil Matters Involving Foreign Elements, ownership of intellectual property completed in the course of employment follows the law governing the employment contract.

## Service inventions default to the employer

[Article 7 of the Patent Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0070007&flno=7) places the right to apply and the patent for an invention, utility model or design completed in the course of employment with the employer. The employer must pay the employee appropriate remuneration, unless the contract says something different.

The same article defines a service invention as one completed in work within the employment relationship. That is a factual question about job duties, not only about cleanroom badges or which entity signs the paycheck.

Funded R&D follows a separate branch of the same article. When one party pays for another person's development, ownership follows their contract. With no contract term, the inventor holds the application right and patent, while the funder may implement the creation. The inventor still keeps the right to be named even when the employer or funder holds the patent.

## Outside work, company resources and six months

For inventions completed outside assigned duties, [Article 8 of the Patent Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0070007&flno=8) starts from the opposite default. Application rights and the patent belong to the employee.

Two qualifications follow. First, if the employee used the employer's resources or experience, the employer may implement the invention in its business after paying reasonable remuneration. Second, the employee must promptly notify the employer in writing and, where needed, describe how the work was done.

Silence has a deadline. If the employer does not object within six months after that written notice arrives, it cannot later treat the invention as a service invention. The clock is easy to miss when engineering managers assume every notebook entry is already company property.

## Clauses that try to take everything away

Under [Article 9](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0070007&flno=9), a contract between employer and employee is void if it prevents the employee from enjoying the rights to their invention, utility model or design under the preceding article—Article 8's non-service entitlements. Article 7 stands apart: service-invention ownership and remuneration can follow the contract when the parties agree. Template language often assigns all past and future inventions worldwide. If it leaves no process for reporting non-service inventions and ignores Article 8 notice and remuneration, that broader sweep may fail. The assignment of in-course work can still stand.

Drafting therefore needs more than a headline assignment. The agreement can still allocate service inventions to the company under Article 7, address pay, and set notice steps for non-service inventions. It cannot use a blanket waiver to deny Article 8 statutory protections.

## Changing the name at TIPO after an agreement

If the employer and employee dispute ownership under Articles 7 or 8 of the [Patent Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0070007&flno=7) and then reach an agreement, [Article 10](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0070007&flno=10) lets them file proof with the patent authority to change the applicant or owner name. The authority may ask for mediation, arbitration or court documents if it considers them necessary.

That route is administrative, not a substitute for a clear employment and IP schedule signed before the filing date.

## Which employment contract's law applies

A US chip-design house may keep engineers on a US payroll while they work daily in Hsinchu, or hire locally through a Taiwan entity while a parent policy governs bonuses and equity. Ownership questions follow the employment contract.

Paragraph 2 of [Article 42 of the Act Governing the Choice of Law in Civil Matters Involving Foreign Elements](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000007&flno=42) provides that ownership of intellectual property an employee completes in the course of employment follows the law governing the employment contract. Align the secondment letter, local employment terms and any group IP policy so they do not each name a different employer or governing law.

US invention-assignment rules under federal or state law are governed by US law; confirm them with US counsel.

## Trade secrets and copyright alongside patents

Patent ownership is not the only label on semiconductor work. [Article 3 of the Trade Secrets Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0080028&flno=3) assigns trade secrets developed in the course of duties to the employer unless the contract provides otherwise, and mirrors Article 8's resource-and-remuneration pattern for non-duty development.

Layout databases, verification scripts and internal design documents may also involve copyright. [Article 11](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0070017&flno=11) and [Article 12](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0070017&flno=12) of the Copyright Act set different defaults for employees and commissioned developers. A Taiwan employment agreement that mentions patents but never mentions copyright or trade secrets leaves parallel defaults in place, as discussed in [employee and freelancer ownership of Taiwan-made code](/en/columns/taiwan-copyright-employee-freelancer-work-ownership). Theft and injunction strategy after a departure is a different column from ownership at creation; see [civil remedies under Taiwan's Trade Secrets Act](/en/columns/taiwan-trade-secrets-act-civil-remedies-injunctions-us-tech).

## Secondment letters and group IP policies

The Taiwan subsidiary may employ the engineer while the US parent holds service-invention rights under valid contractual arrangements; patent rights can be assigned ([Article 6](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0070007&flno=6)). That split is not a defect by itself. Trouble starts when the documents disagree. A secondment letter may say the US company remains the employer while the Taiwan labor paperwork points at the subsidiary, or the group policy and the local contract may set different notice and remuneration terms. Those terms need to be reconciled. If no valid governing-law choice exists, [Article 20](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000007&flno=20) of the choice-of-law act points to the law most closely connected with the contract.
