# A ride-hail passenger left on Route 64: what Taiwan's lay-judge trial must decide

In the early hours of January 25, 2026, a passenger who had booked a ride from Taipei to New Taipei through a ride-hailing app was taken out of the car on Provincial Highway 64 near Zhonghe. The car drove off. Four vehicles then ran over him, and he died at the scene. On November 2, a lay-judge trial opened at the New Taipei District Court, [CNA reported](https://www.cna.com.tw/news/asoc/202611020261.aspx). Prosecutors have charged the driver with abandonment resulting in death. The driver admits only negligent homicide.

Nothing has been decided. The trial was scheduled to run until November 6.

## What each side says

Prosecutors played the dashcam footage in court. According to the report, it shows the driver growing angry at a noise from the back seat, telling the passenger to get out and stopping on the shoulder. The prosecution told the court that Route 64 is dangerous at any hour. The family's lawyer argued that the passenger's silence during the driver's insults showed he had already lost consciousness.

The defence does not dispute that the passenger was put out of the car. It says he was left between the lane line and the sound barrier, was struck in the inner lane, and that the footage does not show how he got there. It argues there was no causal link to the death and no intent to abandon, and that the driver reported the incident within a minute.

## Abandonment, with or without a duty

Taiwan's Criminal Code treats abandoning a person who cannot protect themselves as its own crime. [Article 293](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0000001&flno=293) covers anyone who abandons such a person; where death results, the penalty is up to five years. [Article 294](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0000001&flno=294) is heavier. It applies to someone who, under a law or a contract, should help, care for or protect that person and abandons them or withholds the help needed to survive. If death results, the range is life imprisonment or at least seven years.

Which article the indictment cites was not in the CNA report. Either way, three questions are likely to matter. Was the passenger unable to look after himself when he was put out? Did the driver owe him a duty to help or protect, for example through the transport arrangement? And did leaving him on the roadside cause his death, given that other vehicles struck him afterwards?

On the driver's account, a different offence applies. [Article 276](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0000001&flno=276), negligent homicide, carries up to five years' imprisonment, short-term detention or a fine of up to NT$500,000.

## Why six citizens are on the bench

Under [Article 5 of the Citizen Judges Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=A0030320&flno=5), and apart from juvenile cases and drug offences under the Narcotics Hazard Prevention Act, first-instance district court cases where an intentional crime results in death go to a mixed panel, as do cases carrying a minimum of ten years. The categories follow the facts and charges in the indictment. [Article 3](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=A0030320&flno=3) sets the panel at three professional judges and six citizen judges.

An abandonment charge with a death is an intentional offence with a fatal result, which brings it within Article 5. A negligent homicide charge alone would not.

For passengers, the dispute concerns a driver's duties when a trip ends partway. This trial is about one driver's criminal liability; the trial days were set for November 2 to 6, and the report did not give a date for the judgment.
