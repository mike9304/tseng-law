# A Baby in a U.S.–Taiwan Family: Citizenship, Birth Records and Taiwan Household Registration

Your baby's hospital certificate records a birth. It does not tell you whether Taiwan household registration is complete or whether a U.S. passport can be issued. Before the first journey, establish the child's citizenship and then work through the records and travel documents that follow from it.

The key facts are birthplace, each parent's nationality at the child's birth, legal parentage and the Taiwanese parent's household status. The appropriate filing procedures depend on those facts.

## Which citizenship can the child acquire at birth?

[Article 2 of Taiwan's Nationality Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0030001&flno=2) includes a child whose father or mother was a Republic of China national at the child’s birth, along with other specified categories. Taiwan is not a general birthplace-citizenship system. Evidence of the parent’s nationality and the legal parent–child relationship is needed to establish the child’s status for official documents. For a child born outside marriage to an ROC father and a foreign mother, BOCA’s first-passport guidance cited below requires paternal acknowledgment registration before the child can apply for a passport through the father. Inconsistent parentage records also need to be resolved.

For a child born outside the United States, the [State Department's citizenship-transmission guidance](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/legal/travel-legal-considerations/us-citizenship/Acquisition-US-Citizenship-Child-Born-Abroad.html) addresses the parents' citizenship and marital circumstances, parentage and required U.S. physical presence. The applicable rule must be identified before counting time in the United States. A U.S. citizen parent does not guarantee transmission in every case. The American Institute in Taiwan (AIT) will need evidence of the parent's relevant U.S. residence and physical presence to determine whether the applicable requirement was met.

## When the baby is born in Taiwan

Compare the hospital certificate's names, dates and identifying information with the parents' records. The certificate supplies evidence for government registration, rather than completing it. Domestic birth registration and initial household registration for a child born overseas follow different procedures. The [Department of Household Registration's guidance](https://www.ris.gov.tw/documents/html/2/3/4/702.html) sets out initial household registration, including the settlement-certificate route for overseas-born children. The parent's actual household status affects which procedure applies.

If the baby acquired U.S. citizenship at birth abroad, a Consular Report of Birth Abroad records that acquisition. The [State Department's birth-abroad guidance](https://travel.state.gov/en/international-travel/living-abroad/birth.html) explains its purpose. A CRBA cannot confer citizenship on an ineligible child, and it is separate from the U.S. passport. Obtain the current Taiwan application and appointment arrangements from AIT.

## When the baby is born in the United States

The U.S. birth record does not automatically add the child to a Taiwan household. The [National Immigration Agency settlement guidance for nationals without household registration](https://www.immigration.gov.tw/5385/7244/7250/7281/定居/36430/) includes overseas-born children in specified categories. The parent's household registration at birth, the child's age and the passport used for entry are among the relevant conditions.

Identify the category before assuming that the child can register immediately or must first live in Taiwan for a fixed period. An eligible child can obtain settlement permission and then proceed to initial household registration. These routes for eligible overseas-born children require a parent who was a national residing in Taiwan with household registration when the child was born. If neither parent met that condition, the child's eligibility must be examined under a different route. Parentage and any necessary parental consent must also be established.

For U.S.-issued documents, arrange verification and Chinese translation with the responsible Taiwan representative office and receiving authority. Giving those offices the document type and issuing authority lets them identify the authentication required before you order extra copies.

A child may be eligible for an ROC passport before settlement or household registration, as [BOCA’s overseas-born first-passport guidance](https://www.boca.gov.tw/fp-27-22-83434-1.html) explains. After initial household registration through the cited NIA route, a specific departure requirement applies: the first departure must use an ROC passport bearing the assigned national identification number. NIA notice (7) states this requirement. Before the return journey, obtain the ROC passport bearing the child’s assigned identification number.

## Registration status affects the travel documents

The child's citizenship and Taiwan household-registration status determine which passport application applies. The [State Department’s passport guidance for children under 16](https://travel.state.gov/en/passports/apply/child/under-16.html) addresses citizenship evidence, parental consent and the child's application. A parent's passport, hospital certificate or pending CRBA cannot replace the child's required travel document.

[BOCA’s household-office identity verification guidance](https://www.boca.gov.tw/cp-35-6012-14fbe-1.html) applies to eligible applicants who already have Taiwan household registration; it should not be used as though all children have that status. Parents should confirm the necessary passports and entry documents with both countries' authorities before booking travel.

If the parents' records need attention, [the U.S.–Taiwan marriage registration checklist](/en/columns/marrying-taiwanese-national-registration-checklist) discusses that earlier step.
