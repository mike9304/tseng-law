# Your Taiwan exchange says the withdrawal passed your password and OTP. Is the loss yours?

An exchange may say that using your password and one-time code made the withdrawal your instruction. Taiwan's registration regulation lets a custodian move customer assets on the customer's instruction, yet never says whether an order from someone who took over the account counts as the customer's. You can claim return or compensation from the exchange. The result depends on the terms, the care the exchange owed, how its security handled the withdrawal, and whether anything you did contributed.

Records come first. Lock the account, put the dispute to the exchange in writing, and ask it to keep and release the logs of the session that sent the coins. Then go to the police with the transaction hash.

## What the registration rules ask of the exchange

Taiwan's Money Laundering Control Act (洗錢防制法), in [Article 6](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0380131&flno=6), bars firms that have not completed anti-money-laundering registration from offering virtual-asset services. Under the registration regulation (提供虛擬資產服務之事業或人員洗錢防制登記辦法), in force since November 30, 2024 ([Article 31](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0400162&flno=31)), firms register with the Financial Supervisory Commission (金融監督管理委員會) category by category and must not operate a category they have not registered ([Article 3](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0400162&flno=3)). A firm that keeps or manages virtual assets for others as a business, or provides tools to manage them, is a custodian (虛擬資產保管商) under [Article 2](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0400162&flno=2), the category that [Articles 23](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0400162&flno=23), [24](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0400162&flno=24) and 25 to 27 address.

The exchange's answer leans on [Article 25](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0400162&flno=25), which says a custodian must not use a customer's assets except to dispose of them on the customer's instruction (客戶之指示), to set off fees the customer owes it, or for another reason the Commission permits. Whose instruction is it when an intruder holds your credentials? The regulation leaves that open.

Under [Article 12](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0400162&flno=12), the exchange must build an information security management system suited to its information and systems, operate stable and secure systems matched to the scale and nature of its business, and adopt measures and procedures that keep data, and its transmission, exchange or processing, available, accurate, confidential and secure. It also needs enough qualified security staff.

A custodian must adopt and publish clear custody policies and procedures and build them into its internal controls. [Article 26](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0400162&flno=26) requires four items: policies and procedures against the risk of customer assets being lost, stolen or made unusable; the split between cold and hot wallets; specifications and procedures for wallet access and private-key custody; and a procedure for events affecting customers' asset rights. The Commission sets that wallet split separately.

[Article 14](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0400162&flno=14) requires the exchange to post its information security policy, customer protection measures and complaint procedure in a prominent place on its website, or by another method the Commission designates. A major incident (重大偶發事件) must be announced immediately, with the important facts or a clarification, and reported to the Commission.

None of this says who pays. The published policies are a yardstick for reading your log.

The Virtual Asset Service Act (虛擬資產服務法), promulgated on July 22, 2026, does not apply yet. As of November 19, 2026 the [national law database](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawHistory.aspx?pcode=G0400163) lists it as not yet in force, with the effective date left to the Executive Yuan (行政院). A custodian slowing its own withdrawals is covered in [the column on a custodian slowing withdrawals](/en/columns/taiwan-crypto-custodian-shutdown-virtual-asset-act-not-in-force).

## How the Civil Code weighs an authenticated withdrawal

Which Civil Code (民法) rules govern an exchange account is for a court to decide; the label in the terms does not settle it alone. A keeper under a deposit (寄託) must use the same care it uses for its own affairs or, if it is paid (受有報酬), the care of a good administrator (善良管理人之注意) ([Article 590](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=590)). [Article 535](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=535) holds an agent under a mandate (委任) to the principal's instructions and to the same two levels of care. [Article 529](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=529) applies mandate rules to service contracts of no other type the law defines, and [Article 544](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=544) makes an agent liable for damage from its negligence in handling the matter or from acts beyond its authority.

Fees therefore bear on the standard.

Then the log takes over. Was the login from a new device? Had the email or phone number just changed? Did the coins go to a fresh address without the checks its published policy describes?

Your own fault counts too: where it helped the loss happen or grow, [Article 217](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=217) lets a court cut the damages or deny them. A code typed into a phishing page would give the exchange that argument. So would days of silence after the takeover.

Terms that make every authenticated instruction yours, and exclude the exchange's liability for it, meet three limits. [Article 222](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=222) says liability for intent or gross negligence cannot be excluded in advance. Where one party drafted the terms in advance for contracts of the same kind, [Article 247-1](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=247-1) voids, to the extent it is obviously unfair in the circumstances, a clause that exempts or reduces the drafter's liability, increases the other party's liability, makes the other party waive or restrict rights, or otherwise puts the other party at a serious disadvantage.

If you used the account as a consumer, the Consumer Protection Act (消費者保護法) adds tests. Under [Article 11](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0170001&flno=11), standard terms must follow equality and reciprocity, and a doubtful term must be read in the consumer's favor. [Article 11-1](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0170001&flno=11-1) requires a reasonable period of up to 30 days for the consumer to review all the terms before a standard contract is concluded, and a term making the consumer give up that review is void. If the period was not given, the terms are not part of the contract unless the consumer claims they are. [Article 12](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0170001&flno=12) voids terms that breach good faith and are obviously unfair to the consumer. It presumes that unfairness where a term breaks equality and reciprocity, plainly contradicts the purpose of the default rule it excludes, or limits the main rights or duties so far that the contract's purpose is hard to achieve. Articles 247-1 and 12 both turn on the circumstances, and the login steps you agreed to are among them.

## Records to request, and where the coins went

Write through the exchange's complaint procedure, which [Article 13](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0400162&flno=13) requires it to have and to run fairly and promptly. Ask it to freeze the account and to keep and provide the session's login times, IP addresses and device identifiers, any recent change to contact or two-factor settings, when the destination address was added, and the withdrawal record with its transaction hash.

Under [Article 27](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0400162&flno=27), a custodian must record each customer's name, wallet address, asset amount and quantity, and transfers in and out, and must keep those records and provide them to the customer. They must be kept for five years after the custody business relationship ends and, if there is a dispute, until it is resolved. [Article 15](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0400162&flno=15) requires records of services provided to customers to be kept for inspection on the same terms, counted from the end of the service relationship. Neither article names login IP addresses or device data, and Article 15 does not list what its records include. Ask for those logs by name and keep the reply.

Keep your own evidence too: any SIM-replacement record from the carrier, email security alerts, the one-time codes received or missing, and any message that asked for them.

[Article 358 of the Criminal Code](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0000001&flno=358) (中華民國刑法) covers intruding, without cause, into another person's computer or related equipment by entering their account password, cracking its protective measures, or exploiting a system flaw. [Article 359](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0000001&flno=359) covers obtaining, deleting or altering the electronic records of another's computer or related equipment without cause, causing damage to the public or others. [Article 339-3](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0000001&flno=339-3) covers computer fraud: with intent of unlawful gain for oneself or a third person, improperly entering false data or commands into a computer or related equipment to create records of property rights being acquired, lost or changed, and so obtaining another's property, or an unlawful benefit for oneself or a third person. Attempts are punishable. By [Article 363](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0000001&flno=363), Articles 358 to 360 are complaint-only offenses (告訴乃論), so prosecution for them needs your complaint. Article 339-3 is not on that list. Complaint timing is covered in [the column on a hacked offshore forex account](/en/columns/taiwan-overseas-fx-broker-account-hacked-criminal-complaint-jurisdiction).

If the coins reached a deposit address at another Taiwan-registered exchange, ask it in writing to hold the deposit, and give the police the address and hash. The registration regulation sets no procedure for such a request.

## Bringing the claim

A Taiwan lawyer can compare your terms and authentication clauses with the exchange's logs and published policies, then demand return of the coins or compensation in writing, with a records request. Counsel can also file the criminal complaint and sue the exchange, or an intruder once identified. If enforcing a money claim, or one readily convertible to money, risks becoming impossible or very difficult, provisional attachment (假扣押) can secure it first under [Articles 522](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=522) and [523](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=523) of the Code of Civil Procedure (民事訴訟法). [Article 526](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=526) requires a prima facie showing (釋明) of the claim and that risk. If the showing falls short, the court may still order attachment against security, where the creditor offers it or the court thinks fit, and it may demand security even after a full showing.
