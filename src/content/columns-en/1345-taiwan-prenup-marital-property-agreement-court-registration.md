# Will Taiwan treat your US prenup as a marital property agreement?

Taiwan may give effect to a written prenup, but not simply because the couple signed a document called a prenup in the United States. For an American–Taiwanese couple, [Article 48 of the Act Governing the Choice of Law in Civil Matters Involving Foreign Elements](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000007&flno=48) first asks which country's law governs the marital property regime. If Taiwan law governs, the Civil Code then asks whether the spouses made the kind of marital property contract that Taiwan law recognizes. A US-style prenup does not automatically become Taiwan's separate-property regime (分別財產制).

Article 48's third paragraph can take the Taipei apartment out of its first two paragraphs only when the law of the place where the apartment sits requires a special rule. The brokerage account is not an immovable, so it stays on Article 48's ordinary sequence. A transaction involving Taiwan property and a good-faith third party may also bring Taiwan law into the analysis under [Article 49](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000007&flno=49).

## One writing can raise two different legal questions

Spouses may agree in writing under [Article 48](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000007&flno=48) that their marital property regime will follow the national law or domicile law of either spouse. If there is no written agreement, or if the agreement is invalid under the national or domicile law selected under the first paragraph, the sequence is fixed: common national law, then common domicile law, then the law of the place most closely connected with the marriage. A clause that merely says an apartment remains one partner's property may allocate an asset, yet it may not identify any national or domicile law. The exact wording matters.

Choosing Taiwan law is not the same step as choosing a Taiwan contractual marital property regime (約定財產制). [Civil Code Article 1004](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=1004) permits spouses, before or after marriage, to select one of the contractual regimes provided by the Code. Under community property (共同財產制), their property and income are combined, except for special property (特有財產), and held by the spouses in undivided common ownership (夫妻公同共有). Under separate property, each spouse keeps ownership and independently manages, uses, receives returns from, and disposes of their property ([Articles 1031](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=1031) and [1044](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=1044)).

Written form is mandatory under [Article 1007](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=1007). It applies to the creation, amendment, or termination of a marital property contract. A US agreement may contain a valid choice-of-law clause, a valid Taiwan regime selection, both, or neither. No label decides that analysis.

## The Taipei apartment needs its own analysis

Paragraph 3 of Article 48 prevents a blanket answer for real estate. The first two paragraphs do not apply to a spouse's immovable when the law of the place where it sits requires special provisions. For a Taipei apartment, Taiwan is the place where the property sits. Location alone is insufficient. The paragraph is conditional; counsel needs to identify the precise issue and any Taiwan special rule that applies to it.

If Taiwan's statutory property regime (法定財產制) governs, a spouse's property is classified as premarital or postmarital and remains owned by that spouse. Property that cannot be shown to be either premarital or postmarital is presumed to be postmarital, while property that cannot be proved to belong to one spouse is presumed jointly owned. Fruits (孳息) produced by premarital property during the marriage are treated as postmarital property ([Article 1017](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=1017)). Each spouse manages, uses, receives returns from, and disposes of their own property under [Article 1018](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=1018).

For an apartment bought before the wedding, the acquisition record and current land-registration transcript therefore matter. Marital property contract registration does not replace the land record: [Article 1008](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=1008) expressly says it does not affect property-right registration made under other laws. Registration does not retitle the apartment.

## The brokerage account turns on records and the governing regime

Brokerage analysis starts with Article 48's written choice and default sequence because the account is not an immovable. If Taiwan law governs and the couple has not made a marital property contract, [Article 1005](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=1005) places them under the statutory regime unless the Civil Code provides otherwise. A court declaration switching the couple to separate property is one example: after the judgment becomes final, the second paragraph of [Non-contentious Matters Act Article 104](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010008&flno=104) requires the court to direct the registry to register the change. Statements showing the account and its balance before the marriage can support premarital classification; without proof of when property was acquired, Article 1017 presumes it to be postmarital. Proof sets the starting classification. Article 1017 also treats fruits from premarital property during marriage as postmarital, but it does not classify every type of brokerage-account growth.

Ownership during the marriage and a later balancing claim are separate points. When the statutory regime ends, [Article 1030-1](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=1030-1) subtracts debts incurred during the marriage from each spouse's existing postmarital property. If a balance remains, the difference between the two balances must be divided equally. Inherited or otherwise gratuitously acquired property and compensation for non-pecuniary harm are excluded. The court may adjust or waive a spouse's distribution amount when equal division would be unfair, and that unfairness must come from the spouse having made no contribution or cooperation toward married life or from other circumstances. In making that ruling, the court must consider together household labor, child care, overall cooperation toward the family, the duration of living together and separation, when the postmarital property was acquired, and each spouse's economic ability. The broader calculation is explained in [Taiwan marital property division with an American spouse](/en/columns/taiwan-marital-property-division-us-assets).

A separate-property contract changes that framework if Taiwan law recognizes it: Article 1044 leaves each spouse with ownership and independent control. A community-property contract points in another direction under Article 1031. That is why a clause listing one existing account is not, by itself, a complete answer to which Taiwan regime the couple selected.

## Third-party effect and registration require separate checks

When foreign law governs the marital property regime, [Article 49 of the choice-of-law act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000007&flno=49) applies if the spouses enter into a legal act with a good-faith third party concerning their property in Taiwan. Taiwan law then decides the effect of the marital property regime against that third party.

[Civil Code Article 1008](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=1008) answers a narrower question about a Taiwan-law marital property contract. Its creation, amendment, or termination cannot be asserted against third parties without registration. Read with Article 1007, the provisions distinguish written form from third-party effect. The writing may govern the spouses' relationship, but registration is necessary before they rely on that contract against third parties.

The district-court registry (地方法院登記處) handles the filing and keeps the marital property contract register under [Articles 101](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010008&flno=101) and [103](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010008&flno=103) of the Non-contentious Matters Act. The court at the spouses' domicile has jurisdiction. If registration cannot be made there, or their principal property is at their residence, the court at the residence may have jurisdiction; if neither rule identifies a court, jurisdiction lies with the court where the Judicial Yuan is located.

[Article 104](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010008&flno=104) requires the spouses to attach the marital property contract, a property inventory with supporting documents, and each spouse's signature specimen or seal specimen (簽名式或印鑑). Property that must be registered under law also requires a transcript from the responsible registry. Both spouses apply, unless the contract has been notarized, in which case one may apply.

Before filing, confirm the fee and expected processing time with the registry, along with any Chinese-translation or authentication requirements.

For the brokerage account, Article 1008 answers the third-party question in principle but does not say how a particular broker will change an account record or respond operationally. Those details should be checked before the couple assumes that a court registration alone has changed either asset.
