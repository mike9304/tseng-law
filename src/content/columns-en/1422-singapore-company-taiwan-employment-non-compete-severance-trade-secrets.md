# Hiring in Taiwan from Singapore: non-compete pay, termination notice and trade secrets

An unpaid post-employment non-compete binding a worker covered by Taiwan's Labor Standards Act is void. As of November 24, 2026, [Article 9-1 of the Labor Standards Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0030001&flno=9-1) (勞動基準法) bars post-departure non-compete covenants unless four statutory conditions are all met, including separate post-termination pay. [Article 7-3 of the Enforcement Rules of the Labor Standards Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0030002&flno=7-3) (勞動基準法施行細則) weighs reasonable compensation on four factors, starting with monthly pay of no less than 50% of one month's average wage at termination.

When a Singapore tech, fintech, or regional-HQ company hires engineers, product leads, or senior sales staff in Taiwan using a regional offer letter, non-compete pay is only the first gap. The same templates need two more Taiwan fixes: statutory termination grounds paired with a 30-day dismissal clock, and confidentiality measures that can be shown on paper.

## Four statutory conditions for a valid non-compete

Under [Labor Standards Act Article 9-1, paragraph 1](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0030001&flno=9-1), an employer must not bind a worker after exit unless all four conditions hold:

1. Legitimate protectable business interest (正當營業利益).
2. A position or duties granting access to or use of the employer's trade secrets.
3. Reasonable limits on duration, geographic area, occupational scope, and restricted prospective employers.
4. Reasonable compensation for losses the worker suffers by refraining from competition.

Missing one condition voids the clause. Paragraph 3 renders the agreement void if any of those four requirements fails. Duration carries a strict ceiling under paragraph 4: a non-compete cannot exceed two years, and any longer term is shortened to two years.

[Article 7-1 of the Enforcement Rules](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0030002&flno=7-1) requires a written agreement detailing the scope limits and compensation terms. Both employer and employee must sign or seal it, and each party keeps one copy.

A generic regional clause can fail any of the four scope tests in [Article 7-2 of the Enforcement Rules](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0030002&flno=7-2). Duration cannot outlast the lifecycle of the protected trade secrets or technical information, capped at two years. Territory must remain within the employer's actual operating area. Restricted activities must be concrete, clear, and identical or similar to the worker's former role. Restricted competitors must likewise be concrete, clear, and limited to businesses in identical or similar competing activities. Barring a Taipei firmware engineer from any software job across all of Asia, when the employer sells only in Taiwan, fails those rules (an invented example).

## The fifty-percent monthly wage benchmark and payout timing

Payments earned during employment do not count. [Article 9-1, paragraph 2 of the Labor Standards Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0030001&flno=9-1) excludes payments received while employed from statutory non-compete compensation. Labeling part of base salary or a retention bonus as non-compete pay will not satisfy the statute.

[Article 7-3 of the Enforcement Rules](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0030002&flno=7-3) lists four factors to be weighed together. The first is numerical: monthly compensation of no less than 50% of one month's average wage at termination. The amount should also be enough to cover the former worker's living needs during the restriction and be commensurate with the losses tied to the duration, area, activity, and competitor scope. Other matters bearing on reasonableness count as well.

Paragraph 2 of Article 7-3 requires stipulating payment after termination, either as an advance lump sum or in monthly installments. Should a company ask every Taiwan hire to sign a non-compete just in case? For a role with no access to trade secrets, the covenant fails the second condition of Article 9-1 and is void under paragraph 3 of that article, whatever the company pays. For everyone else, each signed covenant commits the company to post-exit pay weighed against at least half a month's average wage per restricted month.

## Termination grounds, notice periods and the thirty-day clock

Unilateral dismissal on notice alone does not fit Taiwan. Under [Labor Standards Act Article 11](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0030001&flno=11), an employer must not terminate with advance notice unless one of five statutory grounds exists: business closure or transfer, losses or business contraction, force majeure halting work for one month or longer, a change in business nature requiring staff cuts with no suitable reassignment, or proven incompetence for the job. Notice alone cannot replace those grounds.

When an Article 11 ground applies, [Article 16](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0030001&flno=16) sets mandatory advance notice tiers:

| Continuous service with the employer | Minimum advance notice |
|---|---|
| 3 months to less than 1 year | 10 days |
| 1 year to less than 3 years | 20 days |
| 3 years or more | 30 days |

During notice, Article 16, paragraph 2 grants paid job-search leave of up to two working days per week. Terminating immediately without notice requires paying notice-period wages under paragraph 3. Under [Article 17, paragraph 2](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0030001&flno=17), severance must be paid within 30 days after termination. Article 17, paragraph 1 sets the Act's baseline formula: one month's average wage per full year of service (prorated for partial years, counting under a month as one month), though employers must verify which statutory pension regime governs each employee's severance calculation before computing payouts.

Serious misconduct sits outside the Article 16 notice-and-severance track. [Article 12, paragraph 1](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0030001&flno=12) permits termination without notice on six listed grounds, including a material breach of the employment contract or work rules (item 4) and intentionally damaging company property or intentionally leaking technical or business secrets causing harm (item 5).

Thirty days run from the day the employer learns the facts. Article 12, paragraph 2 applies that strict 30-day window to dismissals under items 1, 2, 4, 5, and 6. Slow cross-border sign-offs can forfeit summary dismissal completely.

## Trade secrets require real safeguards before a leak happens

The Trade Secrets Act's remedies and penalties reach only information that qualifies under [Article 2 of the Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0080028&flno=2) (營業秘密法). Methods, techniques, processes, formulas, programs, designs, or other information usable in production, sales, or operations must meet all three tests: not generally known in the field, actual or potential economic value from secrecy, and reasonable confidentiality measures (合理之保密措施) taken by the owner.

A generic NDA paragraph does not by itself show the third element. Role-based repository permissions, confidentiality markings, download logs, and written exit instructions document that the owner actually took reasonable measures.

Under [Article 10](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0080028&flno=10), infringement covers acquiring a trade secret by improper means—which paragraph 2 defines to include unauthorized reproduction and breach of a confidentiality duty—as well as the improper use or disclosure of a secret acquired through a legal act such as an employment contract. Civil claims under [Article 11](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0080028&flno=11) let owners halt or prevent infringement and demand destruction of items produced by or exclusively used for the infringement. [Article 12](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0080028&flno=12) makes intentional or negligent infringers liable for damages, jointly and severally where several act together. The damages claim lapses if not exercised within two years after the claimant learns of the act and the liable party, or ten years after the act. [Article 13](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0080028&flno=13) gives owners two alternative measures. The first is damages under Civil Code Article 216 or, where loss cannot be proven, the gap between normally expected profit and profit earned after the infringement. The second is the infringer's gains, with total infringing revenue treated as gain if the infringer cannot prove its costs. For intentional infringement, courts may award up to three times proven damages upon request.

Criminal penalties reach five years in prison under [Article 13-1](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0080028&flno=13-1). Acting with intent to obtain unlawful benefit or harm the owner through theft, unauthorized copying, or unauthorized use or disclosure carries up to five years in prison or detention, plus an optional fine of NT$1,000,000 to NT$10,000,000. Item 3 also penalizes failing to delete or destroy—or concealing—a trade secret after owner notice to delete or destroy it. Attempts are punishable. If the offender's illicit gain exceeds the maximum fine, the court may raise the fine to as much as three times that gain.

The ceiling doubles to ten years when the secret is headed abroad. Under [Article 13-2](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0080028&flno=13-2), committing an Article 13-1 crime with intent to use the secret in a foreign country, the Mainland Area, Hong Kong, or Macau carries one to ten years in prison and an optional fine of NT$3,000,000 to NT$50,000,000. Attempts are punishable, and if the offender's gain exceeds the maximum fine, the court may raise the fine to between two and ten times that gain. Written deletion notices at offboarding build an Article 13-1, item 3 record immediately.

## Aligning regional offer letters and offboarding with Taiwan law

Separate confidentiality and IP-assignment terms from any paid non-compete before onboarding Taiwan staff. Identify roles with verified trade-secret access, budget the 50% monthly benchmark up front, enforce access controls supporting Trade Secrets Act Article 2, and brief managers on the 30-day dismissal clock in Labor Standards Act Article 12. Singapore employment rules require separate local confirmation.
