# Analysis of Inheritance and Parental Rights Issues Between Koo Jun-yup and Barbie Hsu

![Featured image](../images/016-taiwan-inheritance-custody-analysis/featured-01.jpg)

The death of Barbie Hsu (大S) was a profound shock to people in Taiwan.

​

Barbie Hsu and her younger sister Dee Hsu debuted in their teens and

worked as idols, popular variety-show hosts, drama actors, and in many other fields.

People in Taiwan had watched Barbie Hsu grow up and marry,

so her sudden death felt like the loss of a girl who had grown up with them, and the grief runs deep.

![](../images/016-taiwan-inheritance-custody-analysis/img-01.jpg)

SBS News asked me to explain Taiwan law on this matter for Korean viewers,

but because of editing many important points were not broadcast, which was unfortunate,

and many people also seem curious about the inheritance and parental-rights issues in this case,

so I would like to share them with you in Q&A form.

![](../images/016-taiwan-inheritance-custody-analysis/img-02.jpg)

![](../images/016-taiwan-inheritance-custody-analysis/img-03.jpg)

**Q1. Under Taiwan’s Civil Code, how would Barbie Hsu’s estate be inherited?**

Under Articles 1138, 1141, and 1144 of Taiwan’s Civil Code,

a spouse is an absolute heir and has inheritance rights regardless of rank.

Heirs other than the spouse are ordered by rank. Children are first-rank heirs, so the spouse and children inherit.

If there are no children, the next rank—parents—inherit, so the spouse and parents inherit together.

If both parents have died, the next rank—siblings—inherit, so the spouse and siblings inherit together.

When a spouse and children inherit together, the estate is divided equally by the number of persons.

​

If Barbie Hsu did not leave a special will,

her spouse Koo Jun-yup and her two children would be the heirs. Each would inherit one-third of the estate.

​

​

**Q2. Because Koo Jun-yup’s marriage to Barbie Hsu was short, would it be difficult for him to inherit property?**

No.

Regardless of how long or short the marriage was, the estate can be inherited.

However, there is a difference regarding the **spousal residual property distribution claim** (夫妻剩餘財產分配請求權).

A residual property distribution claim arises when the marital relationship ends—for example upon divorce or the death of one spouse—

and the spouse with less property may claim the difference from the other.

But when calculating whose property is greater, only property acquired after marriage is counted.

That is because post-marriage property is property obtained through the two persons building a household and working together.

If Barbie Hsu accumulated more property after marriage than Koo Jun-yup,

then before calculating the estate to be inherited, Koo Jun-yup could assert the spousal residual property distribution claim and claim half of the difference in the two sides’ post-marriage property.

Koo Jun-yup cannot claim difference distribution against property Barbie Hsu acquired before marriage.

The two had been married only about three years, and Barbie Hsu had relatively few entertainment activities in recent years,

so it is reasonable to infer that her property did not increase much over those three years.

Most of Barbie Hsu’s estate appears likely to be pre-marriage property.

Therefore, on a reasonable inference, Koo Jun-yup either could not make a residual property distribution claim,

or even if he did, the amount would not be large.

​

**Q3. Under Taiwan law, when Barbie Hsu dies, is Wang Xiaofei, the children’s biological father, automatically designated as the caregiver for the two children?**

Yes.

Under Taiwan’s Civil Code, only when the parents are “both” unable to exercise parental rights over the children does the law provide for designating a parental-rights holder in the order of cohabiting grandparents, cohabiting siblings, then non-cohabiting grandparents.

Therefore, after Barbie Hsu’s death, Wang Xiaofei automatically becomes the sole holder of parental rights over the two children without any lawsuit procedure.

Even if Barbie Hsu had designated Koo Jun-yup, her mother, or her younger sister as parental-rights holders for the two minor children by will during her lifetime, that would have no effect. Designating a parental-rights holder by will takes effect only when the last parental-rights holder has died.

​

Only if Wang Xiaofei, the former husband, had died before Barbie Hsu

could Barbie Hsu have designated a parental-rights holder by will.

​

**Q4. If Wang Xiaofei, who had been in conflict with Barbie Hsu, becomes able to take the children’s share of the property, is there a possibility that Barbie Hsu’s family would oppose this and file a lawsuit?**

After Barbie Hsu’s death, Wang Xiaofei automatically becomes the sole holder of parental rights over the two children without any lawsuit procedure.

Wang Xiaofei can solely manage the children’s property,

and Barbie Hsu’s family cannot file an opposition lawsuit against this.

However, through a will, Barbie Hsu could have specified how the estate is to be used.

For example, she could have placed property in trust to be paid when the children reach adulthood.

​

And Barbie Hsu’s relatives can request, through a **“change of parental-rights holder”** (改定親權) lawsuit,

that the children’s parental-rights holder be changed from Wang Xiaofei to a relative of Barbie Hsu.

But in practice, changing the parental-rights holder is difficult.

Only if the court finds that Wang Xiaofei has failed to perform protection and custody duties or that there is a situation adverse to the children

is there an opportunity to change the parental-rights holder.

​

​

**Q5. If Wang Xiaofei raises the two children and manages their share of the estate, and uses the property arbitrarily, can a lawsuit be filed?**

If Wang Xiaofei abuses parental rights (for example, embezzling the estate or failing to care for the children),

the court may suspend all or part of Wang Xiaofei’s rights in the children’s interest,

after which the court will designate another parental-rights holder.

​

**Q6. As a Taiwan lawyer, how do you think this matter will develop? What legal disputes or trials are likely?**

In my view, there are likely to be lawsuits on two fronts going forward.

​

**1. Lawsuits that existed between Barbie Hsu and Wang Xiaofei:**

Barbie Hsu’s heirs—Koo Jun-yup and the two minor children—will succeed to the litigation.

Because the two minor children are still young and have interests adverse to Wang Xiaofei,

for the two minor children the court may, upon application, appoint a special representative to exercise their rights.

In that case it could develop into civil litigation among Wang Xiaofei, Koo Jun-yup, and the two children.

​

**2. Litigation related to parental rights over the children:**

Barbie Hsu’s family may file a parental-rights change lawsuit against Wang Xiaofei,

seeking to change the children’s parental-rights holder from Wang Xiaofei to a relative of Barbie Hsu.

Barbie Hsu’s relatives may argue the **“wishes of the minor children”** and the **“principle of least disruption.”**

The “principle of least disruption” means minimizing changes in the child’s life—such as avoiding a move or school transfer and not leaving a familiar living environment—so as to minimize the shock caused by change.

The court will decide whether to change parental rights in light of the children’s best interests.

However, under current Taiwan judicial practice, actually changing parental rights is not easy.

​

But even after Wang Xiaofei obtains parental rights over the two children, the two children are not necessarily required to leave Taiwan.

If Wang Xiaofei does not wish to raise them personally,

the two children can continue to receive education in Taiwan,

and certain parental-rights matters can be temporarily delegated to relatives in Taiwan for custody,

so it is not necessary to take the children out of Taiwan.

​

Or, if Wang Xiaofei later abuses parental rights (for example, monopolizing the estate or failing to care for the children),

the court may declare a suspension of all or part of his rights in the children’s interest,

after which the court may appoint another parental-rights holder.

​

​

No one can predict the future, and life is uncertain,

so I recommend carefully considering property distribution and parental rights during your lifetime and preparing a will.

Only then can property and parental rights be properly arranged,

and only then can you protect the people you cherish and care for.
