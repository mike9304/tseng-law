# Winning a Taiwan employment case does not repay every legal bill

An American employee applying for help with an unpaid-wage claim must report income and assets at the time of the application. Losing the salary may change that assessment while savings remain relevant. The Ministry of Labor project sets its own means thresholds.

## Savings, legal residence and the support application

[Legal Aid Act Article 14](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=A0030157&flno=14) includes foreigners legally residing in Taiwan. That is an eligibility gateway. The relevant means and case criteria still determine the grant.

[Article 6 of the Ministry of Labor aid regulations](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0020018&flno=6) treats monthly total income over NT$65,000 or total assets over NT$3 million as sufficient means, excluding an owner-occupied home. It permits specified deductions where necessary family medical expenditure, single parenting or care of direct relatives causes serious hardship. Occupational accidents causing serious injury or death receive the separate means exception in Article 3. The assessment uses the applicant’s position when applying.

An occupational accident can also change the language assistance available. The Ministry’s [2026 measures](https://www.mol.gov.tw/1607/1632/1633/87257/) describe lawyers accompanying occupational-accident cases through settlement, mediation and litigation, with full-process interpretation for migrant workers injured at work. That provision belongs to the occupational-accident scheme. It does not promise the same service for every unpaid-wage or dismissal case. What the assistance approval covers remains relevant to the client’s own budget.

For the Ministry of Labor project, [Article 3 of the Regulations on Aids for Legal Service and Living Expenses of Labor-Management Disputes](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0020018&flno=3) covers specified disputes, including Labor Standards Act termination, unpaid wages, severance and retirement benefits. Ordinarily, the worker needs unsuccessful mediation by the competent administrative authority before court labor mediation or litigation, plus satisfaction of the means condition. The preliminary-mediation requirement has listed exceptions for appeals, retrial and final enforcement, and for preservation or enforcement after an employer breaches a successful administrative mediation. Applications are due within 180 days after the relevant procedure begins.

Language assistance at the earlier administrative mediation has its own basis. The Ministry’s [administrative-mediation legal assistance](https://www.mol.gov.tw/1607/28752/28774/28776/28778/30712/) provides lawyer consultation and accompaniment in eligible disputes, with interpretation where language needs call for it. A grant for that program does not establish payment for later court proceedings or privately arranged interpretation.

## The type of claim changes the filing payment

A dismissal dispute can produce more than one remedy. Confirmation that employment continues, wage arrears and other damages must be classified by what is requested. For joined claims outside Labor Incident Act Articles 11–13, [Article 11(2) of the labor case hearing rules](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010069&flno=11) directs the court to calculate fees under the Code of Civil Procedure.

Under [Labor Incident Act Article 12](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010064&flno=12), two-thirds of the court fee are temporarily not collected when an employee brings or appeals their own claim to confirm employment or obtain wages, retirement benefits or severance. Ordinarily, one-third is paid initially. The final costs decision determines who bears them.

Court relief is another application. [Labor Incident Act Article 14](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010064&flno=14) provides relief on application in occupational-injury labor litigation unless there is plainly no prospect of success, and deems workers in listed welfare categories unable to pay.

Court labor mediation also has a fee. [Code of Civil Procedure Article 77-20](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=77-20) exempts non-property disputes and property claims below NT$100,000, with bands of NT$1,000–5,000 for property claims at or above that level. Administrative and court mediation have different roles, discussed in the [foreign employee mediation column](/en/columns/taiwan-labor-mediation-foreign-employee).

Security for provisional attachment can tie up cash before judgment. For its listed claims, including wages, occupational-injury compensation or damages, retirement benefits, severance and confirmation of employment, [Labor Incident Act Article 47](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010064&flno=47) caps security ordered under Code Article 526(2) or (3) for the specified provisional measures at one-tenth of the claim’s amount or value. A prima facie showing that security would cause serious livelihood hardship prevents the court from ordering it under that provision.

Workers approved for representation support under Article 3(1) of the Ministry of Labor regulations can also make a separate application for necessary expenses under [Article 15 of the aid regulations](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0020018&flno=15). Court, application and enforcement fees, witness allowances and travel, and expert fees are among the listed items. [Article 16](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0020018&flno=16) caps labor-case support at NT$100,000 per case. The deadline is sixty days after the relevant court labor mediation, litigation or enforcement procedure ends. When a later ruling or court refund notice first settles the amount, the sixty days run from the ruling becoming final or service of that notice.

## The retainer and the amount fixed after judgment

Before retaining counsel, an English-speaking client can obtain a quote identifying who commissions language services and who pays the provider. Counsel may arrange the work, or the client may contract with the provider separately. The engagement can record whether those charges are included in the lawyer’s fee or billed separately, and what the client pays if an aid application is refused. These are terms to settle in the engagement, not an assurance of reimbursement by the employer.

Ordinary private counsel fees for the first and second instances are outside the litigation-cost categories in [Code Article 77-25](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=77-25). The included categories are lawyers appointed by the court or presiding judge under a statute and third-instance counsel under [Article 466-3](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=466-3). The court assesses the recoverable amount within statutory caps. The retainer sets the client’s obligation to private counsel.

[Code Article 78](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=78) generally places litigation costs on the losing party. But success on wages and failure on an additional damages demand can produce a mixed result. Under [Article 79](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=79), the court may allocate costs proportionately, charge one side or leave each side to its own expenditure.

At enforcement of the payment claims in Article 12, fees on the amount exceeding NT$200,000 are temporarily not collected and are deducted from enforcement proceeds.

Receipts remain relevant after judgment. If the costs decision does not fix an amount, [Article 91](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=91) provides for an application to the first-instance court after the case ends. It requires a costs statement, copies for the other party and evidence supporting the amount. For a translation charge, the statement can identify the document used in the case and the payment claimed.

That expense still has to qualify as litigation costs. [Code Article 77-23](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=77-23) covers necessary expenses including translation of litigation documents, with items and standards set by the Judicial Yuan. Its rules also address court-ordered advances, their use for the specified items and return of any balance after the litigation ends. A private provider’s invoice establishes a charge; it does not by itself establish an amount recoverable from the employer. The costs calculation therefore distinguishes court advances, qualifying expenses and private charges that remain with the client.

A Taiwan lawyer can assess assistance eligibility, distinguish the remedies and costs, pursue provisional measures and apply to fix recoverable costs. An instruction identifying each procedural stage and who arranges translation makes the estimate usable even if assistance is refused or the eventual judgment gives only partial relief.
