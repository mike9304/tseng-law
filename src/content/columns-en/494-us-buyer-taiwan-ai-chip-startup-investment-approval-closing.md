A small investment in a private Taiwan AI-chip company can still require a prior foreign-investment application. The Ministry of Economic Affairs’ [official guidance](https://investtaiwan.nat.gov.tw/faqQContent?lang=cht&search=19) says the requirement applies to foreign investment in companies outside Taiwan’s listed, over-the-counter and emerging-stock markets regardless of the amount invested. A US buyer should build that step into the share purchase or subscription timetable.

The buyer’s US incorporation does not finish the analysis. Taiwan has a separate regime for Mainland Area investors, including certain companies incorporated elsewhere. Before agreeing to a firm closing date, identify the actual investing entity, its ownership chain and the rights that determine who controls it.

## A cap table shows ownership, but may omit control

[Article 3 of the Measures Governing Investment Permit to the People of Mainland Area](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawAll.aspx?pcode=Q0040015) covers a third-area company where Mainland Area persons or entities directly or indirectly hold more than 30% of its shares or capital, or have control over it. Those are alternative tests. The threshold is more than 30%, not 30% or more; a holding below it does not eliminate the separate control inquiry.

For a US acquisition vehicle owned through several companies or funds, counsel needs more than the immediate shareholder list. The Ministry provides a separate [interpretation for calculating indirect holdings](https://www.moea.gov.tw/Mns/dir_e/investment/wHandDirApply_File.ashx?file_id=372). The ownership chart should identify the relevant upstream entities and percentages so that calculation can be checked under the applicable rules.

The Ministry’s [control interpretation](https://www.moea.gov.tw/Mns/dir_e/investment/wHandDirApply_File.ashx?file_id=374) includes control through voting agreements, contractual power over financial, operational or personnel policies, and rights concerning a majority of the board or its votes. Supply the shareholder agreements, board appointment provisions and relevant side letters, not just a fully diluted capitalization table. An individual veto or protective right needs analysis in context; its label alone does not establish control.

A third-area company falling within Article 3 does not use the ordinary Foreign Nationals Act for its Taiwan investment. The classification affects the applicable permission regime and permitted investment scope. It should be resolved before the transaction documents assume which approval the buyer can obtain.

## Match the application to the actual transaction

For an ordinary foreign investor, [Articles 4 and 8 of the Act For Investment by Foreign Nationals](https://law.moj.gov.tw/ENG/LawClass/LawAll.aspx?pcode=J0040002) cover shareholdings and require an investment application supported by an investment plan and relevant documents. Buying existing shares and subscribing for newly issued shares should be described accurately in the filing and closing documents. Match the legal names, share classes, quantities and investment amount across the application, transaction agreement and capitalization table. If a financing round or share conversion changes the proposed ownership before closing, have counsel check whether the application or its supporting records need to change.

If an existing foreign investor transfers its approved investment, [Article 10](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0040002&flno=10) requires the transferor and transferee to apply jointly for approval. That can make seller cooperation a closing requirement, not simply an administrative task left to the buyer.

Confirm the receiving authority too. The Ministry’s [agency-routing guidance](https://investtaiwan.nat.gov.tw/faqQContent?lang=cht&search=2) distinguishes applications handled by its Department of Investment Review from matters delegated to science-park or industrial-park authorities. A target’s registration and location can matter. This article concerns shares in a private Taiwan company; an offshore holding-company acquisition, asset purchase or public-company deal needs its own analysis.

## Investment approval does not answer the merger question

Taiwan’s Fair Trade Commission applies a separate merger-control regime. [Fair Trade Act Article 10](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0150002&flno=10) defines a merger more broadly than two companies combining into one. Its categories include holding or acquiring at least one third of another enterprise’s voting shares or capital, acquiring all or a major part of its business or assets, and specified forms of control.

Fitting that definition does not automatically mean a filing is required. [Article 11](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0150002&flno=11) supplies the notification triggers, including market-share and announced sales thresholds; [Article 12](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawAll.aspx?pcode=J0150002) supplies exemptions. The analysis needs the relevant parties, markets and current thresholds. A small target or a minority stake alone does not settle it.

The one-third acquisition test here is different from the more-than-30% ownership test used to classify certain Mainland Area investors. They examine different relationships for different purposes. Put the investment-permission analysis and any merger notification on separate lines of the closing schedule.

The deal team also needs different evidence for each workstream:

| Review | Record needed for the closing decision |
|---|---|
| Investment permission | The applicable authority’s approval, its conditions and confirmation that the approved investor and transaction match the deal being closed |
| Merger control | A documented assessment of whether notification is required; if it is, the relevant FTC communications and confirmation of when the statutory waiting restrictions permit closing |

A filing receipt does not establish investment approval. For a notifiable merger, Article 11 restricts completion during the applicable waiting period, which the FTC can shorten or extend under the statute. The closing plan should identify the lawful basis for proceeding; it should not assume that submitting the forms finishes either process.

## Give regulatory requests a place in the deal documents

An approval condition is more useful when the agreement also allocates the work: who supplies ownership records, who answers authority questions, which changes require the other party’s consent, and what happens if approval includes a condition the parties did not anticipate. The outside date should account for assembling a complete application and responding to requests.

[Article 8](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0040002&flno=8) measures its decision periods from completion of the application procedures. It does not promise that a transaction will close a fixed number of days after signing. If the acquisition vehicle, funding or control rights change during negotiations, update the analysis and supporting documents before relying on the earlier plan.

For an initial [Taiwan investment consultation](https://tseng-law.com/en/contact), describe the proposed transaction, the target’s location and any signing or closing deadline without disclosing confidential terms. Do not submit cap tables, chip designs, passports or banking details through the general inquiry form; confirm document arrangements with the attorney. US regulatory and tax questions require separate advice from appropriately qualified US counsel.
