# Systex tender offer: the payment date still matters after conditions are met

61.02% is the stake Taiwan Mobile says it will hold in Systex after completing its tender-offer transaction. [CNA reported on November 6](https://www.cna.com.tw/news/afe/202611060190.aspx) that the offer through its wholly owned subsidiary had met all conditions. Payment combines cash and Taiwan Mobile shares. The reported holding is a post-transaction figure, not confirmation that each tendering investor has received the consideration.

An overseas holder selling through a Taiwan tender offer follows a procedure outside ordinary exchange trading, as defined in [Article 2 of the Regulations Governing Public Tender Offers for Securities of Public Companies](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0400063&flno=2). The prospectus and the offeror's announcements therefore matter alongside the investor's instructions to a broker or custodian.

## An announcement can restrict withdrawal

Under [Article 19](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0400063&flno=19), the conditions are met when the minimum purchase quantity is reached before expiry and any required regulatory approvals or effective filings are in place. The offeror must report and announce that event within two days. If the offer expires without those conditions being met, deposited securities must be returned on the next business day.

Once the offeror makes that announcement, tenderers generally cannot withdraw. Exceptions include a competing tender offer under Article 7(2), an announced extension of the offer period, or another legal ground for withdrawal. A withdrawal application must be written. Waiting for payment is not, by itself, an unrestricted opportunity to change one's mind.

For an investor communicating through a custodian abroad, the useful chronology records the tender instruction, the statutory announcement and any asserted exception. A news headline can alert the holder to the transaction's progress. It does not replace the announcement that Article 19 uses to restrict withdrawal.

## The terms continue to govern payment

[Article 7-1](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0400063&flno=7-1) requires uniform acquisition conditions. An offeror cannot lower the price, reduce the proposed purchase quantity or shorten the offer period. Side agreements giving a particular shareholder special rights after tendering are also prohibited where they produce unequal substantive acquisition conditions.

Payment terms have their own protection. The offeror cannot change the prospectus's payment time, method or place, except for a natural disaster or emergency event whose occurrence and end are determined and announced by the relevant authority. A buyer's revised commercial preference is not that exception.

If the conditions are met but payment misses the prospectus deadline, [Article 19](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0400063&flno=19) lets the tenderer terminate without first demanding payment. The appointed institution must return the deposited securities on the next business day. If the prospectus provides for securities to be returned before the payment date, that agreed return time applies.

## Shares tendered and shares purchased

Oversubscription introduces a further distinction. [Article 23](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0400063&flno=23) requires proportionate purchases from all tenderers, down to whole shares, if the quantity tendered exceeds the planned purchase quantity. Any remaining allocation follows random order. Deposited shares not purchased are returned.

That rule does not establish that this Systex offer was oversubscribed. It explains why a tender instruction alone cannot prove the final quantity sold in every Taiwan offer. For the reported cash-and-share transaction, an investor's settlement record can show the shares accepted and each form of consideration received. Any unresolved discrepancy can then be tied to the actual prospectus payment date and allocation, rather than the headline ownership percentage.
