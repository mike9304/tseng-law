# Writer evidence — unordered-cash-on-delivery-parcel-taiwan

Checked: 2026-10-03, Asia/Seoul. Locale: zh-hant, Taiwan. Status: writer self-check complete; independent legal/source and voice review required. Publication and image inspection are not completed by this writer.

## Scope and audience

- Reader: a Taiwan household receiving an apparently unordered cash-on-delivery parcel, particularly where a relative already paid it.
- The article separates four facts that change the response: whether anyone ordered, whether money was paid, the actual carrier, and whether collected funds have been remitted. It does not treat every unfamiliar parcel as fraud or equate receiving a parcel with a conclusive purchase contract.
- Independent Taiwan treatment is necessary because Consumer Protection Act article 20 governs unsolicited goods, while Chunghwa Post supplies particular operational refund conditions. A Japanese, Korean or English consumer process cannot simply be translated into those Taiwan rules.
- The opening is an ordinary possible household scenario, not a reported incident or invented legal consultation. No actual person or sender is accused.
