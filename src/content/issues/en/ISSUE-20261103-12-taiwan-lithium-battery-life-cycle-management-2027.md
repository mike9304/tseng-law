# Taiwan's 2027 lithium battery plan and rules already in force

More than 300 fires related to lithium batteries occurred in Taiwan over three years, Environment Minister Peng Chi-ming told reporters on November 2. [CNA's report, republished by UDN](https://udn.com/news/story/7266/9852539), presented that figure as the minister's account of lithium-battery-related fires generally and did not limit the count to phones, power banks or electric vehicles or provide a device-by-device breakdown.

Peng said the Ministry of Environment aims to issue guidance and begin life-cycle management in the first quarter of 2027. The work would span producer responsibility, online sales, consumer use, sanitation workers' safety and final treatment. The legal starting point is different. The Waste Disposal Act already imposes duties.

## Producer duties already cover all secondary lithium batteries

[Article 15](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=O0050001&flno=15) allows the central competent authority to designate regulated recyclable waste and the enterprises responsible for its recycling, clearance and treatment. [Article 16](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=O0050001&flno=16) requires responsible enterprises to register and pay recycling clearance and treatment fees according to the prescribed volume and rate.

A [ministry announcement from November 12, 2024](https://enews.moenv.gov.tw/Page/3B3C62C78849F32F/7897af6c-fbeb-4240-a3f1-0c4507a0b0cf) expanded the designated dry-battery category to secondary lithium batteries with individual cells weighing at least one kilogram. The change took effect on July 1, 2025, and because lighter cells were already covered, the current designation covers all secondary lithium batteries for producer-responsibility purposes, whether supplied individually or assembled.

Manufacturers and importers must complete registration and declarations and pay the relevant fees under that existing scheme. These duties already exist. They are not a 2027 registration requirement imposed on every battery user.

For a company reviewing its position, useful records include its manufacturing or importing role, the battery's form, registration and declaration history, and fee-payment documents. The promised guidance will need a separate check when its final text appears.

## The 2027 plan does not announce a consumer penalty

The reported life-cycle proposal reaches beyond the current producer-fee system. It includes possible controls for online sales channels and guidance for consumers, as well as measures intended to protect collection workers and improve downstream handling. An expert meeting on sanitation-worker protection has already taken place, according to the minister.

A plan is not a penalty provision. The November 2 report did not publish final guidance, a new statutory duty for consumers or a schedule of administrative fines. Nor did it say that the existing producer obligations would be replaced.

When the ministry issues the guidance, its wording and legal status will matter. Safety advice, an administrative procedure and a newly enforceable duty require different readings. The November 2 announcement supplied a target date and policy scope, not the final text.
