# Can I return an online order in Taiwan if I’ve opened the box?

Opening a delivery box to check what’s inside usually leaves your right to return the goods intact. If you ordered from a business in Taiwan without being able to inspect the item first, you generally have seven days after receiving it to cancel without giving a reason or paying a fee. A seller relying on a listed product exception must have told you about it when you ordered. Livestream and social media orders can qualify as [distance sales](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0170001&flno=2) under [Taiwan’s Consumer Protection Act (消費者保護法)](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0170001&flno=19).

The seven days usually start [the day after delivery](https://dlacp.gov.taipei/News_Content.aspx?n=9572812F3B49CFD7&sms=5452BB868182EC0A&s=9DFA6D62FDD2DFFA). Hand the goods to a carrier or send the seller written notice of cancellation within the seven days and the law treats the contract as cancelled. The parcel can arrive later. You can also cancel in writing before delivery under [Article 18 of the Act’s enforcement rules (消費者保護法施行細則)](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0170002&flno=18).

## Which seal did you break?

For a cosmetic purchase, distinguish the shipping carton from the seal on the product. The Executive Yuan’s Department of Consumer Protection (行政院消費者保護處) advises interpreting the personal hygiene exception narrowly. If all you opened was the outer carton and the contents can still be used as before, the seller cannot refuse the return on hygiene grounds alone. The exception requires an unsealed personal hygiene item. Whether a particular cosmetic qualifies is ultimately for a court to decide. [Department’s guidance on hygiene seals](https://www.ey.gov.tw/Page/8F51FEE53AB45EA9/0f556196-c5e1-4113-895e-c5c1ae9c2c84).

You can open ordinary packaging to check the model, colour or contents. You keep the right to cancel even if necessary inspection damages or alters the goods, or if they are lost, damaged or altered through no fault of yours. A blanket ban on returns after opening cannot override the Act. Penghu District Court (澎湖地方法院) held such a term invalid for an underwater scooter and spare battery because no listed exception applied (111年度馬小更一字第1號, May 31, 2022). [Enforcement Rules, Article 17](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0170002&flno=17) and [district court decision summaries, item 26](https://cpc.ey.gov.tw/Page/C0D78C3C8137E3E2).

Using an item beyond what inspection requires can affect how much you recover. If use reduces its value, the seller may seek compensation under Article 259, item 6 of Taiwan’s Civil Code (民法). Taipei City’s Department of Legal Affairs (臺北市政府法務局) advises sellers to consider how the buyer used the item and whether it can be restored. The city advises against a flat restocking fee and says the charge should be no more than the item’s actual loss in value. If the seller charges a fee, ask how it was calculated. [Taipei City guidance on restocking fees](https://dlacp.gov.taipei/News_Content.aspx?n=9572812F3B49CFD7&sms=5452BB868182EC0A&s=9DFA6D62FDD2DFFA).

Under [Article 19-2 of the Consumer Protection Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0170001&flno=19-2), a term governing what each side must return after cancellation is void if it leaves you worse off than you would be under [Article 259 of the Civil Code](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=259).

## The seven exceptions and the notice that goes with them

Even for a product on this list, you can claim the seven-day right if the seller gave you no advance notice of the exclusion. Taiwan’s rules on exceptions for distance sales (通訊交易解除權合理例外情事適用準則) require both the listed condition and notice. For online orders, the seller must provide the required information electronically in a form you can read in full and save. The Act also covers unsolicited door-to-door sales, but these exceptions apply only to distance sales. [Act, Article 18](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0170001&flno=18), [exceptions rules, Article 2](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0170012&flno=2) and [official explanatory notes](https://cpc.ey.gov.tw/File/A5B8EF9EE1A960F8?A=C).

| What you bought | When it fits the exception |
|---|---|
| Perishable, short-dated or nearly expired goods | Examples include freshly prepared meals that spoil easily and milk with a shelf life under seven days, or slightly longer if it is about to expire when you cancel. Other foods may still qualify for return. |
| A custom order | The seller made it to your requirements, such as clothing sewn to your measurements. Choosing an existing size or colour does not make an order custom. |
| Newspapers, periodicals or magazines | The exception covers current and past issues alike if the seller told you when you ordered that the seven-day right would not apply. |
| Audio or video recordings, or computer software, on physical media | You opened the sealed product. |
| Digital content supplied without a physical medium, or an online service completed as soon as it is provided | For either, you agreed in advance and the supplier began providing it. An e-book is one example of digital content. |
| An opened personal hygiene item | The product was sealed for hygiene and opening it raises a hygiene concern for the next user. Examples include underwear and razors. |
| International air passenger transport | Having goods shipped by air does not qualify. See Kaohsiung District Court (高雄地方法院), 110年度小上字第44號, April 27, 2022 ([item 25](https://cpc.ey.gov.tw/Page/C0D78C3C8137E3E2)). |

If you book a room directly with a hotel, the government’s published cancellation rules for individual guests apply. The published standard terms for online retail (零售業等網路交易定型化契約應記載及不得記載事項) expressly preserve the statutory right and forbid terms that waive or restrict it. [Exceptions rules, Article 3](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0170012&flno=3) and [online retail terms](https://www.ey.gov.tw/File/2D44FB65E5FF31C9?A=C).

## Giving notice and getting your refund

What if the seller never told you how or when to cancel? If that information was missing when you received the goods, the seven days start the day after the seller supplies it. The right expires once four months have passed from the original start of the seven-day period. For the deadline in [Article 19](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0170001&flno=19), keep a record of what you saw and when you acted.

- Save the order page as it appeared when you bought the item, including the selected size or model, any exception notice and the shipping terms.
- If cancelling in writing, identify the order and say clearly that you are cancelling. Keep the sent email or the shop’s submitted return form with its date and time.
- Keep the delivery record and, if you send the goods back yourself, the carrier’s receipt. The date you handed over the parcel establishes when you acted.

Return shipping is the seller’s expense when you exercise this right. For the original delivery, the retailer must state how shipping is calculated and who pays. If the terms are silent, the retailer bears that cost. [Executive Yuan Consumer Protection Committee (行政院消費者保護會) guidance on shipping](https://cpc.ey.gov.tw/Page/BC16ACF0BBB9CCC2/d3b2324c-f465-45ec-a52e-1daa3b050c41).

After you cancel in writing, the seller must collect the goods from the original delivery address or another agreed location within 15 days, counting from the day after it receives your notice. That collection rule applies unless you and the seller individually agree otherwise. For goods, the refund clock starts the day after the seller collects them or receives your return. It then has 15 days to repay you. For a service, [Article 19-2](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0170001&flno=19-2) starts the refund period the day after the seller receives the cancellation notice.

## Private sellers, overseas shops and faulty goods

A social media seller may trade as a business even under a personal name. Reviews and completed sales can show whether someone sells as a business. The seller need not aim to make a profit. Someone occasionally selling their own used belongings may fall outside the Act. [Taipei City’s guidance on private sellers](https://dlacp.gov.taipei/News_Content.aspx?n=9572812F3B49CFD7&sms=5452BB868182EC0A&s=E0342A1073466A06) and [Enforcement Rules, Article 2](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0170002&flno=2).

With an overseas seller, check which law governs the contract. Under Taiwan’s law governing civil matters involving a foreign element (涉外民事法律適用法), a valid express choice by the parties governs the contract’s formation and effect. Otherwise, the law most closely connected to it applies. The Consumer Protection Committee suggests contacting the seller directly. If you paid by credit card, you can ask the issuer whether its dispute process covers the purchase. [Choice of law, Article 20](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000007&flno=20) and [cross-border dispute guidance](https://cpc.ey.gov.tw/Page/20290F5D17E9002C).

Faulty goods raise a separate claim, even in a private sale or where a distance-sale exception applies. Inspect the goods promptly on receipt. Tell the seller immediately about any defect you discover, including one that only becomes apparent later. If the seller is liable for a defect that existed when the risk of loss passed to you, you may cancel or seek a price reduction. Where cancellation would be manifestly unfair, only a reduction is available. These rights expire six months after notice or five years after delivery, whichever comes first. Only the six-month limit falls away if the seller deliberately kept the defect from you. [Civil Code, Articles 354](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=354), [356](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=356), [359](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=359) and [365](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=365).

## If a Taiwan shop refuses the return

You can complain to the business, a consumer protection group or a local government consumer service centre (消費者服務中心). The business must handle the complaint properly within 15 days of receiving it. If the complaint remains unresolved, you can contact a local consumer protection officer (消費者保護官) or apply in writing to the local consumer dispute mediation committee (消費爭議調解委員會). A second complaint is optional. You can also bring a consumer lawsuit directly. [Act, Article 43](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0170001&flno=43) and [Article 44](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0170001&flno=44), [consumer dispute mediation rules (消費爭議調解辦法), Article 5](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0170008&flno=5) and [complaint and mediation channels](https://cpc.ey.gov.tw/Page/71E988F034956960).

The mediation session must be scheduled for a date within 30 days of acceptance, with an extension of up to ten days possible at either party’s request. The only charges are actual inspection or expert appraisal costs. A court-approved settlement has the same effect as a final civil judgment. [Mediation rules on scheduling](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0170008&flno=7), [costs](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0170008&flno=24) and [court approval](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0170008&flno=30).
