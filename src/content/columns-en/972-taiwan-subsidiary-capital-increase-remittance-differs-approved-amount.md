# When the Wire for a Taiwan Capital Increase Doesn't Match the Approval: Verification, CPA Audit and the 15-Day Filing

Years after a capital increase, the U.S. parent may want its money back out of Taiwan. On an approved share transfer, withdrawal or capital reduction, it can apply to convert its verified investment amount in full, in one go, and capital gains from the investment get the same treatment ([Act for Investment by Foreign Nationals, Article 12](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0040002&flno=12), paragraph 2). The verified amount is the figure the Ministry of Economic Affairs fixed after the funds landed.

That verified number is built from what arrived. The approval is stated in New Taiwan dollars. The parent wires dollars.

## Whose name is on the wire

A group that funds subsidiaries through a finance affiliate can trip at the first step. The Q&A published by the former Investment Commission of the Ministry of Economic Affairs, now the Department of Investment Review, says the remittance documents filed for verification must be in the investor's name, and that the investor may not have anyone other than a co-investor remit on its behalf ([archived Q&A](https://web.archive.org/web/20231211060158/https://www.moeaic.gov.tw/businessPub.view?lang=ch&op_id_one=1)). Where several investors share one approval and one of them wires for the group, the file needs a breakdown of amounts and a remittance-on-behalf declaration ([verification regulations, Article 4](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0040014&flno=4), paragraph 3).

## How dollars become a verified NT dollar figure

Once the contribution is made, the investor applies for verification of the investment amount (Act, [Article 9](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0040002&flno=9), paragraph 3). The arithmetic sits in [Article 3](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0040014&flno=3) of the Regulations for Verification of Investment by Overseas Compatriots and Foreign Nationals.

Two rules matter for a cash wire. Foreign currency sold for NT dollars counts at the net amount after handling fees and other related costs. Currency kept as such is converted at the receiving bank's buying rate when it issues the transaction voucher. The approved amount does not enter the formula. Neither does any intermediary-bank fee taken before the money reached Taiwan; that money never arrived.

For the treasury file, the application attaches the inward remittance advice, the bank's exchange memo or other transaction voucher, and the subsidiary's bank statement or passbook copy (Article 4). Those are the papers that later support the verified figure.

## What the subsidiary resolves

On the Taiwan side, the number that matters is set by resolution: new shares and their issue price, or for a limited company the amount of the increase. Who adopts it? That depends on the company's form and on who owns it.

A company limited by shares (股份有限公司) wholly owned by one corporate shareholder, such as the U.S. parent, has its board exercise the powers of the shareholders' meeting ([Company Act, Article 128-1](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0080001&flno=128-1), paragraph 1). Its articles may even dispense with a board and provide for one or two directors; a sole director then exercises the board's powers (paragraph 2).

Issuing new shares takes a board resolution with at least two-thirds of the directors present and a majority of those present in favour ([Article 266](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0080001&flno=266), paragraph 2). Going past the total shares authorized in the articles means amending them first. In a company that has not publicly issued shares, the amendment needs holders of at least two-thirds of the issued shares present and a majority of the votes present, or any higher threshold the articles set ([Article 277](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0080001&flno=277), paragraphs 2 and 4).

Employees normally get first call on 10% to 15% of new shares under [Article 267](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0080001&flno=267)(1), unless the central authority for the business approves otherwise. A foreign investor holding 45% or more of the subsidiary's capital may disapply that reservation when it increases capital in cash ([Act, Article 15](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0040002&flno=15), paragraph 2).

For a limited company (有限公司), the consent of a majority of the shareholders' voting rights is enough, and a consenting shareholder need not contribute in proportion to its existing stake ([Article 106](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0080001&flno=106), paragraph 1). Shareholders pay the capital in full, with no instalments ([Article 100](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0080001&flno=100)).

## Certification, verification and the 15 days

Dates drive this stage. A CPA cannot certify the new capital before the banks close on the record date of the increase ([Regulations Governing Independent Certified Public Accountant Auditing the Registered Capital Amount of Companies, Article 9](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0080021&flno=9), paragraph 5). Certification is required before the change of registered capital is filed ([Company Act, Article 7](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0080001&flno=7), paragraph 2), and the company supports it with a statement of changes in capital and a schedule of each shareholder's cash payments, backed by deposit records ([Article 2](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0080021&flno=2); [Article 3](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0080021&flno=3), subparagraph 1).

The CPA's report then restates the resolution in figures: issue price, number of shares issued, capital amount. Shares issued above or below par also call for the price per share and the accounting treatment ([Article 7](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0080021&flno=7), paragraph 1). With a premium, the registered capital and the verified investment are different numbers by design.

Verification is the other gate. Under the same Q&A, a subscription for cash capital-increase shares may be registered only after the whole investment amount has been verified. Taipei City's Department of Commerce lists the Department of Investment Review's approval letter and capital verification letter among a foreign-invested company's filing documents in its [guidance for a limited company's capital increase](https://tcooc-co.gov.taipei/cp.aspx?n=F405CD7A4FA69267). No provision says whether verification or certification comes first, and when verifying, the authority may ask for a copy of the CPA's report if it considers this necessary ([verification regulations, Article 13](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0040014&flno=13)).

Then the filing itself. It is due within 15 days after the change, unless the competent authority for the business has approved registration on a specific date ([Regulations Governing Company Registration, Article 4](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0080020&flno=4), paragraph 1). A late filing exposes the company's representative to a fine of NT$10,000 to NT$50,000 ([Company Act, Article 387](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0080001&flno=387), paragraph 4). If the responsible person representing the company does not make the required filing, the authority orders that person to correct the omission within a set period and imposes a fine of NT$10,000 to NT$50,000. If the omission remains uncorrected, it keeps setting correction deadlines and imposes fines of NT$20,000 to NT$100,000 on each occasion until the filing is made (paragraph 5).

## When the wire falls short

Article 9(1) of the Act requires the approved contribution to arrive in full within the period fixed in the approval, with a report to the authority for review. Whatever has not been contributed by the end of that period is revoked when it ends (paragraph 2). With good cause, the investor may apply for an extension before the period expires, and the period is extended only if the authority approves.

Timing decides what the parent's treasury can still do. Before the period ends, another wire keeps the approved amount whole, and the tranches can be verified one by one, each within two months of its contribution, or together within two months after the last ([verification regulations, Article 2](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0040014&flno=2)). Once the period has ended without an approved extension, the missing part of the approval has been revoked, and the subsidiary resolves again so that the number of new shares, or a limited company's increase, matches what was actually paid.

## When the wire goes over

Padding a wire to absorb exchange-rate swings has a cost of its own. The last sentence of [Article 8](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0040002&flno=8), paragraph 1, of the Act requires an application for approval when the investment plan changes, so counting the surplus as share money would raise that question. It would also leave the money out of line with the issue price and number of shares the subsidiary resolved on. What should happen to the surplus itself is a question neither the Act nor the verification regulations answers, which makes the margin for exchange-rate movement something to settle with the Department of Investment Review before the money is sent.

## A filing cannot get ahead of the money

Registering the approved figure and topping up afterwards would describe unpaid money as paid. Where shareholders have not actually paid share money but the filing documents present it as received in full, each responsible person of the company faces up to five years' imprisonment, detention, or a fine of NT$500,000 to NT$2.5 million, or both ([Company Act, Article 9](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0080001&flno=9), paragraph 1).

Article 9(1) treats returning share money to the shareholder after registration, or letting it take the money back, the same way.
