# Taiwan Company Formation: Capital Remittance, Banking, and Foreign Hiring

![Featured image](../images/005-taiwan-company-establishment-advanced-2/featured-01.jpg)

In [Taiwan Company Formation — Basics](/en/columns/taiwan-company-establishment-basics) and [Taiwan Company Formation — Advanced Part 1](/en/columns/taiwan-company-establishment-advanced-1), we explained the basic flow of entity types, the foreign investment application, company registration, the business address, and account opening. This article organizes five topics that come up frequently in practice after those steps: remitting capital from Korea, paying in funds already held in Taiwan, converting the company preparatory account into a formal account, online and mobile banking, and work permits for foreign nationals.

These are areas where Korea's foreign exchange procedures, Taiwan's foreign investment procedures, banks' identity verification and transaction review, and the Ministry of Labor's work-permit review overlap. Even for the same company formation, the required steps change with the investor's place of residence, the source of funds, the approved contribution method, the chosen bank, the representative's residency status, and the positions to be filled. Wei Tseng (曾雋崴), Taiwan Attorney, explains in Q&A format the checkpoints that keep these systems from being confused with one another.

![](../images/005-taiwan-company-establishment-advanced-2/img-01.jpg)

## 1. What should I check before remitting capital from Korea to a Taiwan company's preparatory account?

An investment by a Korean resident in a Taiwan company may require an overseas direct investment report or similar procedure under Korea's foreign exchange system. Before remitting the capital, confirm with your bank the reporting destination, the designated foreign exchange bank, the timing of the report, the remitter's name, the required documents, and the remittance method. It is not possible to state categorically, for all banks, whether an in-person visit, a proxy application, or an online procedure is available.

Overseas direct investment is a capital transaction involving a movement of funds. Under Korea's foreign exchange system, capital transactions are subject to procedures set out by transaction type, by payment and receipt, and by method of payment, and as a rule the applicable report, confirmation, or other procedure must be completed before the remittance. Who accepts the report and which forms and supporting documents are used, however, can vary with the form of the investment, the ownership ratio, the investor's attributes, the remittance method, and the rules currently in force.

Overseas direct investment transactions are conducted through a designated foreign exchange bank. Much of the authority under Korea's foreign exchange system is delegated to foreign exchange banks, but the body that accepts a report and the documents required differ with the substance of the transaction and Korea's current regulations. It is therefore important not simply to send an international wire from an account you use every day, but to confirm which bank to designate, which branch or counter handles the transaction, and what must be submitted before the remittance.

The way visits are handled is not uniform either. Depending on the bank and the type of procedure, you may be asked to visit in person for identity verification, while in other cases an application by a representative holding a power of attorney may be accepted, or documents may be submitted in advance and part of the procedure completed online. Confirm with both your bank and the person handling the procedure on the Taiwan side so that the remitter's name, the name of the receiving account in Taiwan, the statements in the investment approval documents, and the purpose of the remittance all match.

Do not assume that a report will automatically be accepted at the moment the capital is remitted. Document review, correction, or confirmation by the designated bank may be required first, so confirm the order of reporting and remittance in advance, and coordinate the schedule with the Taiwan-side investment approval and payment deadlines. If a required report is not filed, corrective, administrative, or other measures may apply depending on the circumstances and the applicable law. Rather than predicting a specific consequence in the abstract, confirm the current rules and your particular transaction before remitting.

## 2. Can I pay New Taiwan dollars I hold in Taiwan into the company's preparatory account?

You need to confirm the approved investment details, how the funds were acquired, the remittance or payment method, and the requirements of your bank. When using funds held in New Taiwan dollars, you may be asked for materials showing lawful acquisition and the flow of funds, but the required documents differ by source of funds, such as salary, dividends, or business income, and by the individual case. Source-of-funds documentation may still be required for a remittance from abroad.

Whether New Taiwan dollars you hold in Taiwan can be deposited into the preparatory account cannot be judged by whether a transfer between accounts is technically possible. The payment must be consistent with the approved investment amount, the contribution method, the investor and remitter, and the current investment procedure guidance. If the approval assumes a remittance of foreign currency from abroad, or if the investor and the actual remitter differ, prior confirmation or an additional procedure may be necessary.

What banks and the competent authorities examine is the whole chain: where the funds originated, through which accounts they passed, and as whose contribution they were paid into the company. For salary, dividends, and business income, the materials showing how the funds were acquired and their lawfulness can differ. Pay statements, withholding documents, dividend resolutions and payment records, contracts, invoices, tax records, or account statements are all possibilities, but no single document can be treated as mandatory for every case.

Likewise, source-of-funds documentation may still be required for a remittance from abroad. A bank's customer due diligence, anti-money-laundering controls, verification of the transaction's purpose, and the investment review each rest on separate legal bases, so the mere fact that the funds come from a Korean bank does not eliminate the need for proof. Keep the documents the bank requests, the investment approval documents, and the actual records of the fund movements so that they correspond to one another.

Under Taiwan's foreign investment procedure, after the contribution is made in the approved manner, the investor is expected to report receipt of the invested funds and then proceed to verification of the total invested amount. If the bank's deposit and withdrawal records, the remittance messages, the currency-exchange records, the account names, and the evidence for the source of funds contradict one another, supplementary explanation or additional documents may be required. It is important to design the flow of funds so that this reporting and verification procedure can ultimately be completed.

## 3. When can the company preparatory account be converted into a formal company account?

After company registration, you submit the registration documents, proof of representative authority and identity, and any other documents the bank requires, and the conversion is carried out after the bank's review. The timing of the conversion, whether the company's responsible person (representative) must visit in person, any additional documents, and when the funds become available differ by bank and by case, so confirm with your bank before forming the company.

Completion of company registration is the prerequisite for presenting the registered company's documents to the bank. However, the preparatory account does not automatically become a formal account the moment registration is completed. You apply for the conversion by assembling the documents the bank designates, such as the company registration record, the articles of incorporation, materials confirming representative authority, identity documents for the company's responsible person (representative) and beneficial owners, the company seal, and tax or business-related materials.

The required documents and procedure differ by bank, branch, company form, the representative's residency status, and the transactions planned. Whether the company's responsible person (representative) must visit in person, whether submission by an agent is accepted, and whether an additional interview or an explanation of the transaction's purpose is required must all be confirmed with the chosen bank. Publicly available general account-opening materials are useful preparation references, but they do not by themselves guarantee that an individual conversion will be completed.

The account name, representative authority, user permissions, any additional review, and when use of the funds begins are also confirmed case by case. Acceptance of the conversion to a formal account, the bank's internal review, registration of seals and signatures, configuration of transfer authority, and the point at which funds can be withdrawn do not necessarily fall on the same day. If you plan to pay rent, salaries, suppliers, or taxes soon after formation, allow sufficient funds and lead time.

## 4. Can I use online banking right after converting to a formal account?

Online banking, mobile banking, transfer limits, and authentication methods differ according to each bank's products, review, and settings. Neither a Taiwan mobile phone number nor an account usage history is a universal requirement; confirm with your chosen bank when to apply, what devices are needed, how the representative's identity is verified, how authority is configured, and the date on which use begins.

Converting to a formal company account and starting to use online and mobile banking are not necessarily the same procedure. Some banks let you apply at the same time as the account conversion, while others require a separate application form, consent to the terms of service, device registration, receipt of an authentication device, or activation on the bank's side. Distinguish and confirm separately the day the application is accepted and the day you can actually log in and make transfers.

The items to confirm include the channels available, authentication devices and phone numbers, transfer limits, the roles and authority of corporate users, the approval method, and the conditions for use from overseas. For a corporation, the setup may separate the person who enters transactions from the person who approves them, require multi-stage approval, set limits by transaction type, or require registration of the devices used. If people other than the responsible person, such as accounting staff or an outside accountant, will use the service, confirm in advance what authority can be granted to each user.

Whether a Taiwan mobile phone number, a particular authentication device, or a certain history of account use is required cannot be described as a condition common to all banks. The identity-verification method, devices, phone number, and start date required differ by bank and by product. If you plan domestic or international transfers soon after formation, ask about the conversion to a formal account and the application procedure for electronic transaction services together at the stage when you choose the bank for the company preparatory account.

## 5. What work-permit requirements apply when a Taiwan company hires a Korean national?

For a foreign national, including a Korean, to work in Taiwan, the planned duties must satisfy the category and requirements of the corresponding work permit. Even when applying under the managerial category known in Taiwan's system as a manager of a general overseas Chinese or foreign invested business, hiring is not unrestricted merely because the person is the employer's first foreign employee. Confirm the covered positions and ownership relationship, the foreign national's own qualifications, the employer's capital and revenue requirements, and the application documents, and obtain the permit before work begins.

Foreign nationality by itself does not create an unrestricted hiring slot. First confirm which work-permit category the actual job falls under: the managerial category known in Taiwan's system as a manager of a general overseas Chinese or foreign invested business, specialized or technical work, contract-performance work, or another category. Rather than simply changing a title to a managerial one and applying, the substance of the duties, the organizational authority, the ownership relationship, and the employment contract must match the category applied for. A foreign national who requires a permit must obtain it before starting work.

Those who may fall within this managerial category include the business manager of a company or branch who corresponds to a manager (經理人) under Taiwan law. Specifically, this covers the business manager of a company in which overseas Chinese or foreign investors collectively hold more than one third of the total shares or total capital, the business manager of a foreign company's branch, and the representative of a representative office established with the permission of the competent authority. In addition, there are other categories recognized by the competent authority, such as certain managerial positions in new businesses with innovative capacity. Not every foreign employee is therefore eligible to be sponsored under this category.

Under this managerial category, what is relaxed for the first foreign national the employer hires is only the education, work-experience, and average-salary standards that apply to specialized or technical work. The position and ownership relationship, the employer's qualifications, the application, and the work permit are still required; this is not a system under which a company may hire freely merely because the person is its first hire. When hiring a second or subsequent foreign national under this category, the individual's education, work experience, and average salary are governed by the education, experience, and average-salary standards for specialized or technical work.

The employer must also satisfy one of the current qualification standards. For a company established for less than one year, the standard is any one of the following:

- Paid-in capital or working capital in Taiwan of at least TWD 500,000
- Revenue of at least TWD 3,000,000
- Import/export performance of at least USD 500,000
- Agency commissions of at least USD 200,000

For a company established for at least one year, the employer must satisfy at least one of the following for either the most recent year or the average of the preceding three years: revenue in Taiwan of at least TWD 3,000,000, import/export performance of at least USD 500,000, or agency commissions of at least USD 200,000. Confirm the point at which the company's age is measured, the period over which performance must be proven, and the documents to be submitted in accordance with the guidance in effect at the time of application.

Representative offices and specially recognized cases are subject to separate standards. Representative offices have rules concerning their date of establishment and track record of activities, and there is also a system under which the competent authority individually recognizes substantial contributions to the domestic economy or special circumstances. Do not confuse these with the capital and revenue standards for ordinary companies.

For foreign nationals handling sales, engineering, design, interpretation, on-site work, or other duties, use the work-permit category that corresponds to the actual position. The standards of this managerial category are not a blanket exception that applies to foreign employment generally. Before applying, it is important to check the duties, the foreign national's own qualifications, the employer's qualifications, the salary, the contract period, and the required documents against each category.

For capital remittance and account procedures, consolidating the Korea-side report, the Taiwan-side investment approval, reporting, and verification, and the bank's confirmations into a single timeline makes it easier to spot mismatches in names or timing. When hiring a foreign national, check separately that company registration and investment approval are complete and that the work-permit requirements are satisfied.

This article provides educational information about these general procedures; it is not legal, tax, accounting, foreign exchange, or banking-practice advice for any individual matter. Laws, administrative guidance, and bank practices may change. Before any actual transaction or application, confirm the latest official information, your bank's practices, and appropriate professional advice.
