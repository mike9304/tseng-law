# Crashed a Rented Scooter or Car in Taiwan? Repair Bills, Loss-of-Use Caps and Township Mediation

On day three of a week on Taiwan's east coast, your rented scooter slides on gravel outside Hualien, clips a parked car's mirror and goes down. You are bruised, the scooter's side panel is cracked, and the car's owner is walking over (an invented example). Three bills may now be in play: yours, the rental shop's and the car owner's. Each follows different rules.

## Two calls first

The Ministry of Transportation's mandatory terms for scooter rental contracts took effect on January 1, 2022. After a collision or damage, the renter must report to the police straight away, unless there is good reason not to, and notify the rental company. Repairs go to a shop both sides agree on, or to the manufacturer's shop if they cannot agree. The equivalent terms for car rentals require the same report and notice.

## When the rental vehicle is damaged

A tenant must look after a leased item with the care of a good administrator and is liable for damage caused by failing to do so ([Civil Code Article 432](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=432)). The rental terms turn that duty into numbers.

For scooters, necessary and reasonable towing and repair costs caused by the renter are shared according to the renter's degree of fault. If the repair finishes within the rental period, the renter pays the rent for that time. If it runs past the return date, the shop can charge no more than 50% of the corresponding rent for the first seven days and 40% for days eight to fifteen. Fifteen days is the limit, except for a large heavy motorcycle waiting more than fifteen days for parts: 25%, for up to thirty days.

Car rentals work slightly differently. Rent during a repair is capped at 70% for up to ten days, 60% for days eleven to fifteen and 50% from day sixteen, with twenty days as the maximum. Any agreed depreciation charge may not exceed 20% of the repair bill. A vehicle that cannot be repaired is paid for at its market value in both cases.

## The other side's injuries and car

A driver must compensate damage caused to others while the vehicle is in use, unless the driver shows due care was taken to prevent it ([Civil Code Article 191-2](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=191-2)). If the other person is hurt or killed, compulsory automobile liability insurance pays regardless of fault ([Article 7](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0390060&flno=7) of that Act). Its benefits, though, are limited to medical costs, disability and death (Article 27). The parked car's mirror is not on that list.

## What to read in the contract before you ride

Start with the insurance box. The scooter terms require a checklist that adds, beyond compulsory cover, third-party liability, vehicle damage, theft, driver injury and passenger liability insurance. Which boxes are ticked decides much of what you pay later.

Then the pickup record. The rental form must note that the vehicle's condition can be recorded with photos where needed, and shared-scooter operators must tell renters they may take their own. If, within the time stated in the contract (at least five minutes), you find the scooter does not match the agreed condition and return it to the same spot, no charge may be made. Traffic fines during the rental are yours. A shop may not include a clause letting it change the contract on its own or excluding the small-claims court's jurisdiction.

## Township mediation

For settling with the other party, each township, town and city office runs a mediation committee, and districts of municipalities and cities apply the same Act. It handles civil disputes and criminal cases that need a complaint to proceed, and charges no fee apart from actual inspection costs ([Township and County-Administered City Mediation Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawAll.aspx?pcode=I0020003), Articles 1, 23 and 35). An oral application is enough (Article 10). The office sends the agreed record to the court within ten days (Article 26). Once the court approves it, the same matter cannot be sued on or complained about again, and a civil mediation has the effect of a final judgment (Article 27). Failing to appear at the session without good reason means the mediation is treated as failed (Article 20). Grounds for invalidity or rescission can still be raised by suit within 30 days of service (Article 29).

Attendance is the catch. That rule matters most to a visitor with a flight booked. A session set after your departure date that you cannot attend can turn a settlement you could have closed in one afternoon into a lawsuit.
