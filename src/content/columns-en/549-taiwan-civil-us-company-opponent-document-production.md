# A US Company's Request for an Opponent's Documents in Taiwan Civil Litigation

Five items belong in a motion asking a Taiwan court to order an opponent to produce a document. [Article 342 of the Code of Civil Procedure](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=342) requires the document, the fact it should prove, its contents, the reason it is held by the opponent, and the basis for a duty to produce it. Each item answers a different question.

For a US company pursuing a claim against a Taiwan counterparty, the missing record may be central to proving performance or a payment calculation. Start with that disputed fact. A useful preparation note connects it to an identifiable record and explains why the counterparty should have that record. This article concerns documents held by the opposing party in ordinary Taiwan civil proceedings.

## The document and the fact to be proved

Knowing that a counterparty maintains records does not identify which record supports the claim. Describe the transaction, period and document type as far as the available information permits. Explain the connection. A court-facing request should distinguish what the document is expected to establish from what the company merely hopes to find.

What if the document's exact name or contents cannot be specified? Article 342 expressly addresses manifest difficulty in identifying the document or stating its contents. In that situation, the court may order the opponent to provide necessary assistance. The provision is discretionary. It does not dispense with explaining the difficulty or the other elements of the motion.

[Article 343](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=343) requires a production order where the court finds that the fact to be proved is material and that the motion is justified. Those are cumulative requirements. Filing a request does not by itself establish that both are met.

## Why the opponent has a duty to produce

Documents held by the other side are not all treated identically. [Article 344](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=344) lists five categories carrying a production duty: documents that party cited in the proceedings; documents the requester may legally demand or inspect; documents made for the requester's benefit; commercial account books; and documents made concerning matters related to the litigation.

Identify the applicable category. If the request relies on a legal right to inspect a particular document, explain that right rather than assuming that its existence follows from the lawsuit. For company staff collecting instructions for Taiwan counsel, a short note beside each requested record can identify the claimed basis and any supporting material already available.

## Privacy and business secrets

Does a confidentiality label settle the request? Article 344(2) allows a qualified refusal for the last of those five categories where the document contains a party's or a third party's privacy or business secrets and disclosure risks substantial harm. The condition is specific. It should not be paraphrased as a universal exemption for everything marked confidential.

The court may, when necessary to assess whether refusal has a proper basis, require production through a non-public procedure. A company's explanation should therefore identify the information concerned and the harm said to follow from disclosure. That preparation does not decide how the court will rule.

## Failure to obey an order

[Article 345](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=345) addresses unjustified failure to comply with a production order. After considering the circumstances, the court may treat the requesting party's assertions about the document or the facts it should prove as true. Before deciding, it must give the parties an opportunity to argue.

The consequence concerns proof. It is not an automatic judgment for every amount claimed. Preserve the request, the order, the stated reason for non-production and the precise factual issue that remains disputed. Those materials keep the later evidentiary argument tied to the document the court actually ordered the opponent to produce.
