# When a Taiwan Groomer or Pet Hotel Injures, Loses or Kills Your Dog: Who Pays?

Three nights at a Taichung pet hotel for a Maltese, NT$1,200 a night. On the second evening the hotel calls: the lead slipped on a walk, the dog bolted toward a main road, and two staff have searched in vain (an invented example).

That week a groomer's scissors nicked a poodle's ear. The incidents look alike. The rules differ.

## Boarding needs a licence; grooming has contract terms

Under [Animal Protection Act art. 22](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=M0060027&flno=22)(1), only operators licensed by the city or county authority may breed, sell or board specified pets, for terms of up to three years. The specified pets are dogs and cats ([Regulations Governing Specified Pet Businesses art. 2](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=M0060031&flno=2)). Grooming is not on the list, though the ministry's Q&A says some localities, such as Taichung, require groomers to hold a boarding licence as local policy.

Unlicensed dog or cat boarding draws fines of NT$100,000 to NT$3 million and an order to stop ([art. 25-2](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=M0060027&flno=25-2)(1)). Boarding operators must show their licence number in ads (art. 22-2(3)) and hang the licence where customers can see it ([Regulations art. 10](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=M0060031&flno=10)(1)(v)).

Licensed operators also keep records: a dedicated staff member ([Regulations art. 4](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=M0060031&flno=4)(1)) logs daily care and keeps the log for two years (art. 10(1)(i)).

## Terms that enter every grooming contract

On May 12, 2025, the ministry [announced mandatory and prohibited terms for dog and cat grooming contracts](https://gazette.nat.gov.tw/EG_FileManager/eguploadpub/eg031086/ch07/type1/gov88/num15/Eg.htm) (犬、貓美容服務定型化契約應記載及不得記載事項), effective immediately, under [Consumer Protection Act art. 17](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0170001&flno=17)(1). Under art. 17(5), they bind even if the shop's form omits them. The ministry's [practice guide](https://animal.moa.gov.tw/public/upload/Know_ListFile/2602060329408762245TMTE.pdf) says they cover oral bookings too.

Mandatory term 5 covers accidents. If a groomer notices a problem with the treated area or the animal's health, the shop must deal with it at once, then notify the owner or emergency contact. If the animal needs urgent care, the shop notifies the owner and takes it first to the owner's chosen vet, or else to a suitable nearby clinic. If it dies, the shop notifies the owner, preserves the body and reports to the animal protection authority. If the cause is attributable to the shop, it is liable for damages and under the Animal Protection Act.

Time is short for the body. Uncollected on the day of notice, it goes to the animal protection authority by the next day (term 5(3)). Decide on a post-mortem that day.

Two records matter. Before grooming, the shop asks about temperament, aggression, illness and history, checks the animal, and gives the owner a signed copy of the answers (term 4); the owner must answer truthfully. That form and the service record (term 3) are the first evidence.

A dog left overnight at a salon falls under term 10: after closing the shop must notify the owner or emergency contact and keep caring for the animal, may charge an agreed penalty of up to three times that visit's service fee per day, and after three days notifies the animal protection authority.

## How far a waiver goes

As for no-liability forms, prohibited term 6 bars clauses excluding or limiting the shop's liability under the Consumer Protection Act, and term 2 bars clauses that breach mandatory law or are obviously unfair. A clause that breaches the announced terms is void (art. 17(4)). Liability for intent or gross negligence cannot be waived in advance anyway ([Civil Code art. 222](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=222)). A shop that ignores a correction order faces fines of NT$30,000 to NT$300,000, then NT$50,000 to NT$500,000, repeatable per violation ([Consumer Protection Act art. 56-1](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0170001&flno=56-1)).

Prepaid sessions left after a pet dies or falls too ill? Either side may end the contract (term 9(1)(ii)). Within three days the shop refunds what was paid, or after grooming has started, the balance less sessions used, with no handling fee (terms 6, 7, 9(2)).

## Boarding has only a model contract

As of November 3, 2026, boarding has no equivalent mandatory-terms notice. Instead, by a letter of June 17, 2025 (農護字第1140072491號), the ministry circulated a model dog and cat boarding contract. It is a reference form the parties may change; it does not bind automatically.

Its accident clause (art. 6) mirrors grooming term 5. Article 1 bars taking the animal off the premises without consent, except in special emergencies or force majeure, and promises a full refund if that is proven; an unagreed walk may fall here. Under art. 5, an operator that was not negligent owes nothing if the owner hid an illness. Under art. 8, the owner may end the stay at any time (not stays under a day); within three days of termination the operator refunds the amount received minus boarding fees already earned and any agreed cancellation fee. Under art. 10, either side may end it for force majeure or the animal's death, illness, poor health or refusal that makes continued boarding impracticable, with no cancellation fee. Loss is not an art. 10 ground; a hotel at fault settles under art. 9 with no cancellation fee and pays further damages, alongside art. 14 and the Civil Code.

## Deposit and the standard of care

A contract in which one party hands over a thing and the other agrees to keep it is a deposit ([Civil Code art. 589](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=589)(1)). A paid keeper must take the care of a good administrator ([art. 590](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=590)). The keeper must keep the thing itself unless the depositor agrees, custom allows or necessity requires ([art. 592](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=592)), and answers for the fault of its staff as its own ([art. 224](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=224)).

If the dog cannot be returned for reasons attributable to the hotel, the owner may claim damages ([art. 226](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=226)(1)). Grooming may be read as a contract for work ([art. 490](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=490)(1)), and defective performance attributable to the shop that causes further harm also supports a claim ([art. 227](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=227)(2)).

Deadlines are tight. Damages claims under a deposit contract lapse one year after the deposit relationship ends ([art. 601-2](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=601-2)). A tort claim ([art. 184](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=184)(1)) lapses two years after the victim knows of the damage and the liable party, and ten years after the act ([art. 197](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=197)(1)).

## What can be claimed

Damages aim to restore the prior position, or pay what restoration costs ([art. 213](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=213)(1), (3)); where that is impossible, money is paid ([art. 215](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=215)). The measure is loss suffered and profit lost ([art. 216](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=216)(1)). For the poodle: stitches and follow-ups. Damaging another's property also supports a claim for lost value ([art. 196](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=196)).

Grief is harder. [Article 195](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=195)(1) provides non-pecuniary damages for injury to a person's body, health, reputation, liberty and similar interests. The wording of art. 195 does not settle whether a pet's death qualifies (as of November 3, 2026); build the claim on receipts.

If another boarder bit your dog, the animal's possessor is liable unless it shows proper restraint for the animal's kind and nature, or that harm would have occurred despite it ([art. 190](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=190)(1)). Who controlled the biter matters; see [Bitten by someone else's dog in Taiwan](/en/columns/taiwan-dog-bite-owner-liability-civil-code-190).

## Administrative exposure

The Animal Protection Act treats as an "owner" anyone who actually controls an animal ([art. 3](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=M0060027&flno=3)(7)). Owners must keep animals from harassment, abuse or injury and move them to safety, with a chance to escape, when danger looms ([art. 5](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=M0060027&flno=5)(2)(iv), (viii)), duties that can reach a shop during custody. Negligently injuring an animal so that it suffers severe limb damage, loss of a vital organ's function, or death carries fines of NT$15,000 to NT$75,000 ([art. 30](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=M0060027&flno=30)(1)(i)). The ministry's [Q&A](https://animal.moa.gov.tw/Frontend/Know/Detail/LT00000952?parentID=Tab0000171) leaves that call to the animal protection authority; a report can run alongside a civil claim.

## A lost pet: five days to report

The owner or boarding operator must report a lost pet, with its registration certificate, to the registration agency within five days ([Regulations on Pet Registration art. 7](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=M0060029&flno=7)(1)). A pet not found within a year of the report is deemed dead and deregistered (art. 7(2)). If the hotel did not report, do so yourself. Keep vet reports, receipts, photos, messages, copies of the intake form or care log, and flyer costs.

## Small claims

Money claims of NT$100,000 or less go through the small claims procedure ([Code of Civil Procedure art. 436-8](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=436-8)(1)). A consumer suit may be brought where the consumer relationship arose ([Consumer Protection Act art. 47](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0170001&flno=47)), and prohibited grooming term 4 bars contracts from excluding that rule or the small-claims venue rules. See [small claims in Taiwan](/en/columns/taiwan-small-claims-simplified-civil-procedure).
