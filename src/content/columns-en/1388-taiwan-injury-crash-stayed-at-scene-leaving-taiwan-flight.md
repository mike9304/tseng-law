# You stayed after a crash that injured someone, and no exit notice has come. Can you still fly out of Taiwan?

If you gave aid, called the police and gave your statement, [Article 185-4 of the Criminal Code](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0000001&flno=185-4) (中華民國刑法) does not by its words treat a flight days later as fleeing (逃逸). Nothing in that article bans the flight itself. What can stop you at the gate is an exit restriction (限制出境、出海), which a prosecutor (檢察官) or a judge (法官) may impose before any written notice reaches you. If a case is already open, file a Taiwan address or an agent for service (送達代收人) before the flight.

Imprisonment for that fleeing runs from six months to five years, as the statute stands on November 19, 2026. Where the fleeing follows death or serious injury (重傷), the range is one year to seven. Article 185-4 does not define fleeing. A driver who fled without fault for the death or injury has still committed the offense. The penalty is reduced or waived (減輕或免除其刑).

## Aid at the scene, and the license

After a crash that injures or kills someone, Paragraph 3 of [Article 62 of the Road Traffic Management and Penalty Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=K0040012&flno=62) (道路交通管理處罰條例) addresses the driver of a vehicle the Act calls 汽車. That driver must immediately give aid, take the measures the rules require, and notify the police, and must not move the vehicle or the traces at will. A violation draws an administrative fine (罰鍰) of NT$3,000 to NT$9,000. Where every party agrees, an injury case requires the vehicle to be marked and then moved clear of traffic.

A driver who flees after causing injury loses the license (吊銷). For a driver who fled after serious injury or death, [Article 67](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=K0040012&flno=67) generally bars a new license for life. [Article 67-1](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=K0040012&flno=67-1) allows an application to take the test if specific conditions are met and more than ten years have passed since enforcement of the revocation for serious injury, or more than twelve years for a death.

## The order can exist before the letter

Exit from Taiwan and going out to sea can be restricted directly under [Article 93-2 of the Code of Criminal Procedure](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0010001&flno=93-2) (刑事訴訟法). A prosecutor or a judge may do it when necessary, if suspicion is grave (犯罪嫌疑重大) and one of three grounds is present. The grounds are no fixed domicile or residence (無一定之住、居所); reasonable grounds to believe there is a risk the defendant (被告) will flee (逃亡之虞); or reasonable grounds to believe there is a risk that evidence will be destroyed, forged or altered, or that the defendant will collude with an accomplice or a witness. Direct restriction is forbidden when the heaviest penalty is detention (拘役) or a fine alone (專科罰金).

Negligent injury falls outside that exception. [Article 284 of the Criminal Code](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0000001&flno=284) punishes it with imprisonment of up to one year, detention, or a fine (罰金) of up to 100,000 (十萬元). For serious injury, the range is imprisonment of up to three years, detention, or a fine of up to 300,000 (三十萬元). A death is outside Article 284. No home in Taiwan, and a ticket out this week, is the kind of fact the first two grounds speak to. Whether suspicion is grave, a ground is met, and a restriction is necessary remains for the prosecutor or the judge.

Does a silent inbox mean no restriction is in force?

Not by itself. The third paragraph of Article 93-2 requires the written order to be sent within six months after the restriction, unless the defendant's domicile or residence is unknown and notice cannot be given. If you are questioned (訊問) before the notice goes out, you must be told at that session (當庭) and handed the written order. Nothing in the article treats the statement you gave the police at the scene as that questioning. If you learn of the restriction before the written order arrives, you may ask for it. The first news can come at the gate.

The written order must identify the defendant, the case and the statute, the reason and the period, the agency that carries the restriction out, and the way to challenge it.

During an investigation (偵查), [Article 93-3](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0010001&flno=93-3) keeps a prosecutor's restriction within eight months. A longer period takes a court ruling, on the prosecutor's written application with concrete reasons, and only two extensions are allowed. Trial-stage limits are in the column on [temporary travel under a criminal exit ban](/en/columns/taiwan-criminal-exit-ban-foreign-defendant-temporary-travel).

[Article 93-6](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0010001&flno=93-6) allows an exit restriction when a provision outside that chapter permits bail (具保), release to a custodian (責付), or a residence limit (限制住居). Paragraph 2 of Article 93-2, and Articles 93-3 through 93-5, then apply.

Under [Article 228](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0010001&flno=228), a prosecutor who learns of the suspicion from a complaint, a report, a surrender, or any other circumstance must open an investigation at once. The defendant must not be summoned first unless that is necessary. After a summons, a surrender, or a voluntary appearance, if questioning shows a ground for detention and no need to seek detention, the prosecutor may order bail, release to a custodian, or a residence limit.

## A summons can still find you

The summons (傳票) is governed by [Article 71](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0010001&flno=71). It records the defendant's name, sex, date of birth, identification number and address, the case, and when and where to appear. It must warn that failing to appear without a good reason may lead to compulsory appearance (拘提). An unknown birth date, identification number, or address may be omitted.

Compulsory appearance is available, under [Article 75](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0010001&flno=75), when a defendant ignores a lawful summons without a good reason.

Flight or hiding (逃亡或藏匿) is the condition [Article 84](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0010001&flno=84) sets for a wanted notice (通緝). Its text does not say that leaving Taiwan before any summons is either one. A summons served at the address or on the agent you reported, and then ignored without a good reason, can lead to compulsory appearance under Article 75.

Once you are a defendant, [Article 55](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0010001&flno=55) requires you to give the court or the prosecutor a domicile, a residence, or an office for service. With none of those where the court sits, you must name a person who has one there, the agent for service. Service on that agent counts as service on you, at every level of court in that city.

## Papers, the complaint, and the insurer

Scene papers are timed by [Article 13 of the rules on handling road traffic accidents](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0080090&flno=13) (道路交通事故處理辦法). A party, or someone with an interest, may ask at the scene for the registration slip (道路交通事故當事人登記聯單). The diagram and photographs wait until seven days have passed, and the preliminary analysis (道路交通事故初步分析研判表) until thirty days have passed. Viewing is at the police office. The file must not be removed or altered, and the applicant pays for copies. A flight this week can fall before day seven, so the diagram may not be requestable yet. How to ask is in the column on [police records after a crash](/en/columns/taiwan-accident-police-records).

Prosecution of negligent injury requires a complaint. [Article 287](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0000001&flno=287) makes Article 284 an offense prosecuted only on a complaint (告訴乃論). [Article 237](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0010001&flno=237) counts six months from the day the person entitled to complain learns who the offender is, and one person's delay does not affect the others. Article 287 names Article 284, which covers injury only, so the complaint rule does not reach a case in which the injured person dies. Under Article 228 the prosecutor must open an investigation as soon as the suspicion becomes known, whether or not anyone has complained. The complaint is what Article 284 needs before prosecution.

Injured people can claim without proving your fault. [Article 7 of the Compulsory Automobile Liability Insurance Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0390060&flno=7) (強制汽車責任保險法) lets a claimant seek insurer benefits, or compensation from the fund (汽車交通事故特別補償基金), after injury or death. [Article 40](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0390060&flno=40) limits the fund to an untraceable vehicle, an uninsured one, use or management without the insured's consent, or some or all of the vehicles involved not being required to carry this insurance. Where every vehicle involved was exempt, the drivers of those vehicles may not claim from the fund. Against the insurer, [Article 14](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0390060&flno=14) sets two years from knowledge of the harm and of the insurer, and ten years from the accident. A timely request pauses that count until the insurer's decision notice arrives. Fund claims follow the same two-year and ten-year limits. The two years start on the dates Article 14 sets for each situation, for example from learning of the harm and confirming that the vehicle cannot be traced.
