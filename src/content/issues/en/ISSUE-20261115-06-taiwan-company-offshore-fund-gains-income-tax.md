# Offshore fund gains in a Taiwan company's tax return

Does a Taiwan company's profit from selling a fund qualify for the securities-income tax exemption? The fund's name alone cannot answer that question. [Economic Daily reported on November 13](https://money.udn.com/money/story/6710/9875301) that the Northern Area National Taxation Bureau had drawn a distinction between domestic and offshore funds. Its [November 11 release](https://www.ntbna.gov.tw/singlehtml/374fe8c4b18e48c691a6806ac02b9984?cntId=643a7971e9274028aea01b511bdc4bb6) treats offshore fund disposal gains as taxable foreign property-transaction income.

For an international group's Taiwan subsidiary investing its own cash, the taxpayer's identity comes first. [Income Tax Act Article 3(2)](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0340003&flno=3) taxes an enterprise with its head office in Taiwan on its combined domestic and foreign business income. A shareholder's overseas location does not change that statutory test. This discussion concerns the Taiwan enterprise's portfolio, not the US parent's or an employee's personal investments.

## Where the fund was issued

The bureau identifies the fund's issuer and place of registration as relevant to classification. Its release explains that a gain from disposing of an offshore fund does not fall within the securities-transaction income suspension in [Article 4-1](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0340003&flno=4-1). The gain enters the Taiwan enterprise's annual taxable income. A general ledger description such as “fund investment” leaves the instrument's identity unresolved.

A fund bought through the group's usual banking relationship still needs to be identified from its own documents. A familiar bank name cannot supply the fund's registration details.

Domestic fund gains follow another calculation. Where a gain qualifies under Article 4-1, [Income Basic Tax Act Article 7(1)(1)](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0340115&flno=7) adds that income to business basic income. The ordinary income-tax suspension therefore does not remove the amount from every tax calculation.

Additional tax is not automatic. [Article 4](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0340115&flno=4) compares regular income tax with basic tax: when basic tax is higher, the difference is additionally payable. Classification identifies where the gain belongs; the company's complete calculation determines the resulting liability.

## An omitted gain and the timing of correction

In the bureau's example, a company omitted an offshore fund gain from its taxable income in its 2024 return. The omitted gain was NT$500,000. Following the audit, the bureau assessed NT$100,000 in additional income tax and imposed a penalty under [Income Tax Act Article 110](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0340003&flno=110). No penalty amount was disclosed. Its figures describe that assessment, not a fixed charge applicable to every company or fund disposal.

A company discovering the same classification error may still have a correction route. [Tax Collection Act Article 48-1](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0340001&flno=48-1) provides relief from specified tax-evasion penalties where the taxpayer voluntarily reports and pays the omitted tax before any report of the case or investigation by the tax authority or a Ministry of Finance-designated investigator. Both reporting and payment are required.

Interest remains due. It runs daily from the day after the original payment deadline through the date of supplementary payment. A revised spreadsheet or an amended return without the required payment does not, by itself, establish entitlement to the relief.

For a finance team working across jurisdictions, the fund prospectus and issuer-registration details can explain what the investment account's abbreviated description leaves out. Acquisition and disposal statements establish the transaction; the filed return shows how the gain was classified. If those records reveal an omission, the next factual question is whether a report or investigation has already occurred. That timing determines whether voluntary correction remains within Article 48-1's conditions.
