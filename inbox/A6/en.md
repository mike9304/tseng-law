# Hurt in a Taiwan Traffic Accident: Claiming Compulsory Auto Insurance, and the July 2026 Increase

Who pays the first hospital bills if a scooter knocks you down in Taipei and the rider insists you stepped out without looking? (The scene is an invented example.) Taiwan's answer starts with compulsory automobile liability insurance, which the owner of a vehicle required to be insured must take out ([Article 6](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0390060&flno=6)). It pays injured people without anyone first having to prove fault.

## A no-fault layer

[Article 7 of the Compulsory Automobile Liability Insurance Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0390060&flno=7) lets the claimant recover from the insurer whether or not the person who caused the accident was at fault. The accidents covered are those in which using or managing a vehicle injures or kills a passenger or someone outside the vehicle ([Article 13](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0390060&flno=13)). An injured person claims personally; after a death, parents, children and spouse come first in the order of family claimants, followed by grandparents, grandchildren and siblings ([Article 11](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0390060&flno=11)). If there is no family claimant at all, whoever paid the funeral costs may claim up to those costs.

Where the vehicle cannot be identified or was uninsured, or where the insured vehicle was being used or managed without the insured's consent, the claim goes to the Special Compensation Fund instead, within the same limits ([Article 40](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0390060&flno=40)). Two differences matter. Time for a Fund claim runs from when the claimant knows of the damage and confirms the relevant fact: that the vehicle cannot be traced, was uninsured, or was an insured vehicle used or managed without the insured's consent ([Article 14(4)](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0390060&flno=14)). And the Fund's medical payment excludes amounts paid by National Health Insurance.

## What it pays

Only three benefits are listed: injury medical expenses, disability and death ([Article 27(1)](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0390060&flno=27)). Repairs to your bike or car are not among them.

Under the [benefit standards](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawAll.aspx?pcode=G0390067), medical expenses are paid at necessary and reasonable actual cost, up to NT$200,000 per person per accident (Article 2). That covers emergency transport, treatment, travel to and from outpatient visits or on discharge (capped at NT$20,000), and nursing care during a hospital stay at up to NT$1,200 a day for no more than 30 days. Items outside National Health Insurance have their own caps inside that total, such as a ward-fee difference of up to NT$1,500 a day and hospital meals of up to NT$180 a day. None of that changed in 2026.

Disability and death did change. For accidents on or after July 1, 2026, disability is paid on a 15-grade scale from NT$80,000 to NT$3 million, death at NT$3 million, and the combined ceiling per person at NT$3.2 million (Articles 3, 6 and 7). Before that date the figures were NT$50,000 to NT$2 million, NT$2 million, and NT$2.2 million. The accident date decides which set applies ([Article 27(3)](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0390060&flno=27)).

## Claiming, and the clock

Claims go straight to the insurer of the vehicle involved. Once the documents are complete, the insurer must pay within ten working days, and if it is late through its own fault it owes interest at 10% a year ([Article 25](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0390060&flno=25)). The right to the benefit cannot be seized, assigned or pledged.

Two years is the main deadline. It runs from when the claimant learns of the damage and of the insurer, with an outer limit of ten years from the accident ([Article 14](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0390060&flno=14)). A claim lodged before the period expires stops the clock until the insurer's decision arrives. Article 14 has no separate rule for claimants who have gone home; the same two years apply wherever the claimant lives. In practice that means the hospital records and receipts need to be gathered before leaving Taiwan, or requested from abroad in good time.

## How it fits with the other side's liability

Compulsory insurance is not the whole of what an injured person can recover. Benefits stop at the standard's caps, and loss beyond them is claimed from the person responsible under the Civil Code's tort rules ([Civil Code Article 184](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=184)). What the insurer pays counts toward the damages the insured owes, and that party may deduct it when sued ([Article 32](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0390060&flno=32)). A settlement figure only adds up if the document says whether the compulsory benefit is inside it or on top. The criminal side of an injury accident, including the six-month complaint period and withdrawal after settlement, is covered in [our column on injury settlements](/en/columns/taiwan-complaint-only-offense-settlement-withdrawal-deadline).

The accident date, the other vehicle's insurer, and the medical receipts are where a claim starts. The deadline also turns on when the injured person learned of the loss and of the insurer, and a claim already lodged with the insurer stops the clock from the time it takes effect until the insurer's decision arrives ([Article 14(1)–(2)](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0390060&flno=14)). A disability benefit needs its own assessment: the condition must have stabilized after treatment, and a qualified doctor must diagnose it as permanent ([Benefit Standards, Article 3](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0390067&flno=3)).
