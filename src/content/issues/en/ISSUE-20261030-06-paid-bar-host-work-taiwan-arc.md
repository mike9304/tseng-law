# Paid host work at a Taiwan bar: what happens to your ARC?

On October 15, 2026, Taiwan's [National Immigration Agency posted amended criteria](https://www.immigration.gov.tw/5475/5478/141478/141482/419882/cp_news) for conduct it calls “hindering good social customs.” One category describes a person paid to host or accompany customers in a bar, nightclub, karaoke venue, or another place open to the public. It asks what the person did and was paid to do. The name of the workplace alone does not answer it.

That distinction can matter to a U.S. student, employee, or spouse who holds a Taiwan Alien Resident Certificate (ARC). A shift behind a counter, a paid customer-hosting role, and a police case are different records. When the agency cites one of them in a residence decision, the written ground needs to match the conduct and its evidence; a workplace name alone cannot fill that gap.

## The agency lists three kinds of conduct

The October criteria implement [Immigration Act Article 18(1)(14)](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0080132&flno=18) and [Article 24(1)(13)](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0080132&flno=24). Their first category requires a final guilty judgment for an offense in the Criminal Code's chapter on offenses against morality. A police interview or an unproven charge is not that judgment. An accused person remains presumed innocent.

For the second category, the agency requires a final police disposition or a final, binding court ruling under the Social Order Maintenance Act's vice chapter. A report that police questioned someone does not establish finality. The date and status of the actual disposition matter.

Paid customer-hosting is the third category. It covers services as a host, hostess, escort companion, or similar attendant for customers at the venues the criteria name, with payment for those services. No final conviction or disposition appears in that category. Nor does the agency say every person employed by a bar is a customer attendant. Work schedules, assigned duties, messages about the role, and payment records may show whether the described service occurred. Was the payment for attending to customers in the way the criterion describes?

## Residence and entry require their own decisions

[Article 24](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0080132&flno=24) lets the agency refuse an application for residence or a change in the reason for residence on this ground. If residence was already permitted, it may rescind or revoke that permission and cancel the ARC. The statute says “may.” Cancellation is not automatic whenever someone is reported to have worked at a venue. A student moving to a work ARC, for example, needs a decision based on the particular conduct and the applicable residence ground.

For a person leaving Taiwan and hoping to return, [Article 18](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0080132&flno=18) also permits an entry ban for the conduct described. A residence refusal is not itself proof that an entry ban was imposed. The agency's written decision, if any, shows the measure actually taken.

Routine extensions deserve care too. [Article 31](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0080132&flno=31) says a foreign resident who needs to remain must apply before the residence period expires. The October criteria identify residence applications and changes of purpose; they do not state that every extension is refused. An existing permission can nevertheless be considered under Article 24. The ARC's expiry date and the exact application or cancellation notice therefore matter more than a general warning about “renewal.”

## Work permission poses another question

Taiwan's [Employment Service Act Article 43](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0090001&flno=43) generally bars foreign nationals from working without an employer-applied permit unless the Act provides otherwise. [Article 68](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0090001&flno=68) sets consequences for a violation, including an order to depart. Whether a particular resident needs a permit, and whether one covers this work, depends on that resident's status and authorization. A permit for a different job does not describe what happened during a bar shift.

Immigration Act Article 24 also lists [work or activity inconsistent with the permitted residence reason](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0080132&flno=24) as another possible ground for refusing residence. An agency letter may rely on that ground, the social-customs ground, or both. For a refusal based on inconsistent work or activity under Article 24(1)(12), Article 24(3) sets a period of at least one and no more than seven years, counted from the day after departure. It does not assign that period to the social-customs ground in item 13. The ground written in the letter thus affects both the appeal and any statutory refusal period.

## When the decision arrives

An adverse residence decision is an administrative disposition. Under the [Administrative Appeal Act, Articles 1](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=A0030020&flno=1) and [14](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=A0030020&flno=14), the ordinary appeal period is 30 days from the day after the disposition reaches the person or the announcement period ends. [Article 58](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=A0030020&flno=58) sends the appeal through the original agency, which first reviews its own decision. The notice and its envelope or electronic delivery record show both the stated ground and when the 30-day appeal period began.

An appeal does not itself pause execution. [Article 93](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=A0030020&flno=93) allows a separate request to suspend all or part of a decision when its conditions are met. It is especially relevant if the agency has already cancelled a card. An appeal about a paid host role should address the duties and payment the agency attributes to that person, alongside any final judgment or disposition it cites. What did the agency find? Without those details, a denial is hard to assess.

If the letter cites the paid-hosting category, the actual shift may be decisive: who assigned the resident to customers, what the resident did with them, and whether any fee was received for that service. Job labels can mislead. Those questions differ from whether a criminal or social-order matter has reached the final stage the other two categories require.
