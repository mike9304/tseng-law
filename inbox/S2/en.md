# A Divorce 'Only on Paper' in Taiwan: The Witnesses Decided Which Ones Were Void

Divorce on paper, get the debt restructured, register the marriage again once things settle down. That was the plan your Taiwanese spouse put to you, and you signed (an invented example). Taiwanese judgments describe divorces said to have been made for reasons like these: to apply for debt negotiation, to qualify for single-parent subsidies, to keep property away from a relative's creditors.

In the cases below, no court held that the motive alone made a divorce void.

## How a divorce by consent is made

One assumption runs through this piece: the divorce is governed by Taiwan law and made in Taiwan's form. For an international couple, divorce is governed by the spouses' common national law at the time of the agreement, failing that by the law of their common domicile, and failing that by the law of the place most closely connected with the marriage ([Act Governing the Choice of Law in Civil Matters Involving Foreign Elements, Article 50](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000007&flno=50)). The form of the act follows the law governing it or the law of the place where it is done ([Article 16](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000007&flno=16)).

Article 1049 of the Civil Code lets spouses divorce by mutual consent. [Article 1050](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=1050) sets the form: a written agreement, the signatures of at least two witnesses, and registration at the household registration office. A juridical act that does not follow a required form is void under [Article 73](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=73).

Case law fills in the witness requirement. Witnesses need not be in the room when the spouses sign, but each must have personally seen or heard that both spouses genuinely intended to divorce. Where Taiwan law governs, that includes the foreign spouse. A witness who signs on the Taiwanese spouse's word alone has not personally confirmed the foreign spouse's intent.

## How far "it was only on paper" got

A separate rule voids a fictitious expression of intent made in collusion with the other party ([Article 87](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=87)). None of the judgments below decided the case under it. The New Taipei and High Court judgments said they did not need to reach it, and the other two did not mention it. They questioned the witnesses closely instead: when they signed, who was there, and how they learned what each spouse wanted.

The New Taipei District Court ruled on December 20, 2024 ([113年度婚字第455號](https://judgment.judicial.gov.tw/FJUD/data.aspx?ty=JD&id=PCDV%2c113%2c%e5%a9%9a%2c455%2c20241220%2c1)) on a divorce registered in 2010, fourteen years earlier. The plaintiff said it had been done to apply for debt negotiation. The court turned to the witnesses. One had signed at the plaintiff's workplace with only the plaintiff present and never checked, by any means, what the other spouse wanted. The divorce was void, and the court said it did not need to decide whether the declarations were collusive.

In Taitung the result was the same ([113年度婚字第27號](https://judgment.judicial.gov.tw/FJUD/data.aspx?ty=JD&id=TTDV%2c113%2c%e5%a9%9a%2c27%2c20250109%2c2), January 9, 2025). The husband said he signed so his wife could get subsidies. Neither witness knew him, and neither checked that the man sitting in the car was actually her husband.

Taoyuan went the other way ([112年度婚字第449號](https://judgment.judicial.gov.tw/FJUD/data.aspx?ty=JD&id=TYDV%2c112%2c%e5%a9%9a%2c449%2c20241115%2c1), November 15, 2024). The plaintiff said the divorce was meant only to shield the plaintiff's property from creditors of the other spouse's parents, who had guaranteed someone's debts. A party relying on a fact must prove it ([Code of Civil Procedure Article 277](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=277)), and there was no evidence. Affectionate messages exchanged after the registration did not change that. The two witnesses, a married couple who knew both spouses, had come to the home and heard each of them say they wanted a divorce. The divorce stood.

At the Taiwan High Court ([114年度家上字第28號](https://judgment.judicial.gov.tw/FJUD/data.aspx?ty=JD&id=TPHV%2c114%2c%e5%ae%b6%e4%b8%8a%2c28%2c20250423%2c1), April 23, 2025), the witness lines carried the names of one spouse's younger brother and sister, filled in without their knowledge. Both spouses' convictions for uttering forged private documents had become final. One of them argued that, having grown up in mainland China, they had not known Taiwan's formal rules. The divorce was still void. In the same judgment, though, the court upheld a judicial divorce because the marriage had broken down ([Article 1052(2)](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=1052)).

## What a void divorce leaves behind

A void divorce means the marriage never ended. Because the register still showed a divorce, each of these courts found that the plaintiff had a legal interest in a suit to confirm that the marriage exists ([Code of Civil Procedure Article 247](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=247)).

Remarriage is where it bites. Article 985 forbids bigamy, and [Article 988(3)](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=988) makes a bigamous marriage void. It also makes an exception: if both parties to the new marriage relied in good faith and without negligence on the registered divorce, the new marriage stands, and the old one is treated as dissolved on the day the new one was formed, with the effects of divorce applying ([Article 988-1](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=988-1)). A remaining-property distribution or agreement already made still controls and cannot be claimed afresh (paragraph 2). Otherwise, a claim to share the remaining marital property expires two years after the claimant learns of the difference, and in any case five years after the divorce registration is revoked. The first spouse is not left empty-handed either: a spouse without fault may claim damages from the other, including for non-pecuniary loss.

Registration steps and the witness formalities themselves are covered in [our column on divorcing a Taiwanese spouse by agreement](/en/columns/taiwanese-spouse-divorce-agreement-registration). In every judgment above, the challenge came as a suit to confirm the marriage. The three divorces held void all failed on the witness requirement; in the one that stood, both witnesses had heard the spouses say in person that they would divorce.
