# A Taiwan company had crypto on Binance on October 30. Is there a tax loss to book?

Probably not, at least not yet. The answer depends on what the company actually lost, and on how the holding is recorded in its books.

Binance's [Security Incident Update #2](https://www.binance.com/en/support/announcement/detail/d3306d9dca5243548c813b605e1d1751) said hot wallets were compromised at about 20:05 UTC on October 29, early on October 30 in Taiwan, with a total impact of about US$6.3 billion. Its [October 30 announcement](https://www.binance.com/en/support/announcement/detail/c5a8729a97ac486d963784d96c5642d7) said it would cover all losses through its SAFU fund and company funds. [Recovery Update #3 on October 31](https://www.binance.com/en/support/announcement/detail/1fdf1f61559c401da0435895ffcccb62) put the replenished share at about 90 percent: the top ten tokens were fully backed, and the remaining 10 percent, in less liquid tokens, was still being bought. The sources named were a portion of the futures insurance fund, the company treasury and a SAFU deployment expected within 24 hours. Binance said it would absorb the financial impact of the stolen funds, not its users. A [later update](https://www.binance.com/zh-CN/support/announcement/detail/185bd9a4717f44088b3be70287b8237f) put the covered share at about 95 percent.

For a Taiwan subsidiary that kept stablecoins on the exchange for payments, the year-end question is practical. Does anything go into the tax return?

## What the Ministry of Finance said about FTX

Taiwan's tax authority has addressed a similar situation once. When FTX filed for bankruptcy protection, the Ministry of Finance published [a statement on November 18, 2022](https://www.mof.gov.tw/singlehtml/384fb3077bb349ea973e7fc6f13b6974?cntId=8a1a083b299e49828ecb86da6463b2e0) on whether Taiwan investors could deduct their losses.

It described two paths for companies. Gains and losses from selling or exchanging virtual assets are computed under [Article 24, paragraph 1 of the Income Tax Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0340003&flno=24). Separately, where the bankruptcy filing left a company's claim against FTX wholly or partly unrecoverable, the company could treat it as an actual bad debt under Article 94, subparagraph 5 of the [Regulations Governing Assessment of Profit-Seeking Enterprise Income Tax](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0340051&flno=94), with the court's bankruptcy ruling and related documents under subparagraph 7, item 2.

## What Article 94 asks for

Subparagraph 5 covers accounts receivable, notes receivable and other claims. It deems a bad debt to have occurred in two situations. Item 1: the debtor closes down, absconds, reorganizes, reaches a settlement or is declared bankrupt, or for another reason the claim cannot be recovered in whole or in part. Item 2: the claim has gone unpaid for two years counted from the day after it originally fell due, and neither principal nor interest has been collected after demand.

Each situation has its own evidence. Closing down or absconding is shown under subparagraph 6, mainly with an undeliverable registered letter sent to the debtor's actual business address. Reorganization, settlement, bankruptcy or another reason falls under subparagraph 7: a court ruling, a settlement record, a court-issued certificate of claim after enforcement finds too little property, or, for a debtor liquidating under foreign law, documents proving completion verified by a Taiwan mission or trade office. The two-year route under subparagraph 8 needs a delivered registered letter, one returned as refused or because the addressee died, or proof of a court claim.

Whether a particular holding is a claim at all is a separate question. The 2022 statement dealt with FTX holdings that became unrecoverable. Crypto a company records as inventory, for instance, may follow a different route, and that needs checking against how the books are kept.

## Why Binance is different, for now

Binance's announcements mention no bankruptcy or liquidation filing, and they say the losses are being covered from its own funds. A company whose balance shows the same coins it held before the incident has no unrecoverable claim to deduct.

What if part of a balance is never restored? The company would then look for a subparagraph 5 event and the matching evidence. An impairment entry in the accounts does not by itself meet those requirements.

## Individuals: overseas income and a narrow offset

The same 2022 statement set out the individual side. Taiwan's individual income tax is territorial. Overseas income enters the basic income amount under the [Income Basic Tax Act, Article 12](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0340115&flno=12) once a household's overseas income for the year reaches NT$1 million. The statement cited a NT$6.7 million exemption, which applied then. For the 2026 income year, the Ministry's notice of November 19, 2025 sets the amount at NT$7.5 million ([Executive Yuan Gazette](https://gazette.nat.gov.tw/egFront/eguploadpubWrapper?metaid=161623&file=/EG_FileManager/eguploadpub/eg031218/ch04/type3/gov30/num13/Eg.htm)).

An overseas property transaction loss is a loss from buying or exchanging overseas property that is not held as a frequent buy-and-sell business. It can be deducted from overseas property transaction income in the same year, capped at that income, and the individual should keep transaction documents and proof of acquisition cost.

The hack itself did not create such a loss for users whose balances are intact. No buying or exchanging took place.

## Records to keep before year-end

A balance statement from just before the incident, another after replenishment, the exchange announcements and the terms of service in force are the core file. Binance posted a proof-of-reserves snapshot taken at 00:00 UTC on October 31, and its November 2 update said users can now verify the one-to-one backing of the top ten tokens. Checking your own balance against it gives a dated record.
