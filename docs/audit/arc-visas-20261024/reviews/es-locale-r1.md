## Per-article observations

### 704 — PASS

The order follows a first applicant's decisions: entry/eligibility, the correct date, category evidence, foreign-document preparation, submission channel, approval/payment/collection, and the issued card. `visado`, `resguardo`, `subsanación`, `ventanilla` and `certificado de empleo` are natural professional Spanish. The prose does not import Spain's NIE/TIE process.

Lines 24 and 32 correctly distinguish the entry-next-day deadline from the domestic resident-visa issuance date. Line 26 prevents that 30-day rule being read as a conversion entitlement. The student collection instructions at line 64 preserve approval → payment → collection and scope the three working days to completion of payment. Fees at line 68, the first spouse-card rule at line 46, and the processing exclusions at line 70 fit the sources opened. Documents issued in Mexico do not become Spanish documents because the holder resides in Spain (line 54).

All three FAQs answer distinct first-application questions. The final substantive paragraph returns to the issued card and the separate work authorization. No firm filing, translation or consultation-language promise appears.

### 705 — PASS

The family-first opening gives this Spanish article its own reader decision, while the remainder compares work, degree study and Mandarin study. `reagrupante` describes the sponsoring family role in the expressly Taiwanese discussion; it does not purport to apply Spain's immigration rules. The prose stays professional and uses short declarative sentences without becoming a repeated checklist.

Spain's 90-day visa-exemption example is explicitly nationality-dependent, qualified by entry conditions and separated from Spanish-language readership (line 30). Lines 38–44 distinguish a Taiwan national with household registration, an eligible foreign professional, and other resident sponsors. The work-permit exception in line 46 includes both marriage and authorized residence. Lines 50–54 preserve the authorization/exemption requirement and the separate migrant-worker procedure. The degree/Mandarin conditions in lines 58–66 match the official passages opened; the student hours limit is expressly not a substitute for permission.

The manuscript does not assert a universal right to convert visa-free entry. Its professional paragraphs are expressly limited to persons satisfying Article 16, and its student route has separate entry restrictions. It makes no claim that every dependent ARC confers work permission. Adding every niche entry scheme or every dependent work-permit path is unnecessary for this bounded article.

### 706 — PASS

The article separates expiry, a move, a change/loss of residence purpose, a new employer, document replacement and travel. The table is useful because its rows compare different legal triggers. It is not a decorative summary of interchangeable deadlines.

Lines 30–32 preserve the ordinary three-month window, the Gold Card four-month window, the entrepreneur business-based four-month window, and the distinct three-month route after that business has ceased. Lines 46–50 correctly distinguish the day after a domicile/workplace change from the event day for the qualifying residence-purpose change. The separate 15-day BOCA path remains scoped to the regulation's category. Lines 56–60 do not turn a NIA data update into work permission or make every divorce/employment dispute a continued-residence exception.

The passport/loss discussion at lines 64–68 avoids an unverified universal 30-day passport-update deadline and a universal NT$500 data-change charge. Lines 72–76 preserve re-entry permission, the APRC exception, the particular pending-application departure rule and the absence of an automatic grace period. No blanket six-month passport-validity condition for valid ARC/APRC re-entry has been added. The last substantive paragraph names the four dates needed for the travel question.
