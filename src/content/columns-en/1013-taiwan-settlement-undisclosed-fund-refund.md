# Paid NT$150,000 by the driver, then signed a 'no compensation received' form: the rider was ordered to repay Taiwan's fund

A long title headed the form: a declaration of not having received compensation from the person liable for the damage. The rider had signed the declaration personally. On November 13, 2013, the Taipei District Court's small-claims division ordered the rider who signed it to return the NT$33,256 the Special Compensation Fund had paid out ([Taipei District Court small-claims civil judgment 102 Bei-Xiao No. 2308](https://www.mvacf.org.tw/ckfinder/connector?command=Proxy&type=Attach&currentFolder=/tblResearches/&fileName=%E8%87%BA%E7%81%A3%E8%87%BA%E5%8C%97%E5%9C%B0%E6%96%B9%E6%B3%95%E9%99%A2%E6%B0%91%E4%BA%8B%E5%88%A4%E6%B1%BA102%E5%B9%B4%E5%BA%A6%E5%8C%97%E5%B0%8F%E5%AD%97%E7%AC%AC2308%E8%99%9F.pdf&c=7&p=9399565cd46041db913333d3573d04bc&l=3)).

It is a first-instance small-claims judgment. An appeal was possible only on the ground of a breach of law, within 20 days of service, and whether anyone appealed cannot be confirmed from the public copy.

## From the crash to the court

The accident, the mediation and the Fund payment were undisputed. At about 2:35 a.m. on August 10, 2012, the rider's motorcycle collided with a car at an alley junction in Zhongshan District, Taipei. The rider suffered cuts to the face and neck and grazes to the left knee. The car had no compulsory insurance.

On February 5, 2013, the rider and the car's driver settled for NT$150,000 before the Wanhua District mediation committee. The mediation record stated that the sum did not include compulsory automobile liability insurance. When the Fund processed the claim in May, the rider submitted the declaration of no compensation received; the Fund paid NT$33,256 on May 15. It said it learned of the settlement only afterwards.

## What the rider told the court

In defence, the rider gave several reasons. The hospital had estimated further treatment at about NT$600,000, so a higher figure had been sought; the other side promised the payment would exclude compulsory insurance; and the six-month window for a criminal complaint was about to close, so the rider settled. The rider accepted signing the declaration but said an insurance company had called it a required step, and that the rider's mother had passed it over to sign. The Fund, the rider argued, should sue the driver rather than the victim; the scars were still there, and there was no unjust enrichment.

## The two rules the court applied

[Article 43 of the Compulsory Automobile Liability Insurance Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0390060&flno=43) does two things. Paragraph 1 says a settlement, waiver or other agreement between the claimant and the liable party that obstructs the Fund's subrogation does not bind the Fund if the Fund did not agree to it. Paragraph 2 adds that money the claimant received from the liable party must be deducted when the Fund pays, and if it was not deducted, the Fund may claim it back up to that amount.

First, the court held that the "excludes compulsory insurance" wording, made without the Fund's agreement, did not bind the Fund. It then described what the Fund is for: a supplementary liability that the law places alongside the liable party, for cases where the vehicle's insurer need not or cannot pay. The NT$150,000 should therefore have been deducted, and the NT$33,256 paid without that deduction had to be returned, with 5 percent interest from October 11, 2013, the day after the complaint was served.

One more sentence closed the reasoning. If the rider had in fact suffered losses beyond what had been paid, those could be pursued separately through the proper procedure.

## What the settlement and the form each do

Why did a clause written into the mediation record count for nothing against the Fund? When the vehicle at fault is uninsured, the Fund compensates the victim and then steps into the victim's claim against the liable party ([Article 40(1)(ii)](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0390060&flno=40), [Article 42(2)](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0390060&flno=42)). Words agreed between the two people in the crash cannot settle the Fund's share for it.

For the same reason, the declaration matters. It can look like paperwork, yet the Fund relies on it to decide what to deduct. Anyone negotiating with an uninsured driver in Taiwan may find it simpler later to keep a dated list of what has been paid and what is still owed, and to fill in the Fund's forms to match.

In all, NT$33,256 was ordered back, about 22 percent of the NT$150,000 the rider had received from the driver.
