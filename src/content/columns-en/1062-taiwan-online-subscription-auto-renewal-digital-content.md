# A free trial became a paid subscription. What can you cancel in Taiwan?

A free trial can end in a paid charge without the consumer noticing the conversion date. Start with the checkout record. The trial end date, paid price, billing cycle, renewal term and named seller belong in one timeline.

A card statement shows who processed payment. It does not answer every legal question. The remedy depends on whether the consumer seeks to rescind the initial contract within seven days, end the subscription at the current period's end, or stop the next renewal. Those requests can produce different refund and access outcomes.

## Trial conversion begins with the checkout terms

[Article 13 of the Consumer Protection Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0170001&flno=13) requires a trader to disclose standard terms expressly. [Article 11-1](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0170001&flno=11-1) also requires a reasonable opportunity to review all clauses before contracting. Under [Article 11](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0170001&flno=11), the terms must follow equality and reciprocity, and ambiguity is interpreted in the consumer's favor.

These provisions matter when a free trial converts into a recurring paid plan. A prechecked renewal box is not a conclusive answer about disclosure, assent or fairness. [Article 12](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0170001&flno=12) separately makes a standard term void if it violates good faith and is manifestly unfair to the consumer.

Where the [current online-retail standard terms](https://www.ey.gov.tw/File/2D44FB65E5FF31C9) apply, point 5 requires a precontract confirmation mechanism for the type, quantity, price and other important matters. Point 3 makes the product information displayed on the transaction page part of the contract. Their prohibited point 5 also bars terms making the consumer waive or restrict a statutory right to rescind or terminate. [Article 17](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0170001&flno=17) supplies the framework for those mandatory and prohibitory provisions.

## Renewal notices and the January draft need separate treatment

[Article 18](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0170001&flno=18) requires clear written information for distance contracts, including the seller's contact details, service content and price, payment dates and methods, cancellation period and procedure, any exclusion of the seven-day right, and complaint handling. Internet traders must provide that information in an electronic form that consumers can fully access and store.

That is not a current, renewal-specific duty to send a separate reminder before every charge. The [January 16, 2026 draft](https://gazette.nat.gov.tw/EG_FileManager/eguploadpub/eg032011/ch05/type3/gov87/num14/Eg.pdf) would add a prohibited term covering preset consumer consent to automatic-renewal charges and the absence of separate notice before charging.

The ministry's [October 1 legislative plan](https://www-api.moda.gov.tw/File/Get/moda/zh-tw/hbcX1L2Jx47pU1D) lists December 2026 for publication. As of November 3, however, this remains a draft. A planned publication month is not an effective date, so the proposal should not be cited as an existing prohibition.

## Digital content is not a blanket seven-day exception

For a qualifying distance transaction, [Article 19](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0170001&flno=19) allows rescission within seven days after receiving goods or services, without giving a reason or bearing an expense or consideration. Reasonable exceptions exist. A clause contrary to Article 19 is void.

Digital content not supplied on a tangible medium and online services completed as soon as they are provided fall within Article 2(5) of the [Regulations on Reasonable Matters as Exceptions to Rescind the Distance Sales](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0170012&flno=2). That category alone does not exclude rescission. The consumer must have consented in advance to provision, and the trader must have informed the consumer that the seven-day right would be excluded. These are cumulative conditions.

Recurring access does not by itself prove that either service category applies or that the required consent and disclosure occurred. The actual service and checkout evidence matter. [Article 3 of the regulations](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0170012&flno=3) also directs transactions covered by announced standard terms to the cancellation rules in those terms.

If the trader did not provide the cancellation information required by Article 18 when the consumer received the service, Article 19(3) changes the seven-day starting point. The period then starts on the day after the information is supplied, subject to the statutory four-month limit measured from the original starting point.

## Rescission, period-end termination and stopping renewal

Do you want the first payment returned, or only no further charges? The notice should answer that question clearly.

Initial rescission uses the Article 19 route where that right applies. Written notice issued within the applicable period is sufficient under Article 19(4). For a service contract, [Article 19-2](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0170001&flno=19-2) requires the trader to refund the consideration paid within 15 days from the day after receiving the rescission notice.

Termination at the end of an already paid period is a later contractual step. It does not automatically rescind the original transaction or trigger Article 19-2. Any prorated refund needs a contractual or other legal basis; neither later termination nor nonuse guarantees one.

Stopping only the next renewal addresses a future charge. Access may continue through the paid period if the contract provides for that result, but the earlier payment remains a separate issue. Short notices help. State the account, the charge, the requested effective date and which of these three outcomes is sought.

## The seller and payment processor are not interchangeable

The order confirmation, receipt and contract identify the contracting seller. Use the stated cancellation route and ask for written confirmation of the effective date and refund treatment. Save the checkout screen, any digital-content consent and exception notice, renewal messages, the cancellation timestamp and the seller's response.

Payment processors and card networks may appear on the statement without being the seller of the subscription. A card dispute or chargeback request follows the issuer and card-scheme process. It does not replace notice to the seller, establish initial rescission by itself, or guarantee a refund.

For the specific charge, keep the seller’s written confirmation of the effective date and refund treatment with the checkout and cancellation records.
