# A Taiwan interview form asks about family and past pay. How much is necessary?

Marital status, relatives' employers and job titles, previous pay, and two former supervisors' contact details appeared on a pre-interview form described in [Next Apple's November 2 report](https://news.nextapple.com/life/20261102/82ABCF47CEBBAD4811DB417D90043AC5). An applicant had posted objections online to being asked for the information before even meeting the employer.

Some commenters said optional fields could be left blank; the applicant worried that doing so would cost her the interview. The report did not identify a final regulatory decision.

## The question is why this job needs the information

For a US HR team using a global application form in Taiwan, a field's place in the template does not explain its local purpose. Each requested item needs a connection to the recruitment decision. A relative's job title and an applicant's work experience do not answer the same question.

[Employment Service Act Article 5, paragraph 2, subparagraph 2](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0090001&flno=5) prohibits employers from demanding private information that is not necessary for employment. It covers recruitment as well as hiring.

The scope is elaborated in [Article 1-1 of the Enforcement Rules of Employment Service Law](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0090006&flno=1-1). Its categories include physiological information, psychological information and information about personal life, with examples such as credit records, pregnancy plans and background checks. Employers must respect the person's interests, stay within what is necessary for a specific purpose such as economic needs or protection of the public interest, and show a proper, reasonable relationship to that purpose.

That requires a factual explanation. It does not establish a blanket prohibition on every question about prior pay or a work reference. For a particular role, the assessment turns on what is requested, why it is needed and how far the request extends. An employer collecting a whole family's employment details needs to account for that scope, not merely the fact that it is recruiting.

## Consent and an optional label

Ordinary personal data is also subject to [Personal Data Protection Act Article 19](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=I0050021&flno=19): a nongovernment collector needs a specific purpose and one of the listed bases. These include consent and a contractual or similar relationship supported by appropriate security measures. [Article 6's](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=I0050021&flno=6) specially protected data follows separate rules.

Consent does not make every field necessary. Under [Article 5](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=I0050021&flno=5), collection, processing and use must respect the person's interests, proceed in good faith, stay within the necessary scope of the specific purpose and bear a legitimate, reasonable relationship to it. A signed form does not replace that assessment.

Where [Article 8](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=I0050021&flno=8) requires notice, the employer must explain its identity, collection purpose, data categories, period and territory of use, recipients and methods, and the person's rights and how to exercise them. The article contains exceptions, including where the person already knows the required information. If providing the information is optional, the notice must also describe how withholding it will affect the person's rights and interests.

What happens to an application with the field blank? The answer reveals more than an “optional” label alone. It can also be compared with what the privacy notice actually promised the applicant.

## A complaint still needs the recruitment record

A violation of the employment-privacy provision carries an administrative fine of NT$60,000 to NT$300,000 under [Employment Service Act Article 67](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0090001&flno=67). Applying that provision to the reported form would require the facts behind the request. Online criticism alone does not establish them.

For an English-speaking applicant or a Taiwan hiring team, the useful record is the original form, the job description, the privacy notice and any explanation of why a field was needed. Those documents show whether the employer requested information for this vacancy, and what it told the applicant would happen if the answer was left blank.
