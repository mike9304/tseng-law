# Home-safety help in Taiwan: when the service is regulated therapy

Who will actually assess a relative's mobility when a Taiwan service advertises home-safety planning? [Focus Taiwan reported on November 22](https://focustaiwan.tw/society/202611220009) that the health minister had warned about people using titles such as home safety planner or exercise therapist. His concern was unqualified people performing services reserved to licensed medical professionals.

For an English-speaking family arranging support from overseas, a familiar-sounding job title may leave the proposed work unclear. The report says the titles have recently appeared on social media, with people promoting services that assess older people's homes, identify fall risks and recommend improvements. It does not establish that every home-safety service is unlawful.

## What the therapy statutes cover

[Article 12 of the Occupational Therapists Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=L0020039&flno=12) includes occupational-therapy assessments and the design and manufacture of splints and functional aids, and training in their use. Physical therapy has its own statutory scope. [Article 12 of the Physical Therapists Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=L0020044&flno=12) includes assessments, treatment planning, therapeutic exercise, and training in the use of walking aids and other specified devices.

Those provisions give a family concrete services to compare with an advertisement or proposed appointment. If a provider promises a therapy assessment or therapeutic exercise, a family can reasonably ask exactly what will be done and who will do it. The wording of an advertisement is relevant evidence of what is offered, but it cannot by itself establish what happened during a visit or whether an offense occurred.

Criminal consequences are possible. [Article 41 of the Occupational Therapists Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=L0020039&flno=41) and [Article 32 of the Physical Therapists Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=L0020044&flno=32) penalize performing the respective therapy without the legally required qualifications. Both preserve an exception for students in the respective therapy programs, and graduates within six months of receiving their graduation certificate, interning under the direction of a therapist in that profession.

## The physician-order exception has a limited purpose

Both Article 12 provisions generally require therapists to work from a physician's diagnosis, referral or orders. They expressly exempt work whose purpose is not disease treatment. A physician's order is therefore not required in every instance of qualified professional practice.

That exception leaves the qualification rules intact. It also does not eliminate practice registration: [Article 7 of the Occupational Therapists Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=L0020039&flno=7) and [Article 7 of the Physical Therapists Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=L0020044&flno=7) require registration with the authority responsible for the place of practice and issuance of a practice license before practice begins.

A provider who says no physician referral is needed has answered only part of the question. The family still needs to know whether that person holds the Taiwan qualification and practice license for the work.

Place-of-practice limits also remain. Under Article 9 of the [Occupational Therapists Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=L0020039&flno=9) and [Physical Therapists Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=L0020044&flno=9), practice is generally limited to one authorized medical institution, therapy clinic or other recognized institution. There are exceptions for support between institutions or prior approval. A home visit has to fit within those limits too.

## An overseas qualification does not settle Taiwan authorization

The same caution applies when a provider advertises credentials from the United States or another country. Foreign nationals may sit Taiwan's therapist examinations under [Article 58-1 of the Occupational Therapists Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=L0020039&flno=58-1) and [Article 58-2 of the Physical Therapists Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=L0020044&flno=58-2). Those who pass and hold the Taiwan certificate still need permission from the central competent authority to practice. They must also follow Taiwan's relevant laws and the charter of the relevant therapists' association.

For a proposed home visit, the useful details are the assessment or training promised, the person who will perform it, and that person's Taiwan authorization. The minister urged the public to check providers' professional licenses before using their services. Focus Taiwan notes that, under regulations, licensed medical professionals must display their credentials while providing services.
