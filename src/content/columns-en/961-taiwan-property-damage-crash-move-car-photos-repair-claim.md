# Photograph, Then Move: What Taiwan's Fender-Bender Rule Means for Your Repair Claim

"In incidents involving only property damage, drivers should photograph the scene and move their vehicles to a safe location before reporting the incident, rather than waiting for police to arrive." That was the advice of Wu Tung-ling (吳東凌), director-general of the transport ministry's Department of Railways, Highways and Road Safety, as reported by the [Taipei Times](https://www.taipeitimes.com/News/taiwan/archives/2026/10/28/2003866752). He was worried about secondary collisions on highways, with six reported in August.

It is safety advice. It is also the law.

## What the rule requires

Taiwan's [Road Traffic Accident Handling Regulations (道路交通事故處理辦法), Article 3](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0080090&flno=3)(1)(4), generally forbids moving crash vehicles and marks on the road. Where nobody is hurt or killed and the vehicles can still be driven, drivers must first mark the vehicles' positions and the evidence at the scene, then move the vehicles to a place where they do not obstruct traffic. For these crashes, Article 3(2) allows the marking to be done by photo or video.

On a traffic lane or shoulder, a warning triangle or other visible device goes behind the crash and comes away once the scene is cleared (Article 3(1)(1)). [Article 4](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0080090&flno=4) sets the distances: 100 meters on a national freeway; 80 meters on an expressway or where the speed limit exceeds 60 km/h; 50 meters where the limit is above 50 and up to 60 km/h; 30 meters at 50 km/h or below; and 5 meters in congestion or below 10 km/h. Rain or fog calls for more.

## Photos that stand in for chalk marks

Once the cars are at the curb, the photos are the scene. The Taoyuan District Court had a parking-lot scrape in front of it that was not repaired until 53 days later (114 Hsiao-Shang No. 37, small-claims appeal decided September 30, 2025, final).

Photos sent by messaging app on the evening of the crash showed scratches and a dent on the right front bumper and fender, where both drivers said the cars had touched. With no sign of another accident in between, the delay alone did not break the link. Two items billed for the left front fender did not match the point of impact and were cut, and the NT$52,089 claim became an NT$33,887 award.

## Fault, after the cars are gone

Taiwan's [Civil Code, Article 191-2](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=191-2), makes the driver of a motor vehicle liable for damage caused while it is in use, unless the driver shows that reasonable care was taken to prevent it. Breaking a traffic rule written to protect others raises a presumption of negligence under [Article 184](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=184)(2). When the injured party was also at fault, a court may reduce the award or deny it ([Article 217](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=217)(1)).

## Calling the police, or settling on the spot

Your insurer has a say before any roadside deal. [Article 93 of the Insurance Act (保險法)](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0390002&flno=93) lets an insurer provide that it is not bound by an admission, settlement or payment you make to the other side without its participation, unless you notified it and it refused to take part, or stalled, without good reason. Notice of the accident is due within five days of learning of it, unless the law or the policy says otherwise ([Article 58](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0390002&flno=58)).

The reporting rule itself allows a roadside deal. Drivers must normally report a crash and cooperate with the police, but not when no one is hurt or killed and the parties settle with each other on the spot (Handling Regulations, Article 3(1)(5)). Skipping the police has a cost in paper: no party registration slip at the scene, no scene diagram or photos to request after seven days, and no preliminary analysis report after thirty ([Article 13](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0080090&flno=13)). Our column on [when police accident records become available](/en/columns/taiwan-accident-police-records) covers how to request them. If the police did come but you could not give a statement at the scene, you contact the handling unit within seven days to complete the record ([Article 10](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0080090&flno=10)(3)).

## Who pays for the repairs

Not the compulsory policy. The [Compulsory Automobile Liability Insurance Act (強制汽車責任保險法)](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0390060&flno=13) defines a covered accident as one in which passengers or people outside the vehicle are injured or killed (Article 13). Repairs come from the driver at fault, from that driver's voluntary liability policy, or from your own collision cover.

The amount is set by [Article 196 of the Civil Code](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=196): the decrease in value caused by the damage, which courts usually measure by necessary repair costs. New parts replacing old ones are depreciated. In a 2025 Keelung small-claims judgment (114 Chi-Hsiao No. 952, first instance, June 18, 2025), a collision insurer had paid NT$51,894 and sued the motorcyclist at fault. Parts of NT$34,934 on a car almost five years old were written down to NT$3,494; labor was not. The court awarded the insurer NT$20,454, because an insurer subrogated under [Article 53 of the Insurance Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0390002&flno=53) can claim no more than its insured could have. The motorcyclist's appeal was dismissed as procedurally defective on September 23, 2025 (114 Hsiao-Shang No. 27), with no further challenge allowed.

If the other driver is insured, [Article 94](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0390002&flno=94) of the Insurance Act bars the liability insurer from paying its own policyholder before the injured party is compensated, and lets the injured party claim directly from the insurer, within the policy limit, once liability is established. Before that point, the insurer may pay the injured party directly on its policyholder's notice ([Article 95](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0390002&flno=95)).

## Small claims and the two-year clock

Both cases above were small-claims matters. That procedure covers money claims of NT$100,000 or less, and parties can agree in writing to use it up to NT$500,000 ([Code of Civil Procedure, Article 436-8](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=436-8)). The claim itself expires two years after you know of the damage and who is liable, or ten years after the crash ([Civil Code, Article 197](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=197)(1)). Appealing a first-instance small-claims judgment from overseas has its own limits, set out in [our column on small-claims appeals from abroad](/en/columns/taiwan-small-claims-appeal-from-abroad-deadline-violation-of-law).

A Taiwan lawyer can request the police scene diagram and preliminary analysis report for you, read what you signed or texted at the roadside to see what it commits you to, and send a repair demand to the other driver or that driver's insurer before filing a small-claims case. If an insurer sues you after paying for the other car, the lawyer can check whether replaced parts were depreciated, as the Keelung court required, and whether the bill matches the damage.
