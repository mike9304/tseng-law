# Taiwan's 6G trials: network approval and the limits on user fees

US testing company VIAVI is among the international participants in Taiwan's newly activated 6G-FORMOSA network, [CNA reported on November 20](https://www.cna.com.tw/news/afe/202611200279.aspx). The report describes cooperation with overseas research networks and satellite operators, including a drone-detection test on a 6G base station and a phone sending rescue messages directly by satellite.

Who will establish and manage the network for an overseas supplier's proposed trial? A supplier delivering equipment to an existing operator occupies a different position from a company setting up its own network. That distinction determines which party's approvals and testing plan need to match the work.

## Permission to build and permission to use

[Article 50 of the Telecommunications Management Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=K0060111&flno=50) requires approval before establishing a dedicated telecommunications network, including subsequent additions or changes. For this provision, a dedicated network uses radio frequencies allocated by the competent authority for the operator's own use. Foreign persons may establish such a network with special approval.

Research networks have a further sequence. Under [Article 3 of the experimental-network regulations](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=K0060144&flno=3), both technical and commercial experimental networks require approval before establishment. Use begins only after completion and issuance of a network inspection certificate. Their radio stations also require establishment approval, followed by inspection and a station license. These are separate steps.

CNA's launch report names the partners but not who holds these documents or what they cover, so it cannot establish the approval category or conditions for a particular supplier's proposed work.

## A technical trial has a charging boundary

[Article 4](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=K0060144&flno=4) includes developing or testing network technology, equipment and services among technical purposes, while commercial experiments test whether a service can be used commercially and are limited to services telecommunications enterprises do not already provide. A commercial network's purposes may also include the technical ones.

Specially approved research networks may serve others and collect fees under Article 50, but [Article 5 of the regulations](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=K0060144&flno=5) expressly prohibits a technical experimental network's manager from charging users for its telecommunications services. An agreed equipment deposit is possible. It cannot exceed the cost of the equipment lent to the user.

Both network categories must remain within their establishment purpose, and managers must prohibit users from granting third parties use of the network's services, including equipment. A contract offering customer access therefore raises a different issue from an equipment supply agreement. Article 5's service-fee restriction does not itself classify every hardware invoice or development payment.

## What the network plan has to show

For a technical network, [Article 9](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=K0060144&flno=9) requires the establishment plan to identify its purpose, geographic area, experiments, operating period and user arrangements, alongside the architecture, frequencies, bandwidth, power, information security and response to interference. Where the applicant does not build the network itself, the plan needs the provider's network-use authorization.

Those details give an international project team something concrete to compare with its agreement: the proposed equipment, test location, users and access arrangements. Pushing a station's frequency, power or bandwidth past the approved values, or moving it outside the approved area, requires a reviewed and approved plan change under [Articles 31](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=K0060144&flno=31) and [32](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=K0060144&flno=32). A change limited to the communication method or system architecture may instead be reported to the authority for the record. That filing needs reasons, a revised plan and a comparison of the changes.
