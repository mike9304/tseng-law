# An offshore exchange enters insolvency: what a Taiwan resident can still claim in Taiwan

An offshore insolvency notice may require a customer in Taipei to file a claim abroad while a search for Taiwan assets is still under way. The foreign procedure determines whether a filing is needed, where it goes and when it is due.

For a US citizen living in Taiwan, the notice also raises an identity question: is the company in the foreign proceeding the company that promised to return the account assets? A familiar exchange brand is not enough to answer it.

## The claim described in the foreign filing

Account terms, dated transaction exports and withdrawal-rejection messages connect the balance to a company and a promised service. Custody of specific assets, a lending product and a cash balance may support different ownership or payment claims. The applicable terms include the versions accepted when those transactions occurred, not just the page now displayed on the website. A change of registered address after moving to Taiwan may also make the account’s contract history relevant.

[Article 20 of the Act Governing the Choice of Law in Civil Matters Involving Foreign Elements (涉外民事法律適用法)](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000007&flno=20) generally gives effect to the parties’ chosen law for contractual formation and effect. If there is no choice, or the choice is invalid under that law, the most closely connected law applies. The statute also presumes a connection to the domicile, at the time of the act, of the party owing the characteristic obligation. A Taiwan court can therefore have to apply foreign contract law.

Copies of the foreign insolvency notice and any claim submission belong with those account records. They identify the procedure in which a distribution is sought. Whether that submission preserves a particular right, and what further steps it requires, depend on the foreign procedure and law; an unanswered support ticket does not resolve those questions.

## Finding property in Taiwan

Who owns it? [Bankruptcy Act Article 4](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010006&flno=4) states that a composition concluded or bankruptcy declared abroad has no effect on the debtor’s or bankrupt’s assets in Taiwan. Other foreign restructuring or administration procedures must first be classified to determine whether they fall within that rule. Article 4 gives a customer neither ownership nor priority over a Taiwan asset.

Wallet addresses and transaction hashes can help trace movements. They do not alone establish the owner, the contractual debtor or the legal location of the property. A local office bearing the exchange’s brand likewise leaves the company’s identity to be established. These distinctions affect which assets a repayment claim could reach.

Taiwan connections such as a corporate office or attachable property are examined using the domestic rules in [Code of Civil Procedure Article 2](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=2) and [Article 3](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=3). International jurisdiction still needs separate assessment. An asset lead does not settle jurisdiction.

Court-selection terms also enter that assessment. [Article 24](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=24) permits a documented agreement on first-instance jurisdiction for disputes from a specified legal relationship. [Article 28(2)](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=28) provides a pre-merits transfer mechanism for manifestly unfair standard jurisdiction terms prepared by a corporation or merchant. It excludes cases where both parties are corporations or merchants. This domestic mechanism does not itself invalidate every exclusive foreign-court agreement.

## A repayment lawsuit can be stopped for arbitration

Even with a Taiwan asset identified, the customer may face an arbitration objection. Under [Article 4 of Taiwan’s Arbitration Law (仲裁法)](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=I0020001&flno=4), where a valid agreement covers the dispute, the court must on the other party’s application stay the lawsuit and order the claimant to commence arbitration within a specified period. A defendant that has already argued the merits orally falls within the statutory exception. Missing that arbitration deadline requires dismissal.

Ending the account contract does not automatically remove this obstacle. [Article 3](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=I0020001&flno=3) treats the arbitration clause’s validity independently of the main contract’s non-formation, invalidity, annulment, rescission or termination. The repayment demand and the agreement to arbitrate therefore need their own legal analysis.

An unfair-term challenge depends on the applicable law and the particular clause. Where Taiwan law applies, [Civil Code Article 247-1](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=247-1) addresses manifestly unfair standard terms. A qualifying consumer relationship also raises [Consumer Protection Act Article 12](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0170001&flno=12), with its good-faith and fairness test. Investment use does not establish consumer status automatically.

## The award file for a Taiwan enforcement application

Winning abroad leaves another Taiwan proceeding. Recognition under [Arbitration Law Article 47](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=I0020001&flno=47) gives a foreign award the effect of a final judgment between the parties and allows it to serve as an enforcement title. The company ordered to pay still has to be connected to the property targeted in Taiwan.

A recognition application brings its own notice and deadline. Under [Article 50](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=I0020001&flno=50), the other party has twenty days after receiving notice to request refusal on the listed grounds. That period governs a response to the Taiwan application; the overseas insolvency claim has the deadline set by its own procedure.

Preparing for that response involves more than translating the award. The arbitration agreement and the law governing its validity bear on an invalid-agreement objection. Records of arbitrator appointment and procedural notices address proper notice and due process. Whether the award is already binding, or has been set aside or suspended by a competent authority, is another statutory issue. Keeping the procedural correspondence allows those questions to be answered from the file.

The payment ordered also has to fit the dispute submitted to arbitration. Article 50 addresses an award unrelated to the agreed dispute or beyond the agreement’s scope. If the excess can be removed and the remainder can stand, that remainder is excluded from refusal on this scope ground. Other statutory grounds remain available. This limit affects how much of the award can survive that particular objection when enforcement against Taiwan property is sought.

For an American account holder working with an English-language arbitration file, [Article 48](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=I0020001&flno=48) sets the documents accompanying the recognition application:

| Part of the arbitration file | Form required for the Taiwan application |
|---|---|
| Award | Original or authenticated copy |
| Arbitration agreement | Original or authenticated copy |
| Applicable foreign arbitration law, or institutional or international arbitration rules | Full text |

Foreign-language documents require Chinese translations. Article 48 limits authentication of the award and agreement to Taiwan’s overseas embassies, consulates, representative offices, other offices, or other government-authorized institutions. An English file alone is incomplete.

Recognition also depends on the limits in [Article 49](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=I0020001&flno=49). A Taiwan court must refuse where recognition or enforcement would violate Taiwan public policy, or where Taiwan law does not permit arbitration of the dispute. It may refuse if the award’s country or the country whose arbitration law was applied does not recognize Taiwan awards. Refusal is discretionary. The relevant country comes from the arbitration file, rather than the customer’s US citizenship.

## What a warning list or criminal complaint establishes

[BlockTempo reported on August 11, 2026](https://www.blocktempo.com/binance-taiwan-vasp-association-warning-list-aml-registration/) that Taiwan’s VASP industry association (中華民國虛擬通貨商業同業公會) had added Binance to its warning list for incomplete AML registration on August 10. The warning does not identify a repayment fund, determine jurisdiction or prove a customer’s entitlement. The [domestic exchange dispute column](/en/columns/taiwan-crypto-exchange-vasp-dispute) covers registration and the new statute; the [account-control column](/en/columns/taiwan-crypto-exchange-account-fraud-control-warning-foreign-residents) addresses fraud-related restrictions and warning designations on Taiwan accounts.

As of October 30, 2026, the official materials do not confirm a commencement date for the [Virtual Asset Service Act](https://law.fsc.gov.tw/LawContent.aspx?id=GL004301).

Evidence of deception inducing delivery of property may support a complaint under [Criminal Code Article 339](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0000001&flno=339). [Article 342](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0000001&flno=342) concerns someone handling another’s affairs who breaches that duty with the specified unlawful-benefit or harmful intent and causes financial harm. A hack or inability to pay does not alone establish those elements. A criminal complaint does not commence the agreed arbitration.
