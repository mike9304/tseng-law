# Borrowing to invest in Taiwan? Banks and brokers can now see each other's loan data, if you consent

Until last week, a Taiwan bank reviewing a loan could not see how much the borrower owed on a margin account. A broker checking a client's bank credit saw little more than whether a bad record existed. The bank-side check began on October 31. Brokers followed on November 2.

[Eastern Broadcasting reported on October 30](https://news.ebc.net.tw/news/business/578186) that the Financial Supervisory Commission had launched a cross-industry credit inquiry between banks and securities firms. The stated aim is to curb stacked leverage from mortgages, personal loans, car loans and share-pledge borrowing, a pattern Taiwanese media call "four loans under one roof." November 2 was the brokers' first business day under the new rules.

Foreign residents with Taiwan salaries borrow to invest too. What exactly is shared, and what happens if you say no?

## Two directions, two triggers

The bank side has no threshold. [Storm Media reported](https://www.storm.mg/lifestyle/11177171), quoting a deputy director-general of the Commission's Banking Bureau, that a bank making a new personal loan whose purpose is investment or wealth management must query the borrower's records at securities firms. The categories are margin purchases and short sales, securities business lending, securities borrowing and lending, and unrestricted-purpose loans.

The broker side is narrower. A broker must check an individual's bank borrowing when it newly grants or increases that client's credit line to NT$300 million or 1 percent of the broker's net worth, whichever is higher, according to the same report. Existing credit lines that are not increased are outside the check, EBC reported. Brokers then see real estate, movable property, business investment and working capital loans.

Data move through the Joint Credit Information Center and the Taiwan Stock Exchange. The two bodies amended their data-exchange rules in an [October 15 exchange announcement (Tai-Zheng-Jiao No. 1150206064)](https://www.rootlaw.com.tw/LetterContent.aspx?LCode=31&SOID=258648), based on two Commission letters of October 6. It widened the exchange from negative records to loan-level credit data, starting October 31. Brokers have until March 31, 2027 to finish their system changes, and may use manual or alternative procedures until then. Each query record must be kept for five years. A [September 22 CNA report carried by the Economic Daily News](https://money.udn.com/money/story/5612/9771284) said the earlier system showed a broker only whether a negative record existed, without amounts or the lending banks' names.

## Consent comes first

Both sides must explain the scope of the query and get the customer's consent. A customer who refuses cannot be queried, and the loan or credit line will not be granted, officials told reporters.

That matches the general rule for private institutions. Under [Article 19 of the Personal Data Protection Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=I0050021&flno=19), a non-government body collecting or processing personal data needs a specific purpose and one of the listed grounds, consent among them. Before signing, read the clause that describes the query's scope and the consent requested.

Query results are one input, not the decision. The Banking Bureau official said banks must still assess borrowers under their usual credit principles, covering purpose, repayment ability and credit standing.

## Seeing your own record

Can you check what the bank saw? [Article 3](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=I0050021&flno=3) lists rights a person cannot waive in advance, including asking for access and for copies. [Article 10](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=I0050021&flno=10) requires an institution to answer such requests, with exceptions such as harm to the significant interests of the collecting institution or a third party.

For a foreign resident, timing matters. Broker data in the system are daily; bank data reaching brokers are generally as of the previous month-end, the reports said. A margin balance you paid down yesterday may already be current on the broker side, while a bank loan repaid this month may still show on the bank side.
