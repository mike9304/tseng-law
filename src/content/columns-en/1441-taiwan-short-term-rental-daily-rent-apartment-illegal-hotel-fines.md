# Can I rent my spare room in Taiwan to travelers by the night?

Taiwan’s Act for the Development of Tourism (發展觀光條例, the Tourism Act) requires lodging registration if you offer the public nightly or weekly stays in your spare room for profit. The registration rules cover [hotels, Article 24(1)](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=K0110001&flno=24) and [homestays, Article 25(2)](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=K0110001&flno=25), whether you own the apartment or rent it. Under [Article 55-1](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=K0110001&flno=55-1), advertising without a registration certificate can cost you NT$100,000 before anyone books.

What if you rent the room only between long-term tenants? [Article 2, item 8 of the Tourism Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=K0110001&flno=2) defines a hotel business (旅館業) as a for-profit operation that provides the public with a place to stay or rest by the day or week for a fee. The name you give the arrangement makes no difference. In a [2010 order](https://gazette.nat.gov.tw/EG_FileManager/eguploadpub/eg016251/ch06/type2/gov50/num19/Eg.htm), Taiwan’s Ministry of Transportation and Communications (交通部) confirmed that such stays count as hotel operations even when arranged as property rentals. The order excluded lawfully operated tourist hotels and homestays. In 2022, [Taipei’s Department of Information and Tourism](https://www.tpedoit.gov.taipei/News_Content.aspx?n=603755835E928BED&s=332223E05449CFF9) (臺北市政府觀光傳播局) said it could neither waive the fine for a first offense nor reduce it below the statutory minimum. That applied even to hosts who rented only occasionally or between leases.

## When can a host face separate fines for advertising and operating?

The higher penalties introduced in April 2025 are in force as of November 24, 2026. Publishing an unregistered lodging offer on social media or a booking site carries a fine of NT$100,000 to NT$1.5 million. The authority must also set a deadline for removal. If the listing stays up past that deadline, the authority may fine the advertiser again. Under the hotel and homestay penalty schedules, the first fine is NT$100,000 and repeat fines may double up to the statutory ceiling. [Article 55-1](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=K0110001&flno=55-1), [hotel schedule](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawGetFile.ashx?FileId=0000404783&lan=C), [homestay schedule](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawGetFile.ashx?FileId=0000404786&lan=C) and [national guidance on the amendment](https://admin.taiwan.net.tw/News/NewsTravel?a=35&id=34340).

Operating without registration also brings a mandatory closure order (勒令歇業). For an unregistered hotel business, the fine ranges from NT$100,000 to NT$2 million. For an unregistered homestay (民宿), the range is NT$100,000 to NT$1 million. One or two guest rooms draw NT$100,000 in either category. For three to five rooms, the hotel fine is NT$300,000 and the homestay fine is NT$180,000. Where advertising and operating constitute distinct violations, the tourism penalty standards (發展觀光條例裁罰標準) require separate penalties. [Article 55](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=K0110001&flno=55), [penalty standards, Article 4](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=K0110026&flno=4), [Schedule 2](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawGetFile.ashx?FileId=0000404783&lan=C) and [Schedule 5](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawGetFile.ashx?FileId=0000404786&lan=C).

Continued operation after a closure order can bring further fines. For an unregistered hotel, the authority may double the amount for the rooms found on that occasion, up to NT$2 million. For either type of lodging, it may refer the case to other agencies to cut water or power, seal the premises or carry out compulsory demolition. The operator bears the cost. For serious violations covered by Article 55(3)–(7), the authority must publish the business’s name and address, the name of the person in charge or the operator, and details of the violation. Taipei’s policy took effect on January 1, 2026. The city cuts water and power if an operator stays open after one coercive fine (怠金) for disobeying the closure order. [Article 55](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=K0110001&flno=55), [hotel penalty schedule](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawGetFile.ashx?FileId=0000404783&lan=C) and [Taipei’s January 15 notice](https://www.tpedoit.gov.taipei/News_Content.aspx?n=603755835E928BED&sms=72544237BBE4C5F6&s=277E30E12346D085).

## Can an apartment qualify as a homestay?

A Taiwanese homestay is a family side business run from a home the operator uses or owns, where guests can experience local life, culture or the natural surroundings. Under the homestay regulations (民宿管理辦法), a single apartment in a multi-unit residential building cannot qualify on its own. The exception is for an entire building within a residential complex. The unit owners’ meeting must consent. The local authority may then approve the application on condition that it retains the power to revoke registration. [Tourism Act, Article 2](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=K0110001&flno=2) and [homestay regulations, Article 8](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=K0110012&flno=8).

You can open a homestay only on non-urban land, in a national park area, or in one of eight types of area within an urban plan. The site must also meet all applicable land-use requirements. The ordinary limits are eight guest rooms and 240 square meters of guest-room floor area in total. Certain areas qualify for limits of 15 rooms and 400 square meters, with a 300-square-meter cap for farmhouses there. Ordinarily, the person who uses the building must run the homestay personally and may operate only one. Before opening, the operator must obtain the registration certificate and official sign from the local authority. [Location](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=K0110012&flno=3), [room and floor-area limits](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=K0110012&flno=4), [operator requirements](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=K0110012&flno=8) and [homestay registration](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=K0110001&flno=25).

Hotel operators must register a company or business and obtain a local hotel registration certificate and official sign before opening. The hotel regulations (旅館業管理規則) require proof of approved building use and liability insurance. Applicants who do not own the land or building must document permission to use it. A hotel needs a fixed place of business with a reception area, guest rooms and bathrooms. It cannot share those premises with another lodging business. [Tourism Act, Article 24](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=K0110001&flno=24) and hotel regulations [Articles 4](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=K0110014&flno=4), [5](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=K0110014&flno=5) and [6](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=K0110014&flno=6).

## Where a residential lease fits in

Taiwan’s Rental Housing Market Development and Regulation Act (租賃住宅市場發展及管理條例) excludes leases shorter than 30 days. It also excludes leisure or travel rentals of any length. Signing a 30-day lease does not authorize a host to offer nightly or weekly stays to the public. [Rental housing law, Article 4](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0060125&flno=4) and [Tourism Act, Article 2](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=K0110001&flno=2).

What about renting the room to someone who will live there? The Civil Code (民法) generally requires the landlord’s consent to sublet. It allows a tenant to sublet part of a house unless the lease says otherwise. A landlord may terminate for a sublet that violates this rule. For a residential sublet covered by the rental housing law, the tenant needs the landlord’s written consent to sublet even one room. Without that consent the landlord may end the lease early by giving the tenant 30 days’ written notice with supporting evidence. The tenant cannot claim compensation for that termination. [Civil Code, Article 443](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=443), and rental housing law [Articles 3](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0060125&flno=3), [9](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0060125&flno=9) and [10](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0060125&flno=10).

If you plan to rent your room to travelers for the night, start with your lease. Taiwan’s model residential lease restricts the home to residential use and requires consent to let others use all or part of it. Check whether you signed those terms. Under Civil Code Article 438, a landlord may terminate if a tenant uses the property contrary to the agreement and continues after the landlord tells them to stop. A landlord’s written consent still leaves you responsible for lodging registration. [Model residential lease, clause 7](https://www.ey.gov.tw/File/5E3A0378DE78B213?A=C), [Civil Code, Article 438](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=438) and [hotel registration requirement](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=K0110001&flno=24).

## What the building committee can enforce

Owners and tenants must use their units in line with the building’s use permit and bylaws under the Condominium Administration Act (公寓大廈管理條例). Ask the management committee for both documents. By hosting short-stay guests, you may breach Article 15(1) if the bylaws prohibit that use or the permit designates the unit for an incompatible use. The manager or management committee must intervene. If the resident refuses to comply, management must report the matter to the local authority and demand that the original use be restored. To take effect, the building’s own procedure for dealing with breaches must appear in its bylaws. [Articles 3](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0070118&flno=3), [15](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0070118&flno=15) and [23](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0070118&flno=23).

[Article 49](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0070118&flno=49) requires the local authority to fine a resident NT$40,000 to NT$200,000 for an unauthorized change of use under Article 15(1). The authority may order a correction and impose further fines if the resident misses the deadline it sets.

Eviction requires a court proceeding. Grounds include a serious breach of law or bylaws, or a violation that continues or recurs after a fine under Article 49(1), items 1–4. If the resident fails to remedy the breach within three months of management’s demand, the owners’ meeting may authorize management to sue for eviction. Where the resident owns the unit, the meeting can authorize a suit to compel transfer of the unit and the owner’s share of the land. Management may apply for a judicial auction if the owner fails to complete the transfer and registration within three months after the judgment becomes final. [Article 22](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0070118&flno=22).

## What guests can check before booking and on arrival

Look for the registration number in the advertisement. Hotels and homestays must include it. Taiwan’s Tourism Administration (交通部觀光署) recommends checking the property in its Taiwanstay directory (臺灣旅宿網) before booking. At the property, look for the certificate and official sign. Hotels and homestays must carry liability insurance, and hotels must post an escape route map in each guest room. New Taipei City’s tourism department (新北市政府觀光旅遊局) warns that an unregistered apartment may lack public liability coverage and fail fire safety requirements. [Hotel advertising](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=K0110014&flno=18-1), [homestay advertising](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=K0110012&flno=31), [directory guidance](https://admin.taiwan.net.tw/News/NewsTravel?a=35&id=34340), [insurance](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=K0110001&flno=31), [escape routes](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=K0110014&flno=21) and [New Taipei City’s safety notice](https://www.ntpc.gov.tw/ch/home.jsp?id=28&dataserno=202506250024).
