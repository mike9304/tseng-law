## First-two-paragraph deletion test

The final opening paragraphs were read sentence by sentence, not inferred from the metric.

- 704 paragraph 1: the first sentence identifies the resident-visa entrant, the ARC obligation, 30 days and Article 22. Removing it loses the trigger, addressee and duty. The short second sentence supplies the next-day starting point; removing it makes the time calculation incomplete. The last sentence preserves the exception for a valid integrated title that already includes ARC functionality.
- 704 paragraph 2: the question introduces a different entry category, so the following “O pedido só é possível…” does not refer to every resident-visa entrant. The statutory-route restriction and NIA category sentence prevent the 30-day rule from being misread as a universal conversion entitlement. The link identifies the companion treatment of those routes. The unrelated language/intention admonition was removed.
- 705 paragraph 1: the question is about residence after visa-free entry, which the title does not ask. “Algumas categorias…” answers the conditional possibility; the BOCA sentence defines what visa-free entry itself permits and does not permit. Deleting any of those functions either leaves the question unanswered or obscures the separation between visit, residence and work.
- 705 paragraph 2: each sentence adds a distinct operational fact: Portugal's eligible-passport 90-day visit; six-month passport validity and onward/return ticket; and the document-specific treatment of Brazilian and other Portuguese-language passports. These are not a generic scene-setting introduction.
- 706 paragraph 1: the first sentence explains why a future printed ARC date can coexist with loss of residence authorization. The second supplies the Article 31 exceptions and their conditional nature. Both are needed for scope.
- 706 paragraph 2: the first sentence identifies the continued-ground condition and ordinary three-month renewal window. “O pedido deve entrar antes do termo” supplies the deadline. The last sentence preserves that the general rule does not grant an extra period after expiry. This replaces the old administrative preparation paragraph.
