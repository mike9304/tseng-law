# Ending a Taiwan Phone Contract Early, and Bills for a Number Opened in Your Name

Transferred home after a year in Taichung, an Australian engineer walks into his carrier's store; the clerk prints out what he owes for leaving a 30-month plan with 18 months to run (an invented example). The same week a collection notice arrives for a number he has never seen. The name and resident certificate number are his.

Two pieces of paper, two sets of rules: one is the price of ending a contract early. The other bills him under a contract he never made.

## What governs a phone plan

Taiwan has announced mandatory and prohibited terms for many consumer contracts, from laundries to gyms. Mobile service is not among them: the Executive Yuan's [published list](https://www.ey.gov.tw/opendata/BF42D887A7B414E2/192) held 88 sets of terms on the check date, none for mobile service.

Instead, the Telecommunications Management Act regulates the contracts directly. Under [art. 17](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=K0060111&flno=17)(1), designated carriers must draft standard-form service terms and obtain approval from the National Communications Commission (NCC) before using or changing them. The terms must cover rates and conditions, complaint channels and the competent court, among other items (art. 17(2)).

Discounts sit in a separate plan agreement (專案同意書): how much the handset was subsidized, the monthly fee discount, and the term. Advertised conditions count too. A business must keep its advertising truthful, and its obligations to consumers may not fall below what it advertised ([Consumer Protection Act art. 22](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0170001&flno=22)(1)). Keep the leaflet; trial or free services may be charged after they end only with the subscriber's consent (Far EasTone contract art. 6(3)).

## Repaying subsidies by days left

Take Far EasTone's [mobile broadband service contract](https://www.fetnet.net/content/dam/fetnet/user_resource/corp/documents/about-us/fetnetrule/FET%E8%A1%8C%E5%8B%95%E5%AF%AC%E9%A0%BB%E6%9C%8D%E5%8B%99%E5%A5%91%E7%B4%84.pdf), approved by the NCC on December 18, 2025 (Tong-Chuan-Ping-Tai-Jue No. 11400418260). Article 39(2) lists three amounts due on termination: unbilled usage, the device subsidy, and the fee subsidy. Both subsidies are counted by the day.

| Item | Contract formula |
|---|---|
| Device and other bundled goods subsidy | total subsidy × (days not yet reached ÷ contract days) |
| Fee subsidy | actual subsidy received × (days unused ÷ contract days) |

Take a NT$12,000 handset subsidy on a 730-day plan with 365 days left: the device part is NT$6,000. Discounts of NT$300 a month for a year add up to NT$3,600 received; half of that, NT$1,800, is added. Because received discounts pile up while the remaining share shrinks, the exit charge on a heavily discounted plan may fall more slowly than expected.

Other carriers' wording may differ.

So what does the engineer owe? That depends on the subsidy total and the discounts actually received. If the printout does not match the formula, ask the carrier in writing for its calculation. The final month is prorated at one-thirtieth of the monthly fee per day, but a plan ended within its first month is charged a full month (art. 17(1)).

## Can the charge be reduced as a penalty?

The contract calls the money subsidy repayment, not a penalty. [Civil Code art. 250](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=250) lets the parties agree that a debtor who fails to perform will pay a penalty, and [art. 252](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=252) lets a court cut an excessive penalty to a reasonable amount. Whether an exit charge qualifies may be disputed. Court reductions of penalties are covered in [our article on art. 252](/en/columns/taiwan-contract-penalty-clause-reduction-civil-code-252-2026-judgments).

Consumer law adds three points. A standard-form clause contrary to good faith and obviously unfair to the consumer is void ([art. 12](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0170001&flno=12)(1)). Before signing, the business must give a reasonable review period of up to 30 days; otherwise the clauses do not form part of the contract, though the consumer may choose to rely on them ([art. 11-1](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0170001&flno=11-1)(1), (3)). And ambiguous clauses are read in the consumer's favor ([art. 11](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0170001&flno=11)(2)). Service lost to network failure must be discounted ([Telecommunications Management Act art. 8](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=K0060111&flno=8)(3)); Far EasTone's art. 30 cuts at least 5% of the monthly fee for carrier-caused outages of two hours, continuous or accumulated over six months, up to the full fee or equivalent service at 72 hours.

## Leaving Taiwan before the plan ends

Far EasTone's contract has no exemption for moving abroad. Article 39(1) requires termination in writing at a store, with the subscriber's identity documents under art. 7 (for foreigners, a passport or valid Alien Resident Certificate) or an agent carrying a power of attorney and their own ID.

After you leave, a friend in Taiwan holding a power of attorney can do it. Until then the monthly fee runs. Seven days after a missed payment deadline the carrier may give notice of suspension; 90 days after it, following a demand, it may terminate (art. 21(2)). Unpaid fees survive, offset first against any deposit; that clause does not mention subsidy repayment, so check the plan agreement. By statute, a carrier must give a reasonable deadline before cutting service for unpaid telecom fees (Telecommunications Management Act art. 8(1)(iii)). Suspending the line is no shortcut: the monthly fee stays due for up to three months (contract art. 17(3)).

Money may also come back. Any deposit, after offsetting arrears, is refunded within ten days of the next billing date, or 45 days if international roaming was used (art. 18(1)); overpayments come back within seven days of termination (art. 20). Prepaid SIMs held by foreigners are checked periodically against a designated database and restricted once the holder has left Taiwan or overstayed their stay or residence period ([Fraud Crime Hazard Prevention Act art. 22](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0080226&flno=22)(1)–(2)).

## A number you never applied for

A contract forms only when the parties' declarations of intent agree ([Civil Code art. 153](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=153)(1)). If someone posed as your agent, an act by a person without authority does not bind you unless you ratify it ([art. 170](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=170)(1)). No agreement, no basis for the bill.

Carriers must check identity. Applicants show their own ID, agents also show theirs and proof of authority, and the carrier must verify and record the match before contracting ([Fraud Crime Hazard Prevention Act art. 15](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0080226&flno=15)(1)–(3)). It must also cross-check identity against a database designated by the authority ([art. 19](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0080226&flno=19)(1)). The records should show who applied, with what documents, at which store.

Under Far EasTone's contract, foreigners are limited in principle to one number, and more requires an explanation of use and supporting documents (art. 10(4)). A first-time applicant is photographed, and records are kept at least a year after the contract ends (art. 7). A photo of someone else is strong evidence. Travel records help if you were abroad.

Lending your SIM is different: under art. 19, a subscriber who lets someone else use the device or SIM still owes the fees. If your own number is cloned, disputed charges may be withheld unless the carrier traces them to your device (art. 33(3)), and you can request your own call and billing records (art. 24(2)).

Start with a written dispute; art. 22 says the carrier holds off collection or suspension while it determines responsibility. Ask for the application form, the identity-check record and the store. If the number was used in a scam and police have contacted you, see [our article on that situation](/en/columns/taiwan-phone-number-identity-theft-aiding-fraud-suspect).

Forging an application is a crime. Forging a private document in a way that may harm the public or others carries up to five years ([Criminal Code art. 210](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0000001&flno=210)), and using it is punished the same way ([art. 216](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0000001&flno=216)). A police filing record also supports the billing dispute.

## When the carrier says no

Complain to the carrier first: a business must handle a consumer complaint properly within 15 days ([Consumer Protection Act art. 43](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0170001&flno=43)(1)–(2)); after that come the local consumer protection officer (art. 43(3)) and mediation ([art. 44](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0170001&flno=44)). Telecoms also have a dedicated dispute body that designated carriers must jointly set up ([Telecommunications Management Act art. 20](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=K0060111&flno=20)(1)), which Far EasTone's art. 44 names as a complaint channel.

In court, an action to confirm that no debt exists requires an immediate legal interest in the ruling ([Code of Civil Procedure art. 247](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=247)(1)); continuing collection demands make that easy to show. Money claims of NT$100,000 or less use small claims procedure ([art. 436-8](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=436-8)(1)); see [our small claims article](/en/columns/taiwan-small-claims-simplified-civil-procedure). Far EasTone's art. 45 states that its forum clause cannot exclude the small-claims venue rules in Consumer Protection Act art. 47 or Code of Civil Procedure art. 436-9.
