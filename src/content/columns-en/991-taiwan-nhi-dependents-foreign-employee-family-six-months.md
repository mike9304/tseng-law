# Who in Your Family Waits Six Months for Taiwan Health Insurance?

It depends on whose family it is, and on which family member. [Article 9 of the National Health Insurance Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=L0060001&flno=9) requires a non-citizen to hold a Taiwan residence document and to meet one of three conditions: six months of residence, employment by a fixed employer, or birth in Taiwan. Family members who are not employed can, as a rule, only meet the first. [Article 23 of the Act for the Recruitment and Employment of Foreign Professionals](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=A0030295&flno=23) removes that wait for many of them.

## The rule: dependant status does not replace residence

[Article 2](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=L0060001&flno=2)(2) defines who may be a dependant: a spouse with no occupation; lineal ascendants such as parents with no occupation; and lineal descendants within the second degree who are minors with no occupation, or adults unable to support themselves or still in school with no occupation. That answers who can be insured through the employee. It does not answer when. Each person must first qualify under Article 9, and cover starts on the date they do ([Article 14](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=L0060001&flno=14)).

## The exception: a foreign professional's spouse and minor children

Under Article 23, when a foreign professional is employed in professional work, the professional, their spouse, their minor children and adult children unable to live independently because of disability must join NHI once they hold a residence document, free of the six-month limit. Foreign special professionals and senior professionals who qualify as first-category insured persons as employers or self-employed people are treated the same way.

Who counts as a foreign professional? [Article 4](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=A0030295&flno=4) of the same act: a foreigner permitted to do professional work in Taiwan. Professional work includes the jobs in [Article 46](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0090001&flno=46)(1)(1)–(3), (5) and (6) of the Employment Service Act: specialized or technical work, managing a foreign-invested business, teaching at the schools listed there, coaching and athletics, and religious, artistic or performing work; Article 4(4) also lists some cram-school and bilingual-education teachers, among others. A manager or engineer hired by a Taiwan subsidiary on such a permit is the typical case.

Some relatives fall outside it. Adult children who are students and parents are not listed in Article 23, so the six-month rule applies to them. Families of foreign workers in fishing, domestic help and care, or designated construction under Article 46(1)(8)–(10) are also outside it.

## Counting the six months

For those who must wait, the method is in [Article 8](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=L0060002&flno=8)(2) of the Enforcement Rules. Six months of continuous residence after entering on a residence basis counts. So does a period with a single trip abroad of no more than 30 days, once the days away are deducted.

A ten-day trip home at Christmas? One trip, under 30 days: deduct ten days and keep counting. Two trips, or one trip longer than 30 days, falls outside that rule. How the count restarts in that case is worth confirming with the insurer, the National Health Insurance Administration of the Ministry of Health and Welfare.

The count starts when residence starts; at the point of enrolment the person must hold a residence document (Article 9). Residence documents include the Alien Resident Certificate and the Alien Permanent Resident Certificate (Enforcement Rules, Article 8(1)). Deadlines for a first permit are covered in [Taiwan's first ARC application](/en/columns/taiwan-arc-first-application).

## Babies born in Taiwan

Newborns born in Taiwan have their own item, Article 9(3), which also presupposes a residence document. The insurer's English guidance states that since December 1, 2017, a foreign newborn born in Taiwan with an Alien Resident Certificate is enrolled from the date of birth ([NHI guidance](https://www.nhi.gov.tw/en/dl-136-b5c867bf98fc44dd803358cb9f9aad89-2.pdf)). If the certificate is applied for late, ask the insurer how the enrolment date is handled. Leave entitlements around birth are in [maternity, paternity and parental leave in 2026](/en/columns/taiwan-maternity-paternity-parental-leave-2026).

## Enrolment through the employer within three days

Dependants are enrolled and withdrawn together with the insured employee ([Article 12](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=L0060001&flno=12)). The employer files, within three days of the date a person qualifies ([Article 15](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=L0060001&flno=15), last paragraph). Telling HR in advance when each family member's residence document arrives or six months are reached keeps that window from slipping.

Skipping it is not an option. A person who should be insured and is not faces a fine of NT$3,000 to NT$15,000, back-dated enrolment, and suspended benefits until the fine and premiums are paid ([Article 91](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=L0060001&flno=91)). An employer that fails to file must pay the premiums plus a fine of two to four times the premiums due ([Article 84](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=L0060001&flno=84)). Neither the back-payment nor the fine under Article 84(1) applies to the employer where the failure is not attributable to the employer, such as an employee not reporting the dependants (Enforcement Rules, [Article 64](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=L0060002&flno=64)(2)).

## Who pays

Dependants' premiums are calculated on the employee's insured amount and rate and paid by the employee; beyond three dependants, only three are counted ([Article 18](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=L0060001&flno=18)). For private-sector employees and their dependants, the employee pays 30 percent, the employer 60 percent and the central government 10 percent ([Article 27](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=L0060001&flno=27)(1)(ii)).

## Long trips home no longer pause cover

People leaving Taiwan for six months or more used to be able to suspend cover and stop paying. Following Constitutional Court Judgment 111-Hsien-Pan-19 of December 23, 2022, suspension on the old basis has not been available since December 23, 2024 (Enforcement Rules, [Article 36-1](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=L0060002&flno=36-1)). Anyone whose earlier suspension runs past that date cancels it and resumes cover, or settles the premiums, on the day they return, and cannot suspend again afterwards.

Once a family member leaves Taiwan and no longer meets Article 9, that person ceases to be insured and is withdrawn ([Article 13](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=L0060001&flno=13)(2)). Payroll withholding and social insurance for the employee are covered in [payroll for foreign employees of a Taiwan subsidiary](/en/columns/taiwan-payroll-foreign-employees-withholding-social-insurance).
