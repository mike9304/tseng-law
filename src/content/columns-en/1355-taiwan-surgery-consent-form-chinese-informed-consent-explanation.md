# You signed a Chinese surgery consent form in Taiwan. What did the hospital have to explain?

Hours after signing two Chinese forms, the patient was back on the ward with a complication nobody had mentioned during the short talk beside the gurney (an invented example). Before that operation, [Article 63 of the Medical Care Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=L0020021&flno=63) (醫療法) required the hospital to explain the reason for the operation and either its success rate or the complications and risks it could bring. It also had to obtain consent, with a signed surgery consent form and a signed anesthesia consent form (手術同意書、麻醉同意書), unless the situation was an emergency.

Those signatures are evidence of the patient's consent, but on their own they do not show what was or was not said beside the gurney. What the patient can do now is build a record: get the chart and the consent forms, find out whether the outcome triggers the hospital's separate duty to explain after a medical accident, and expect a mediation step before any civil suit over it.

## What the hospital owed before and during treatment

Article 63 says who receives that explanation: the patient, or the patient's legal representative, spouse, relative, or related person (法定代理人、配偶、親屬或關係人). When the patient is a minor or cannot sign in person, any of those people may sign both forms. The central competent authority sets the format of the two forms.

Surgery is not the only trigger. [Article 64](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=L0020021&flno=64) applies the same sequence of explanation, consent, and a signed form to invasive examinations and treatments designated by the central competent authority, with the same emergency exception and substitute signers.

Then there is the information owed throughout care. Whenever a medical institution (醫療機構) diagnoses or treats a patient, [Article 81](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=L0020021&flno=81) requires it to tell the patient, or that same circle of people, about the condition (病情), the treatment plan (治療方針), the measures taken (處置), medication (用藥), the prognosis (預後情形), and possible adverse reactions (可能之不良反應). Article 81 has no signing step and states no emergency exception.

## What a signature on a Chinese form does and does not show

On their own, the signatures do not establish that the Article 63 explanation or the Article 81 information was given, and they do not establish that either was left out. Neither article mentions language. Nor does either one make the Chinese wording of a form, by itself, a basis for liability.

So the real question is narrower. Was the complication among the risks someone actually described? The forms may answer part of that, if the complication is written on them. The rest has to come from the surrounding record: the anesthesia record and nursing notes, the name of whoever interpreted (a nurse, a friend, a relative on speakerphone), and any messages with the hospital from before or after the operation.

Liability for harm in medical practice follows [Article 82](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=L0020021&flno=82). Medical practice must meet the care that is medically necessary (醫療上必要之注意). Medical personnel (醫事人員) owe damages for harm caused to a patient in their practice only for intent, or for a breach of that duty of care that also exceeds reasonable clinical professional discretion (合理臨床專業裁量). For negligently causing a patient's death or injury, they bear criminal liability only where the conduct both breaches the duty of necessary medical care and exceeds reasonable clinical professional discretion. Breach of that duty and the scope of clinical discretion are judged by the objective circumstances in that medical field at that time and place: medical custom, the medical standard, facilities, working conditions, and urgency. The institution itself is liable in damages only for intent or negligence (過失).

The Civil Code (民法) adds a general tort basis. Under [Article 184](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=184), anyone who intentionally or negligently and unlawfully infringes another's right owes damages. After an unlawful injury to body or health, [Article 193](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=193) requires compensation for lost or reduced earning capacity and increased needs of daily life, and [Article 195](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=195) lets the injured person claim a reasonable sum even for harm that is not financial. A claim can also be framed as incomplete performance (不完全給付). [Article 227](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=227) applies when incomplete performance is attributable to the debtor. The creditor may then invoke the rules on delayed or impossible performance and also claim other damage the incomplete performance causes.

## Getting the chart and the consent forms

No dispute is needed for a basic copy. [Article 71](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=L0020021&flno=71) requires the institution that treated the patient to provide a copy of the medical record on request and, when necessary, a Chinese summary of it (中文病歷摘要). It must not delay or refuse without cause. The patient bears the cost, though the article sets no amount.

Once a medical dispute (醫療爭議) exists, a second route with a deadline opens. Under [Article 3](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=L0020227&flno=3) of the Medical Accident Prevention and Dispute Resolution Act (醫療事故預防及爭議處理法), that means the patient's side believes medical personnel or the institution should answer for an adverse result. The Act has been in force since January 1, 2024, according to its [legislative history](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawHistory.aspx?pcode=L0020227). Its [Article 10](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=L0020227&flno=10) requires the institution to provide copies of the chart and of the consent forms kept with it within seven working days. That period runs from the day after the patient, an agent, a legal representative, or an heir applies. Again, the applicant pays, and a request that names the anesthesia record and nursing notes alongside both consent forms makes a missing page easier to spot.

## When the outcome counts as a medical accident

Not every complication qualifies. Article 3 defines a medical accident (醫療事故) as a major injury or death resulting from medical services, excluding outcomes that are unavoidable because of the disease itself or the medical treatment. An unexplained complication does not become a medical accident for that reason alone.

When the definition is met, [Article 6](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=L0020227&flno=6) requires the hospital or clinic to explain, communicate, and offer help and care to the patient, the family, or an agent within five working days from the day after the accident. A medical-accident care team (醫療事故關懷小組) does this, although hospitals with 99 beds or fewer, and clinics, may instead designate a professional or entrust a professional institution or group with these duties. One provision speaks directly to foreign patients: if language or cultural factors, or a hearing, speech, or other impairment, make communication difficult, a person with relevant training must assist with the explanation and communication. The institution must keep a record of that explanation and care for at least three years.

## Mediation before a civil suit

Suing comes second. [Article 15](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=L0020227&flno=15) requires a party to apply for mediation under the Act before bringing a civil action over a medical dispute. If a party sues without applying, the first-instance court must transfer the case to the competent mediation committee, and the lawsuit stops while mediation runs. If mediation fails, a suit filed within six months from the day after the certificate of unsuccessful mediation is served counts as filed on the date mediation was applied for.

Some of what is said in mediation is shielded. [Article 23](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=L0020227&flno=23) bars the use of the mediator's guidance, or of a party's regret, apology, statement against its own interest, or concession, as evidence or as the basis of a judgment in the case or of a related administrative sanction, unless every party to the dispute agrees. Mediation under that chapter is also free of charge under [Article 30](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=L0020227&flno=30).
