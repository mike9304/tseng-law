# Cancelling a Taiwan Postpartum Care Center: Deposit Caps and Infection Claims

Cancel a Taiwan postpartum care center booking the day before check-in, and the center may keep at most half of your deposit. The base is the deposit, not the package price, and the deposit may not exceed 10% of the total.

A family books a 28-day stay at NT$200,000 and pays a NT$20,000 deposit. Twenty-five days before check-in, the family switches to another center (an invented example). The most the first center can keep is 20% of the deposit: NT$4,000.

## Where the percentages come from

On September 2, 2010 (No. 0992862165), Taiwan's former Department of Health, now the Ministry of Health and Welfare, revised the mandatory and prohibited terms for postpartum nursing institution and postpartum center contracts first announced on August 21, 2002. The [Executive Yuan's consumer protection list](https://www.ey.gov.tw/Page/DFB720D019CCCB0A/0b8f63ef-22a6-4373-be97-c90e86590864) still shows this version as of the check date.

[Consumer Protection Act Article 17](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0170001&flno=17) gives them force: they form part of the contract even if left out (paragraph 5), and standard-form clauses violating them are void (paragraph 4). Point 11 keeps clauses more favourable to the consumer.

| Cancelled | Cap, as share of deposit |
|---|---|
| 31+ days before check-in | 10% |
| 21–30 days before | 20% |
| 2–20 days before | 30% |
| 1 day before | 50% |
| On the day | 100% |

Point 8, paragraph 2 sets these caps.

## When the mother or baby cannot go

If, before check-in, poor health, illness or death of the mother or baby, or another cause not attributable to the consumer, prevents the service, the consumer may rescind and gets the whole deposit back without interest (point 8, paragraph 1).

If a natural disaster, war, an epidemic or another cause not attributable to the center stops it from performing, the consumer is released from paying and gets the deposit back in full, interest-free (paragraph 3). If the cause is attributable to the center, the consumer may rescind and the center must return double the deposit (paragraph 4). That tracks the default deposit rules in [Civil Code Article 249](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=249), items 3 and 4.

What if the baby comes two weeks early? Article 2 of the [model contract](https://www.ey.gov.tw/Page/AABD2F12D8A6D561/512f8570-60c2-4953-bb78-0949e358cd1a), revised October 6, 2010, allows check-in any time within three weeks either side of the scheduled date and bars refusal without a legitimate reason. The model is a template. Check your signed contract.

The model allows at least five days' review, which prohibited term 1 bars waiving. Under [Consumer Protection Act Article 11-1](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0170001&flno=11-1), the reasonable review period runs up to 30 days; clauses denied it are excluded unless the consumer elects to keep them.

## Leaving after check-in

After check-in, the question becomes termination. Under point 9, paragraph 1, if health, illness, death or another cause not attributable to the consumer ends the stay, the consumer may terminate for the mother, the baby, or both. The center credits the deposit, bills only services provided, adds no other fees and refunds any overpayment.

Where a cause attributable to the center stops it continuing, the consumer may terminate, recover overpayments and claim an agreed percentage, not below 10%, of the fees for the unused days, plus proven further losses (paragraph 2). If the center cannot continue through no fault of its own, billing stops at services provided (paragraph 3).

If a cause attributable to the consumer ends the stay, the center may terminate, bill for services provided, and claim no more than 10% of the unused-day fees (point 10).

Simply changing your mind is not addressed head-on. Prohibited terms 4 and 3 ban clauses forbidding early termination or holding mother and baby until fees are paid. Point 10's 10% cap is a useful yardstick.

## The center's duties on infection and injury

Point 5 requires the center to look after mother and baby with the care of a good administrator. Under point 6, in an acute or serious illness or other emergency it must send the patient for treatment at once, take rescue measures suited to its equipment and staff, and notify the emergency contact immediately. The model contract, not automatically binding, lets the center refuse visits when infection is a risk (Article 6).

The infection-control regulation under [Communicable Disease Control Act Article 33](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=L0050001&flno=33), paragraph 3, covers postpartum nursing homes set up under the Nurses Act ([Article 2](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=L0050038&flno=2), item 1). On a suspected infection, the home must report, isolate appropriately, and keep an investigation and improvement report with follow-up records ([Article 8](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=L0050038&flno=8), paragraph 2). It must keep staff with contagious skin, gastrointestinal or respiratory illness away from care and food preparation ([Article 9](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=L0050038&flno=9)), collect health data ([Article 10](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=L0050038&flno=10)), and keep visitor rules and logs ([Article 12](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=L0050038&flno=12)).

## Two routes to compensation

In contract, [Civil Code Article 227](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=227) covers defective performance attributable to the debtor, including further losses. Staff intent or negligence counts as the center's own unless the parties agreed otherwise ([Article 224](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=224)). Where non-performance injures personality rights, [Article 227-1](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=227-1) applies Articles 192 to 195 and 197 mutatis mutandis.

In tort, [Article 184](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=184) and [Article 188](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=188) make the employer jointly liable for staff wrongs on duty. Proof of due care in selecting and supervising staff, or that the harm was unavoidable, frees the employer, though on the victim's application the court may still order full or partial compensation given both sides' finances (paragraph 2). The baby's own injury is claimed this way, even if a parent signed.

Damages cover lost or reduced working capacity and increased living needs ([Article 193](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=193)), and solatium for bodily or health injury ([Article 195](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=195)); paragraph 3 extends solatium to serious harm to the parent-child relationship. Time is short. Tort claims lapse two years after the claimant learns of the damage and who is liable, or ten years after the act ([Article 197](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=197), paragraph 1).

Whether [Consumer Protection Act Article 7](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0170001&flno=7) (joint liability for services lacking reasonably expected safety, reducible if the business proves no fault) reaches postpartum nursing care is not settled by the text; this article assumes neither answer.

## Mediation before suing?

Since January 1, 2024, [Article 15](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=L0020227&flno=15) of the Medical Accident Prevention and Dispute Resolution Act has required mediation under that Act before a civil suit over a medical dispute; handling by local medical review committees under Medical Care Act Article 99(1)(iii) or township mediation does not substitute for it (paragraph 1). A suit filed without mediation is referred to the competent mediation committee and stayed meanwhile (paragraph 2). Where a party applied for mediation and it failed, suing within six months from the day after the failure certificate is served counts as suing on the application date (paragraph 3).

Under [Article 3](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=L0020227&flno=3), a medical institution is one opened with approval by medical personnel listed in [Medical Care Act Article 10](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=L0020021&flno=10), paragraph 1, which include nurses; yet [Nurses Act Article 17](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=L0020166&flno=17) also lets foundations and other legal persons open nursing institutions. Whether a center fits is case-specific, and the definition says nothing of businesses offering only meals and lodging.

Businesses must handle a consumer complaint properly within 15 days ([Article 43](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0170001&flno=43), paragraph 2); failing that, the consumer may complain to a local consumer ombudsman (paragraph 3) or apply to a mediation committee ([Article 44](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0170001&flno=44)).

## Licensed center or not

Under the nursing institution classification standards, a postpartum nursing home is a type of nursing home ([Article 2](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=L0020035&flno=2)) serving mothers within two months of birth and infants under two months, unless a doctor diagnoses a special need ([Article 4](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=L0020035&flno=4)). The Nurses Act requires permits ([Articles 16](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=L0020166&flno=16) and 17), bars non-institutions from using the name, or a similar one, or advertising nursing ([Article 18](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=L0020166&flno=18), paragraph 2; [Article 18-1](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=L0020166&flno=18-1), paragraph 2), requires a referral contract with a nearby hospital that has passed government evaluation, with a new contract reported within 15 days of any termination or change ([Article 20](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=L0020166&flno=20)), and forbids charging above locally approved fees ([Article 21](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=L0020166&flno=21), paragraph 2).

Some centers offer only meals and lodging. A [Ministry of Health and Welfare release](https://www.mohw.gov.tw/cp-3219-22584-1.html) on its first (2013) evaluation of postpartum nursing institutions said such centers may not provide nursing. The mandatory terms cover both kinds, but the infection-control regulation lists only homes set up under the Nurses Act. Whether the terms fit home-visit postpartum care is unclear, since they are written around check-in; Consumer Protection Act [Article 12](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0170001&flno=12) still voids grossly unfair standard clauses.
