# Taiwan's December 1 CFC deadline can affect years of tax treatment

For certain calendar-year businesses, December 1, 2026 is the approved deadline to supply 2025 financial statements for their controlled foreign companies (CFCs). [Taiwan News Wire reported the reminder on November 20](https://newswire.com.tw/news/20261119-cfc-report-extension-dec1-deadline), following the National Taxation Bureau of the Northern Area's [November 19 announcement](https://www.mof.gov.tw/singlehtml/384fb3077bb349ea973e7fc6f13b6974?cntId=303e5846ede74cca8c39b1571ab85e9d). It concerns businesses that applied with their corporate return and received approval for that extension.

An international group's Taiwan finance team already within the corporate CFC rules depends on financial records arriving from overseas. Timely submission helps preserve an elected financial-instrument treatment and access to qualifying CFC loss deductions. The bureau has not announced a fresh extension for every company with foreign holdings.

## The statements must match the Taiwan reporting period

[Article 10 of Taiwan's corporate CFC regulations](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0340145&flno=10) requires financial statements for the same accounting period as the Taiwan taxpayer's return, generally audited by a qualified accountant in the CFC's jurisdiction or Taiwan. Other evidence may substitute. That route is available only if the evidence sufficiently establishes the statements' authenticity and the taxpayer's local tax authority confirms it.

An extension application must explain the reasons and reach the tax authority before the income-tax return filing period ends. At most six months are available. Only one extension is allowed. According to the bureau, the approved December 1 extensions for the specified calendar-year filers were notified by public announcement on June 30. The November notice reminds those filers of an existing deadline.

## Missing records can end the FVPL election

FVPL means financial instruments measured at fair value through profit or loss. Under [Article 7](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0340145&flno=7), a Taiwan business may elect a method that removes the relevant fair-value changes when calculating CFC earnings and brings in adjustments when instruments are disposed of or reclassified. It must use the same method for all CFCs it holds directly. The election is generally binding.

What happens if the required records are late? Paragraph 5 excludes the election for ten years beginning with the year to which the missing documents relate, and requires previously adjusted gains and losses accumulated through that year's closing date to enter that year's CFC earnings. Inconsistent use of the method triggers corresponding consequences beginning in the year of the inconsistency.

The provision identifies particular documents. These include the structure and ownership information, financial statements, loss-deduction schedule and investment-income schedule required by Article 10, paragraph 1, items 1–4. It also covers the separate audit report on financial instruments that paragraph 2, item 4 requires when requested. Delivering financial statements alone does not satisfy every documentary condition for the election.

## CFC losses have a separate ten-year period

[Article 8](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0340145&flno=8) makes timely specified documents, proper calculation and reporting, and assessment by the tax authority conditions for deducting a CFC's losses. These requirements begin in the year the entity qualifies as a CFC. Assessed losses are deducted in sequence from that CFC's earnings within the ten years beginning in the year after each loss arose. This period governs loss use; Article 7's ten-year period governs exclusion from the election.

A Taiwan tax filing may depend on work performed by a foreign accountant, but an overseas audit timetable does not establish a Taiwan extension. For the filers covered by the notice, December 1 remains the approved date. Their submission needs statements for the matching period and, where substitute evidence is proposed, the Taiwan tax authority's confirmation that it is acceptable.
