# Taiwan's 'Paper Divorces': How Courts Assessed the Witnesses

Divorce on paper, get the debt restructured, register the marriage again once things settle down. That was the plan your Taiwanese spouse put to you, and you signed (an invented example). The four cases discussed below all involve one spouse later saying the divorce was only on paper, and in three of them the court held the divorce void for want of qualified witnesses. The reasons given included these: to apply for debt negotiation, to make subsidies easier to get, to keep property away from a relative's creditors.

In the cases below, no court held that the motive alone made a divorce void.

## How a divorce by consent is made

One assumption runs through this piece: the divorce is governed by Taiwan law and made in Taiwan's form. For an international couple, divorce is governed by the spouses' common national law at the time of the agreement, failing that by the law of their common domicile, and failing that by the law of the place most closely connected with the marriage ([Act Governing the Choice of Law in Civil Matters Involving Foreign Elements, Article 50](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000007&flno=50)). The form of the act follows the law governing it or the law of the place where it is done ([Article 16](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000007&flno=16)).

Article 1049 of the Civil Code lets spouses divorce by mutual consent. [Article 1050](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=1050) sets the form: a written agreement, the signatures of at least two witnesses, and registration at the household registration office. A juridical act that does not follow a required form is void under [Article 73](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=73).

Case law fills in the witness requirement. Witnesses need not be in the room when the spouses sign, but each must have personally seen or heard that both spouses genuinely intended to divorce. Where Taiwan law governs, that includes the foreign spouse. A witness who signs on the Taiwanese spouse's word alone has not personally confirmed the foreign spouse's intent.

## How far "it was only on paper" got

A separate rule voids a fictitious expression of intent made in collusion with the other party ([Article 87](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=87)). None of the four cases' main judgments (three from district courts, one from the High Court on appeal) decided the case under it. The New Taipei and High Court judgments said they did not need to reach it; the Taitung and Taoyuan judgments did not mention it.

The New Taipei District Court ruled on December 20, 2024 ([113年度婚字第455號](https://judgment.judicial.gov.tw/FJUD/data.aspx?ty=JD&id=PCDV%2c113%2c%e5%a9%9a%2c455%2c20241220%2c1)) on a divorce registered in 2010, fourteen years earlier. The plaintiff said it had been done to apply for debt negotiation. The court turned to the witnesses. One had signed at the plaintiff's workplace with only the plaintiff present and never checked, by any means, what the other spouse wanted. The divorce was void. An appeal was withdrawn on May 27, 2025.

In Taitung the result was the same ([113年度婚字第27號](https://judgment.judicial.gov.tw/FJUD/data.aspx?ty=JD&id=TTDV%2c113%2c%e5%a9%9a%2c27%2c20250109%2c2), January 9, 2025). The husband said he signed so his wife could get subsidies. The two witnesses testified that they asked at the car whether the couple really meant to divorce, and signed after the two nodded or said yes. The court doubted that account, because what they said about the order of signing contradicted the wife's own version. It also found that neither witness knew the husband or checked that the man who came with her was actually him, so neither was a qualified witness. The Hualien Branch of the Taiwan High Court dismissed the wife's appeal on December 24, 2025 ([114年度家上字第6號](https://judgment.judicial.gov.tw/FJUD/data.aspx?ty=JD&id=HLHV%2c114%2c%e5%ae%b6%e4%b8%8a%2c6%2c20251224%2c1)), and on May 7, 2026 the Supreme Court rejected a further appeal as inadmissible ([115年度台上字第756號](https://judgment.judicial.gov.tw/FJUD/data.aspx?ty=JD&id=TPSV%2c115%2c%e5%8f%b0%e4%b8%8a%2c756%2c20260507%2c1)).

Taoyuan went the other way ([112年度婚字第449號](https://judgment.judicial.gov.tw/FJUD/data.aspx?ty=JD&id=TYDV%2c112%2c%e5%a9%9a%2c449%2c20241115%2c1), November 15, 2024). The plaintiff said the divorce was meant only to shield the plaintiff's property from creditors of the other spouse's parents, who had guaranteed someone's debts. A party relying on a fact must prove it ([Code of Civil Procedure Article 277](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=277)), and there was no evidence. Affectionate messages exchanged after the registration did not change that. The two witnesses, a married couple who knew both spouses, had come to the home and heard each of them say they wanted a divorce. The divorce stood, and the Taiwan High Court dismissed the appeal on October 22, 2025 ([114年度家上字第95號](https://judgment.judicial.gov.tw/FJUD/data.aspx?ty=JD&id=TPHV%2c114%2c%e5%ae%b6%e4%b8%8a%2c95%2c20251022%2c1)), also finding that both witnesses had heard the spouses' intention to divorce.

At the Taiwan High Court ([114年度家上字第28號](https://judgment.judicial.gov.tw/FJUD/data.aspx?ty=JD&id=TPHV%2c114%2c%e5%ae%b6%e4%b8%8a%2c28%2c20250423%2c1), April 23, 2025), the witness lines carried the names of one spouse's younger brother and sister, filled in without their knowledge. Both spouses' convictions for uttering forged private documents had become final. One of them argued that, having grown up in mainland China, they had not known Taiwan's formal rules. The divorce was still void. The Keelung District Court had reached the same result at first instance on March 11, 2024. In a separate judgment on May 30, 2024, though, it granted a judicial divorce because the marriage had broken down ([Article 1052(2)](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=1052)). The High Court heard both appeals together and dismissed them. A further appeal was dismissed as improper on June 25, 2025, because no court fee was paid and no lawyer was appointed ([ruling](https://judgment.judicial.gov.tw/FJUD/data.aspx?ty=JD&id=TPHV%2c114%2c%e5%ae%b6%e4%b8%8a%2c28%2c20250625%2c3)).

## What a void divorce leaves behind

A void divorce means the marriage never ended. Because the register still showed a divorce, all four main judgments found that the plaintiff had a legal interest in a suit to confirm that the marriage exists ([Code of Civil Procedure Article 247](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=247)).

Remarriage is where it bites. Article 985 forbids bigamy, and [Article 988(3)](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=988) makes a bigamous marriage void. It also makes an exception: if both parties to the new marriage relied in good faith and without negligence on the registered divorce, the new marriage stands, and the old one is treated as dissolved on the day the new one was formed, with the effects of divorce applying ([Article 988-1](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=988-1)). A remaining-property distribution or agreement already made still controls and cannot be claimed afresh (paragraph 2). Otherwise, a claim to share the remaining marital property expires two years after the claimant learns of the difference, and in any case five years after the divorce registration is revoked. The first spouse is not left empty-handed either: a spouse without fault may claim damages from the other, including for non-pecuniary loss.

Registration steps and the witness formalities themselves are covered in [our column on divorcing a Taiwanese spouse by agreement](/en/columns/taiwanese-spouse-divorce-agreement-registration). In the Taoyuan case, the one where the divorce stood, both witnesses had heard the spouses say in person that they would divorce.
