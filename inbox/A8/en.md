# Many Victims, One Scam: How Taiwan's Fraud Crime Hazard Prevention Act Ties Sentences to Repayment and Lets Victims Sue Together

Two hundred people put money into the same fake trading app, run from Taiwan (an invented example). Some lost a few thousand dollars, some their savings. Do they each have to bring their own case? Taiwan's Fraud Crime Hazard Prevention Act, enacted on July 31, 2024, gives them another option, and it also gives defendants a reason to pay.

## Which frauds the Act covers

Under [Article 2](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0080226&flno=2), "fraud crimes" means aggravated fraud under [Criminal Code Article 339-4](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0000001&flno=339-4), offences under Articles 43 and 44 of the Act, and offences tried together with them as a single offence. Aggravated fraud covers four situations: impersonating a government agency or public official; three or more people acting together; spreading the scheme to the public through broadcast, telecom, internet or other media; and using false images, sounds or electromagnetic records about another person, made by computer synthesis or other technology. It carries one to seven years. For an organized ring, the first question is whether three or more people acted together. A romance scam run by one person is different, and whether the Act's victim provisions apply there has to be checked separately.

## Amended Articles 46 and 47: payment and sentence relief

The Act links repayment to sentencing. [Article 47](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0080226&flno=47), as amended on January 21, 2026 and in force since January 23, allows a reduced sentence for a defendant who confesses during the investigation and at every trial level and, within six months of first confessing during the prosecutor's investigation, pays the full amount agreed with the victim in mediation or settlement. [Article 46](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0080226&flno=46) does the same for a defendant who turned themselves in, allowing reduction or even exemption on full payment within six months of surrender. Paragraph 2 of each article goes further where, on top of the paragraph 1 conditions, the case results in catching those who started, ran or directed the fraud organization, or in seizing all the property the organization obtained from all its victims. Article 46 then allows exemption from punishment; Article 47 allows reduction or exemption.

Timing matters for older cases. Under [Criminal Code Article 2(1)](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0000001&flno=2), the law in force at the time of the conduct applies unless a later law is more favourable to the offender, so conduct before January 23, 2026 calls for a comparison of the old and new versions. A [Judicial Yuan report on its briefings](https://www.judicial.gov.tw/tw/cp-1429-1504544-47483-1.html) also notes that where these two articles do not apply, for instance because the settlement was reached or paid after the six-month period or the fraud stopped at an attempt, other relevant provisions can still apply. Payment is the condition for relief under these two articles, not the only thing that shapes a sentence.

Prosecutors and judges have tools of their own. Under [Article 53](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0080226&flno=53), they may use mediation and settlement to get defendants to pay victims; where a defendant has not paid, the prosecutor may ask the court to weigh that in sentencing and must appeal if necessary. When granting a deferred prosecution ([Code of Criminal Procedure Article 253-1](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0010001&flno=253-1)), prosecutors should give priority to ordering compensation. Courts must also pay particular attention, before the victim is paid, to whether the defendant has been living beyond ordinary standards: luxury goods or services, expensive transport, investments, high-end venues, gifts or loans to others, or monthly living costs ([Article 50(2)](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0080226&flno=50)).

The benchmark is the agreed amount. It need not match the full loss. What a victim controls is the paper: the amount, the payment date, and what happens if the money does not arrive.

## Suing together, at lower cost

Where some money is still sitting in a frozen account, there may be a quicker route. If a bank or virtual-asset provider suspended an account on police notice and a victim's transfer has not been withdrawn, it may return the remaining funds on police notice ([Article 11](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0080226&flno=11)). How a victim overseas reports the loss and gets the receiving account flagged is covered in [our column for overseas victims](/en/columns/us-victim-scam-funds-taiwan-bank-complaint).

Article 11 deals only with balances nobody has withdrawn. A civil claim or a negotiated settlement does not have to wait until the account is empty. For the civil claim, [Article 55](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0080226&flno=55) lets victims whose principal grounds supporting their claims or defences are the same choose one or more of themselves to sue for all, applying the civil-procedure rules on appointed parties. The Article sets no minimum number of victims; what it requires is that the principal grounds supporting their claims or defences be the same. The appointed party sues for everyone who appointed them. Once that happens after the case is pending, the others drop out, and changing the appointed party only takes effect after the other side is notified ([Code of Civil Procedure Article 41](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=41)).

Costs come down too. A fraud-crime victim suing or appealing against those liable is temporarily exempt from court fees, and from enforcement fees when applying for enforcement. Security ordered for a provisional attachment, a provisional injunction or provisional execution cannot exceed one tenth of the claim ([Article 54](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0080226&flno=54)). Police, prosecutors and judges must tell victims who meet the legal-aid criteria that they may apply ([Article 52](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0080226&flno=52)).

Damages claims inside the criminal case are covered in [our column on the incidental civil action](/en/columns/taiwan-criminal-accessory-civil-suit-fraud). For a group of victims, three things come first: a list of who was hit, the transfer records, and the messages that show the same script.
