# Suing in Taiwan Over a Debt Created in the U.S.: Governing Law, Forum Clauses and the Cost Bond

When the borrower lives in Taiwan, the district court where the borrower is domiciled can hear a claim on a loan made in New York as a new lawsuit. If the contract chose New York law, that is in principle the law the judge applies. No American judgment has to come first.

## Whose law the judge applies

Taiwan judges apply foreign law whenever the choice-of-law rules point to it. Article 20 of the [Act Governing the Choice of Law in Civil Matters Involving Foreign Elements](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000007&flno=20) gives the parties' choice first place. Without an express choice, or if the chosen law itself treats that choice as invalid, the court uses the law most closely connected to the contract. Paragraph 3 then presumes that law to be the law of the domicile, at the time of the act, of the party who owes the obligation that characterizes the contract. Macau matters arrive at the same act by another door: [Article 38 of the Act Governing Relations with Hong Kong and Macau](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=Q0010003&flno=38) applies it by analogy to civil matters involving Hong Kong or Macau.

The chosen law also sets the deadline. [Article 36](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000007&flno=36) makes the limitation period follow the law governing the underlying relationship. A New York-law loan is timed under New York's rules. Taiwan's general 15-year period in [Civil Code Article 125](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=125) does not automatically step in.

Who proves what New York law says? [Article 283 of the Code of Civil Procedure](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=283) puts the burden on the parties when the court does not know a foreign law, though the court may investigate on its own. In a New York-law case, that means filing the New York statutes or decisions you rely on, translated into Chinese.

## Interest above 16 percent, and the public-order check

Taiwan caps contractual interest. Agreed interest above 16 percent a year is void for the excess under [Civil Code Article 205](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=205). For a late money debt, [Article 233](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=233) allows default interest at the statutory rate, or at the agreed rate if that is higher. That statutory rate comes from [Article 203](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=203): 5 percent a year for a debt that bears interest when neither the parties nor another law fixes the rate.

Those limits bind when Taiwan law governs. With a foreign law in charge, whether the interest clause is valid is in principle a question for that law, and bringing the claim in Taiwan does not, by itself, establish that Article 205 applies. The provision in the choice-of-law act that lets a Taiwan court set a foreign rule aside is [Article 8](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000007&flno=8), which applies if the result of applying the foreign law would offend Taiwan's public order or good morals. The test looks at the outcome in the case at hand.

Debts tied to gambling can draw the same review. Gambling for money in a public place, or over the internet, is a crime in Taiwan punishable by a fine of up to NT$50,000 ([Criminal Code Article 266](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0000001&flno=266)), and Civil Code [Article 72](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=72) voids juristic acts contrary to public order or good morals.

## Which court, and what a forum clause does

The defendant's domicile is the starting point for jurisdiction. Under [Article 1 of the Code of Civil Procedure](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=1), a lawsuit goes to the court where the defendant is domiciled. When the defendant has no domicile in Taiwan, [Article 3](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=3) allows a property claim to be filed where attachable property or the subject of the claim is located. A contract that fixes a place of performance opens that court too ([Article 12](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=12)).

Forum clauses need a closer reading. [Article 24](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=24) lets parties agree on the court of first instance for disputes arising from a particular legal relationship, and the agreement must be evidenced in writing. Article 24 does not itself settle the effect of choosing a foreign court. An exclusive clause may raise a jurisdictional objection, but its effect in Taiwan cannot be decided from this provision alone.

## If you already hold a U.S. judgment

Recognition is the second route, open to a creditor who already holds a final U.S. judgment. [Article 402 of the Code of Civil Procedure](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=402) denies a foreign judgment effect on four grounds, among them a defendant who never appeared and was not properly served, and the absence of mutual recognition. Enforcement then needs a Taiwan judgment permitting execution under [Article 4-1 of the Compulsory Enforcement Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010004&flno=4-1). That path has its own piece: [enforcing a foreign judgment against assets in Taiwan](/en/columns/enforce-foreign-judgment-in-taiwan). A new case has no foreign judgment to test; the trade-off is that the Taiwan court hears the evidence on the loan itself.

## The cost bond for a creditor with no base in Taiwan

A U.S. creditor suing in Taiwan should budget for security. Under [Article 96 of the Code of Civil Procedure](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=96), when a plaintiff has no domicile, office or place of business in Taiwan, the court must order the plaintiff to post security for litigation costs if the defendant asks. The order does not cover any part of the claim the defendant does not dispute. It also does not issue if the plaintiff has assets in Taiwan sufficient to cover the costs.

Once the defendant has argued the merits orally, the request is barred, unless the ground for it only came to light later ([Article 97](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=97)). The amount is measured by the defendant's total expected costs at each instance ([Article 99](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=99)). If the security is not posted within the period the order sets, the court must dismiss the suit, unless the plaintiff posts it before that dismissal ruling ([Article 101](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=101)). Cash, securities the court accepts, or a guarantee from an insurer or a bank that conducts guarantee business can serve ([Article 102](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=102)).
