# Can a US judgment get your molds back from a Taiwan factory?

A final US judgment ordering a Taiwan factory to return identified injection molds does not, by itself, let a Taiwan enforcement court collect them. The owner must first obtain a Taiwan judgment declaring enforcement permitted, and the US judgment must pass the recognition review in Article 402 of the Code of Civil Procedure (民事訴訟法). Once that step is complete, Taiwan's rules for delivery of specific movables may apply.

With a schedule tying each mold number to photographs, tooling agreements, invoices, and payment records, the Taiwan filings get a clearer path from ownership to the exact objects requested. When the molds may be moved before the permission action is finished, the owner may separately seek a provisional disposition (假處分). It is not an automatic freeze.

## Recognition comes before collection

[Article 402 of Taiwan's Code of Civil Procedure](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=402) starts with a final foreign court judgment (外國法院之確定判決). Taiwan does not recognize its effect when any one of four grounds exists:

1. Under Taiwan law, the foreign court lacked jurisdiction.
2. The losing defendant did not appear, unless the notice or order commencing the action was lawfully served in that country with adequate time, or was served through judicial assistance under Taiwan law.
3. The judgment's content or the proceedings conflict with Taiwan's public order or good morals (公共秩序或善良風俗).
4. Reciprocal recognition (相互之承認) is absent.

Those are four separate gates. A default does not end the analysis when the statutory service exception applies. Whether that exception is met depends on the notice or order that started the case and on how it was served. Reciprocity also calls for a case-specific decision. Nothing in Article 402 labels every US judgment accepted or rejected.

So the Taiwan filing should include the complete judgment and the record showing that it is final, along with the papers needed to assess jurisdiction and service. Chinese translations should let the Taiwan court match the parties, the obligation, and each identified mold without guessing. For the broader recognition framework, see [Enforcing a Foreign Judgment Against Assets in Taiwan](/en/columns/enforce-foreign-judgment-in-taiwan).

## A Taiwan permission judgment opens the enforcement route

[Article 4-1 of the Compulsory Enforcement Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010004&flno=4-1) (強制執行法) supplies the next requirement. Compulsory enforcement based on a final foreign judgment is available only when that judgment has none of Article 402's grounds and a Taiwan court has entered a judgment declaring enforcement permitted (判決宣示許可其執行). The US order alone is not yet a Taiwan enforcement title (執行名義).

Venue follows the same provision. The action belongs in the court for the debtor's domicile. When the debtor has no domicile in Taiwan, the court where the enforcement property is located or where the enforcement act should occur has jurisdiction. A factory address, a registered office, and a related company's warehouse may point to different places, so the proposed target and the debtor's domicile need to be identified before filing.

Carry the judgment's description through to the requested enforcement. “Return the tooling” leaves a practical identification problem; a mold schedule with numbers, photographs, component descriptions, and present-location evidence narrows it. The tooling agreement, invoices, and proof of payment can connect the ownership claim to that schedule. None of those records should silently substitute one mold for another.

## Delivery turns on who possesses the molds

Once there is an enforceable title ordering delivery of specified movable property (一定之動產), [Article 123](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010004&flno=123) gives a direct method. If the debtor does not deliver, the enforcement court may take the movable and hand it to the creditor. [Article 125](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010004&flno=125) applies the rules on execution against movables and immovables, mutatis mutandis (準用), to the delivery situations in Articles 123 and 124; for molds, that brings in the movable-execution procedures.

To take a mold, the court has to connect the enforcement title to the physical object. A stable number, a photograph showing distinctive features, and a component list can make that connection clearer, while ownership documents answer a different question: why the creditor says the item should be returned.

What changes if the molds sit in a related company's warehouse? [Article 126](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010004&flno=126) addresses movable property in third-party possession (第三人占有). The enforcement court must issue an order transferring to the creditor the debtor's right to demand delivery from that third party. The decisive statutory link is the debtor's delivery right against the current possessor. Common ownership or a shared business address does not establish that link on its own.

Location evidence should therefore distinguish possession from ownership and from a contractual duty to return. Warehouse records, transfer records, current photographs, and the agreements governing storage or custody may bear on those separate points. The statutory route depends on what right the debtor has against the possessor, not simply on the possessor's relationship to the OEM.

## Preserving the molds before they move

A non-money claimant may apply for a provisional disposition under [Code of Civil Procedure Article 532](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=532) to preserve compulsory enforcement. The court may not grant it unless a change in the existing state of the claim's subject creates a risk that later enforcement will become impossible or very difficult. Evidence of the molds' present location and a concrete risk of transfer or removal must be tied to that standard.

Provisional-attachment (假扣押) rules apply to a provisional disposition mutatis mutandis through [Article 533](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=533), except where the result differs because of Articles 535 and 536. Venue comes next. Under [Article 524](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=524), the application goes to the Taiwan court of the main case or to the district court where the molds are located. The court of the main case is the first-instance court where the Taiwan case is pending or should be filed; if the case is already pending on appeal, the second-instance court may serve as that court.

Showing the claim and posting security are separate issues. [Article 526](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=526) requires the applicant to show (釋明) the claim and the reason for provisional relief. If that showing falls short, and the creditor offers security or the court finds it appropriate, the court may fix suitable security and grant the measure after it is posted. Even when the showing is sufficient, the court may still order security first.

How the molds are held is the court's call. [Article 535](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=535) leaves the necessary method to the court, which sets it by ruling (裁定). That ruling may appoint an administrator and may order or prohibit the debtor (債務人) from specified acts. A mold-specific application might ask that the debtor be barred from transferring or removing the molds, but it should identify the items and location closely enough for a workable order. Relief is decided on the submitted claim, risk evidence, and proposed terms.
