A Taiwan civil defendant who wishes to oppose withdrawal may have only ten days to object. [Code of Civil Procedure Article 262](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=262) provides for deemed consent when the defendant makes no objection within that period. Hearing and service records establish the starting point.

Under the same article, a plaintiff may withdraw all or part of the action before the judgment becomes final and binding, but must obtain consent once the defendant has argued the merits.

## The hearing history can determine whether you have a choice

Keep the withdrawal pleading with the hearing records. Those records can show what the defendant or its representative has already said about the claim, rather than only whether a hearing occurred.

In [Taiwan High Court Tainan Branch case 111 Kang 89, August 31, 2022](https://judgment.judicial.gov.tw/FJUD/printData.aspx?id=TNHV%2C111%2C%E6%8A%97%2C89%2C20220831%2C1), the court examined substantive participation in a preparatory procedure. It treated statements on the merits of the legal relationship in dispute as equivalent to merits argument for the consent requirement.

During preparation, the parties had advanced competing land-division proposals and responded to each other's proposals, with appraisal evidence also obtained. The defendant objected to withdrawal and sought continued proceedings; the appellate court set aside the lower court's refusal to continue and sent the case back.

That ruling concerns substantive participation in the merits; mere attendance, mediation or a document submission must be assessed on its own facts.

Receiving a complaint is not merits argument. The consent requirement depends on the defendant's subsequent participation, which counsel should assess from the actual hearing records.

## Ten days can pass while head office is considering the notice

Article 262 specifies how silence can become consent:

| Situation | Statutory reference point for the ten-day objection period |
|---|---|
| Defendant attends the hearing and expresses neither consent nor refusal | The hearing date |
| Defendant is absent, or withdrawal is made by pleading | Service of the withdrawal record or pleading |

An oral withdrawal at a hearing must be recorded in the minutes, and those minutes must be served if the other side is absent. These requirements concern the court proceedings; an informal message that the plaintiff plans to drop the case is not the same record.

For a notice forwarded from Taiwan to a US head office, keep the court's service evidence as well. Counsel can calculate the applicable deadline from that record.

In [Supreme Court case 112 Tai-Shang 2088, decided May 9, 2024](https://judgment.judicial.gov.tw/FJUD/printData.aspx?id=TPSV%2C112%2C%E5%8F%B0%E4%B8%8A%2C2088%2C20240509%2C2), the court applied deemed consent after a written withdrawal was served and no objection was made within ten days. No separate signed consent was needed.

If the defendant wishes to preserve continued adjudication where consent is required, that position should be communicated to the court in time. Refusing internally does not notify the court.

## Withdrawal leaves different questions for the claim and counterclaim

[Article 263](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=263) generally treats a withdrawn action as never filed. An existing counterclaim survives.

Check any pending counterclaim requests and hearing arrangements.

Can the plaintiff bring the same action again? Article 263 adds a bar where withdrawal occurs after a judgment disposing of the case, termed 終局判決.

That is a different stage from the judgment becoming final and binding, 判決確定, which Article 262 uses as the outside limit for withdrawal. Withdrawing during an appeal can bar refiling.

Match the exact part withdrawn to the judgment already delivered and the claims that remain pending.

## Costs and a response require their own records

Litigation costs fall on the withdrawing plaintiff under [Article 83](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=83). Where proceedings end without a decision, [Article 90](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=90) requires a costs ruling on application and sets a three-month fixed period from learning of, or receiving notice of, the end of proceedings.

Alongside the withdrawal papers, preserve the notice ending the case and the court-cost records for that application. A statement that the plaintiff has withdrawn does not by itself specify the amount recoverable under a costs ruling.
