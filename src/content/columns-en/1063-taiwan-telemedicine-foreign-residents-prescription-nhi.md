# Can foreign residents in Taiwan use telemedicine and get prescriptions?

An Alien Resident Certificate (ARC) does not open every video consultation in Taiwan, nor does it establish National Health Insurance (NHI) payment for each one. The care category, visit history, medical institution's approval and prescription route all matter.

Personal examination is the default under Article 11 of the [Physicians Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=L0020001&flno=11), before treatment, prescribing or issuing a medical certificate. Its exceptions allow physicians designated by the municipal or county (city) competent authority to provide telemedicine in mountain, outlying-island and remote areas, and in special or urgent circumstances. A video connection alone does not displace that rule.

## The regulations use defined categories of care

Ten special circumstances appear in Article 3 of the [Regulations for Telemedicine](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawAll.aspx?pcode=L0020197):

- Post-acute care and patients enrolled in chronic-disease care plans
- Long-term care services and family-physician care
- Home healthcare and end-of-life care
- Correctional-institution care and care for impaired mobility
- Care arising from disasters, infectious diseases or other major events
- International medical care

Each category has further conditions in articles 4 to 13. A diagnosis alone does not decide eligibility. Post-acute care, for example, concerns follow-up treatment and care within three months after leaving a hospital or clinic.

Mountain, outlying-island and remote areas are addressed in Article 2, while Article 14 defines urgent circumstances. Wanting a consultation in a familiar language does not itself establish one of these categories. Which category applies to the appointment?

International medical care has a specific boundary under Article 13. It covers consultation, treatment and care for Taiwanese or non-Taiwanese patients outside Taiwan. The label does not generally describe foreign nationals living in Taiwan. An ARC holder still needs a qualifying care category.

## Some first visits are prohibited

For post-acute care, chronic-disease plans, long-term care, family-physician care, home healthcare, end-of-life care and impaired-mobility care, Article 20, paragraph 1, subparagraph 2, prohibits telemedicine for new patients. Article 16, paragraph 1, subparagraph 1, then limits prescriptions in those categories to follow-up patients whose conditions are stable. Both restrictions apply.

Other routes have different prescription wording. New and follow-up patients may qualify under Article 2 for mountain, outlying-island and remote areas; Article 3(7) for correctional institutions; Article 3(9) for disasters and similar events; Article 3(10) for international medical care; and Article 14 for urgent circumstances. Qualification is not automatic prescribing.

Services listed in Article 15 include history-taking, examination and diagnosis, test and examination orders, psychiatric psychotherapy, prescribing and adjustment of existing prescriptions. Electronic prescriptions must follow the central competent authority's announced format. Under Article 20, paragraph 2, a physician who considers the condition unsuitable for telemedicine may decline that format and recommend another form of care.

## Controlled drugs, dispensing and delivery remain separate checks

Controlled drugs are generally excluded from telemedicine prescriptions by Article 16(2). Exceptions exist for Article 2, end-of-life care under Article 3(6), urgent circumstances under Article 14, and the circumstances described in the provision as 精神病. Those exceptions do not guarantee every drug or every psychiatric medication.

The [Pharmaceutical Affairs Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=L0030001&flno=50) prohibits dispensing or supplying prescription-only medicines without a physician's prescription under Article 50, subject to its stated exceptions. Article 37 of [the same Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=L0030001&flno=37) assigns dispensing principally to pharmacists. After an electronic prescription is issued, a pharmacy must still be able to receive and dispense it.

Does the patient have a legal right to home delivery? The provisions discussed here create no such general right. The hospital and pharmacy should confirm their linkage, electronic-prescription system, collection or delivery options, charges and identity checks. Delivery is a separate arrangement.

## Institutional approval and records are part of the process

A medical institution providing telemedicine for special circumstances must prepare an implementation plan and obtain approval from the municipal or county (city) competent authority under Article 18. Correctional-institution care also requires that institution's prior consent. Article 18(3) allows approval documents issued by the central competent authority or its subordinate agency under other legislation to replace the plan, with a filing to the local authority. An app or booking page does not show that every department and patient falls within an approved plan.

Requirements under Article 20 include informed consent except in urgent circumstances, verification of the patient's identity, performance by the physician principally within a medical institution, and privacy protection. The record must state that care occurred by telecommunication. Records of medical orders carried out by nurses and other medical personnel must be kept with it.

Medical records remain necessary under Article 68 of the [Medical Care Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=L0020021&flno=68), which requires institutions to have their medical personnel write records personally, sign or seal them, and add the date. Article 72 of [the same Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=L0020021&flno=72) prohibits institutions and their personnel from disclosing patients' medical or health information without cause. Telemedicine does not remove those duties.

## ARC status and NHI payment answer different questions

For insured telemedicine patients, Article 21 directs NHI benefits to the National Health Insurance Act and related rules. Coverage requires another inquiry. Article 9 of [that Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=L0060001&flno=9) provides routes for holders of residence documents: six months of residence in Taiwan, employment with a specific employer, or birth in Taiwan as a newborn.

Residence documents under Article 8 of the [Enforcement Rules](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=L0060002&flno=8) include ARCs and Alien Permanent Resident Certificates. For the six-month route, it permits one departure of no more than 30 days while excluding departure days from actual residence. The card alone establishes neither the enrollment date nor payment for a service.

Even an enrolled patient must confirm the benefit rules for the particular telemedicine item and the hospital's processing. At booking, ask about the care category, new or follow-up status, the receiving pharmacy, NHI handling and out-of-pocket charges. Those answers determine the practical route after the video visit.
