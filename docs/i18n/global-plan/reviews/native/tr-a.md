variety used: Türkiye Turkish (standard Istanbul legal prose), consistent; no Azerbaijani mix. Number punctuation is mixed: thousands as `500.000` (correct) but percentages as English/German `10 %` instead of Turkish `%10`, and the bio amount as `1.57M`.
systemic patterns (max 6, each one line, with 1 example quote):

- Korea left as the default reader where English already generalised: `"Kore ana şirketi Tayvan’a giriş yapsa bile"`.
- Digit legalese for 第三人 / 一審 / 二審: `"3. kişi"`, `"2. derecenin sözlü tartışması"` instead of üçüncü kişi / istinaf.
- English chrome and Hangul leftovers: `OUR TEAM`, `OFFICES`, `(English)`, Hangul slugs in 005/008/009.
- Register split: guidance + 001/002/004/007 are stiff siz-prose; 003 Q16–Q20, 005, 006, 008, 009 keep a Korean-blog voice (`"Merhaba, ben Tayvan avukatı"`, `"yorumlara bırakınız"`).
Gender of attorney Wei Tseng: no finding. Turkish *avukat* is epicene; bios use *yetkilidir*, *temsil etti*, *değişim öğrencisi* with no masculine *bey*, *o* as “he”, or *avukat bey*. Column 007’s `kişisel ilişkileri (會面交往)` and `velayet (親權)` are clearer than the guidance pack. Columns 001–002 and 004 are dense, usable legal Turkish once the Korea-audience holes and `tutuklama`/`%` issues are closed. 006 is the most natural stretch of prose in this part.
