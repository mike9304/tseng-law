# Approving a Taiwan Ride-Hailing Launch from US Headquarters

A US headquarters reviewing a Taiwan launch needs to know whether the proposed local operator will actually control the service described in its operating plan. A contract might assign dispatch to a Taiwan fleet while the foreign platform's team controls the relevant booking functions and driver accounts.

## A launch date cannot rely on the proposed one-year period

The [Ministry of Transportation and Communications announcement of October 22, 2026](https://www.motc.gov.tw/ch/app/data/view?id=14&module=news&serno=f2444dd7-f8b3-446b-9346-4a2dfcaa2bf5) proposes expressly regulating platforms entrusted to dispatch taxis online. It calls for an operating license within one year after amendment. As of October 23, this is a proposed amendment, not a finalized rule in force. The announcement started no countdown. A launch team cannot use it to postpone examining the permissions its planned operation needs today.

Which entity will apply? For a taxi passenger service business, [Highway Act Article 56](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=K0040001&flno=56) requires approval from the local highway authority. Identifying that business requires an account of how the service works. The operating plan should be checked against the actual allocation of booking, dispatch and fee collection, including functions retained by the foreign platform.

Two existing dispatch methods are set out in [Article 2 of the taxi-service approval regulations, 計程車客運服務業申請核准經營辦法](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=K0040027&flno=2). After receiving a consumer's ride request, the business provides a paid service to taxi operators and drivers. It either assigns a specific taxi within the same operating area as the pickup location, or assigns one or more taxis for the consumer to choose before the chosen vehicle goes to collect the passenger. Selection alone does not resolve licensing.

The vehicle operation needs its own classification too: [Highway Act Article 34](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=K0040001&flno=34) separates taxi passenger transport, passenger-car rental and other transport businesses. For dispatch licensing, [Article 4 of the approval regulations](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=K0040027&flno=4) requires preparatory approval and an operating plan. Registration, establishment approval and receipt of the operating license precede lawful operation under [Article 6](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=K0040027&flno=6). Company registration alone does not suffice. Related transport-entry background appears in [setting up a Taiwan logistics business](/en/columns/taiwan-logistics-business-setup).

## The partner agreement and control over drivers

Headquarters must decide which partnership responsibilities to accept. A contract assigning driver management to the fleet should be examined alongside the instructions that staff will actually give and the powers retained in the platform. This matters to a liability assessment as well as to the operating plan. The label does not decide liability.

In [Taiwan High Court case 110年度上更一字第40號, decided May 28, 2025](https://judgment.judicial.gov.tw/FJUD/data.aspx?ty=JD&id=TPHV,110,上更一,40,20250528,1), a fleet membership agreement formed part of the evidence supporting the fleet's joint liability with its driver. A motorcyclist had died in a collision with the taxi. The court considered the agreement alongside company markings, the roof sign and dispatch equipment. Objectively, the driver appeared to perform services under the fleet's selection, direction and supervision. No formal employment contract was needed.

Liability rested on [Civil Code Article 188](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=188), concerning unlawful injury caused in the execution of duties. Its defense allows the employer to establish due care in selection and supervision, or that the harm would still have occurred with such care. The fleet did not establish either ground.

This was an appellate decision after a Supreme Court remand. The judgment states that the fleet and driver could not appeal further; the victim's family retained an appeal route. Finality of the entire case has not been verified. For a foreign platform negotiating a different arrangement, the case supports examining the working relationship. It supplies no automatic answer about that platform's liability.

## Service functions that need an assigned owner

Booking screens and data retention belong in the same launch discussion. For operators providing diversified taxi services, [Regulations for Automobile Transportation Operators Article 91](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=K0040003&flno=91) already requires vehicle, driver, route and fare information and limits rides to advance bookings. Operating records must be kept for at least two years. These are requirements for that specified class of operators, not a rule imposed indiscriminately on every software vendor. The commercial agreement needs to account for who will provide the relevant functions when the service falls within that class.

Proposed changes extend beyond licensing. The [October 22 proposal](https://www.motc.gov.tw/ch/app/data/view?id=14&module=news&serno=f2444dd7-f8b3-446b-9346-4a2dfcaa2bf5) addresses service quality, driver training, checks on third-party liability insurance and consumer disputes. It also proposes regulatory approval of driver service fees, participation in multiple fleets and independent handling of complaints about suspension or contract termination. A US product team can identify which of its fee settings, account controls and support arrangements would need review if those provisions are adopted. Their final wording and commencement remain unresolved.

Accidents test that allocation. Booking records, the terms applicable to the trip, management instructions and insurance documents help counsel assess the parties' roles. A passenger's injury, a driver's own injury and harm to another road user may involve different claimants and legal relationships. The motorcyclist case above did not decide whether a platform must compensate a driver's own injury.

For headquarters, a useful review result identifies which entity seeks the current permission, which contractual duties it can perform, and which proposed changes still require a later decision. A Taiwan lawyer can prepare licensing and operating-plan submissions, review that allocation and submit comments on the draft.
