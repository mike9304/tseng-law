# Taiwan's 400th high-skill naturalization: the two-year route and paper review

Taiwan's Ministry of the Interior said on [September 12, 2026](https://www.moi.gov.tw/News_Content.aspx?n=4&s=340921) that it had approved the 400th high-skill naturalization on August 26. The figure counts people approved since the program began in December 2016.

Official figures report 170 people from Asia, 128 from the Americas, 76 from Europe, and 13 each from Oceania and Africa; education and academia account for 149, the economy 85, science and technology 61, and culture and the arts 50. A [TVBS feature on November 2](https://news.tvbs.com.tw/politics/4043722) reported 101 Americans, a media figure separate from the ministry's announcement.

## The two-year residence route eased in 2024

The high-skill route appears in [Article 5](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0030001&flno=5)(1)(3) of the Nationality Act. It requires recommendation by the relevant central agency, a contribution to national interests and a joint review by the Ministry of the Interior. Domicile in Taiwan is also required. [Article 3](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0030001&flno=3)(1)(2)–(5) covers capacity, conduct and criminal record, assets or professional skills for self-support, basic language ability, and knowledge of citizens' rights and duties.

Two residence routes exist. An amendment dated May 24, 2024 eased one route to at least 183 days of lawful residence per year for two consecutive years. The other calls for five continuous years of lawful residence in Taiwan, with no annual 183-day requirement. Recommendation and review remain necessary under either route.

## Paper review for specified permanent residents

Article 25(3)(2) or (3) of the [Immigration Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0080132&flno=25) allows high-skill professionals needed by Taiwan to seek permanent residence without the usual residence period. The plum-blossom card is the permanent-residence card for this high-skill route.

Article 3(2) of the [standards for identifying high-skill professionals for naturalization](https://www.ris.gov.tw/documents/data/2/1/a62bfcb8-c985-4e7b-9eb2-337e6dc800d9.pdf) allows the ministry to seek the relevant agency's agreement to a recommendation and conduct a paper review. It is limited to people who obtained permanent residence under Article 25(3)(2) or (3), not every APRC holder. Permanent residence and naturalization remain separate procedures.

## An Employment Gold Card is not permanent residence or naturalization

Under Article 9 of the [Act for the Recruitment and Employment of Foreign Professionals](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=A0030295&flno=9), an employment gold card is initially valid for one to three years. A qualifying holder may extend it before expiry, with each extension lasting up to three years. Holding one neither qualifies the holder for this paper review by itself nor automatically produces naturalization approval.

## Original citizenship: general caution and one country's example

Taiwan's one-year deadline for proof of loss and the high-skill exemption are covered in [our earlier naturalization column](/en/columns/taiwan-naturalization-keep-original-nationality). The effect on original citizenship varies by country. For example, Article 11(1) of Japan's [Nationality Act](https://laws.e-gov.go.jp/law/325AC0000000147) provides that a Japanese national who voluntarily acquires a foreign nationality loses Japanese nationality. Taiwan's document waiver does not decide that separate question under the applicant's home-country law.
