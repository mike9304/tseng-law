## Exact FAQ/body contracts

### FAQ 1

- Q:
  `What is the difference between a subsidiary, branch, and representative office when establishing a business in Taiwan?`
- A:
  `A Taiwan subsidiary (limited company or company limited by shares) is a separate legal entity under Taiwan law. A Taiwan branch of a foreign company is not a separate legal entity; it operates in Taiwan as part of the foreign company. A representative office may not conduct profit-making business in Taiwan; its activities are limited to liaison work and legal acts on behalf of the foreign company. Liability, tax treatment, licensing requirements, and eligibility for government procurement must be evaluated based on the chosen structure and the circumstances.`
- Repeat the answer verbatim as the first paragraph immediately after section 1.

### FAQ 2

- Q:
  `Does forming a company automatically qualify me for a Taiwan work permit or residence?`
- A:
  `No. Forming a company does not by itself confer work authorization or residence status. A foreign national who will manage or operate a business in Taiwan must meet the applicable work-permit requirements, including requirements relating to the proposed role, the investment relationship, and the employer’s business performance. After obtaining the work permit, the individual must separately apply for an Alien Resident Certificate (ARC) appropriate to the purpose of residence.`
- Repeat the answer verbatim as the first paragraph immediately after the first
  section-4 H3.

### FAQ 3

- Q:
  `Is minimum capital required for a work permit and Alien Resident Certificate (ARC)?`
- A:
  `Taiwan does not impose a generally applicable statutory minimum capital requirement for company formation. Industry-specific capital requirements, the reasonableness of the business plan, bank review, and employer-qualification rules for work permits must be assessed separately. The foreign-manager work-permit category for a foreign-invested business covers, among others, the manager (經理人) of a company in which overseas Chinese or foreign investors collectively hold more than one-third of the issued shares or total capital, the manager of a Taiwan branch of a foreign company, and the representative of a representative office. For a company or branch established for less than one year, the employer generally must satisfy at least one of the following: paid-in capital or Taiwan working capital of at least NT$500,000; revenue of at least NT$3 million; import-export performance of at least US$500,000; or agency commissions of at least US$200,000. For a company or branch established for at least one year, the employer generally must satisfy at least one of the following, measured by the most recent year in Taiwan or the average of the preceding three years: revenue of at least NT$3 million; import-export performance of at least US$500,000; or agency commissions of at least US$200,000. A representative office established for at least one year must have a record of activities in Taiwan; this requirement is waived if it has been established for less than one year. Special approval may be available where the business makes a substantial contribution to Taiwan’s economic development or special circumstances exist.`
- Repeat the answer verbatim as the first paragraph immediately after the second
  section-4 H3.

## Body fact contract

### Introduction

Describe, without marketing statistics, why the legal structure depends on the
business model, counterparties, cash flow, personnel, and premises. Explain that
company formation, foreign-investment review, banking, tax registration,
business premises, work authorization, and residence are related but separate.

### 1. Entity forms and the income-tax agreement

- A limited company or company limited by shares is a separate Taiwan entity.
- A Taiwan branch is part of the foreign company, has no shareholders of its
  own, and its foreign head office bears its obligations.
- A representative office cannot conduct ordinary sales or provide paid
  services.
- Explain why structure names alone do not answer liability, capital,
  remittance, tax, licensing, employment, or procurement questions.
- The Taiwan–Korea agreement entered into force December 27, 2023 and applies
  from January 1, 2024.
- The maximum source-country rate for qualifying dividends, interest, and
  royalties is 10%.
- Explain all four possible permanent-establishment categories:
  1. fixed facilities such as a place of management, branch, or office;
  2. projects exceeding six months;
  3. services exceeding 183 aggregate days in any 12-month period;
  4. an agent repeatedly exercising authority to conclude contracts.
- Never reduce the PE analysis or business-profit taxation to the service-day
  threshold.

### 2. General formation outline

Present this as a qualified overview, not a universal fixed checklist:

1. Preliminary review and reservation of the company’s Chinese name and
   registered business activities
2. Notarization or authentication of foreign documents, including powers of
   attorney, and Taiwan overseas-office authentication where required
3. Foreign-investment application to the Department of Investment Review,
   Ministry of Economic Affairs (MOEA), where applicable
4. Opening a preparatory bank account
5. Remittance of foreign investment funds
6. Investment amount verification (投資額審定)
7. Company registration
8. Tax registration
9. Conversion of the preparatory account into a regular company account
10. Additional import-export, industry-license, work-permit, or residence
    procedures, where applicable

Explain that order, necessity, and timing depend on entity form, investment,
industry, review, bank process, corrections, and whether the investor is an
individual or entity of a particular nationality.

### 3. Activities and premises

- Separate registered business activities from permission to operate.
- Address prohibited/restricted industries, professional qualifications,
  industry licenses, land-use zoning, building rules, lease terms, and
  suitability for tax registration.
- Limit the advance inquiry for business premises
  (營業場所預先查詢) to Taipei City and do not say it replaces other permits.

### 4. Work permits, residence, and capital

- A student may invest or form a company, but investment/shareholder status
  does not authorize work or management under the current immigration status.
- The FAQ 3 figures are employer qualifications for a work permit, not general
  formation capital; meeting them does not guarantee approval.
- A qualifying spouse and minor children must separately apply for dependent
  residence; it is not automatic.
- Ordinary foreign nationals generally need five consecutive years of lawful
  residence and at least 183 days in each year for permanent residence, subject
  to conduct, assets/skills, excluded periods, and other requirements.
  Some foreign professionals may have different calculations. Five years of a
  work permit or ARC is not automatic permanent residence.

### 5. Taxes

Include this paragraph verbatim:

`Taiwan’s general business tax rate is 5%, and returns are generally filed every two months. The general profit-seeking enterprise income tax rate is 20%, although actual liability depends on taxable income and the applicable rules. Under Taiwan domestic law, dividends paid to a nonresident are generally subject to withholding at 21%. Dividends that qualify for the Taiwan–Korea Income Tax Agreement are subject to a maximum source-country rate of 10%. The applicable filing and withholding treatment depends on the taxpayer’s residence status, beneficial ownership, the character of the income, and the documents required to claim treaty benefits.`

Connect the treaty dates, 10% interest/royalty caps, four PE types, and claim
documents without duplicating the opening explanation.
