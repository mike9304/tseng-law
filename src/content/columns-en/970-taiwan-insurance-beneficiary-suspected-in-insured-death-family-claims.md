# Your Parent's Spouse Is Under Investigation for the Death: Taiwan Life Insurance, the Estate and a Two-Year Clock

Two years. A claim under a Taiwan insurance contract lapses if no one exercises it within two years from the day it could be made ([Insurance Act, Article 65](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0390002&flno=65)). The same article moves the start date in three listed cases, including where an interested party proves that, through no carelessness of their own, they did not know the insured event had happened; the two years then run from when they learned of it. A criminal case about how the insured died can take longer than that.

A Taiwanese father dies in Kaohsiung (an invented example). His second wife is the named beneficiary of his life policy and one of his heirs, and prosecutors are investigating her in connection with the death. His daughter from his first marriage is a U.S. citizen living in Ohio. She is also an heir, and because her father was a Taiwan national, Taiwan's Civil Code governs the inheritance ([Act Governing the Choice of Law in Civil Matters Involving Foreign Elements, Article 58](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000007&flno=58)). The wife has not been charged, and even if she is, she is presumed innocent until a guilty judgment becomes final ([Code of Criminal Procedure, Article 154](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0010001&flno=154)).

## The insurer's timetable and the family's

Insurers in this position may hold the money until the facts are clearer. The statute gives them a timetable. Once the policyholder or insured has handed in all the supporting documents, the insurer must pay within the period the contract sets, or within 15 days of notice if the contract sets none. Late payment for reasons attributable to the insurer carries default interest of 10 percent a year ([Insurance Act, Article 34](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0390002&flno=34)). Whether "the cause of death is still under investigation" counts as such a reason is something the two sides can argue about.

What the daughter can claim comes first. She is not named on the policy. Her route to the policy money opens only if the wife has lost her right and no other beneficiary remains, so that the sum falls into the estate under Article 121(2). The estate's claim is then held jointly by all the heirs until division. Exercising a jointly held right needs every joint holder's consent unless a law provides otherwise ([Civil Code, Article 831](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=831), applying [Article 828](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=828), para. 3). Until the disinheritance question is decided, the wife is one of those heirs.

Then the clock. A written demand takes effect when it reaches the insurer ([Article 95](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=95), para. 1), and that demand interrupts the limitation period ([Article 129](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=129), para. 1, item 1). If no suit follows within six months, the law treats the period as never interrupted ([Article 130](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=130)). Applying to the court for mediation has the same effect as filing suit (Article 129, para. 2, item 2). That effect also falls away if the application is withdrawn or dismissed or the mediation fails ([Article 133](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=133)). After a failed mediation, a suit filed within ten days of service of the certificate of failure counts as filed on the day mediation was sought ([Code of Civil Procedure, Article 419](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=419), para. 3).

Filing does not force a fast answer. When a suspected crime bears on the outcome, the civil court may stay the case until the criminal proceedings end ([Code of Civil Procedure, Article 183](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=183)). The claim stays alive while it waits.

## Where the policy money goes if the wife loses it

Normally the payout is not part of the estate at all. Money a policy says will be paid on the insured's death to a named beneficiary may not be treated as the insured's estate ([Insurance Act, Article 112](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0390002&flno=112)).

[Article 121](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0390002&flno=121) changes that picture. Its first paragraph reads: 「受益人故意致被保險人於死或雖未致死者，喪失其受益權。」 A beneficiary who intentionally causes the insured's death, or intentionally tries to and the insured survives, loses the beneficial right. Under the second paragraph, if that loss leaves no beneficiary to receive the money, the sum becomes part of the insured's estate. The article says nothing about a criminal sentence.

Who signed the policy also matters. Under the third paragraph, if the policyholder (要保人) intentionally causes the insured's death, the insurer owes no benefit at all; where premiums have been paid for two years or more, it pays the policy value reserve (保單價值準備金) to the person entitled to it, or to the national treasury if no one is.

Accident cover follows a different article. For personal accident insurance, [Article 134](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0390002&flno=134) denies the claim of a beneficiary who intentionally injured the insured, and [Article 135](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0390002&flno=135), which lists the life-insurance rules that also apply to accident insurance, does not include Article 121. Travel policies are built in different ways, so the policy wording decides which set of rules covers the death benefit.

## Could the money come back to her as an heir?

If the life insurance money enters the father's estate under Article 121(2), the wife's right to inherit becomes a separate question. She is still his spouse. Does that give her a spouse's share of the money she lost as beneficiary?

That depends on [Civil Code Article 1145](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=1145), para. 1, item 1, which disinherits a person who 「故意致被繼承人或應繼承人於死或雖未致死因而受刑之宣告者」: someone who intentionally caused the death of the deceased or of a person entitled to inherit, or who did not cause death but was sentenced for the act. Does the phrase about a criminal sentence attach only to the "did not cause death" case, or to the death case as well? The wording does not settle it, and readings differ. Unlike Article 121, this article does mention a sentence.

If she keeps her status, she shares equally with the father's children ([Article 1144](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=1144), item 1). If she loses it, her share does not pass to her own descendants, because substitution under [Article 1140](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=1140) covers only the share of a first-order heir, that is, a descendant of the deceased.

Until that question is answered, the estate sits in joint ownership among all heirs ([Article 1151](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=1151)). Selling or otherwise disposing of jointly owned property needs the consent of every joint owner unless a law says otherwise ([Article 828](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=828), para. 3). The heirs may pick one of themselves to manage the estate in the meantime ([Article 1152](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=1152)). And if the estate is divided with her included and she is later found to have lost her right, the daughter can sue to recover her inheritance within two years of learning of the infringement, and no later than ten years after the death ([Article 1146](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=1146)).
