# Taiwan biotech licenses: sublicensing patents and know-how

Can an exclusive technology license cover onward use by an affiliate or outside developer? [Economic Daily reported on November 13](https://money.udn.com/money/story/5612/9877137) that Taiwan's OBI Pharma had signed a collaboration and license agreement with Novartis Pharma AG. The report describes a worldwide exclusive license to OBI's antibody-drug conjugate platform technology, patents and know-how for specified, undisclosed payloads.

For an international biotech team negotiating a comparable package, each type of right carries its own permission question. Taiwan's rules for invention patents and protected trade secrets differ. No governing-law clause appears in the report. Nor does it disclose sublicensing terms, so those rules cannot establish what the parties to this particular agreement authorized.

## The exclusive patent grant and its limits

[Patent Act Article 62](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0070007&flno=62) allows exclusive and nonexclusive invention-patent licenses. Within an exclusive license's scope, the licensee may exclude the patentee and third parties from practicing the invention. The scope still matters: exclusivity attaches to the rights actually granted.

Under [Article 63](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0070007&flno=63), an exclusive licensee may sublicense those rights to another party. The contract may provide otherwise. A nonexclusive licensee needs consent from the invention patentee or exclusive licensee before sublicensing. A clause restricting permitted recipients or requiring approval therefore deserves attention even where the commercial summary calls the arrangement exclusive.

Recording has a separate function. Article 62 says a license cannot be asserted against third parties without recording with the patent authority; Article 63 applies that rule to sublicenses. These provisions concern Taiwan patent rights. They do not turn a worldwide announcement into proof that any particular Taiwan license has been recorded, or make recording synonymous with the parties' agreement on consent.

## Know-how must qualify for trade-secret protection

A description such as know-how does not settle its legal status. [Trade Secrets Act Article 2](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0080028&flno=2) requires information not to be generally known to people who ordinarily deal with that type of information, to have actual or potential economic value because it is secret, and to be protected by reasonable secrecy measures taken by its owner. All three elements are required.

For information meeting those conditions, [Article 7](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0080028&flno=7) leaves the licensed territory, term, content and methods of use to the parties' agreement. It also prohibits the licensee from sublicensing the secret without the owner's consent. Calling the package exclusive does not remove that consent requirement. Consent may already be expressed in the contract, but its reach has to cover the proposed use and recipient.

## The work assigned to another company

An outsourcing plan brings the distinction into practical focus. The team may intend to give another company a process description, access to technical information and permission to carry out development work. Whether that arrangement constitutes a sublicense depends on the rights granted and the actual work; an outside provider's involvement alone does not answer it.

The same review applies when the proposed recipient is an affiliate. A group organization chart identifies the company, while the license identifies the permitted users and activities. Reading the patent grant, the information-use provisions and the consent clause together exposes gaps that a single description of the deal may hide.

A transfer record can connect the recipient's work to the relevant patent rights and protected information. That connection lets the parties resolve consent before the recipient receives material the existing grant does not authorize it to use.
