# When a Taiwan Moving Company Adds Fees on Moving Day or Breaks Your Things

Everything is on the truck when the crew leader says the new building's elevator is down: NT$4,000 more for the stairs, or nothing goes up. Do you have to pay? If the mover used its own standard-form contract, Taiwan's mandatory moving terms bar that charge unless a method was agreed beforehand.

Someone moves from a studio in Taipei's Da'an District to a fifth-floor flat in Banqiao, New Taipei, for a flat NT$18,000. The elevator is down on the day. Two days later a crack turns up in a glass cabinet door (an invented example).

## Mandatory terms that apply even when unwritten

On April 5, 2004, the Ministry of Transportation and Communications announced the Mandatory and Prohibited Terms for Standard-Form Moving Contracts (搬家貨運定型化契約應記載及不得記載事項, Jiao-Lu No. 0930003581). The [Executive Yuan's posting](https://www.ey.gov.tw/Page/DFB720D019CCCB0A/dfd3e696-8ff5-4708-9738-7763b15b37a1) shows that date and no amendment.

Why do they bind a contract that omits them? Under [Consumer Protection Act art. 17](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0170001&flno=17)(5), published mandatory terms form part of a standard-form contract even if left out. An office relocation, not done for consumption, may fall outside ([art. 2](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0170001&flno=2)(1), (7), (9)).

The terms assume the mover shows its license first. A carrier dedicated to moving must put "搬家" (moving) in its business name ([Motor Transport Business Regulations art. 104](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=K0040003&flno=104)(2)). Operating without approval can draw a fine of NT$100,000 to NT$25 million ([Highway Act art. 77](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=K0040001&flno=77)(2)).

## Pricing stops at the agreed method

Under Clause 4(1) of the mandatory terms, the mover must charge by the agreed pricing method and may not raise the price on any pretext or demand any additional fee. Crew meals and travel, bridge and road tolls, and fuel are on the mover (Clause 4(2)). [Regulations art. 111-1](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=K0040003&flno=111-1) likewise bars arbitrary increases.

Clause 3 allows a flat price or per-trip pricing with a unit price, estimated trips and a total ceiling.

Per-trip pricing has a built-in limit: trips up to one fifth (20%) over the estimate are billed at the unit price, and trips beyond that are free. Five estimated trips at NT$5,000, seven needed: trip six costs NT$5,000, trip seven nothing. A lower total ceiling, if written, binds first.

## The elevator that stopped

If the elevator cannot be used for reasons other than the mover's fault, a charging method must be agreed; with no agreement, there is no extra fee (Clause 4(3)). The Highway Bureau, MOTC publishes a [model moving contract](https://ws.thb.gov.tw/Download.ashx?u=LzAwMS9VcGxvYWQvT2xkRmlsZS9yZXNvdXJjZS91cGxvYWQvRG93bmxvYWQvNjNhNTQ1ZGEtNTBiZi00ZGY1LTk4YzItOTJiZjk2N2VjYWMwLnBkZg%3D%3D&n=5pCs5a626LKo6YGL5a6a5Z6L5YyW5aWR57SELnBkZg%3D%3D) whose estimate sheet has boxes for this: a per-floor rate, "no extra charge" or other. Its own Clause 4 omits the no-agreement, no-fee rule; this column follows the mandatory terms.

Estimate sheets form part of the contract (Clause 12). Blank elevator box, flat-price contract: the NT$4,000 has no contractual basis. The customer must confirm in advance that the elevator works (Clause 6(2)); keep the building's booking record.

## "Pay, or we don't unload"

[Article 124-1](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=K0040003&flno=124-1) of the regulations requires a moving carrier to take items to the place the customer designates and forbids refusing midway on a pretext. Clause 2(2) of the mandatory terms requires delivery to the designated spot but has no refusal wording. Breaches draw a NT$9,000–90,000 fine from the highway authority (Highway Act art. 77(1)).

Carriers do hold a lien over the goods, in proportion, to secure freight and other charges ([Civil Code art. 647](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=647)(1)). A consignee disputing the amount may deposit it with the court and demand delivery (art. 647(2)), a court procedure.

Can money paid under pressure be recovered? A benefit gained without legal ground at another's expense must be returned ([Civil Code art. 179](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=179)). Ask for the receipt the mover must issue (Clause 5) to say "stair surcharge, amount disputed," or send that by message.

## Late starts and unfinished jobs

If the mover starts late, the customer may seek a price cut or cancel, and claim damages (Clause 10). Where the mover's side makes the job overrun, the customer may cancel and claim restoration and compensation (Clause 11(1)). If the customer's side is the cause, the mover may cancel, charge pro rata and claim up to one fifth of the remainder. Where non-completion is due to the mover, the model contract caps the mover's compensation at one fifth of the original estimate (its Clause 14). The mandatory terms set no such cap on that compensation.

## Who proves what when something breaks

Civil Code carriage rules apply: a carrier carries goods or passengers for freight as a business ([art. 622](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=622)), and [Regulations art. 107](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=K0040003&flno=107) sends freight-loss and damage claims to the Civil Code unless the Highway Act provides otherwise.

[Civil Code art. 634](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=634) makes the carrier liable for loss, damage or delay unless it proves force majeure, the nature of the goods, or fault of the shipper or consignee. Clause 7(1) lets the mover escape liability by proving force majeure, the goods' nature, or fault of the customer or the customer's employees.

Your part comes first. A party asserting a favorable fact must prove it ([Code of Civil Procedure art. 277](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=277)), so show the cabinet door was intact before the move. Photos and video from the day before do that. So does the item list on the estimate sheet.

Packing matters. With an obvious packaging defect, the carrier is liable if it accepted the goods without reservation ([art. 635](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=635)). Self-packed boxes invite a nature-of-goods or customer-fault defense. Photograph the packing.

Damages follow the item's value at the destination when delivery was due ([art. 638](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=638)(1)). For a years-old cabinet, new retail price is not the measure. Intent or gross negligence adds other losses (art. 638(3)).

## Three days, ten days, one year

Notice is due within 3 days of completion, or 10 days for damage not easy to discover (Clause 7(2)). Nothing says what happens after day 3. Send photos in writing the day you find it.

The Civil Code is stricter. If the consignee takes the goods and pays the freight and other charges without reservation, the carrier's liability ends ([art. 648](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=648)(1)), unless hidden internal damage is notified within 10 days (648(2)) or the carrier concealed it by fraud or acted intentionally or with gross negligence (648(3)). When paying the balance, note "damage not yet checked, claims reserved" on the receipt or by message.

One year from the end of carriage is the outer limit ([art. 623](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=623)(1)). [Highway Act art. 54](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=K0040001&flno=54) also sets one year, counted from the day delivery was due.

## Valuables and "NT$3,000 per item"

Money, securities, jewelry and other valuables are not covered unless their nature and value were declared, and then only up to the declared value ([art. 639](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=639)). Clause 8 adds precious metals, art and antiques, yet keeps the mover liable for undeclared valuables if it was at fault. The model estimate sheet has a valuables list.

Movers sometimes cite a NT$3,000-per-item cap, found in [Highway Act art. 64](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=K0040001&flno=64)(2), for goods damaged in a traffic accident (行車事故) involving a motor carrier, unless nature and value were declared before loading and noted in the contract; [Regulations art. 105](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=K0040003&flno=105)(3) puts the cap in posted carriage contracts. The trigger in the text is a traffic accident. Clause 7 has no per-item cap.

Limitation wording faces two more rules. A liability exclusion or limit in a document the carrier gave the shipper is void unless the shipper's express consent is proven ([art. 649](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=649)). Standard terms clearly unfair to consumers are void ([CPA art. 12](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0170001&flno=12)), and doubtful terms are read in the consumer's favor ([art. 11](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0170001&flno=11)(2)).

## Copies, review time and complaints

Each side keeps a copy of the contract and estimate sheet; the mover may not take it back (Clause 14; [CPA art. 13](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0170001&flno=13)(3)). Consumers get a reasonable review period of up to 30 days, and a waiver is void ([CPA art. 11-1](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0170001&flno=11-1)(1), (2)).

Complaints go to the business, a consumer group or a consumer service center, and the business must handle them properly within 15 days of filing ([CPA art. 43](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0170001&flno=43)); then to the local consumer protection officer, then mediation ([art. 44](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0170001&flno=44)). Claims of NT$100,000 or less use small claims procedure ([Code of Civil Procedure art. 436-8](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=436-8)(1)); see the [small claims column](/en/columns/taiwan-small-claims-simplified-civil-procedure) and the [consumer complaint column](/en/columns/taiwan-online-consumer-dispute-foreigners). A forum clause cannot displace statutory venue rules (Clause 13), and consumer suits may be filed where the consumer relationship arose ([CPA art. 47](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0170001&flno=47)).
