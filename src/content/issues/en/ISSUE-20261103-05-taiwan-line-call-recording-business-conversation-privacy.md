# Recording a LINE business call in Taiwan: who may keep and share it?

LINE opened a free trial of recording for one-to-one voice and video calls on October 30, according to its [announcement](https://www.linecorp.com/pr/news/2026/1030-2) and [Central News Agency's report](https://www.cna.com.tw/news/ait/202610300163.aspx). The feature lets users save a conversation to their device or Keep Memo after the call. LINE's [Help Center](https://help.line.me/line/smartphone?contentId=200003130&fid=default&fpid=fp_66oabpxlmvuafl35&lang=en&var=A) says both accounts must have the same registered country or region, currently Japan, Taiwan or Thailand.

Both participants normally receive a recording alert, but LINE says someone using an older app version may miss it. For an English-speaking manager in Taiwan discussing an order with a local supplier, the recording button alone does not establish what the supplier knew or agreed to.

## The participant exception has a condition

[Article 29, subparagraph 3 of the Communication Security and Surveillance Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=K0060044&flno=29) exempts a person from punishment under that Act when the person conducting surveillance is a party to the communication, or has one party's prior consent, and is not acting for an unlawful purpose. Both parts matter. Participation alone does not remove the purpose requirement.

For a manager who actually joins the call, the statutory question includes why it was recorded. A colleague's access to the manager's phone does not make that colleague a participant. The alternative in the article requires a participant's prior consent, together with the same restriction on unlawful purpose.

Civil privacy remains relevant. Under [Civil Code Article 195](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=195), an unlawful infringement of privacy can support compensation for nonfinancial harm. A person cannot resolve that question merely by pointing to the exception in the surveillance law. The purpose, circumstances and extent of disclosure still need examination.

## A company copy carries its own data questions

A business conversation can contain personal data. [Personal Data Protection Act Article 2](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=I0050021&flno=2) covers information that directly or indirectly identifies an individual, including occupation and contact details. The Act's exemption for an individual's purely personal or household activities, in [Article 51](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=I0050021&flno=51), does not turn a company's customer-call archive into a household activity.

Where the Act applies, [Article 19](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=I0050021&flno=19) requires a specific collection purpose and a listed legal basis for ordinary personal data. Consent is one basis. A contractual or similar relationship is another, but that basis also requires appropriate security measures. The specially protected data identified in [Article 6](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=I0050021&flno=6) is outside this ordinary-data route.

Direct collection generally also brings the notice requirements in [Article 8](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=I0050021&flno=8), unless an exception applies. Those requirements cover who is collecting, why, the data categories, how and where the data will be used, for how long and by whom, and the person's rights. Where provision is optional, the consequences of withholding the data are part of the notice. A brief app alert addresses only part of that information.

## What happens after the call

Who will hear the file next? An internal recipient reviewing a disputed order, a training audience and the public are different audiences. The intended use and people with access need to be understood before the recording moves beyond its original conversation.

[Article 20](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=I0050021&flno=20) generally confines use of ordinary personal data to the necessary scope of the collection purpose. Another purpose requires one of its listed grounds, such as the person's consent. Whether a proposed use stays within the original purpose depends on that purpose and the actual use; describing every later use as “business” supplies no answer.

For a Taiwan office receiving a saved call, the useful record includes who participated, why recording began, what notice was given and the proposed recipients. The same audio file may document an order accurately while still carrying information that the next audience has no reason to receive.
