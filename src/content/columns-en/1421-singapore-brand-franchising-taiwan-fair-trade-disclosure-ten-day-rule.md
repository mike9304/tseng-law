# Franchising a Singapore brand in Taiwan: what must you disclose ten days before signing?

Can a Singapore food, retail, education, or wellness brand take a Taiwan franchise deposit before handing over its full fee breakdown? Taking a binding deposit early can draw substantial administrative fines. As of November 24, 2026, Taiwan's Fair Trade Commission (公平交易委員會) polices franchise recruitment through its [Disposal Directions on the Business Practices of Franchisors](https://www.ftc.gov.tw/internet/main/doc/docDetail.aspx?uid=167&docid=11795&mid=173) (公平交易委員會對於加盟業主經營行為案件之處理原則, last amended July 11, 2018 and promulgated August 1, 2018). Those rules operate under [Article 25](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0150002&flno=25) and [Article 42](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0150002&flno=42) of the Fair Trade Act (公平交易法).

A Singapore brand that brings its regional franchise binder and expo sign-up forms to Taipei, Taichung, or Kaohsiung faces two questions: when was the information handed over, and can the brand prove it? Disclosure timing, review buffers, and Taiwan trademark status determine whether local recruitment complies from day one.

## What counts as a franchise or preliminary deposit

Contract titles do not decide whether Taiwan's franchise rules apply. Point 2 of the [Disposal Directions](https://www.ftc.gov.tw/internet/main/doc/docDetail.aspx?uid=167&docid=11795&mid=173) defines a franchise relationship (加盟經營關係) by its substance: a continuing contract where a franchisor licenses trademarks or operating know-how, assists or guides the franchisee's operations, and collects specified consideration (對價) from the franchisee.

Consideration reaches beyond the upfront franchise fee. Under Point 2, item 5, specified consideration includes franchise fees, royalties, training fees, and payments for goods, raw materials, capital equipment, or store fit-out works paid to the franchisor or its designee. Item 3 sets one clear carve-out: merely purchasing goods or services at or below wholesale price for resale or rental falls outside the definition.

A deposit taken before the main contract can already count. Point 2, item 4 defines a preliminary franchise relationship (預備加盟經營關係) as an arrangement where the counterparty pays a fee, signs a draft agreement, reservation sheet, letter of intent, or similar franchise document, and agrees that backing out forfeits the payment or creates liability for damages. A brand that takes a non-refundable booking deposit with a signed reservation form at a Taipei expo has formed a preliminary franchise relationship. Point 3 then asks whether the seven categories reached that prospect ten days before the form was signed, or within a case-specific reasonable or agreed period (an invented example).

## Seven disclosure categories and the ten-day clock

Point 3 of the [Disposal Directions](https://www.ftc.gov.tw/internet/main/doc/docDetail.aspx?uid=167&docid=11795&mid=173) treats it as obviously unfair for a franchisor not to provide seven categories of important franchise information to the counterparty for review at least ten days before concluding a franchise or preliminary franchise relationship—or within a case-specific reasonable period or an agreed period—unless a justified reason applies.

Every recruiting packet needs these seven categories:

1. Pre-opening fees: exact or estimated amounts paid to the franchisor or its designee before opening, including franchise fees, training fees, goods, raw materials, capital equipment, and store fit-out.
2. Ongoing operating fees: exact or estimated amounts paid during operations, including how royalties are calculated and collected, plus fees for guidance, marketing, goods, or raw materials.
3. Licensed intellectual property: the name, scope of use, and restrictions for licensed trademarks, patents, copyrights, and other IP rights.
4. Operational assistance and training: the content and methods of business assistance and training guidance.
5. Same-system outlets in the area: business plans or scheduled plans for opening other outlets of the same system within the franchisee's operating area.
6. Contract-term restrictions: obligations such as buying goods or raw materials from designated sources (or specified brands and specs), minimum order items and quantities, designated capital equipment, designated fit-out contractors or specs, and other operating restrictions.
7. Contract changes and exit terms: conditions and procedures for amending, terminating, and rescinding the franchise contract.

Paper, email, electronic storage devices, social media, and messaging apps are all permitted delivery channels under Point 3, paragraph 2. Proof rests on the franchisor. Because the franchisor must prove that it provided the information, keep a dated record of what was sent and when.

Under Point 3, paragraph 3, three justified reasons permit omitting an item: continuing or expanding an existing franchise relationship, an item the franchisor objectively lacks, or another situation with no information asymmetry between the parties. A brand recruiting its first Taiwan franchisee has no existing relationship to continue or expand.

## Five days to review and thirty days to deliver the contract

The contract review period is at least five days. Under Point 4, item 1 of the [Disposal Directions](https://www.ftc.gov.tw/internet/main/doc/docDetail.aspx?uid=167&docid=11795&mid=173), a franchisor must not sign without giving the counterparty at least five days—or a case-specific reasonable period—to review the agreement. If the final terms change on signing day, the counterparty has not had five days with the contract it actually signs.

Thirty days run from the signing date. Point 4, item 2 bars failing to deliver the executed contract within 30 days after signing, unless delay is not attributable to the franchisor. Listed examples cover shops on outlying islands or remote areas, real-estate mortgage steps beyond the franchisor's control, and delays caused by the franchisee.

| Compliance stage | Rule in Taiwan | FTC Directions |
|---|---|---|
| Disclosure of 7 categories | At least 10 days before franchise or preliminary franchise commitment (or case-specific reasonable / agreed period), absent justified reason | Point 3 |
| Contract review period | At least 5 days (or case-specific reasonable period) before signing | Point 4, item 1 |
| Delivery of signed contract | Within 30 days from signing date, unless delay is not attributable to the franchisor | Point 4, item 2 |

## Fair Trade Act penalties and Taiwan trademark rights

Point 5 of the [Disposal Directions](https://www.ftc.gov.tw/internet/main/doc/docDetail.aspx?uid=167&docid=11795&mid=173) ties recruitment defects directly to [Fair Trade Act Article 25](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0150002&flno=25). Violating Point 3 or Point 4, when sufficient to affect trading order, breaches Article 25's ban on deceptive or obviously unfair conduct sufficient to affect trading order.

[Article 42 of the Fair Trade Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0150002&flno=42) sets administrative fines in two tiers. For an Article 25 breach, the Fair Trade Commission may order the business to cease, rectify, or take corrective steps by a deadline, and may impose a fine of NT$50,000 to NT$25,000,000. If the business fails to comply by that deadline, the Commission may issue further orders and fine NT$100,000 to NT$50,000,000 per occasion until compliance. Point 6 notes that franchisors also remain subject to separate Commission principles on Article 21 cases, comparative advertising, and Article 25 cases.

Taiwan grants trademark rights through registration. [Article 2 of the Trademark Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0070001&flno=2) (商標法) requires applying for registration to acquire rights, while [Article 35](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0070001&flno=35) grants exclusive rights for the designated goods or services listed in that registration. Rights run for ten years from publication and renew in ten-year terms under [Article 33](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0070001&flno=33).

Why check Taiwan trademark registration before pitching local operators? If another party has already registered or filed an identical or similar mark for identical or similar goods or services and confusion among relevant consumers is likely, [Article 30, paragraph 1, item 10](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0070001&flno=30) bars the later application unless that owner consents and the consent is not clearly improper. In-force [Article 68](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawOldVer.aspx?pcode=J0070001) defines unauthorized use for marketing purposes as infringement. [Article 69](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0070001&flno=69) gives the infringement remedies—removal, prevention, destruction of infringing items, and damages for intentional or negligent infringement—to the owner of a registered trademark. A franchisor with no Taiwan registration cannot rely on those two articles. Because Point 3, item 3 asks for each licensed right's name, scope of use, and restrictions, the packet should say plainly if the mark is not yet registered in Taiwan.

## Preparing disclosure packets and franchise drafts

Whether a Singapore brand recruits unit franchisees directly or appoints a Taiwan master franchisee that recruits local outlets, the party acting as franchisor under Point 2 should sequence compliance before marketing begins. Assemble a dated disclosure packet covering all seven Point 3 categories and keep signed receipts or timestamped email records. Collect no non-refundable reservation fee and sign no binding reservation form until ten days after delivery—or until the case-specific reasonable or agreed period has run. File Taiwan trademark applications across store services and retail goods early, and confirm domestic franchising rules in Singapore separately.
