The coins were still there. A player converted the remaining game-account balance into a demand for NT$243,548, but the court refused the refund. Both sides agreed that no unused paid points remained, despite the displayed coins ([judgment reproduced by the Executive Yuan's Consumer Protection Department](https://cpc.ey.gov.tw/File/343C8EEBB5F122CF/6abbfdf1-cf31-4198-9eec-7421dab07bb8?A=C), printed pages 140–153).

On September 13, 2021, the Keelung District Court dismissed the player's appeal in case 109年度消簡上字第1號, with no further appeal available. The judgment examined what the coins did in this game and what the refund clause covered. It did not exclude every virtual item from consumer protection.

## What happened after the points were purchased?

Under the contract, the player first bought points and could then use them to obtain game services or goods. The remaining coins served as chips for play against other players. The court held that they were neither the unused paid points the contract promised to refund nor game fees received by the operator, and did not allow their conversion into a cash refund.

Allowing players to redeem these chips for cash from the operator would involve gambling, the court also reasoned. That observation was tied to how this game worked. For another game, the inquiry still concerns how the coins were obtained, what they could buy and whether they had already been used; the word “coins” alone does not answer it ([paragraph 8 of the judgment](https://cpc.ey.gov.tw/File/343C8EEBB5F122CF/6abbfdf1-cf31-4198-9eec-7421dab07bb8?A=C), printed pages 148–152).

Termination did occur. The player had also expressed an intention to end the contract, allowing the court to recognize that effect and then examine whether the remaining coins were refundable. That reasoning did not validate every earlier ground the operator had given for suspending the account or ending the service.

## Termination at any time, cancellation within seven days

Point 18 of the [current mandatory and prohibited standard terms for online game services](https://www.ey.gov.tw/File/C27658C15073FF69?A=C) allows a consumer to notify the operator of termination at any time. The operator must return unused paid points or unused game fees, after deducting necessary costs, within 30 days after termination, or deal with them in another way agreed by both parties.

What was deducted? A refund calculation can separate the unused balance, each deduction and its basis, so that the figures can be checked against the contract. A fixed percentage chosen by the operator does not by itself establish that the deduction is reasonable.

The current terms' latest amendment took effect on January 1, 2023, after this judgment. The 2021 dispute was decided under the contract and rules applicable then.

Point 4 separately allows written cancellation within seven days after starting play, without a need to explain or bear any cost, and a request for return of unused purchased paid points. Passing the seventh day does not remove the right to terminate under point 18. The no-cost cancellation rule and the necessary-cost deduction on termination concern different provisions.

## Reconstructing the displayed balance

A final screenshot may show coins without showing the original paid points or the transactions that consumed them. The missing sequence can be reconstructed from three groups of records.

| Record | What it can establish |
|---|---|
| Payment receipts and top-up statements | The money paid and the points or services acquired. |
| Conversion records and game history | When points were converted and how the resulting coins were used. |
| Termination notice and refund calculation | The termination date, recognized unused balance and reasons for deductions. |

Under point 9, the operator must retain at least 30 days of personal game history and provide it within seven days after receiving a request. An early request helps preserve more than the final balance screen. The terms also prohibit a contractual clause making only the operator's saved history and electronic data authoritative in a dispute ([mandatory point 9 and prohibited point 6](https://www.ey.gov.tw/File/C27658C15073FF69?A=C)).

In [2025 complaint statistics published in May 2026](https://cpc.ey.gov.tw/Page/6C059838CA9744A8/a3642311-5d66-4135-a8da-69c52279e930), online games ranked second among categories of first-stage consumer complaints, although the number fell from the previous year. The Consumer Protection Department also called on operators to address account blocking, suspensions and customer-service problems properly.

Players can raise a refund dispute with the operator first, then use the [government's consumer complaint system](https://appeal.cpc.ey.gov.tw/). Where contract interpretation or access to evidence is disputed, an [email to the firm](mailto:wei@hoveringlaw.com.tw) can identify the issue and response deadline without supplying an account password. A supported refund figure follows the money into the account, through conversion and into actual play.
