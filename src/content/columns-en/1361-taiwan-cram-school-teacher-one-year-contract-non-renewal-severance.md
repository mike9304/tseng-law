# Your Taiwan cram school won't renew your one-year contract. Is that a dismissal?

It is July, your third summer at a cram school in Taichung, and the branch manager sends a short email: your contract ends on August 31, it will not be renewed, thank you for three good years (an invented example). No reason beyond the date. Nothing about severance. Your classes ran all year, the school is already advertising for an English teacher, and you signed a fresh one-year contract every September. The date in the email does not settle the matter: if the teaching was continuing work, the law may treat the job as open-ended.

Whether that email is a dismissal depends less on the word "contract" than on the work. Taiwan's Labor Standards Act (勞動基準法) allows fixed-term contracts (定期契約) only for temporary, short-term, seasonal and specific work. Work of a continuing nature must be on an open-ended contract (不定期契約) ([Article 9](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0030001&flno=9), paragraph 1). If teaching at that school is continuing work, the school cannot end the job simply by letting a date pass. It needs a legal ground. The everyday grounds in Article 11 come with notice and severance (資遣費); the misconduct grounds in Article 12 do not. If the contract really was fixed-term, it ends on the date, and [Article 18](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0030001&flno=18) bars severance and notice pay for a worker who leaves when a fixed-term contract expires.

## Is year-round teaching "continuing" work?

The Act's Enforcement Rules (勞動基準法施行細則) give the four exceptions a narrow meaning ([Article 6](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0030002&flno=6)). Temporary work is unforeseeable, non-continuing work lasting six months or less. Short-term work is non-continuing work expected to be finished within six months. Seasonal work is non-continuing work that depends on seasonal materials or markets and lasts nine months or less. Specific work is non-continuing work that can be completed within a set period; if it runs longer than a year, it must be reported to the labor authority for approval (核備).

Ongoing English classes fit none of these easily. The Ministry of Labor's guidance on fixed-term contracts describes non-continuing work as work tied to an economic activity the employer does not mean to keep going. In practice, it says, the employer's job descriptions are the reference point, and if the same job is done by some staff on open-ended contracts and others on fixed-term ones, that is a reference point for treating the work as continuing. A school that keeps the same level of classes year after year and hires local teachers on open-ended terms for the same role will have trouble calling your post temporary.

A contract's title does not decide this.

## Renewals open a second route

Even a genuine fixed-term contract can turn into an open-ended one. Article 9(2) says that after a fixed-term contract expires, it is deemed open-ended in two situations. One is when the worker keeps working and the employer does not promptly object. The other is when a new contract is signed, the two contracts together cover more than 90 days, and the gap between them is no more than 30 days. Paragraph 3 carves out specific and seasonal fixed-term work from both rules.

Order matters here. Was the work continuing from the start? That question comes first. The renewal rules are a fallback, useful when a school argues that each school year was its own project.

## Your work permit does not make the contract fixed-term

Full-time teachers at short-term cram schools registered under the Supplementary and Continuing Education Act (補習及進修教育法) are hired under item 4 of [Article 46](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0090001&flno=46)(1) of the Employment Service Act (就業服務法). Paragraph 3 of the same article requires written fixed-term contracts, but only for workers hired under items 8 to 10: commercial fishing, domestic help and care work, and work the labor ministry designates for major national construction projects or economic and social development. Item 4 is outside that rule. A permit with an end date does not by itself turn your contract into a fixed-term one.

Coverage is broad: the Labor Standards Act applies to all employment relationships, apart from industries or workers the ministry has designated as excluded ([Article 3](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0030001&flno=3)). Teachers at regular schools, hired under item 3, need a separate analysis because their employment category and rules differ. If the school treats you as an independent contractor, that is a separate fight, discussed in [this column on contractor agreements](/en/columns/taiwan-foreign-freelancer-contractor-worker-status-work-permit).

## If the contract is open-ended, what does the school owe?

An employer may give notice only for a reason listed in [Article 11](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0030001&flno=11): closure or transfer of the business, losses or a downturn, a force majeure suspension of a month or more, a change in the nature of the business that requires fewer workers when no suitable job is available, or a worker who is genuinely unable to do the job. "The contract has expired" is not on the list. [Article 12](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0030001&flno=12) separately allows dismissal without notice on one of six listed grounds, among them a material breach of the contract or work rules, and in those cases Article 18 rules out notice pay and severance. Article 18 does the same when the worker ends the contract under [Article 15](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0030001&flno=15).

Notice runs by length of service under [Article 16](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0030001&flno=16): 10 days for at least three months but less than a year, 20 days for at least one year but less than three, 30 days for three years or more. A school that skips it must pay wages for the notice period instead. During notice you may take paid time off to look for work, up to two working days a week.

Severance now comes in two layers for most foreign teachers. Since January 1, 2026, [Article 24](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=A0030295&flno=24) of the Act for the Recruitment and Employment of Foreign Professionals (外國專業人才延攬及僱用法) has put foreigners doing "professional work" under the pension scheme of the Labor Pension Act (勞工退休金條例), and [Article 4](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=A0030295&flno=4) of that Act lists full-time foreign-language teachers at registered cram schools as professional work. A teacher already employed by the same school before the change could keep the old Labor Standards Act scheme by telling the employer in writing within six months, which the [Ministry of Labor](https://www.mol.gov.tw/1607/28690/2282/2302/2316/86977/) puts at June 30, 2026. That choice cannot be reversed. Teachers who were already in the new scheme, or had already lawfully chosen to stay in the old one, before January 1, 2026 remain under their existing arrangement; the amendment does not reopen that choice (Article 24(5)). A separate transition rule covers foreign professionals who held permanent residence before 2026 (Article 24(6)).

Under the new scheme, severance is half a month of average wages for each year of service under that scheme, pro rata for part of a year, capped at six months ([Article 12](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0030020&flno=12)). Years worked under the old scheme before the switch are preserved ([Article 11](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0030020&flno=11), applied by Article 24(3)). When the contract ends on an Article 11 ground, the school pays for those preserved years on the old formula in [Article 17](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0030001&flno=17) of the Labor Standards Act, using the average wage at termination: one month of average wages for each full year, pro rata for the remainder, with any part month counted as a whole month. A teacher who chose to stay in the old scheme gets the Article 17 formula for all years. The Labor Pension Act also covers foreign spouses of Taiwanese nationals and permanent residents on its own terms ([Article 7](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0030020&flno=7)). Severance is due within 30 days of termination.

The teacher in the email started in September 2023. Assume the teacher stayed in the old scheme until December 31, 2025 and entered the new scheme on January 1, 2026. That puts two years and four months under the old scheme and eight months under the new one. If no written choice was made and the school ended an open-ended contract on an Article 11 ground, the result would be 30 days' notice, the Article 17 formula for the earlier period, and half a month per year, pro rata, for 2026.

## The permit ends with the job

[Article 73](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0090001&flno=73) of the Employment Service Act says the employment permit is revoked when the employment relationship ends. What that means for your residence permit is a separate immigration question. What to check in the first days is covered in [Told your Taiwan job is ending?](/en/columns/foreign-professional-dismissed-taiwan).

Keep every contract, the renewal emails, your teaching schedules, the job ad for your replacement and anything showing local staff in the same role. Those are the documents that answer the Article 9 question.
