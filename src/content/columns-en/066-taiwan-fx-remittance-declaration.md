# Declaring Taiwan FX receipts and remittances from NT$500,000 up

[Regulations Governing the Declaration of Foreign Exchange Receipts and Disbursements or Transactions, Article 2](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawAll.aspx?pcode=G0450009) requires the owner or demander of funds for an FX receipt or transaction of NT$500,000 equivalent or more in Taiwan to declare. The declarant completes the form from underlying contracts and files it through a bank to the Central Bank.

Foreigners with an ARC, Taiwan companies and individuals sit in the same framework. Salary remittances, sale proceeds, royalties and loan repayments use different nature codes and attachments.

## Direct settlement versus document check versus prior approval

[Article 4](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawAll.aspx?pcode=G0450009) lists types that may settle after the form is completed, including certain trade and service payments and remittances within company annual cumulatives of USD 50 million equivalent or individual/group cumulatives of USD 5 million equivalent, subject to the article's conditions. Article 5 items need the bank to match the form to contracts or approval letters first. Article 6 requires prior CBC approval for necessary remittances that exceed those annual cumulatives and for other listed cases. The CBC may designate further categories.

Filing an FX declaration does not settle income-tax or gift-tax questions. See [Taiwan income-tax residence](/en/columns/taiwan-income-tax-residency). This column does not advise on timing or returns of any remittance.
