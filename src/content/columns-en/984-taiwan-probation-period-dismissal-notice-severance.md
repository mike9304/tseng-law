# Can a Taiwan Employer End a Job During Probation Without a Reason?

Can a Taiwan company let someone go before a three-month probation ends, without giving a reason? Many managers assume so, because the Labor Standards Act never uses the word "probation."

The Ministry of Labor disagrees. Its guidance page on probation (Department of Labor Relations, posted August 13, 2025) says an employer ending a contract during or at the end of probation "仍需有勞動基準法第11條、第12條或第13條但書規定之情事始得終止契約" — it still needs a ground under Article 11, Article 12 or the proviso to Article 13.

## A period that exists only in the contract

No current labor statute regulates probation, the guidance confirms. The parties may agree on a reasonable period suited to the job, in good faith. Three months is common. It is not a legal number.

Nothing about pay changes. Wages, hours, leave and benefits follow the Labor Standards Act as for any worker, insurance and pension contributions apply, and an advance agreement to skip overtime pay or pay below the minimum wage is void.

What about extending it? Probation is contractual, so the employer has to negotiate an extension; it cannot impose one. Seniority runs from the hiring date ([Labor Standards Act art. 84-2](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0030001&flno=84-2)).

Head-office templates often say either side may end the contract "at any time" during probation. The guidance rules that out, along with using probation as a reason for a fixed-term contract. [Article 9](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0030001&flno=9) limits fixed-term contracts to temporary, short-term, seasonal and specific work; continuing work requires an open-ended contract.

## Where "failed probation" fits

Only five grounds allow termination with notice under [Labor Standards Act art. 11](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0030001&flno=11): closure or transfer; losses or contraction; a force-majeure suspension of a month or more; a change in the business that requires fewer workers with no suitable post left; and, in item 5, where "勞工對於所擔任之工作確不能勝任" — the worker is clearly unable to do the assigned work. A failed probation review can realistically fit only item 5.

The ministry agrees: a result of "unsuitable" or "not passed" should be handled as a severance termination for inability to do the work, and the employer must not make the worker resign. A resignation letter would erase the dismissal on paper.

Could a generous payout replace a reason? No. Article 11 opens by barring termination with notice "非有左列情事之一者" — unless one of the listed grounds exists. Severance follows a valid Article 11 termination; paying more of it does not supply a missing ground.

Note the word "clearly" (確). The employer relies on the ground and keeps the records: the work assigned, the standard used, the result. A form that says only "below expectations" explains little.

Narrower cases allow termination without notice. [Article 12(1)](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0030001&flno=12) lists, among others, a false statement at signing that misled the employer and risks loss (item 1), a serious breach of the contract or work rules (item 4), and unexcused absence of three straight days or six in a month (item 6). A faked degree found during probation points to item 1. For items 1, 2 and 4 to 6, the employer must act within 30 days of learning the facts (Art. 12(2)). A worker dismissed under Article 12 cannot claim notice pay or severance ([art. 18](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0030001&flno=18)(1)).

## Three months changes the notice period

[Article 16(1)](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0030001&flno=16) sets notice by length of continuous service when a contract ends under Article 11 or the proviso to Article 13:

| Continuous service | Notice (art. 16(1)) |
|---|---|
| Under 3 months | No period stated in the article |
| 3 months to under 1 year | 10 days |
| 1 year to under 3 years | 20 days |
| 3 years or more | 30 days |

Item 1 starts at "three months or more"; nothing is stated below that.

Dates matter. Take a worker hired August 1 with probation to October 31 (an invented example). Notice in mid-October falls under three months. If the review slips to early November, the employer must give 10 days' notice or pay wages for that period (Art. 16(3)). The severance fraction also rises from two-twelfths to three-twelfths.

During notice, the worker may take paid time off to job-hunt, up to two days' working time a week (Art. 16(2)).

## Two months of work, severance still owed

For a foreign employee, first check whether the Labor Pension Act applies. [Its art. 7](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0030020&flno=7)(1) covers workers under the Labor Standards Act who are Taiwanese nationals, foreigners married to a citizen with household registration and allowed to reside, or permanent residents, among others. From January 1, 2026, [Act for the Recruitment and Employment of Foreign Professionals art. 24](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=A0030295&flno=24) adds foreign professionals doing professional work ([art. 4](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=A0030295&flno=4)(4)). Only staff hired before that date could choose the old rules in writing within six months; later hires cannot.

Under [Labor Pension Act art. 12](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0030020&flno=12)(1), a worker in that Act's pension system dismissed under Article 11 receives half a month's average wage for each full year of service, prorated for less than a year, capped at six months' average wage. It is due within 30 days after termination (Art. 12(2)).

For service under six months, the average wage is total wages during employment divided by the days employed ([Labor Standards Act art. 2](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0030001&flno=2)(4)).

One worker on NT$60,000 a month fails probation after two months (an invented example). Half a month times two-twelfths is one-twelfth of a month's average wage. Roughly NT$5,000.

Small, but owed.

Workers who kept the old pension rules, and foreign workers outside the Labor Pension Act, are paid under [art. 17](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0030001&flno=17) instead: one month's average wage per full year, prorated, with any fraction under a month counted as a month, also within 30 days.

## No notice period, but a 10-day report

[Employment Service Act art. 33](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0090001&flno=33), often missed, requires an employer laying off a worker to report, 10 days before departure, the worker's details, job, reason for the layoff and whether employment assistance is needed to the local authority and the public employment service. For layoffs caused by natural disaster or other force majeure, the deadline is three days after departure.

It applies even when no notice period does.

For a foreign worker, [Employment Service Act art. 56](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0090001&flno=56)(1) adds that when employment ends, the employer must notify the local authority, the immigration agency and the police in writing within three days.

[Immigration Act art. 31(4)](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0080132&flno=31) has the National Immigration Agency revoke a residence permit once its basis disappears, but item 5 lets it allow a foreigner to stay while a labor dispute with a Taiwan employer is in litigation. It "may." See also [what foreign professionals should check in the first week](/en/columns/foreign-professional-dismissed-taiwan).

## When probation is no defence

During maternity leave (Article 50) or treatment for an occupational injury (Article 59), the employer may not end the contract, unless force majeure stops the business and the authority approves ([art. 13](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0030001&flno=13)).

[Gender Equality in Employment Act art. 11](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0030014&flno=11) bars discrimination on sex or sexual orientation in layoffs and dismissals (para. 1) and bars marriage, pregnancy, childbirth or childcare as a reason for dismissal (para. 2); a breach makes the clause void and the termination ineffective (para. 3). Once a worker shows prima facie facts of differential treatment based on sex or sexual orientation, the employer must prove a reason unrelated to sex or sexual orientation, or one tied to a specific sex required by the job ([art. 31](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0030014&flno=31)). When a failed-probation notice soon follows an announced pregnancy, the question is whether the review record shows the two are unrelated.

## What a worker given notice can do

Start with a certificate of employment, which the employer cannot refuse on request when the contract ends ([art. 19](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0030001&flno=19)).

Workers who dispute the dismissal or lack severance may apply for mediation to the city or county authority where the work was done ([Act for Settlement of Labor-Management Disputes art. 6](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0020007&flno=6)(1), [art. 9](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0020007&flno=9)(1)). During mediation the employer may not end the contract or take other adverse action because of that dispute ([art. 8](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0020007&flno=8)).

In court, [Labor Incident Act art. 16](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010064&flno=16) generally requires court labor mediation before a lawsuit, and the judge sets the first session within 30 days of the application unless special reasons apply ([art. 23](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010064&flno=23)). Suing to confirm the employment, a worker may seek an interim order of continued employment and wages if the court sees a prospect of winning and keeping the worker is not clearly very difficult for the employer ([art. 49](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010064&flno=49)(1)).

That guidance is the regulator's view; a court decides a real case on the probation clause, the review record and how notice was given.
