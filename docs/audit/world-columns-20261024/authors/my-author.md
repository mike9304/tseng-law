## Article 810 — Myanmar student: part-time work permit, hours, graduation

Reader and trigger. A Myanmar national studying (degree or Mandarin programme) at a Taiwan college who is offered cash work at a shop or restaurant, or who is approaching graduation and wants to stay. Question: "I have an ARC, can I work 20 hours?"

Why Myanmar (labelled hypothesis). Evidence from the ledger: Myanmar nationals are not visa-exempt and are not in the six-country migrant-worker list, so for many Myanmar nationals study is a main lawful route to residence in Taiwan. That is an inference, not a statistic; I did not find or use any official count of Myanmar students. The shop scene is explicitly marked hypothetical in the sentence. Graduate-stay rules matter because the student route is the entry point.

Overlap and new angle. Existing `columns-my` 001-018 contain nothing on students. Structural sources en/637 (permit revocation case) and en/705 were re-checked, not copied. The new angle is the student permit mechanics (Art. 50 + Regulation Arts. 50-55), the consequences of unpermitted work for the student and the shop owner, the three-year look-back, and the two-year post-graduation residence extension with work exemption (Foreign Professionals Act Art. 12), which en/637 and en/705 do not cover.

## Article 811 — Myanmar resident: lending a bank account, carrying cash, police notice

Reader and trigger. A Myanmar student or worker who is asked, in a chat group or by a friend, to lend or "use" a bank account, or to receive and pass on money, or who has received a police notice or found the account frozen.

Why Myanmar (labelled hypothesis). Not documented by any source I could use. The fact pattern (money received into a Taiwan account and passed on to relatives abroad, then called a "transfer help job") is a hypothesis about how such offers are framed for people with family in Myanmar; it is presented only as a clearly marked hypothetical scene. The legal content itself is nationality-neutral, but two parts are specific to foreigners: Criminal Code Art. 95 (deportation after sentence) and FSC regulation Art. 4(2)(8) (accounts of foreigners whose residence document was cancelled early, overstayed, or reported missing are treated as Category 2).

Overlap and new angle. Same topic family as id/666, th/661, vi/038 (read, not copied); those treat the cash-withdrawal job and the Thai/Indonesian worker. New angle here: remittance-style offers and Banking Act Art. 29/125, the student/ARC link to bank scrutiny, and the order warning -> crime triggers -> laundering -> bank freeze -> police questioning, with a statute sentence as the opening.

## Article 812 — Myanmar spouse of a Taiwan national: interview, registration, visa, ARC

Reader and trigger. A Myanmar national engaged or married to a Taiwan national (or the Taiwanese partner) asking whether to marry in Myanmar first or register in Taiwan first, what the interview at the Taiwan office involves, and what comes after.

Why Myanmar. Verified, not hypothetical: Myanmar is on BOCA's list of nationalities that must apply for marriage-document authentication and the dependent visa at the Taiwan mission (BOCA PDF revised 2026-08-24, row "駐緬甸代表處 / 緬甸"); the mission publishes a Myanmar-specific procedure sheet; BOCA's 2026-10-13 page "中華民國國民與泰越菲印緬人士辦理結婚手續" lists Myanmar. The interview-exemption for a degree earned in Taiwan with the couple meeting there is relevant to Myanmar graduates, which links to 810.

Overlap and new angle. en/022 (U.S. citizen marrying a Taiwanese) assumes registration can happen in Taiwan first; here the order is fixed by the specific-country rule. No Burmese column covers marriage. New material: the order of steps, the Myanmar-side documents (separate affidavits, notary + MOFA authentication, full English translations, package processing), the 2026 BOCA vs mission-sheet discrepancy about the police certificate, denial grounds and exemptions, fee/broker warnings (folded into the documents section), and the 30-day ARC step.
