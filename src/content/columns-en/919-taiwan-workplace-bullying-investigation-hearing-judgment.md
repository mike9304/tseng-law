The intended punishment had been announced before the committee deliberated. A civil-service supervisor was then dismissed through the imposition of two major demerits, in a workplace-bullying case that reached the Taipei High Administrative Court. The court set aside both the original disposition and the review decision. Missing opportunities to respond and inconsistencies in the investigation report also featured in its [official explanation](https://www.judicial.gov.tw/tw/cp-1888-1611064-9760a-1.html).

Judgment 114年度訴字第869號 was issued by the court's High Administrative Litigation Division on September 3, 2026. It was appealable. Cancellation of the decisions does not establish that every alleged act was disproved, or that the person had already returned to the job.

## What remained for the committee to decide?

According to the court's account, the agency head publicly stated an intention to impose two major demerits before the performance appraisal committee discussed the matter. The superior agency then held its meeting before the subordinate agency deliberated. That sequence undermined the committee's ability to make a substantive assessment.

Once the conclusion was public, meeting minutes could show that a meeting happened without establishing that evidence was still capable of changing the result.

Proper notice was missing too: the relevant committees had not lawfully notified the person to attend and respond. Within the report used to support punishment, the assessment of evidence for bullying conflicted with the recommended outcome. A later opportunity to argue the case in the review proceedings did not cure the earlier denial of a hearing. These findings appear in the [court's summary of its reasons](https://www.judicial.gov.tw/tw/cp-1888-1611064-9760a-1.html).

## A private employer works under different rules

This was a civil-service performance-assessment case, concerning events before the new workplace-bullying framework. For private employers handling workers' complaints, the applicable law includes the Occupational Safety and Health Act provisions effective July 1, 2026. The two procedures should not be treated as interchangeable ([legislative history](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawHistory.aspx?pcode=N0060001); [Ministry of Labor explanation](https://www.osha.gov.tw/48110/48417/48419/206967/?cprint=pt)).

[Article 22-2](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0060001&flno=22-2) requires investigation or coordination to be objective, impartial and fair. During an investigation, the parties must have sufficient opportunity to give their accounts and respond, and conflict-of-interest rules must be observed. A public statement declaring a violation before findings are reached makes those requirements harder to carry out.

Protection starts before a final report. When an employer receives a complaint from a worker subjected to bullying, it must take measures to prevent the complainant from suffering further bullying, provide counselling, assistance and protection according to need, and conduct an investigation. All of those obligations remain relevant while the facts are being examined.

Coordination is possible if the complainant wishes it. If coordination fails, the investigation must continue. Acting promptly to protect someone does not require deciding the disputed allegations in advance, and giving the other party a hearing does not require postponing protection.

A private employer can use this judgment to inspect the quality of its process. It does not itself determine whether a private company's disciplinary decision is valid.

## From a witness account to a reasoned finding

An HR report needs to show how an account became a finding. Who said what? Which messages, attendance records or meeting documents supported it? Where accounts conflict, recording why one piece of evidence was accepted makes the conclusion open to examination; a gap between an allegation and the records should remain visible too. These are practical document-review suggestions drawn from the problems in the judgment.

The notice shapes the response. “Explain your management style” gives a person much less to answer than a notice identifying the disputed conduct, dates and unresolved questions. Comparing that notice with the interview records and final findings can reveal whether the investigation kept addressing the same events. Publishing everyone's personal information is unnecessary for preserving those reasons.

Sensitive case files can follow through an agreed delivery process. Before announcing a result, retain the notice, the responses and the reasons for accepting evidence: those records show whether different accounts were actually heard.
