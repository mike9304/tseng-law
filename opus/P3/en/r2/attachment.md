Around 4:22 p.m. on Oct 26, 2020, the driver took a company bus through an intersection in Beitou, Taipei, on a red light. The parent rode into the same intersection with the kid on the back, couldn't avoid it, and the front of the scooter hit the bus's right side. Both went down with the scooter.

The kid lost one upper front tooth and broke three, with loosened teeth, an exposed nerve, and scrapes on the lower back and left knee. The parent broke their left collarbone and had surgery. That left about a 9 cm scar, plus scars on the forehead and chin.

The criminal case came first. Prosecutors charged the driver with negligent injury. During that trial the driver paid the two of them NT$100,000 up front. When they withdrew the complaint, they also agreed the money would come off any civil damages later. That was NT$50,000 each, and both sides confirmed it in the civil case. The criminal case then ended without a verdict on the merits (公訴不受理).

The civil claim was filed inside the criminal case and later moved to the civil court. It started against the driver alone for NT$1,612,544; the bus company was added as a defendant later.

The kid claimed NT$723,854: past medical costs of 8,854, future dental work of 343,000, and 372,000 for pain and suffering.
The parent claimed NT$888,690: medical costs, 45 days of care by family and friends at 2,200 a day, future treatment of 30,000, 385,022 in income lost while recovering, 13,300 to fix the scooter, and 300,000 for pain and suffering.

The driver and the company didn't dispute either person's medical bills, but pushed back on the rest. For the kid's teeth, they said option 1 in the hospital's treatment plan would run NT$120,000 to 150,000, and the pain-and-suffering figure was too high. The parent's care, they argued, should be 14 days at NT$1,200, since the doctor's note only called for two weeks of full-time care. They also said the parent's breakfast-shop job (早餐店) had been covered by a part-timer, so lost income should top out at 158 an hour × 8 hours × 22 days = 27,808. The scooter should be depreciated, they said, and the compulsory insurance already paid out (kid 2,030, parent 57,948) plus the 100,000 from the criminal case should come off.

The kid's future dental work was granted in full, 343,000. The kid was still a minor when it happened, and the ruling says teeth aren't just for chewing. They're part of how the whole face looks. So the court used option 3, which includes implants, to set the amount. The plan itself says the actual treatment will depend on the kid's teeth after adulthood. The 372,000 for pain and suffering stood too, which puts the kid's damages at the full 723,854.

The parent's numbers came down.
Family care still got counted, even though nobody was paid for it. The ruling cites two Supreme Court decisions for that, and the court put the going rate for a full-time caregiver at NT$2,000–3,000 a day. But the doctor's note said four weeks of rest with full-time care for the first two, so 14 days at the parent's 2,200 a day: 30,800.
For the 30,000 in future treatment, the court wrote to the hospital. After surgery to remove the fixation, the parent had one follow-up visit and no records after that, and the hospital replied that outpatient care could stop if the wound had no problems. On that basis the court found no further treatment costs were needed and allowed only the 5,999 actually spent.
The parent didn't submit income records or tax filings. Lost income was set at the defendants' own number, 27,808.
The scooter estimate didn't split labor from parts. The court counted all of it as parts and depreciated it for the scooter's 2 years and 1 month of use, leaving 2,735.
Pain and suffering went from 300,000 to 200,000.
The ruling's total for the parent came to 328,980.

From there, each person's NT$50,000 from the criminal case came off, and so did their compulsory insurance payout. Those payouts count as part of the damages. (The ruling cites Arts. 28 and 30 of the Compulsory Automobile Liability Insurance Act; on the current law site nearly the same wording is in Art. 32.)

The court ordered the driver and the bus company to pay this jointly and severally. The kid and the parent can each collect their full amount from the driver, from the company, or from both. Whatever the driver pays counts toward what the company owes, and the other way around (Civil Code Arts. 273 and 274; the ruling doesn't cite these, I've added them to explain the term).

Civil Code Art. 188 says an employer is liable together with the employee when the employee wrongfully harms someone, or their property, on the job. The only way out is for the employer to prove it took proper care in hiring and supervising, or that the harm would have happened even with proper care. The bus company didn't prove its supervision was free of fault.

Interest runs at 5% a year from Oct 1, 2021, the day after the complaint was served, until paid. Everything else was dismissed. Of the NT$17,038 in court costs, the driver and the company jointly and severally bear 9,434 (plus 5% a year from the day after the judgment becomes final) and the kid and the parent the rest.
