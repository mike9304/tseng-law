Taiwan's government-information law permits foreign applicants where their home-country law does not restrict Taiwan nationals from requesting government information there. That condition appears in [Article 9 of the 政府資訊公開法](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=I0020026&flno=9).

A US citizen seeking a Taiwan agency's regulatory record or report can present the [US Department of Justice's FOIA guidance](https://www.justice.gov/oip/foia-procedures.pdf). It describes the federal rule as open to any person regardless of citizenship, with specified exceptions; the Taiwan agency still has to assess the application under Taiwan law.

## The requested information must be an agency record

Recorded information that a government agency created or obtained within its functions falls within [Article 3](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=I0020026&flno=3). Name the record and its likely custodian.

Identifying an existing report is different from asking an official to write a new commercial assessment.

Special rules in other laws take priority under [Article 2](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=I0020026&flno=2). In a [May 12, 2025 explanation](https://mojlaw.moj.gov.tw/LawContentExShow.aspx?id=FE390017&type=E), the Ministry of Justice distinguishes an administrative party's file-access procedure from general information disclosure and says the Archives Act takes priority for records already placed under archival management.

## A US request template needs Taiwan's required particulars

[Article 10](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=I0020026&flno=10) requires the applicant's name, date of birth, identification number, address and contact telephone number, plus nationality, passport number and supporting documents for a foreign applicant. It also requires the information's subject and number of items, intended use, application date and any legal representative's name, birth date and correspondence address.

State the intended use. Omitting it because a US FOIA template did not ask for one would omit an express Taiwan statutory item.

For evidence of foreign eligibility, the [Presidential Office's own application rules](https://www.president.gov.tw/Page/284/1343) expressly require proof that the applicant's home-country law does not restrict Taiwan nationals' access. The proof requirement quoted here is office-specific.

Use the receiving agency's accepted submission channel and form. Article 10 allows written communication and electronic transmission after authentication by an electronic-signature certification authority. Ordinary email does not establish that authentication.

If a remediable application defect is found, [Article 11](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=I0020026&flno=11) requires notice to correct it within seven days. An uncorrectable defect, or failure to correct in time, permits rejection.

## The decision period is not a promise of full delivery

Under [Article 12](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=I0020026&flno=12), the agency must decide approval or refusal within fifteen days after accepting the application, with a necessary extension of no more than fifteen days.

The Presidential Office's rules exclude the applicant's correction period and third-party comment period from its processing time. Keep the correction and extension notices for that calculation.

Where particular third-party interests are involved, Article 12 generally requires written notice allowing that person or entity ten days to comment. Prior consent to disclosure is an exception, and an unknown location calls for public notice of the notification's contents.

Silence is not a veto. If no opinion is given within the specified period, the agency may decide, and the [Ministry's 2025 explanation](https://mojlaw.moj.gov.tw/LawContentExShow.aspx?id=FE390017&type=E) says an opinion that is given does not automatically bind it either.

## Protected business information does not settle every page

Protected secrets and certain investigation or enforcement information are among [Article 18's withholding grounds](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=I0020026&flno=18). Its business-information ground applies where disclosure would harm rights, competitive position or other legitimate interests.

That business ground has exceptions where disclosure is necessary for the public interest, necessary to protect people's life, body or health, or consented to by the person concerned. The statutory privacy and professional-secrecy ground has those exceptions too. A request relying on an exception needs the facts supporting it.

What can be released after the protected material is removed? Article 18's second paragraph requires disclosure of the other portions when restricted matters are included in government information.

The [2025 Ministry explanation](https://mojlaw.moj.gov.tw/LawContentExShow.aspx?id=FE390017&type=E) confirms this separation principle. In presenting a request or challenging a refusal, distinguish the information claimed to be protected from the remaining portion sought.

Available formats under [Article 13](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=I0020026&flno=13) include copies, inspection, transcription and photography. Intellectual-property concerns or difficulty of implementation may limit provision to inspection, and information already lawfully published can be supplied by explaining how to find it.

Fees may apply. [Article 22](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=I0020026&flno=22) allows charges under agency standards, including search, review and reproduction costs, with possible reductions or waivers for academic research or public-interest use.

## A refusal should identify its reason

[Article 16](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=I0020026&flno=16) requires written reasons for a full or partial refusal, while [Article 20](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=I0020026&flno=20) permits administrative remedies against the decision. Preserve the decision and its service information along with the exact application; the scope and timing of a challenge need assessment from those documents.

For an enquiry about access or a refusal, identify the agency and requested record at [wei@hoveringlaw.com.tw](mailto:wei@hoveringlaw.com.tw), 103 臺北市大同區承德路一段35號7樓之2.

If the documents are held by an opponent in a civil case, [court-ordered document production](/en/columns/taiwan-civil-us-company-opponent-document-production) follows another procedure.
