# You Won a Prize Draw in Taiwan: The Prize Never Came, Tax Was Demanded, or the Win Was Cancelled

Ten percent, or twenty. That is what a Taiwan business withholds when it hands out a lucky-draw prize: 10% of the full amount for a resident, 20% for a non-resident (Withholding Rates Standards arts. 2(1)(vii) and 3(1)(vii)).

After the good news, a call like this can follow. A department store draw lands you a fridge worth NT$30,000, and the counter says you must first pay NT$3,000 "for tax" (an invented example). Do you owe it? And if the store simply never delivers, or cancels the win, what can you rely on?

## Prizes are "chance winnings"

[Income Tax Act art. 14](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0340003&flno=14)(1) sorts individual income into ten classes. Class 8 covers prizes from competitions and games of chance (機會中獎). Costs paid to take part may be deducted; only government lottery winnings are settled by withholding and left out of total income. Store and company draws are not.

Withholding is the payer's job. Under [art. 88](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0340003&flno=88)(1)(ii), government agencies, organizations, schools, businesses and professional practitioners, among others, must withhold when paying such prizes. They are the withholding agents; the winner is the taxpayer ([art. 89](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0340003&flno=89)(1)(ii)).

| Winner | Rate | Small-amount rule | Afterwards |
|---|---|---|---|
| Resident | 10% of the full amount | No withholding if the tax per payment is NT$2,000 or less | Included in the May annual return; withheld tax credited |
| Non-resident | 20% of the full amount | None | Withholding settles the tax |

Residence is defined in [art. 7](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0340003&flno=7)(2): a person domiciled in and ordinarily living in Taiwan, or, without a domicile, anyone present for 183 days or more in a tax year. Our [column on Taiwan tax residency](/en/columns/taiwan-income-tax-residency) covers the details.

Under [Standards art. 13](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0340028&flno=13)(1), no withholding is required for residents when the tax due on one payment is NT$2,000 or less, which at 10% means a prize of up to NT$20,000. The tax itself does not disappear. The prize still counts as income, and the payer must still report the recipient and amount by the end of January and give the winner an exemption certificate by February 10 (Income Tax Act art. 89(3); February 5 and 15 if January has three or more consecutive public holidays). That report lists the recipient's name, address and ID number, which is why organizers ask for them. Payments to one person totalling NT$1,000 or less a year need not be reported (Standards art. 13(3)).

Non-residents get no such threshold, and withholding settles their tax on art. 88 income ([art. 73](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0340003&flno=73)(1)). Residents file between May 1 and 31 of the following year and credit the tax withheld ([art. 71](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0340003&flno=71)(1)). The 10% is not the final bill; the annual calculation may mean paying more or getting some back. A resident not otherwise required to file must still file to claim a refund (art. 71(3)).

## The fridge and its NT$3,000

Cash is simple: the payer keeps 10%. A fridge leaves nothing to keep back.

Goods are income too. Art. 14(2) values income in kind at the government-set price when received or, if none is set, the local market price, and [Enforcement Rules art. 89-1](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0340004&flno=89-1) applies that to withholding. A NT$30,000 fridge means NT$3,000 for a resident, above the NT$2,000 line.

Who pays it, and how? The winner is the taxpayer by law (art. 89(1)(ii)). But neither the Act, its Enforcement Rules nor the Standards say a winner must hand over cash first for a prize in kind. Start with the promotion terms: do they say the winner bears the tax, or the organizer? If the fridge was valued above market price, ask how the figure was reached.

Withholding brings paperwork. The agent must notify the taxpayer, issue a withholding certificate, and refund any excess over the tax authority's assessment; a shortfall is paid by the agent, who may recover it from the winner ([art. 94](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0340003&flno=94)). Residents' certificates are due by February 10 of the following year; for non-residents, within ten days of withholding (five more if three or more consecutive public holidays fall in that window), after the tax office checks them ([art. 92](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0340003&flno=92)(1)–(2)). Penalties under [art. 114](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0340003&flno=114) fall on the agent, not the winner.

A demand to wire "tax" before receiving a cash prize does not fit this design. The payer deducts withholding from the prize when paying it (art. 88(1)).

Personal social-media giveaways? Art. 88(1)(ii) lists institutions and businesses, so a private individual giving outside any business may fall outside it. For a resident winner, the prize is still chance winnings.

## When the prize is withheld or swapped

For a store or brand promotion aimed at consumers, start with the Consumer Protection Act. A business must keep its advertising truthful, and its obligations to consumers may not fall below what it advertised ([art. 22](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0170001&flno=22)(1)); once a contract is formed, the advertised content must be performed (art. 22(2)). "Shoppers enter a draw for a fridge" makes the fridge part of the advertisement. Shipping or "collection fees" the advertisement never mentioned are judged by the same yardstick.

The Fair Trade Act may also apply. Businesses may not make false or misleading representations on matters likely to affect trading decisions, which include price, quantity, quality and "other related matters that attract customers" ([art. 21](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0150002&flno=21)(1)–(2), applied to services by (4)). The authority may order the conduct stopped and fine the business NT$50,000 to NT$25 million ([art. 42](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0150002&flno=42)). Victims may claim damages, up to three times the proven loss for intentional conduct ([arts. 30](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0150002&flno=30) and [31](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0150002&flno=31)(1)). Both laws turn on definitions: a business operator under [Consumer Protection Act art. 2](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0170001&flno=2)(ii), an enterprise under [Fair Trade Act art. 2](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0150002&flno=2)(1). A purely private gift may fall back on the Civil Code alone.

Read the small print too. Terms a business prepares in advance for many consumers count as standard terms even when posted online (Consumer Protection Act art. 2(vii)). Ambiguity is read in the consumer's favour ([art. 11](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0170001&flno=11)(2)), and a term that breaches good faith and is clearly unfair to consumers is void ([art. 12](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0170001&flno=12)(1)). "The organizer may change or cancel prizes at any time" must pass that test.

## When the win is cancelled

No Civil Code article deals with lucky draws as such. The closest is the reward advertisement: an advertisement promising a reward to whoever completes a specified act, which binds the advertiser to pay that person ([Civil Code art. 164](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=164)(1)). Whether a draw that rewards only those picked fits that definition depends on the promotion's wording.

Withdrawal has a price. Withdrawing before the act is completed obliges the advertiser to compensate losses suffered in good faith, capped at the promised reward, unless it proves the participant could not have completed the act; a stated completion period creates a presumption that the right to withdraw was waived ([art. 165](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=165)). Contests judged on merit are clearer still: the reward is owed once judging ends, and the judging binds both sides ([arts. 165-1](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=165-1) and [165-2](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=165-2)). Rights arising from the entry belong to the entrant unless the advertisement says otherwise ([art. 164-1](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=164-1), applied by [art. 165-4](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=165-4)).

Cancellation after winners are announced differs: did the stated disqualification ground occur, and does that term survive standard-terms review? Keep the announcement, entry conditions and messages, with dates.

## Year-end party draws

At a company year-end party (尾牙), the company is the payer, and the certificate it issues early the next year shows how it classified the prize and what it withheld. The Act counts bonuses as salary income (art. 14(1), class 3, item 4) and lists prizes separately as class 8; salary and prizes are withheld differently under Standards art. 2(1)(i) and (vii). A non-resident employee's certificate follows the same ten-day rule (art. 92(2)), and if the company treats the prize as chance winnings, the 20% applies however small it is.

## Complaining to the store

The Consumer Protection Act sets a sequence. Complain to the business, a consumer group or a consumer service centre; the business must deal with it properly within 15 days ([art. 43](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0170001&flno=43)(1)–(2)). If it does not, go to the local government's consumer protection officer (art. 43(3)), and then to the consumer dispute mediation committee ([art. 44](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0170001&flno=44)).
