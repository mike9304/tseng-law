## 본문 · 498자

```text
A Taiwan couple signed and registered their divorce the same day, but a trial court ruled it void and found them still married.
One witness never checked that the husband wanted out.

That witness was the wife's old friend, who met only her at a restaurant to sign and never met or contacted the husband then.

Taiwan needs at least two witnesses; under case law, each must know firsthand that both spouses want out. The court didn't count the friend, so even with the other one, there weren't two.
```

## 텍스트 첨부 · 4060자 · 링크 3개

```text
No names here: just the husband, the wife, and the friend who signed as a witness. The speech bubbles and the restaurant drawing on the cards are made-up scenes based on the ruling, not real conversations or photos.

They married in January 2007 and have two minor children. On Dec 14, 2023, they signed a divorce agreement and registered the divorce together at the household registration office (戶政事務所) the same day. Neither side disputes that, and the court checked the registration records.

Then the husband sued, asking the court to confirm they're still married. His version: on Dec 11, his wife asked for a divorce and later handed him an agreement already signed by her and two witnesses. They worked out property, custody and child support, then he signed. But he had no idea who the two witnesses were. Neither witness ever asked if he actually wanted out, and neither was there when the couple signed.

The wife tells it the other way around. She had wanted out for a long time, and after a lot of talking he agreed. They drew up the agreement together on Dec 14, and the two witnesses signed only after seeing and hearing for themselves that both really wanted the divorce. His story was made up for the lawsuit.

The friend testified. From the ruling, roughly:
The friend had known the wife for 7 to 9 years and stayed in occasional touch. About five years earlier they'd seen the couple together at a friends' gathering. The wife didn't introduce him, but they knew he was her husband. Around December 2023, she mentioned wanting a divorce and asked them to be a witness, and they agreed. The two met at a restaurant in Zhudong, with nobody else there. The wife brought out a standard-form agreement that looked downloaded and asked for a signature. The other witness had already signed; neither spouse had. The handwritten parts on the children, the property, and the date were still blank. The friend didn't ask why they were divorcing or about the details. The two ate and went their separate ways. The friend never ran into the husband before or after signing. The friend had no phone number or messaging contact for him, never checked with him about the divorce, and wasn't there on registration day.
Neither spouse objected to this testimony.

Taiwan Civil Code Art. 1050: a divorce by agreement must be in writing, signed by two or more witnesses, and registered with the household registration office. On witnesses, the ruling leans on Taiwan Supreme Court decisions from 1979–82 (No. 68 Tai-Shang-Zi 3792 and others). A witness can sign before or after the couple does, and doesn't have to have been in the room when they agreed. But a witness must have personally seen or heard that both spouses truly intend to divorce, and be willing to vouch for it. Hearing it secondhand isn't enough, even from one of the spouses.

The friend never heard it from the husband, and never checked. So the friend didn't qualify. Even counting the other witness, that left fewer than two, and under Art. 73 the divorce was void. Registering at the office didn't change it.

Judgment came on June 11, 2024: the court confirmed the marriage still existed and put the court costs on the wife. The court didn't rule on whether the other witness qualified, and the friend is the only witness whose testimony appears in the ruling.

Here are the articles in the original:
Art. 1050: 兩願離婚，應以書面為之，有二人以上證人之簽名並應向戶政機關為離婚之登記。
https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=1050
Art. 73: 法律行為，不依法定方式者，無效。但法律另有規定者，不在此限。 (Acts that don't follow the legally required form are void, unless a law says otherwise.)
https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=73

The article itself doesn't say what a witness has to confirm, or whether everyone signs together. That part comes from older cases the ruling quotes.

— Taiwan Hsinchu District Court, No. 113 Hun-Zi 59 · June 11, 2024 · first instance · appeal/finality not confirmed
https://judgment.judicial.gov.tw/FJUD/printData.aspx?id=SCDU%2C113%2C%E5%A9%9A%2C59%2C20240611%2C1
```
