# Taiwan’s new proteomics partnership and consent for human research

Who agreed to the next use of a blood sample? [Economic Daily News reported on October 28](https://money.udn.com/money/story/5612/9842442) that Genomics BioSci had installed technology from US-based Alamar Biosciences following their Taiwan collaboration agreement. The initial focus described in the report was research into Alzheimer’s biomarkers and disease mechanisms.

For a US biotechnology company commissioning work in Taiwan, the proposed samples, data and research purpose need to be defined before the team can assess which approvals and participant agreements the project requires. The report does not mention any project-specific approval.

## Existing samples can still be human research

[Article 4 of Taiwan’s Human Subjects Research Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=L0020176&flno=4) covers research involving the collection, investigation, analysis or use of human specimens, information about a person’s biological behavior, or their physiological, psychological, genetic or medical information. Human specimens include body fluids and materials derived from experimental work on them.

A new procedure on a participant is therefore not the only trigger. Analysis of stored blood samples or existing medical information may fall within that definition. Calling a project a platform demonstration does not itself answer what materials it will use or what it will investigate.

## Approval attaches to the research plan

Before conducting research, the principal investigator must prepare a protocol and obtain approval from an institutional review board, or IRB, under [Article 5](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=L0020176&flno=5). Research within officially announced review-exempt categories is excluded from that requirement. An institution without its own IRB may commission another IRB to review the project.

Changes matter too. Article 5 requires the original reviewing IRB’s agreement before a change to an approved protocol is implemented, including changes that the commercial parties have already agreed between themselves. Another research aim or a revised use of samples needs to be considered against that protocol. The equipment contract does not establish approval.

## Consent has its own scope

[Article 12](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=L0020176&flno=12) requires consent in the form and with the content approved by the IRB, unless the project falls within an officially announced consent-exempt category. The exemptions are separate. Each depends on whether the relevant announced category covers the proposed research.

Under [Article 14](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=L0020176&flno=14), the principal investigator must explain specified matters, in a way the participant or the relative, legal representative, guardian or assistant giving consent can understand, before obtaining the consent required by Article 12. These include the research purpose and methods, funding, foreseeable risks and remedies, protection of personal information, and the right and method to withdraw consent at any time.

The same article requires disclosure of how long research materials will be kept, their planned uses, and potential commercial benefits and arrangements for their application. These are particularly relevant when an academic research plan sits alongside a commercial collaboration. A broad statement that samples will support scientific progress leaves those specific matters unanswered.

Projects involving people who cannot decide for themselves also encounter Article 12’s additional participation and consent rules. An Alzheimer’s research label alone cannot establish a participant’s capacity or identify the person entitled to consent.

For a project of that kind in Taiwan, the research protocol and participant-facing explanation need to describe the same intended use. A collaboration agreement can allocate equipment and commercial tasks, but the remaining question is whether the planned sample analysis fits the approved research and consent arrangements, or a properly applicable exemption.
