# Noisy Neighbors in a Taiwan Apartment: Police, Environmental Protection Bureau, Building Committee or Court

"Making noise or a disturbance late at night, disturbing public peace, and refusing to stop when told to" (製造噪音或深夜喧嘩，妨害公眾安寧，不聽禁止者). That is item 2 of [article 72 of Taiwan's Social Order Maintenance Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0080067&flno=72). The fine reaches NT$10,000, and [article 19](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0080067&flno=19) sets the floor at NT$300.

Three elements must line up. There is noise or a late-night disturbance; it disturbs public peace; and the person ignores an order to stop. Call the police at 2 a.m., and the wording means officers first tell the neighbor to stop. Carrying on completes the offense. Once a resident's report gives the police reason to suspect a violation, they must start investigating ([art. 39](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0080067&flno=39)).

## Sounds the police handle, sounds the bureau measures

Taiwan's [Noise Control Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawAll.aspx?pcode=O0030001) defines noise as sound above the control standards ([art. 3](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=O0030001&flno=3)). For sound that is not continuous or is hard to measure, yet enough to disturb other people's peace, [article 6](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=O0030001&flno=6) sends the matter to the police. A party upstairs or shouting at midnight is police territory.

Measured sources are listed in [article 9](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=O0030001&flno=9): within noise control zones, factories, entertainment venues, business premises, construction work, amplifying equipment, and other places or equipment the authority announces. A restaurant exhaust fan or demolition next door belongs with the local environmental protection bureau. Local bureaus are also charged with coordinating noise disputes ([art. 5](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=O0030001&flno=5)(3)). Police conducting an article 6 inspection who learn of an article 9(1) breach must notify the local authority at once ([art. 21](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=O0030001&flno=21)).

What about footsteps? Ordinary homes are not on the article 9 list. Unless the authority announces otherwise, no decibel limit applies, so footsteps fall to the police, the building committee or the courts.

Over-limit sources face fines under [article 24](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=O0030001&flno=24) once a deadline to fix them has passed. Construction: NT$18,000 to NT$180,000, with no more than four days to comply. Entertainment or business premises: NT$3,000 to NT$30,000 and up to 30 days. Amplifiers: the same range, with ten minutes.

Time of day matters. Under [article 2 of the Noise Control Standards](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=O0030006&flno=2), daytime runs from 7 a.m. to 7 p.m. Evening follows: 7 to 10 p.m. in class 1 and 2 control zones, 7 to 11 p.m. in class 3 and 4 zones. Night then lasts until 7 a.m. Night limits are lower than daytime ones. If total and background sound differ by under 3 decibels, measurement stops and is redone ([art. 3](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=O0030006&flno=3)).

[Article 8](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=O0030001&flno=8) works without a meter. At times and places the local authority announces, firecrackers, temple and shrine events, weddings and funerals, and businesses running powered machinery (food, laundry, printing) must not disturb other people's peace. A breach costs NT$3,000 to NT$30,000 with an order to fix it at once; failing to comply is fined again each time ([art. 23](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=O0030001&flno=23)). Inspectors showing official identification may enter premises to measure, and refusing them costs NT$3,000 to NT$30,000 (arts. [19](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=O0030001&flno=19), [31](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=O0030001&flno=31)).

## Inside the building, the committee moves first

[Article 16 of the Condominium Administration Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0070118&flno=16) bars residents from causing din (喧囂), vibration or similar conduct. Pets must not disturb public peace either: think of a dog barking all night (para. 4). The manager or management committee must stop the conduct or deal with it under the building rules, and may report a resident who ignores it to the local authority (para. 5). That authority may fine NT$3,000 to NT$15,000, set a deadline, and fine again if nothing changes ([art. 47](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0070118&flno=47)(2)).

Stopping violations and providing related records is part of the committee's job ([art. 36](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0070118&flno=36)(5)). If the committee will not act, another owner or interested party may report directly with facts and evidence ([art. 59](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0070118&flno=59)). For an owner who meets the article 22(1) conditions, the committee may, on an owners' meeting resolution, sue to force a sale of the unit ([art. 22](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0070118&flno=22)(2)).

Rules on handling breaches and mediating disputes take effect only if written into the building rules ([art. 23](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0070118&flno=23)(2)(4), (7)). Fees, leaks and short-term letting are covered in [our piece on management committees](/en/columns/taiwan-apartment-management-committee-fees-rules).

## Making it stop through the Civil Code

[Civil Code article 793](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=793) lets a landowner prohibit gas, smoke, odors, noise, vibration and similar intrusions from another's land or building. Its proviso cuts the other way: no injunction if the intrusion is slight, or reasonable given the shape of the land or local custom. Expect that argument near a night market.

Tenants can use article 793 in their own name. [Article 800-1](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=800-1) extends articles 774 to 800 to superficiaries, dian-right holders, tenants and other users of land or buildings.

Intentional or negligent infringement of another's rights creates liability ([art. 184](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=184)). Unlawful harm to "other personality interests" that is serious supports non-pecuniary damages ([art. 195](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=195)(1)), and infringement of personality rights may be ordered removed or prevented ([art. 18](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=18)(1)). Whether broken sleep reaches that level is for the court, weighing timing, frequency and volume.

The victim builds the record: time-stamped recordings, bureau readings, committee complaints and replies, and police call times. Sound travels through slabs; the flat above may not be the source. A complaint must name the parties, the claim and its facts, and the judgment sought ([Code of Civil Procedure art. 244](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=244)(1)), so you need to say who must stop what, and when. Leaving a lease early is covered in [our piece on early termination](/en/columns/taiwan-tenant-early-lease-termination-notice-penalty).

## Mediation at the district office

Mediation committees at township, town and city offices hear civil disputes ([Township and County-Administered City Mediation Act art. 1](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=I0020003&flno=1)); district committees in special municipalities follow the same act ([art. 35](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=I0020003&flno=35)). If both sides live in the same township, town or city, its committee hears the case ([art. 13](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=I0020003&flno=13)(1)). Apply in writing or orally, stating the dispute ([art. 10](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=I0020003&flno=10)); a session must be set within 15 days ([art. 15](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=I0020003&flno=15)).

It is free, apart from actual inspection costs ([art. 23](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=I0020003&flno=23)). But a civil mediation needs the parties' consent ([art. 11](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=I0020003&flno=11)), and if a party skips the session without good reason, the mediation is treated as failed unless the committee sees a chance of settlement and sets a new date ([art. 20](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=I0020003&flno=20)). A settlement approved by the court has the force of a final civil judgment ([art. 27](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=I0020003&flno=27)). A failure certificate is issued within seven days of a request ([art. 30](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=I0020003&flno=30)). A pending lawsuit can still go to mediation before first-instance arguments close ([art. 10](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=I0020003&flno=10)(3)). If the mediation succeeds and the court approves it before the judgment becomes final, the case ends; the plaintiff may seek back two thirds of the court fee within three months of service of the approved mediation ([art. 28](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=I0020003&flno=28)).

## When the foreign tenant is the one reported

A student in a Taichung studio hosts friends one Friday. After midnight, police knock on a downstairs call, and a committee warning follows (an invented example).

Police decisions come first. For fine-only offenses such as article 72, police question the person and, unless further investigation is needed, issue the decision themselves at once ([art. 43](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0080067&flno=43)(1)(1)). Minor, clear-cut cases may be decided without notice or questioning, but only up to NT$1,500 or a reprimand ([art. 44](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0080067&flno=44)). A decision not handed over on the spot must be served within five days ([art. 49](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0080067&flno=49)). The five-day objection period runs from the day after service ([art. 55](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0080067&flno=55)). After two months from the act, or from its end if it continued, police can no longer question or punish ([art. 31](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0080067&flno=31)). Turning the music off when told means the "refusing to stop" element is missing.

Tenants count as residents under the condominium act ([art. 3](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0070118&flno=3)(8)), so articles 16 and 47 apply to them. For a serious breach left unfixed three months after the committee asks, it may, on an owners' resolution, sue to have that resident removed ([art. 22](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0070118&flno=22)(1)(3)).

Then the lease. A tenant must use the flat as agreed, or as suits its nature; if the landlord objects and the misuse continues, the landlord may terminate ([Civil Code art. 438](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=438)). A single complaint does not meet that test. Answer a landlord's objection in writing and keep to what you promise. For residential leases, [article 10 of the Rental Housing Market Development and Regulation Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0060125&flno=10) requires written notice with supporting evidence 30 days before a termination based on law; leisure lets and leases under 30 days fall outside it ([art. 4](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0060125&flno=4)).

Residence status? [Immigration Act article 32](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0080132&flno=32) lists grounds for revoking a residence permit, among them false applications, forged documents, a final sentence of one year or more (excluding negligence and suspended sentences), nationality changes and deportation. A Social Order Maintenance Act fine is not on that list.
