# US tourists investigated over touching a sea turtle in Xiaoliuqiu

A member of the public's video led coast guard officers to question two US tourists over alleged contact with a sea turtle at Xiaoliuqiu's Vase Rock beach, [CNA reported on October 17, 2026](https://www.cna.com.tw/news/asoc/202610170038.aspx). According to that account, officers took the visitors by patrol boat to Taiwan's main island and referred them to Pingtung prosecutors on the evening of the October 15 incident. Specialists identified the animal as a green sea turtle, a protected species.

Neither [CNA's October 17, 2026 report](https://www.cna.com.tw/news/asoc/202610170038.aspx) nor [Focus Taiwan's report of the same date](https://focustaiwan.tw/society/202610170008) identifies the statutory article prosecutors will ultimately apply or reports a final disposition. The tourists are presumed innocent. [Code of Criminal Procedure Article 154](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0010001&flno=154) preserves that presumption until a conviction becomes final and requires criminal facts to be established by evidence. For another visitor facing a similar allegation, the assessment would turn on the nature of the contact and what the evidence shows about the person's actions.

## Disturbance does not require an injury

Taiwan's [Act on Wildlife Conservation](https://law.moj.gov.tw/ENG/LawClass/LawAll.aspx?pcode=M0120001) distinguishes disturbance, 騷擾, from abuse, 虐待. Under [Article 3](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=M0120001&flno=3), disturbance means conduct that interferes with wildlife through drugs, objects or other methods. Abuse means using violence, drugs improperly or other methods that injure wildlife or prevent it from maintaining its normal physiological state.

Evidence of disturbance need not show a wound. What it must establish is interference with the animal. An abuse allegation requires the relevant injury or inability to maintain a normal physiological state to be established.

## Disturbance can carry a criminal penalty

[Article 18](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=M0120001&flno=18) prohibits disturbing, abusing, hunting, killing or otherwise using protected wildlife, and sets out two exceptions. Those concern populations exceeding an area's carrying capacity, and academic research or education approved by the central authority. Use under the population exception requires prior local approval. The central authority must announce which species may be used, where, to what extent, in what numbers, during which period and by what method. A recreational purpose does not itself establish either exception.

For disturbance or abuse, [Article 42](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=M0120001&flno=42) addresses conduct without the population condition in Article 18(1)(1), or without central approval under the research or education provision. The basic penalty is up to one year's imprisonment, detention as a punishment, or a criminal fine of NT$60,000 to NT$300,000. A fine may also accompany imprisonment or detention.

If the conduct causes the animal's death, the maximum prison term becomes two years and the fine range becomes NT$100,000 to NT$500,000. Detention, a fine alone, or a fine alongside imprisonment or detention remain available. [Article 42](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=M0120001&flno=42) also increases punishment by up to one-third for an offense committed within a designated wildlife refuge. Applying that increase requires establishing the designation and the location of the conduct. These provisions describe the legal framework, not a sentence imposed in this case.

## The recording has to explain the contact

Intent also matters. [Criminal Code Article 12](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0000001&flno=12) excludes punishment where conduct involves neither intent nor negligence; negligent conduct is punishable only where legislation specifically provides for it. Article 42 contains no separate negligent-disturbance offense. Under [Criminal Code Article 13](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0000001&flno=13), intent covers knowingly and intentionally bringing about the facts constituting an offense. It also covers foreseeing those facts and accepting their occurrence. The assessment therefore goes beyond whether someone's hand appears near an animal in a photograph.

How did the contact begin? The sequence before and after it can help show whether a person deliberately approached the turtle and whether their actions disturbed it. A clip may leave its beginning unclear. Preserving the original recording allows counsel to examine what it actually captures, including its limits, alongside the visitor's account.

Species identification answers another question. It helps establish whether the animal falls within the protected category to which the relevant prohibition applies. It cannot establish a person's intent or show the effect of a particular movement.

## Understanding the questions is part of giving an account

For an English-speaking visitor, the language used during questioning can affect how precisely the contact is described. [Code of Criminal Procedure Article 99](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0010001&flno=99) requires an interpreter when a defendant cannot communicate in the language used, and also covers hearing or speech disabilities. Written questioning or written statements may additionally be used where necessary. [Article 100-2](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0010001&flno=100-2) applies this chapter's rules to judicial police questioning criminal suspects.

Before questioning, [Article 95](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0010001&flno=95) requires notification of the suspected conduct and all alleged offenses; a change in the alleged offense requires renewed notification. The person must also be told that they may remain silent, appoint defense counsel and request investigation of favorable evidence. A visitor with a recording showing a fuller sequence can therefore discuss with counsel how to request its investigation.

Counsel's absence can also affect whether questioning proceeds. When a defendant without counsel present says they have retained counsel, Article 95 requires questioning to stop immediately unless the defendant agrees to continue.
