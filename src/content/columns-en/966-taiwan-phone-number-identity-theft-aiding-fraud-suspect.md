# A Phone Number in Your Name Was Used in a Scam: What Taiwan Prosecutors Must Prove

First comes a message from your carrier: the number you have used for four years has to be re-verified by a set date, or service will be restricted. Days later, a written police notice (通知書) names you as a suspect (犯罪嫌疑人) in a fraud case and asks you to come in for questioning. The number in that case is one you have never used, registered with the same carrier under your Alien Resident Certificate. (This is an invented example: an American who has lived in Taiwan on an ARC for several years.)

Both letters can start from the same fraud case.

## Why your own line is being re-checked

[Article 18 of the Fraud Crime Hazard Prevention Act (詐欺犯罪危害防制條例)](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0080226&flno=18) starts with a notice to the carrier. When the telecom authority or the police notify a carrier that a subscriber or user has used a telecom service for fraud, the carrier must restrict or stop that service. It must then re-verify and re-register, within a deadline, the data for every other service the same subscriber applied for. Under that paragraph, the other lines must also be restricted or stopped if the subscriber does not cooperate with the re-check or the data turn out not to match the subscriber. [Article 19](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0080226&flno=19) requires the carrier to use a database designated by the telecom authority to help confirm identity when it accepts an application, and also when the authority or the police notify it of a risk of fraud that calls for a re-check.

Three years is the number to know. Under [Article 23](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0080226&flno=23), paragraph 1, a subscriber whose service was restricted or stopped under Article 18, paragraph 1 may apply for at most one new number or service with that carrier for three years from the date of the restriction or suspension notice. A subscriber who still holds another number or service there cannot have any new application accepted in that period. The paragraph has no exception for a subscriber whose identity someone else used. Once the telecom authority or the police notify the carrier that the reason for the restriction no longer exists, the carrier may restore service (Article 18, paragraph 3).

## The police notice

Staying away has a cost. [Article 71-1 of the Code of Criminal Procedure (刑事訴訟法)](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0010001&flno=71-1) lets the police summon a suspect by written notice, and when a lawfully notified suspect stays away without good reason, they may ask a prosecutor for an arrest warrant.

The rules for the interview come from the chapter on questioning defendants, which [Article 100-2](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0010001&flno=100-2) applies mutatis mutandis when the police question a suspect. Before the questions start, you must be told the suspected offenses, that you may stay silent and need not speak against your will, that you may choose a lawyer, and that you may ask for evidence in your favor to be investigated ([Article 95](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0010001&flno=95), paragraph 1). Silence alone cannot support an inference of guilt ([Article 156](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0010001&flno=156), paragraph 4). Someone questioned without a lawyer who says one has been retained is entitled to have the questioning stop at once, unless they agree to continue (Article 95, paragraph 2).

A suspect under police investigation may retain counsel at any time, and a legal representative, spouse, certain relatives or household members may retain counsel for the suspect on their own ([Article 27](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0010001&flno=27), paragraphs 1 and 2). Counsel may sit in, take notes and state opinions. That can be restricted or barred only on grounds listed in [Article 245](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0010001&flno=245), paragraph 2, such as facts showing a risk that evidence will be destroyed or that the suspect will collude with accomplices or witnesses. Investigators must give counsel the date, time and place of questioning, except in an emergency (paragraph 6).

Language can decide what ends up in the record. If you do not understand the language being used, [Article 99](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0010001&flno=99) requires an interpreter. The whole interview must also be audio-recorded without a break, unless there is an emergency and that is noted in the record. Where the written record of what you said does not match the recording, the mismatched part cannot be used as evidence, outside that emergency case ([Article 100-1](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0010001&flno=100-1)). How an interpreter's errors affect the record is covered in [interpreters at Taiwan police stations](/en/columns/taiwan-police-prosecutor-interpreter-oath-challenge-foreign-suspect).

## Naming the records you want collected

What should the carrier have on file? Under [Article 15 of the Fraud Crime Hazard Prevention Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0080226&flno=15), an applicant shows their own identity document. An agent shows that document, the agent's own ID and proof of authorization. Before signing the contract, the carrier must check that the documents match the applicant or agent, and record them.

Those requirements give a written request its list: the application filed on the day the number was opened, the identity document the carrier recorded, the agent's ID and authorization if an agent appears, and the result of the Article 19 database check. In the example, the same carrier also holds the record of the re-check on your own line, made with documents you presented yourself. Investigators can read it side by side with the application for the number in the case. The basis for asking is [Article 95](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0010001&flno=95), paragraph 1, subparagraph 4, read with [Article 2](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0010001&flno=2), which requires officials handling a case to pay attention to circumstances favorable to the accused as well as unfavorable ones.

Records can also be protected before they are lost. When evidence risks being destroyed, forged, altered, hidden or made hard to use, a suspect or defense counsel may ask the prosecutor during the investigation to order a search, seizure, expert examination, inspection, witness questioning or other preservation measure. For a signed form, the expert examination can be a handwriting comparison. Unless the prosecutor rejects the application as unlawful or groundless, the measure must be taken within five days; after a rejection, or if the five days pass, the applicant may go directly to the court ([Article 219-1](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0010001&flno=219-1)).

## Intent, the act and the burden of proof

Two things have to be shown against you: an act that helped, and intent. [Article 30 of the Criminal Code (中華民國刑法)](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0000001&flno=30) makes anyone who helps another person commit an offense an accessory, even when the person helped does not know it, and allows the accessory's punishment to be reduced from that of the principal. Under [Article 13](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0000001&flno=13), intent covers knowing the facts of the offense and meaning to bring them about, and also foreseeing them while accepting that they may happen.

Someone who sold or lent a SIM card did hand it over. What remains for them is intent, including the foreseeing kind. When the application file points to someone else using your documents, the dispute moves back a step, to whether you did anything that helped at all.

Bank accounts are governed by an additional statute. [Article 22 of the Money Laundering Control Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0380131&flno=22) prohibits handing over accounts at financial institutions, or accounts with virtual asset service providers or third-party payment services, unless the handover fits ordinary commercial or financial practice, rests on trust between relatives or friends, or has another legitimate reason. Phone numbers are not on that list. If an account of yours was frozen in the same scheme, [warning accounts in Taiwan](/en/columns/taiwan-warning-account-foreigners) explains what that listing does.

Penalties follow the principal offense. Ordinary fraud under [Article 339](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0000001&flno=339) carries up to five years' imprisonment or short-term detention, and a fine of up to NT$500,000 may be imposed instead or on top. When three or more people commit the fraud together, [Article 339-4](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0000001&flno=339-4) applies. Its range is one to seven years' imprisonment, with an optional fine of up to NT$1 million.

Proving it is the prosecutor's task. A defendant is presumed innocent until a guilty judgment becomes final ([Article 154 of the Code of Criminal Procedure](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0010001&flno=154)), and the prosecutor bears the burden of proving the facts of the offense and pointing to the means of proof ([Article 161](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0010001&flno=161), paragraph 1). When an investigation ends without sufficient grounds for suspicion, the prosecutor must decide not to prosecute ([Article 252](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0010001&flno=252), subparagraph 10).
