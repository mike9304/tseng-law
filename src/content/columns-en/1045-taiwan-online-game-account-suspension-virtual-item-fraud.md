# Taiwan Online Game Accounts: Suspensions, Bans and Item-Trade Fraud

Two days after buying in-game currency on a trading site, an engineer in Taipei finds his three-year-old account locked. The operator's email has one line: "received improperly transferred items." The seller has stopped answering (an invented example). Can he recover the account, or the money?

## The terms above the operator's terms

Online game contracts in Taiwan are usually operator-drafted standard forms governed by the Mandatory and Prohibited Terms for Standard-Form Contracts for Online Connected Game Services (網路連線遊戲服務定型化契約應記載及不得記載事項), covering server-connected online games but not arcade machines or games needing no server.

Its current text was amended by the Ministry of Economic Affairs on August 10, 2022 (No. 11102026090), effective January 1, 2023; per the Executive Yuan, the portfolio moved to the Ministry of Digital Affairs on August 27, 2022. The terms rest on [Consumer Protection Act art. 17](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0170001&flno=17)(1): mandatory terms bind even if omitted (art. 17(5)), and clauses breaking prohibited terms are void (art. 17(4)).

## Suspension: a notice to improve comes first

Point 14 voids game rules that conflict with the contract or strip the player's contractual rights, except action under point 15.

Under point 15, once a violation is shown, the operator posts a notice and contacts the player. A first violation calls for a notice to fix it within a set time; only if that fails may the operator restrict play, by severity. A repeat of the same violation can be restricted at once. Each restriction is capped at the contract's number of days, never more than seven.

Permanent bans differ: immediate termination after notice needs one of point 18's five grounds:

1. malicious attacks on the operator's systems;
2. cheats, viruses, exploited bugs or other unfair play;
3. buying points or items by impersonation or fraud;
4. repeating the same violation a set number of times (at least three) after a notice to improve;
5. illegal conduct found by the judicial authorities.

If the operator got the facts wrong or cannot prove them, it must compensate the player (point 18(4)); on termination it refunds unused paid points or fees, less necessary costs, within 30 days (point 18(5)). Inactive accounts (at least a year, as agreed) may be ended only if the player fails to log in by a deadline of at least 15 days (point 18(2)).

Complaints go in within seven days from the day after the notice, with a reply due within at most 15 days (point 16(1)). Operators must publish contact details and a 24-hour complaint channel, and reports of others' cheats use the same route (point 16(2)-(3)). Accounts are kept at least 30 days after termination; players not at fault may resume within that time (point 7).

## Seven days for whoever received the disputed items

Behind the locked account is point 8. Once the operator verifies a reported illegal login, it may suspend the account or issue new credentials, and immediately restricts the third party who received the transferred records and asks for an explanation. If none comes within seven days of notice, the operator restores the records to the original player, or agrees reasonable compensation if it cannot, and lifts the third party's restriction after restoring. It owes neither restoration nor compensation if the player did not use a free security device the operator offered, or is otherwise at fault. If the third party objects, the original player may report and sue. No one pays fees during the restriction.

A buyer of currency taken from a hacked account is that third party. Screenshots, payment records, seller messages, order numbers and in-game timestamps should go in before then. Game history is kept at least 30 days and supplied within seven days of request, free or up to NT$200 (point 9). Some sellers sell an account, then falsely report it stolen; point 8 makes false reporters fully liable for harm to the operator or third parties.

Contracts may not treat every action under the player's login as the player's own, or make the operator's records the sole basis for disputes (prohibited terms 7 and 6). Operators must keep systems reasonably secure for the time, restore damaged records promptly and charge nothing until repairs are done; they pay for harm from breaches or bugs, and proving no fault only reduces liability (point 13).

All in-game records belong to the operator, and the player's right to use them excludes transfers or profit outside the service (point 11). The operator may also decline to help with disputes over shared accounts or paying through others (point 13(5)).

## When the operator changes things

Within seven days of starting a game, a player may cancel in writing without reasons or cost and recover unused paid points (point 4). Contract changes need public and individual notice or are void; objecting within 15 days of the notice arriving counts as the player's termination (point 17). A fee rise needs public notice 30 days ahead, plus individual notice to players with registered contacts; if the new rate is higher, points or fees already registered keep the old rate (point 5). A shutdown needs the same notice at least 30 days ahead, failing which unused points are refunded in full, plus reasonable compensation (point 19). Planned maintenance needs seven days' notice, barring temporary or urgent needs or causes beyond the operator; outages it causes must be fixed at once, with deducted fees or items returned or otherwise compensated (point 12). Paid points cannot expire (prohibited term 10).

## Against the seller: criminal complaint and civil claim

Deceiving someone into handing over property or a financial benefit, intending unlawful ownership, is fraud, carrying up to five years' imprisonment or other penalties ([Criminal Code art. 339](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0000001&flno=339)); a seller who knowingly passed off stolen items could face it. Entering false data or improper commands into a computer to create property records and take another's property is computer fraud, up to seven years ([art. 339-3](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0000001&flno=339-3)).

Hacked-account owners have other provisions. Entering another's login without authority, breaking protection measures or exploiting loopholes to intrude carries up to three years ([art. 358](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0000001&flno=358)); obtaining, deleting or altering another's records and causing harm, up to five ([art. 359](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0000001&flno=359)); disrupting another's computer by programs and causing harm, up to three ([art. 360](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0000001&flno=360)). These three are prosecuted only on complaint ([art. 363](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0000001&flno=363)), which must be filed within six months of learning who the offender is ([Code of Criminal Procedure art. 237](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0010001&flno=237)(1)). Fraud is not on the art. 363 list. See also [our forex hacking article](/en/columns/taiwan-overseas-fx-broker-account-hacked-criminal-complaint-jurisdiction).

Anyone who intentionally or negligently and unlawfully injures another's rights must compensate ([Civil Code art. 184](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=184)(1)), within two years of knowing the damage and the liable person and ten years from the act ([art. 197](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=197)(1)). A purchase induced by fraud may be rescinded ([art. 92](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=92)(1)) within one year of discovering the fraud and ten years of the declaration ([art. 93](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=93)); it is then void from the start ([art. 114](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=114)(1)), and the price becomes returnable as unjust enrichment ([art. 179](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=179)).

## When a child paid

Under-18s are minors, and those aged seven or over have limited capacity ([Civil Code art. 12](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=12), [art. 13](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=13)). A minor with limited capacity needs the statutory agent's consent, except for acts bringing purely legal benefit or necessary for daily life given age and status ([art. 77](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=77)). A contract made without consent takes effect only on the agent's ratification ([art. 79](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=79)); if the other side sets a deadline of at least a month and the agent gives no definite answer, ratification is deemed refused ([art. 80](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=80)).

Game contracts build this in through point 2: where a minor paid without consent, the agent may apply through the operator's published procedure with supporting documents, and after confirming, the operator refunds unused game fees.

## Complaints and mediation

Consumers complain first to the operator, a consumer group or a consumer service center, and the operator has 15 days to deal with it ([Consumer Protection Act art. 43](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0170001&flno=43)(1)-(2)). If that fails, they may complain to the local consumer ombudsman (art. 43(3)) or seek mediation ([art. 44](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0170001&flno=44)); mediation needs only a prior complaint under art. 43(1) or (3) ([Consumer Dispute Mediation Rules art. 5](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0170008&flno=5)(1)(3)). Suits may be brought where the consumer relationship arose ([art. 47](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0170001&flno=47)), which point 20 bars forum clauses from excluding. See also [our purchase dispute article](/en/columns/taiwan-online-consumer-dispute-foreigners).
