A US finance employee may have to report authority over a Taiwan employer’s foreign bank account even without owning its funds. [FinCEN’s Report of Foreign Bank and Financial Accounts (FBAR) rule](https://www.fincen.gov/report-foreign-bank-and-financial-accounts) covers a US person’s financial interest or signature authority over foreign accounts when the aggregate value exceeds US$10,000 at any time in the calendar year, subject to applicable exceptions.

For an employee preparing to leave Taiwan, the account mandate and the dates of authority need review before access is removed. [31 CFR 1010.350(a)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-31/subtitle-B/chapter-X/part-1010/subpart-C/section-1010.350) concerns each year in which the relevant relationship exists, so removing authority at departure does not by itself erase the earlier part of that year.

## What could you instruct the bank to do?

Under [section 1010.350(f)(1)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-31/subtitle-B/chapter-X/part-1010/subpart-C/section-1010.350), signature or other authority means an individual can control disposition of account assets through direct communication with the institution, alone or together with someone else. Joint approval can count. Instructions may be written or communicated otherwise.

Ask the Taiwan company to identify the account holder, the institution and the powers attached to your name or banking role. Could you transmit a payment instruction, jointly authorize it, or only prepare an internal request for someone else? Keep the bank mandate and approval workflow together when asking the US adviser to classify the role. A job title alone leaves that question open.

Financial interest needs its own check if there is ownership, a nominee arrangement or another relationship covered by paragraph (e). This article concerns authority without financial interest.

## An exception and a shorter report have different effects

The officer and employee exceptions in [section 1010.350(f)(2)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-31/subtitle-B/chapter-X/part-1010/subpart-C/section-1010.350) depend on the specified employer, account and lack of financial interest. They include rules for certain regulated institutions and entities with the stated US securities listing or registration. A listing in Taipei alone does not establish the US exchange condition. The rule for a qualifying US subsidiary in a consolidated report should not simply be applied to a Taiwan subsidiary.

There is also modified reporting for a US person who resides outside the United States, is an officer or employee of an employer physically located outside the United States, and has signature authority over a foreign account owned or maintained by that employer. [FinCEN’s electronic filing instructions, Attachment C, Part IV](https://bsaefiling.fincen.gov/docs/XMLUserGuide_FinCENFBAR.pdf), corroborated by [IRS Publication 5569](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/p5569.pdf), specify Part I, the employer information in Part IV Items 34–43, and the signature section. This concerns signature authority without financial interest.

Modified reporting still requires a filing. The preparer can check residence, the actual employer and account ownership to identify which information this reporting route requires from local staff.

## Leaving the company without taking its banking archive

[IRS recordkeeping guidance](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/report-of-foreign-bank-and-financial-accounts-fbar) generally requires specified account records for five years from the FBAR due date. It expressly makes an exception for an officer or employee reporting signature authority over an employer’s foreign account: the individual need not personally retain those account records, and the employer keeps them.

FinCEN’s [FIN-2011-G003 guidance for former employees](https://www.fincen.gov/resources/statutes-regulations/guidance/reports-foreign-financial-accounts-fbars-requirements) also addresses proprietary and privacy concerns. It does not expect a former employer to provide its account information to a former employee. In that situation, the former employee must supply as much information as possible, including the fact of the authority and, at minimum, the former employer’s information in Part IV Items 34–42 and the employee’s title in Item 43. The guidance does not cover employment changes arranged to evade FBAR requirements.

Before leaving, agree on a contact for filing questions and an authorized way to confirm the information the preparer actually needs. Record the role and its start and end dates. Ask who will process the bank’s mandate change and how completion will be confirmed; returning a token, disabling a user and changing a mandate may involve separate steps to check with the particular bank.

If the employer refuses a request after departure, keep the request and response so the US preparer can assess the available information against the former-employee guidance.

## The filing year determines which extension to check

[FIN-2025-NTC3, dated December 8, 2025](https://www.fincen.gov/system/files/2025-12/FBAR-FBAR-Filing-Requirement-for-Certain-Financial-Professionals.pdf), extends the specified signature-authority filings to April 15, 2027 for individuals covered by the earlier extension notices. It covers authority held during 2025 and the earlier periods already deferred. Working for a Taiwan company does not by itself establish eligibility, and the notice should not be read as a universal rule for the 2026 reporting year.

For ordinary filings, the [IRS states](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/report-of-foreign-bank-and-financial-accounts-fbar) the annual April 15 deadline and automatic extension to October 15, with further relief where applicable. Give the preparer the calendar year and any earlier unfiled periods rather than asking only for “the overseas employee deadline.”
