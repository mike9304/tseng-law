## Fidelity contract

### Q1 — Scene duties and departure

- For injury or death, state the driver's immediate duties to provide aid,
  notify police, and preserve vehicles and scene evidence. Informal consent or
  a recording does not replace required measures.
- In an injury accident, if all parties agree, vehicles may be moved only
  after positions and scene marks are recorded and only to avoid obstructing
  traffic; aid/reporting duties remain.
- For property damage only, if vehicles can move, record positions/marks and
  photographs/video, then move promptly to safety. Leaving without required
  measures can cause administrative sanctions.
- Criminal Code Article 185-4 concerns a driver's departure after a traffic
  accident causing injury or death; application depends on the facts and
  measures taken.

### Q2 — Safety and evidence

- Safety and warning measures come first. For injury/rescue call `119`; for
  crime or urgent public safety call `110` or `112`; make the appropriate
  police report for the traffic accident.
- After safety, take wide and close photographs of vehicle positions, damage,
  road markings, signals, and weather.
- Preserve witness contacts, CCTV/dashcam requests, party/vehicle/insurance
  details, and medical records. Personal recording does not replace required
  police handling in an injury/death accident.
- State the police-document sequence: registration/contact form at the scene;
  scene diagram/photos from day 7; preliminary analysis form from day 30;
  confirm availability and requirements with the competent police agency.

### Q3 — Criminal/civil routes and periods

- Criminal Code Articles 284 and 287: negligent injury/serious injury are
  complaint-based; under Criminal Procedure Code Article 237 the ordinary
  complaint period is six months from learning the offender's identity.
- Civil Code Article 197: two years from knowledge of damage and the person
  liable, and ten years from the tort.
- Criminal Procedure Code Articles 487/488: a crime victim may file an
  attached civil action while the criminal case is pending, no later than the
  close of second-instance oral argument.
- Do not promise a cost-free result. Explain the ordinary filing-fee
  advantage, the Article 503 cost caveat after dismissal and transfer on the
  plaintiff's request, and the relevance of Article 504 transfer/procedure.
- Procedure depends on limitation interruption, defendants, evidence,
  insurance, and venue; there is no universally best route.

### Q4 — Individual criminal negligence and comparative fault

- Criminal liability requires each person's breach of a duty of care and
  causation of the other person's injury; fault on both sides does not
  automatically establish negligent injury.
- Civil Code Article 217 permits reduction or exemption when the injured
  person's fault contributed to causing or increasing damage.
- Preserve the conditional example: recognized damages of `TWD 1,000,000
  (one million New Taiwan dollars)` and 50% injured-party fault may reduce the
  amount to `TWD 500,000` before other adjustments.
- Appraisal/preliminary-analysis material may be important but does not
  mechanically bind the court, which evaluates the whole record.

### Q5 — Settlement scope and complaint withdrawal

- Include accident date/place and parties, amount and timing, insurance,
  included and reserved claims, future treatment or later-discovered injury,
  document delivery, and the relationship between payment and complaint
  withdrawal.
- Civil Code Articles 736–737: settlement is mutual concession, and
  extinguishment is limited to the rights waived by its wording. Do not state
  that every future claim always disappears.
- For a complaint-based offense, Criminal Procedure Code Article 238 permits
  withdrawal before the close of first-instance oral argument; a withdrawn
  complaint cannot be refiled.
- Private settlement does not automatically terminate prosecution for a
  non-complaint offense, and settlement does not invariably require
  withdrawal.
