# Injured at a Taiwan factory and still waiting for the employer's medical form?

A missing form from the employer should not make an injured factory worker wait to see a doctor. Tell the clinic or hospital that the injury happened at work, and keep the diagnosis and receipts. Taiwan's [Workforce Development Agency says](https://fw.wda.gov.tw/wda-employer/home/file/2c95efb390750af1019077a79eab0552) the Labor Insurance Bureau can issue an occupational injury medical form if the employer will not provide one, after checking the circumstances.

The question became urgent after a [November 4 fire at a factory in Hukou](https://www.nfa.gov.tw/pro/index.php?article_id=22250&code=list&flag=detail&ids=114). The National Fire Agency's initial report said five people had been taken to hospital and two were still missing at that stage. It did not establish the cause of the fire, anyone's legal fault or the missing people's eventual status.

## The form and the treatment record

Taiwan's occupational injury system uses a work injury outpatient form or hospitalization application for treatment at a National Health Insurance contracted provider. The [WDA guidance](https://fw.wda.gov.tw/wda-employer/home/file/2c95efb390750af1019077a79eab0552) also tells foreign workers to bring their health insurance card and residence card. A worker who has no form yet should identify the injury as work related when receiving care. Emergency treatment may come first.

What happens to money paid at the hospital? The [Labor Insurance Bureau says](https://www.bli.gov.tw/0109794.html?q=%25e5%2581%25a5%25e4%25bf%259d) a worker without the form may receive care through National Health Insurance and should keep the receipt and diagnosis. Supply the missing documents within ten days of the visit, excluding regular days off, or before discharge. Under the [medical-benefit rules](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0050039&flno=50), the hospital or clinic must then refund the insurance medical fees it collected. If a reason beyond the worker's control prevents that, the [Bureau's FAQ](https://www.bli.gov.tw/0106228.html) says to apply to it within six months of the outpatient visit or discharge; a special reason can extend that application period to five years. The [benefit claim lapses after five years from the date it can first be made](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0050031&flno=37). The earlier reimbursement deadlines still apply. The Bureau also lists a route to obtain the form from its office with supporting injury and identity documents.

A form the employer never issued is a reason to use the Bureau's route. It is not a reason to drop the claim. The Bureau [states that a worker at a unit subject to compulsory occupational injury insurance can still apply for benefits](https://www.bli.gov.tw/0105829.html) when that unit failed to enroll the worker. The Bureau must establish the employment and occupational injury facts. Whether this applies to a particular worker cannot be decided from a news report.

## Care now, income while recovering

Medical benefits and income replacement answer different needs. The government's [occupational injury benefits guide](https://www.gov.tw/News_Content_26_507990) and [Article 42](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0050031&flno=42) say a worker who cannot work because of an occupational injury or disease, does not receive their full usual wages as a result and remains in treatment may claim an injury-or-sickness benefit from the fourth day of that inability. It runs until the day before returning to work, for at most two years. The first two months are paid at 100 percent of the average monthly insured salary; from the third month, the rate is 70 percent. The [Bureau's calculation guidance](https://www.bli.gov.tw/0107053.html) includes the accident month and preceding five months in that average, or the actual insured period if shorter. Payment is made every half month. A worker whose employer was required to enroll them but did not may still claim; if no wage data is filed, the Bureau uses grade one of the salary table. Keep the doctor's account of when work became impossible, not just the hospital bill.

An employer's compensation obligations may also arise under [Article 59 of the Labor Standards Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0030001&flno=59). For the same accident, that article permits credit for sums the employer has already paid under labor insurance or another statute. Where the employer failed to enroll a worker it was required to insure, [Article 36](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0050031&flno=36) permits credit against Article 59 compensation only after the employer pays the amount assessed by the Bureau. The insurance cash benefit does not simply stand in for original wages. A Taiwan lawyer can compare the employment contract, insurance record, medical forms and wage slips before putting a figure on a claim. An injury on the way to work raises a different recognition question, covered in our [guide to commuting injuries](/en/columns/taiwan-commuting-accident-occupational-injury-insurance).

For a worker injured in a Taiwan factory, counsel can request the missing form, assemble the treatment and wage evidence, and pursue an insurance decision or the employer's compensation. The first useful record is the one made at the first hospital visit.
