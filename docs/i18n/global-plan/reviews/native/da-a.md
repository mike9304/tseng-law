## Verdict
naturalness (1 = machine, 5 = native professional): 3/5 for guidance pack, 2/5 for columns
variety used: Standard Danish (rigsdansk) for Denmark, consistent; no Norwegian Bokmål/Nynorsk mix. Isolated Swedish leftovers (`ur`, `artikelns`, `Kildeskattsatsen`) sit inside an otherwise Danish text. Number punctuation is mixed: thousands with `.` (`TWD 500.000`) is acceptable Danish; English `1.57M` and ISO dates are not.
systemic patterns (max 6, each one line, with 1 example quote):
- German pivot, not Danish legal style: "arbejdsgrupper", "ledsager klienter", "farbar kommunikationsvej", "udlovés", "Taiwans ret" (from *Tätigkeitsgruppen / begleiten / gangbar / zugesagt / Taiwans Recht*).
- Capital **De/Deres** throughout (German *Sie*); 2026 Danish law-firm sites use **du**.
- Korean-reader leftovers on Danish pages: Taiwan–Korea tax treaty as the 10 % rate, "Deres hovedbank i Korea", "et koreansk moderselskab", Hangul slugs.
- English chrome left on the Danish surface: `OUR TEAM`, `OFFICES`, `(English)`, `Q&A`.
- Swedish words inside Danish sentences: "tvister ur ansættelseskontrakten", "artikelns originalsprog", "Kildeskattsatsen".
- German *Kräfte* rendered as Danish "kræfter" (forces, not staff); other legal calques: "institutter", "hæfte" for 拘役, "nødunderhold" for 贍養費, "de store dommere" for 大法官.

Gender: Wei Tseng is correctly **hun** / **jeg** / ungendered **advokat**. No masculine form, pronoun or title for her. "han" in 008 is the male employee B, not the attorney. Consultation-language lock and the negative no-Danish / no-interpreter / no-free-first-meeting / no-result lines are intact (wording only). Columns 001–002 and 004 are dense but legally careful; 006 is the most natural stretch of prose; 003 Q16–Q20 and 008–009 keep a Korean blog voice.
