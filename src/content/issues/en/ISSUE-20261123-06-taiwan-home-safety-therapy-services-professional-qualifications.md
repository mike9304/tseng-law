# Home-safety help in Taiwan: when the service is regulated therapy

Who will actually assess a relative's mobility when a Taiwan service advertises home-safety planning? [Focus Taiwan reported on November 22](https://focustaiwan.tw/society/202611220009) that the health minister had warned about people using titles such as home safety planner or exercise therapist. His concern was unqualified people performing services reserved to licensed medical professionals.

For an English-speaking family arranging support from overseas, a familiar-sounding job title may leave the proposed work unclear. The report describes advertisements offering to examine older people's home environments, identify fall risks and recommend improvements. It does not establish that every home-safety service is unlawful.

## What the therapy statutes cover

[Article 12 of the Occupational Therapists Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=L0020039&flno=12) includes occupational-therapy assessments and the design and manufacture of splints and functional aids, and training in their use. Physical therapy has its own statutory scope. [Article 12 of the Physical Therapists Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=L0020044&flno=12) includes assessments, treatment planning, therapeutic exercise, and training in the use of walking aids and other specified devices.

Those provisions give a family concrete services to compare with an advertisement or proposed appointment. A promise to perform a therapy assessment or therapeutic exercise deserves a more precise explanation than a general description of wellness support. The wording of an advertisement is relevant evidence of what is offered, but it cannot by itself establish what happened during a visit or whether an offense occurred.

Criminal consequences are possible. [Article 41 of the Occupational Therapists Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=L0020039&flno=41) and [Article 32 of the Physical Therapists Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=L0020044&flno=32) penalize performing the respective therapy without the legally required qualifications. Both preserve an exception for students in the respective therapy programs, and graduates within six months of receiving their graduation certificate, interning under the direction of a therapist in that profession. The supervision requirement is explicit.

## The physician-order exception has a limited purpose

Both Article 12 provisions generally require therapists to work from a physician's diagnosis, referral or orders. They expressly exempt work whose purpose is not disease treatment. A physician's order is therefore not required in every instance of qualified professional practice.

That exception leaves the qualification rules intact. It also does not eliminate practice registration: [Article 7 of the Occupational Therapists Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=L0020039&flno=7) and [Article 7 of the Physical Therapists Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=L0020044&flno=7) require registration with the authority responsible for the place of practice and issuance of a practice license before practice begins. Qualifications and registration are distinct requirements.

For a family, a statement that no physician referral is needed answers only part of the question. It may address the purpose of the proposed work. It does not establish that the person offering it has the Taiwan qualification and practice license appropriate to that work.

Place-of-practice limits also remain. Under Article 9 of the [Occupational Therapists Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=L0020039&flno=9) and [Physical Therapists Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=L0020044&flno=9), practice is generally limited to one authorized medical institution, therapy clinic or other recognized institution. There are exceptions for support between institutions or prior approval. A practice license alone therefore does not settle the arrangement for a home visit.

## An overseas qualification does not settle Taiwan authorization

The same caution applies when a provider advertises credentials from the United States or another country. For foreign occupational therapists, [Article 58-1](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=L0020039&flno=58-1) provides for taking Taiwan's examination. A foreign national who passes and obtains the occupational-therapist certificate must also obtain permission from the central competent authority to practice, alongside the applicable Taiwan requirements. Overseas credentials alone do not show that these steps have been completed.

The minister's warning concerns the match between services and qualifications. For the proposed home visit, the useful details are the assessment or training promised, the person who will perform it, and that person's Taiwan authorization. A service label cannot supply the missing connection.
