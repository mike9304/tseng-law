# Still on Singapore payroll during a Taiwan assignment in 2027: is your salary taxable there?

Taiwan can generally tax salary for work you do there even if your employer pays it from Singapore. [Income Tax Act Article 8, item 3](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0340003&flno=8) (所得稅法) classifies that pay as Taiwan-source income (中華民國來源所得), subject to its 90-day exception. [Article 2(1)](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0340003&flno=2) makes Taiwan-source income taxable. For a four-month assignment in 2027, [the new Taiwan–Singapore agreement's Article 15](https://www.president.gov.tw/File/Doc/0e675f7d-760c-4de8-bf1c-5529044716ab) allows only Singapore to tax that salary if you are a Singapore treaty resident (居住者) and all three conditions are met. Your total Taiwan stay must not exceed 183 days in any twelve-month period beginning or ending in the relevant calendar year. Your employer must not be a Taiwan resident, and it must pay you directly or someone else must pay you on its behalf. Its Taiwan permanent establishment (常設機構) or fixed base (固定處所) must not bear the salary cost. The agreement's separate limits on who can receive tax benefits also apply.

As of November 23, 2026, the new agreement has entered into force, but its Taiwan tax application dates are still ahead. Under [Article 28(2)(2)](https://www.president.gov.tw/File/Doc/0e675f7d-760c-4de8-bf1c-5529044716ab) and the [Ministry of Finance announcement](https://www.mof.gov.tw/singlehtml/384fb3077bb349ea973e7fc6f13b6974?cntId=fc1efb8360554ee0b248f688b42766ae), Taiwan applies it to tax paid or withheld at source (就源繳納或扣繳之稅款) on income payable on or after January 1, 2027. For other Taiwan income taxes, it covers income for tax periods beginning on or after that date. For Singapore taxes other than tax withheld at source, Article 28(2)(1) applies the agreement from the year of assessment beginning on January 1, 2028—the start of the second calendar year after its entry into force in 2026. Pay connected with work in 2026 requires checking Taiwan's domestic rules alongside the applicable agreement. The earlier agreement remains relevant for Taiwan tax paid or withheld at source if the income became payable before January 1, 2027. For other Taiwan taxes, it remains relevant if the tax period began before that date. The work date alone does not select the agreement.

## Overseas pay and the domestic 90-day exception

Under [Article 8, item 3](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0340003&flno=8), remuneration for services performed in Taiwan is Taiwan-source income (中華民國來源所得). Its exception covers a nonresident (非中華民國境內居住之個人) whose total Taiwan stay does not exceed 90 days in one tax year and who receives the remuneration from an employer outside Taiwan. Staying on Singapore payroll does not extend that exception to a longer stay.

Residence has its own test. [Article 7](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0340003&flno=7) treats someone with a Taiwan domicile (住所) who habitually resides there as a resident (中華民國境內居住之個人). Someone without that domicile is a resident for a tax year if their total Taiwan stay in that year is 183 days or more. People outside both categories are nonresidents. The [income tax residence column](/en/columns/taiwan-income-tax-residency) discusses arrivals and departures more broadly.

An assignee without a Taiwan domicile whose total stay is 91 to 182 days in the tax year remains a domestic nonresident, but the overseas-employer pay exception does not apply. Whether a treaty exemption is available depends on Article 15's separate conditions and treaty eligibility.

## When can only Singapore tax your pay?

For ordinary employment, [Article 15(1)](https://www.president.gov.tw/File/Doc/0e675f7d-760c-4de8-bf1c-5529044716ab) permits Taiwan to tax remuneration for work performed in Taiwan by a Singapore treaty resident. Paragraph 2 reserves taxation to the residence territory when all three conditions hold, subject to the separate limits in Article 27 on entitlement to benefits (享有利益資格):

1. Your continuous or aggregate presence in Taiwan does not exceed 183 days in any twelve-month period beginning or ending in the relevant calendar year.
2. The remuneration is paid by, or on behalf of, an employer that is not a Taiwan resident.
3. The remuneration is not borne by that employer's Taiwan permanent establishment (常設機構) or fixed base (固定處所).

Singapore payroll proves neither residence nor cost allocation. [Article 4](https://www.president.gov.tw/File/Doc/0e675f7d-760c-4de8-bf1c-5529044716ab) first looks to residence under each territory's tax law. If you are resident in both, having a permanent home (永久住所) in only one territory makes you its treaty resident. If you have a permanent home in both, your treaty residence is where your personal and economic ties are closer—the center of vital interests (主要利益中心). If that center cannot be determined, or you have a permanent home in neither territory, habitual abode (經常居所) is next. Where there is a habitual abode in both or neither, the competent authorities must resolve residence by mutual agreement. Singapore residence needs its own confirmation.

## The two 183-day tests can produce different answers

Without a Taiwan domicile, your domestic residence depends on the total days within one tax year. The treaty tests every twelve-month period beginning or ending in the relevant calendar year. Year-end does not reset the treaty's clock.

An employee without a Taiwan domicile spends 100 days there in 2027 and 95 in 2028, with no other Taiwan visits (a fictional example). All 195 days fall within one relevant twelve-month period. Each year's total is below 183, so the employee remains a domestic nonresident in both tax years. Both annual stays exceed 90 days, so Article 8, item 3's exception does not apply in either year. The employee also fails the treaty's day condition. HR needs both annual totals and the timeline across years.

Exactly 183 days meets Article 15's day limit only if no twelve-month period beginning or ending in the relevant calendar year exceeds that total. Under Article 7, a person without a Taiwan domicile is a resident for a tax year with a total Taiwan stay of 183 days or more. The other treaty conditions still require separate checks.

## A recharge needs more than a payroll label

Who ultimately bears the salary cost? Article 15(2) asks whether the employer's Taiwan permanent establishment or fixed base bears the remuneration. If it does, the third condition fails. A recharge agreement can therefore raise a tax issue even while the Singapore company continues making the bank transfers.

A Taiwan subsidiary's involvement requires closer examination. [Article 5(7)](https://www.president.gov.tw/File/Doc/0e675f7d-760c-4de8-bf1c-5529044716ab) says control between companies does not, by itself, make either a permanent establishment of the other. Review the employer's identity, any Taiwan establishment and the cost allocation together. Keep the recharge agreement with the assignment letter.

Directors' fees (董事報酬) have a separate rule. [Article 16](https://www.president.gov.tw/File/Doc/0e675f7d-760c-4de8-bf1c-5529044716ab) permits Taiwan to tax fees and similar payments received by a Singapore resident for serving on the board of a Taiwan-resident company. Those payments need their own classification alongside any employment salary.

[Article 27(1)](https://www.president.gov.tw/File/Doc/0e675f7d-760c-4de8-bf1c-5529044716ab) requires denial of benefits for the income concerned if, considering all facts and circumstances, it is reasonable to conclude that obtaining those benefits directly or indirectly was one of the main purposes of an arrangement or transaction. An exception applies if it is shown that granting the benefits in those circumstances accords with the object and purpose of the relevant treaty provisions.

## Withholding and filing follow the actual tax position

[Article 2](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0340003&flno=2) generally subjects nonresident Taiwan-source income to withholding, except where the Act provides otherwise. As of November 23, 2026, [Article 3 of the withholding-rate standards](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0340028&flno=3) (各類所得扣繳率標準) sets ordinary nonresident salary withholding at 18%. For nonresident individuals other than government personnel posted abroad who receive government pay, the rate is 6% when total monthly salary payments do not exceed 1.5 times the Executive Yuan-approved monthly basic wage (基本工資). These are withholding rules, not the agreement's exemption test.

Under [Article 73(1)](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0340003&flno=73), for a nonresident's income covered by [Article 88](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0340003&flno=88), the withholding agent (扣繳義務人) must withhold the income tax payable, at the prescribed rate, when making the payment, and that income is excluded from the ordinary annual return. For taxable income outside that scope, a nonresident leaving before the filing period begins must file and pay before departure. If still in Taiwan during the filing period, the nonresident must file and pay within that period. If you become a Taiwan resident, [Article 71(1)](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0340003&flno=71) generally requires an annual return (結算申報) for the previous year from May 1 through May 31. Broader payroll issues appear in the [foreign employee payroll column](/en/columns/taiwan-payroll-foreign-employees-withholding-social-insurance).

For a Singapore treaty resident who has actually paid Taiwan tax, directly or by deduction, on income obtained from Taiwan and which Taiwan may tax under the agreement, [Article 23(1)(1)](https://www.president.gov.tw/File/Doc/0e675f7d-760c-4de8-bf1c-5529044716ab) requires Singapore to credit that tax against the Singapore tax due on the same income, up to the amount Singapore law allows.
