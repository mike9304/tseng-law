# CyberLink’s suspended Apple downloads: when disclosure is due

CyberLink said on October 24 that downloads of some products on Apple’s platform had stopped because they did not meet certain Apple requirements. The Taiwan software company had appealed and was still assessing the impact on its operations and finances, according to [CNA](https://www.cna.com.tw/news/afe/202610240065.aspx). For overseas investors following a Taiwan issuer, the timing matters: an operational event can be known before its financial consequences are fully measured.

The company said the affected sales accounted for about 14.69% of its 2025 revenue, while it continued to assess the actual financial and business effect. CNA also reported that products on Windows, Android and the web were unaffected. The announcement concerns some downloads on Apple’s platform, rather than a companywide halt in sales.

## The event starts the statutory timetable

For companies subject to its reporting requirements, [Article 36(3)(2) of the Securities and Exchange Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0400001&flno=36) requires a public announcement and a report to the competent authority within two days of an event materially affecting shareholder interests or securities prices. The statutory trigger is the material event. It is not the completion of a loss calculation.

Materiality still needs to be established. [Article 7 of the Act’s Enforcement Rules](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0400002&flno=7) includes, among other categories, important contract changes with a material financial or business impact. A temporary interruption to downloads does not, by itself, establish that a contract has been terminated. The underlying arrangement and what actually changed remain relevant; the news report does not supply the full contract.

Waiting for a settled revenue forecast and deciding whether a reportable event has occurred are different tasks. The available facts may support an event disclosure while leaving the duration, recovery prospects or ultimate financial effect unresolved.

## TWSE rules add a separate timetable

CyberLink is listed on the Taiwan Stock Exchange, as its [official company profile](https://www.twse.com.tw/pdf/ch/5203_ch.pdf) confirms. The exchange’s material-information procedures therefore matter alongside the statute. The statute’s two-day period is not a general grace period.

For most events covered by Article 4 of those procedures, [TWSE Article 6](https://twse-regulation.twse.com.tw/TW/law/DOC01.aspx?FLCODE=FL007111&FLNO=6) requires disclosure by two hours before trading begins on the next business day after the event. If the company issues a press release sooner, the filing must be simultaneous. Special categories, including trading-suspension announcements, have their own timing; certain media reports also require an immediate explanation, within two hours of discovery. Those deadlines run on Taiwan time.

An overseas communications team may therefore need the exchange filing ready when it releases a statement. A headquarters time zone does not reset the Taiwan timetable. Article 6’s general filing rule also calls for Chinese and English submissions at the same time. Which deadline applies depends on the event category and sequence, including any earlier public statement.

## Accuracy includes the limits of an estimate

[Article 20(2) of the Securities and Exchange Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0400001&flno=20) prohibits false statements or concealment in financial reports and financial or business documents filed or published under the Act. An assumed loss should not be presented as an established figure. Uncertainty needs accurate wording too.

The CNA report does not establish a disclosure violation by CyberLink, nor does the company’s continuing assessment establish one. A compliance assessment would need the relevant event date, the company’s filings and their contents. For readers following this suspension, the useful distinction is between the interruption already announced and the financial impact still being assessed; a later update may resolve one without changing when the other occurred.
