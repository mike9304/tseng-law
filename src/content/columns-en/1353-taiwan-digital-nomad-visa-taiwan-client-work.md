# Can you freelance for a Taipei startup on Taiwan's digital nomad visa?

If a Taipei startup offers to pay for a design project into your US bank account, can you take the job while you're in Taiwan on the digital nomad visitor visa (數位遊牧停留簽證)? Not under that visa's terms. [Article 14](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=A0030295&flno=14) of the Act for the Recruitment and Employment of Foreign Professionals (外國專業人才延攬及僱用法) makes the visa available to a foreign professional (外國專業人才) who works remotely by digital means, provides no services to a business entity or employer in Taiwan (非為我國境內事業單位或雇主提供勞務), and meets conditions announced by the competent authority. A startup in Taipei is a business entity in Taiwan.

Where the money lands doesn't change that. Article 14 asks whom the services are for and never mentions the account that receives payment. Nor does the article grant permission to work. What it lets the Ministry of Foreign Affairs (外交部) or an overseas mission (駐外館處) issue is a visitor visa (停留簽證). Whether you would also need separate work authorization depends on whether the project counts as working in Taiwan under the Employment Service Act.

## Six months at a time, up to two years per stay

Each visa is valid for six months, allows multiple entries, and comes with a six-month stay. It carries no annotation barring extension or imposing other restrictions. Once in Taiwan, a holder who needs more time may apply to the National Immigration Agency (內政部移民署) before the stay runs out. Each extension must not exceed six months, the holder may be exempted from leaving the country, and the total stay each time is capped at two years (每次總停留期間最長為二年).

Family members may apply under the same article. Your spouse, your minor children, and any adult child who cannot live independently because of a physical or mental disability may apply for the same visitor visa.

This text of Article 14 is part of the full revision of the Act promulgated on September 24, 2025. As of November 17, 2026, it has been in force since January 1, 2026, the date set by Executive Yuan (行政院) order and recorded on the [law-history page](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawHistory.aspx?pcode=A0030295).

## What the January 2025 consular page asks for

Article 14 leaves the detailed conditions to the competent authority, which [Article 3](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=A0030295&flno=3) identifies as the National Development Council (國家發展委員會). The Bureau of Consular Affairs (外交部領事事務局) [page on the digital nomad visitor visa](https://www.boca.gov.tw/cp-400-7717-70163-1.html), as checked on November 17, 2026, was published on January 2, 2025 and updated on January 3, 2025. Both dates fall before the current Article 14 took effect. The conditions in force now may differ from what the page lists.

The page limits applicants to nationals of countries that receive Taiwan's visa-exempt treatment. On that page, an applicant shows one of three things. One is proof of a digital nomad visa issued by another country. Another is income of at least US$40,000 in either of the last two years, for applicants aged 30 or over. The third is income of at least US$20,000 in either of those years, for applicants from 20 to under 30. The page separately asks for proof of regular deposits over the last six months, with an average monthly deposit amount of at least US$10,000, and for international medical insurance covering treatment and hospitalization during the planned stay.

Nothing in those thresholds speaks to who your clients are. That limit comes from the statute.

## Article 43 and working in Taiwan

When a foreign professional does professional work or looks for a job in Taiwan, the Foreign Professionals Act applies first. Under its [Article 2](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=A0030295&flno=2), anything that Act leaves open falls to the Employment Service Act (就業服務法), the Immigration Act (入出國及移民法), and other related laws. [Article 43 of the Employment Service Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0090001&flno=43) sets the baseline: unless that Act provides otherwise, a foreigner must not work in Taiwan (在中華民國境內工作) without a permit applied for by an employer.

Article 43 does not spell out when a freelance project for a local client becomes work in Taiwan. If your startup project does, the visitor visa cannot fill the gap. You would need authorization under the Foreign Professionals Act, the Employment Service Act, or another applicable law, or an exemption that one of them provides. Whether a project contract is really employment is a question of its own, and [a related column on contractor status and work permits](/en/columns/taiwan-foreign-freelancer-contractor-worker-status-work-permit) looks at how Taiwan draws that line.

Penalties for a violation of Article 43 are set out in [Article 68](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0090001&flno=68). The fine runs from NT$30,000 to NT$150,000. A foreigner who violates Article 43 must be ordered to leave immediately and is barred from working in Taiwan again. A violator who is given a deadline to leave and is still in Taiwan when it passes may be removed by force by the immigration authority, and may be detained until departure.

## If the Taipei client matters more

One route does not depend on the startup at all: the [Employment Gold Card](/en/columns/taiwan-employment-gold-card) (就業金卡). Under [Article 9](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=A0030295&flno=9), a foreign specific professional (外國特定專業人才) who plans to do professional work in Taiwan may apply directly to the National Immigration Agency for a card combining a work permit, a resident visa, an Alien Resident Certificate (外僑居留證), and a re-entry permit (重入國許可). Before issuing it, the Agency must review the application with the Ministry of Labor (勞動部) and the Ministry of Foreign Affairs. Applicants already in Taiwan may skip the resident-visa application. A card is valid for one to three years. Holders who meet certain conditions may apply before it expires to extend it, up to three years at a time.

Whether you qualify is the open question. [Article 4](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=A0030295&flno=4) defines a foreign specific professional as a foreign professional with special expertise Taiwan needs, as announced by the relevant central authorities, in fields that include technology, digital, culture and the arts, and architectural design, or one the National Development Council recognizes as having special expertise after consulting those authorities. Nothing in the Act says whether a particular designer's portfolio meets an announced standard.

Another route runs through an employer. For specialized or technical work, [Article 5](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=A0030295&flno=5) of the Foreign Professionals Act requires the employer, unless that Act provides otherwise, to submit documents to the Ministry of Labor for a permit and to follow the Employment Service Act. [Article 48 of the Employment Service Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0090001&flno=48) likewise makes the employer's permit application the general rule, subject to three listed exceptions. [Article 46](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0090001&flno=46) limits the jobs a foreigner may be hired for, unless that Act provides otherwise, to listed categories, the first being specialized or technical work (專門性或技術性之工作).

Should you qualify as a foreign specific professional and an employer hire you for professional work, [Article 8](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=A0030295&flno=8) lets that hiring permit run for up to five years. If the employer still needs you when it expires, it may apply for extensions of up to five years each.
