# A 19-year-old's scooter insurance had lapsed, and a Taiwan court held the legal representatives liable too

Twenty percent against eighty. That was how the court split fault after two scooters collided at an intersection in Xinzhuang, New Taipei, in April 2018, with the larger share on the injured rider. Even so, the first-instance court (New Taipei District Court, 109 Su No. 854) ordered the rider with the 20 percent share, together with that rider's two legal representatives, to repay the Special Compensation Fund NT$996,940 plus interest, and on February 24, 2021 the Taiwan High Court dismissed the appeal and upheld that order ([Taiwan High Court civil judgment 109 Shang-Yi No. 1077](https://www.mvacf.org.tw/ckfinder/connector?command=Proxy&type=Attach&currentFolder=/tblResearches/&fileName=%E8%87%BA%E7%81%A3%E9%AB%98%E7%AD%89%E6%B3%95%E9%99%A2+109+%E5%B9%B4%E4%B8%8A%E6%98%93%E5%AD%97%E7%AC%AC+1077+%E8%99%9F%E6%B0%91%E4%BA%8B%E5%88%A4%E6%B1%BA.pdf&c=7&p=3e6b728e9dc54764aecca77731d726ab&l=3)). No appeal was allowed.

Why the representatives? The law required them to prove that their supervision had not lapsed. The court pointed to the rider's expired compulsory insurance, which they had not made sure was renewed, and noted that they offered no other evidence.

## Flashing yellow, flashing red

On April 23, 2018, the rider, a 19-year-old university student, was riding along a main road in Xinzhuang. The signal showed flashing yellow on that side and flashing red on the other. An 83-year-old rider came out of the side road without giving way, which the court found to be the main cause. The younger rider, approaching a flashing yellow, also failed to watch the road ahead and slow down. Neither side disputed the 20/80 split on appeal.

Injuries were severe: the older rider suffered a skull fracture and bleeding in the brain, and was later certified as needing round-the-clock care. Because the younger rider's scooter insurance had lapsed without renewal, the claim went to the Special Compensation Fund, which paid NT$1,722,095 in medical and disability benefits. That figure was not disputed either.

## The rider, the representatives and joint liability

[Article 187(1) of the Civil Code](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=187) makes a person of limited capacity who could understand their conduct jointly and severally liable with their legal representative. To escape, the representative must prove that supervision was not neglected, or that the harm would have happened despite proper supervision (Article 187(2)). The court put that burden on the representatives. For a minor child, the legal representatives are in principle the parents ([Article 1086(1)](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=1086)).

In their defence, the representatives argued that the rider was licensed and that they were not at the scene. The court answered that a licence only shows a person may lawfully ride. Supervision, it said, covers both day-to-day upbringing and guarding against specific risks. The law requires compulsory insurance for a scooter, yet after the policy expired the representatives did nothing to see it renewed. That, the court held, showed a lapse in supervision over the rider's life in society. Each representative was held jointly and severally liable with the rider.

Would the answer be the same today? At the time, adulthood began at 20. [Article 12 of the Civil Code](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=12) now sets it at 18, effective January 1, 2023. A 19-year-old who caused the same crash today would be an adult, and Article 187 would not reach the representatives. For riders under 18 it still applies.

## What was left after 80 percent

Losses came to NT$4,984,698: NT$7,112 in medical costs, NT$4,677,586 for care at NT$60,000 a month over a remaining life expectancy of 7.66 years, and NT$300,000 for non-financial harm. After deducting the injured rider's 80 percent share under [Article 217(1)](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=217), NT$996,940 remained.

On appeal, the rider's side also argued that the Fund had paid only medical and disability benefits, so it could not step into claims for care costs or non-financial harm. The court disagreed. Compulsory insurance reimburses medical costs and pays fixed disability sums regardless of the individual case, so the payment is set off against total damages ([Compulsory Automobile Liability Insurance Act, Article 42(1) and (2)](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0390060&flno=42)). With the Fund's payment of about NT$1.72 million exceeding the NT$996,940 owed, the Fund could claim the full NT$996,940, with interest from June 4, 2020.

## Teenagers on scooters in Taiwan

A vehicle owner must buy compulsory insurance and renew it before it ends ([Article 6](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0390060&flno=6)). Whether the scooter is registered to the teenager or to a parent, someone in the household needs to own the renewal date. In this case, the lapse the court pointed to was not the moment of the crash but a renewal nobody had made sure of.
