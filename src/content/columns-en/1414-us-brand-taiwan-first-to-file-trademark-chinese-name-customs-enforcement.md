# Selling a US brand into Taiwan: why first-to-file trademarks and Customs recordal come first

Years of US sales do not create trademark rights in Taiwan. Under [Article 2 of Taiwan's Trademark Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0070001&flno=2) (商標法), any business seeking trademark rights must apply to the Intellectual Property Office (經濟部智慧財產局, TIPO). A US brand that leaves its Taiwan filing for later (US law needs its own review) risks handing the earliest filing date to a distributor, contract factory, or third party.

Registration grants ten-year rights from the registration publication date, renewable for ten-year terms ([Article 33](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0070001&flno=33)). Once registered, [Article 35](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0070001&flno=35) grants exclusive rights for designated goods or services and, subject to Article 36 exceptions, requires the holder's consent before anyone uses an identical mark on identical goods or services, or an identical or similar mark on identical or similar goods or services where relevant consumers are likely to be confused.

## Registration creates rights, including for a Chinese brand name

File before pitching distributors, sharing samples, or exhibiting in Taipei. [Article 18](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0070001&flno=18) covers any distinctive sign (words, graphics, symbols, colors, three-dimensional shapes, motions, holograms, sounds, or combinations) indicating source. Descriptive or generic signs are barred under [Article 29](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0070001&flno=29) unless descriptive or other non-distinctive elements acquire distinctiveness through trade use. Under paragraph 3, a non-distinctive element that could cast doubt on the scope of the right must be disclaimed, or the mark cannot be registered.

Why file a Chinese equivalent too? A Chinese name that distributors or customers start using can be filed by someone else. If a local party files that name first, [Article 30, paragraph 1, item 10](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0070001&flno=30) blocks later identical or confusingly similar filings for the same or similar goods or services unless the earlier filer consents and the filing is not manifestly improper. Filing both your English mark and chosen Chinese name across core classes prevents a partner from owning your local name.

A California audio brand ships prototypes to a Taichung factory and pitches three Taipei distributors before filing (an invented example). Four months later, one distributor files both the English brand and its Chinese nickname. The brand now has to fight for its own name. Brands comparing subsidiaries, branches, and franchising can read [foreign brand entry in Taiwan](/en/columns/taiwan-franchise-foreign-brand-entry).

## Challenging a local partner or squatter who filed first

An earlier filing by someone else is not final. Anyone may oppose a mark (異議) within three months from the day after registration publication if it violates Article 29, paragraph 1, Article 30, paragraph 1, or Article 65, paragraph 3 ([Article 48](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0070001&flno=48)), targeting all or part of the goods with one filing per mark.

After three months, the route is invalidation (評定): an interested party may apply for it, or an examiner may propose it, under [Article 57](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0070001&flno=57). Against a distributor, OEM supplier, or trade-show contact, Article 30, paragraph 1, item 12 bars registering a mark identical or similar to another's earlier-used mark on identical or similar goods or services where the applicant learned of it through a contractual, geographic, business-dealing, or other relationship and filed with intent to imitate, absent consent. Under Article 30, paragraph 2, prior use and well-known status are judged as of the filing date.

Five years is a hard cutoff for item 12 claims. [Article 58, paragraph 1](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0070001&flno=58) bars invalidation under Article 30, paragraph 1, items 9 through 15 once five full years pass after registration publication. Paragraph 2 lifts that limit only for bad-faith violations of item 9 (wine or spirits geographical indications) or item 11 (well-known marks facing confusion or dilution), not item 12. Citing your own Taiwan registration that is three or more years old under item 10 also requires proof of genuine use in the three years before the invalidation filing, or justifiable reasons for non-use (Article 57, paragraphs 2 and 3).

## Clearing unused registrations after three years

A registration can sit unused. [Article 63, paragraph 1, item 2](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0070001&flno=63) requires TIPO to revoke a registration, ex officio or on application, if it goes unused (or use stops continuously) for three full years without justifiable reason, unless a licensee used it; paragraph 4 permits partial revocation. Hold off on warning a dormant holder before filing: under paragraph 3, revocation fails if the mark is in use when petitioned, except where the holder knew revocation was coming and began use within the prior three months.

| Procedure | Who may file | Statutory deadline | Core trigger |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opposition (Art. 48) | Anyone | Within 3 months after publication | Violates Art. 29(1), Art. 30(1), or Art. 65(3) |
| Invalidation (Arts. 57–58) | Interested party or examiner | Within 5 years after publication (bad-faith exception only for Art. 30(1)(9), (11)) | Prior mark, well-known mark, or bad-faith filing under Art. 30(1)(12) |
| Non-use revocation (Art. 63(1)(2)) | On application, or TIPO ex officio | After 3 full years of non-use or suspension | No genuine owner or licensee use without justifiable reason |

## Recording registered marks with Taiwan Customs

Border interception builds on registration. Under the [Regulations Governing Customs Measures in Protecting the Rights and Interests of Trademark](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawAll.aspx?pcode=G0350069) (海關執行商標權益保護措施實施辦法, last amended September 15, 2021, authorized by [Trademark Act Article 78](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0070001&flno=78)), Articles 2 and 3 allow filing for advice protection (提示保護) in Customs' database. Per the [Customs Administration application guide](https://web.customs.gov.tw/singlehtml/1207?cntId=cus1_147961_1207) (published July 3, 2026), owners apply online via the Customs-Port-Trade Single Window (關港貿單一窗口) with one filing per registration number, submitting genuine-versus-fake descriptions, digital images of registered goods, registration proof, and contact details. Approved protection runs to the end of the trademark term; after a renewal, the owner may apply to Customs with proof of renewal to extend it (Article 4). Recorded exclusive licensees may act within their licensed scope unless agreed otherwise (Article 15).

Offshore US owners need a local agent. Regulations Article 14 mandates that an owner with no domicile or business office in Taiwan appoint an agent domiciled in Taiwan, attaching a power of attorney stating authority scope. Under Article 5, Customs may end protection early if it cannot reach the owner or agent, or if local agency ends without replacement.

Border alerts move in hours under [Trademark Act Article 75](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0070001&flno=75) and Regulations Article 7. From the moment it receives Customs' notice, the owner has four hours for air-freight exports, or 24 hours for air-freight imports and sea cargo, to confirm at the Customs office or a Customs-approved platform that it will examine the goods. Evidence of infringement is due within three working days of that notice. The owner may extend once by three working days if it explains a justifiable reason before the deadline. Platform photos aid review, yet cannot be the sole basis for an infringement finding.

If the owner submits evidence and the trader files no non-infringement proof within its three working days, Customs may suspend release (Article 75, paragraph 3) and must refer suspected Article 95 or 97 offenses to judicial authorities (Regulations Article 8). Where the trader submits proof, the owner has three working days from notice to seek Article 72 detention, or Customs may keep a sample and release the goods (Article 75, paragraphs 4 and 5). Targeted shipment reports are also available under Regulations Article 6.

After a hold, the owner has two tools. Under [Article 76](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0070001&flno=76), Customs may let the owner inspect detained goods on request and, once goods are detained or release is suspended, may provide the names and addresses of the importer or exporter and consignor or consignee, plus the quantity of suspect goods, strictly for investigation and litigation. Where lab testing is needed, [Article 77](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0070001&flno=77) and Regulations Article 12 let the owner apply to borrow a sample against a bond equal to 120% of the sample's Customs-assessed duty-paid value (or FOB value for exports) plus related taxes and fees, with a NT$3,000 minimum and a written undertaking.

## Direct detention, civil claims, and criminal penalties

What if a brand needs to freeze cargo directly? [Article 72](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0070001&flno=72) allows a written detention request backed by preliminary infringement facts and a bond matching Customs-assessed import duty-paid value or export FOB value; the detained party may release cargo with double that bond. Under [Article 73](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0070001&flno=73), Customs must revoke detention unless the applicant files an Article 69 lawsuit and notifies Customs within 12 days from the day after Customs gives notice that it accepted the application; Customs may extend that period by another 12 days where needed. When detention is revoked on any of the first four statutory grounds, the applicant bears demurrage, storage, and handling fees, and [Article 74](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0070001&flno=74) adds damages liability if a final judgment finds no infringement.

Amendments to Articles 68, 70, and 95 to 97 were promulgated on May 4, 2022, though as of November 24, 2026 the Executive Yuan had not set their effective date ([full Act with commencement notice](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawAll.aspx?pcode=J0070001)); the November 30, 2016 text still applies ([prior version](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawOldVer.aspx?pcode=J0070001)). Under that text, Article 68 treats unauthorized use, for marketing purposes, of an identical or confusingly similar mark on identical or similar goods or services as infringement. Article 70, item 3 deems it infringement to knowingly make, hold, display, sell, export, or import labels, hangtags, or packaging not yet attached to goods or services.

Civil remedies sit in [Article 69](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0070001&flno=69). It lets the owner demand that infringement stop or be prevented, and that infringing goods and the materials or tools used be destroyed. Damages require intent or negligence. That damages claim lapses two years after the owner learns of the damage and the liable party, or ten years after the infringing act.

Criminal penalties also follow the November 30, 2016 text. Article 95 sets imprisonment of up to three years, short-term detention, or a fine of up to NT$200,000 (or both) for unauthorized use for marketing purposes. Article 97 sets up to one year, short-term detention, or a fine of up to NT$50,000 (or both) for knowingly selling infringing goods made by another, or holding, displaying, exporting, or importing them with intent to sell, including online. The pending text would add a separate offense for counterfeit labels and packaging and drop the word "knowingly." A distributor exit can turn into a mark dispute; see [terminating a Taiwan distributor](/en/columns/terminating-taiwan-distributor-us-exporter).
