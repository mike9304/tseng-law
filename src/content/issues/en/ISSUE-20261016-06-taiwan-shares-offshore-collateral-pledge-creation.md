# Taiwan shares in offshore financing: how the pledge is recorded

Standard Chartered announced a Taiwan-securities collateral arrangement with J.P. Morgan on October 15, 2026, [CNA reported](https://www.cna.com.tw/news/afe/202610150121.aspx). The bank described a service through which international institutional investors could use Taiwan holdings to support offshore financing and derivatives while the securities remain within Taiwan's custody system. The announcement comes after [TWSE amendments effective March 30, 2026](https://twse-regulation.twse.com.tw/TW/law/DAT01.aspx?FLCODE=FL100919) added specific rules for pledged collateral.

The offshore agreement may identify the debt or derivatives obligations being secured, but a treasury team still needs evidence of the Taiwan pledge entry. A completed book-entry operation records delivery of securities as a pledge. Listing shares in a collateral schedule does not establish that this local operation has occurred.

## The eligible parties and shares are defined

Under [Article 5](https://twse-regulation.twse.com.tw/TW/law/DOC01.aspx?FLCODE=FL100919&FLNO=5), applied to pledges by [Article 18-1](https://twse-regulation.twse.com.tw/TW/law/DOC01.aspx?FLCODE=FL100919&FLNO=18-1) of the current TWSE operating rules, both the pledgor providing the shares and the pledgee receiving them must qualify as offshore foreign institutional investors and meet the specified Taiwan securities-investment registration or approval requirements. Where the pledgee lends foreign-currency funds, it must also have offshore regulatory authorization to operate as a bank or securities firm. Holding a Taiwan brokerage account does not, by itself, satisfy those conditions.

[Article 6](https://twse-regulation.twse.com.tw/TW/law/DOC01.aspx?FLCODE=FL100919&FLNO=6), applied to pledges by Article 18-4, limits eligible collateral to shares listed on the Taiwan Stock Exchange or Taipei Exchange that qualify for margin purchase and short sale. Securities obtained through securities borrowing cannot be used. Eligibility therefore depends on the actual holdings, rather than an assumption that an entire Taiwan portfolio is eligible.

Article 18-1 also requires the pledgee to appoint the prescribed local custodian. Under [Article 18-2](https://twse-regulation.twse.com.tw/TW/law/DOC01.aspx?FLCODE=FL100919&FLNO=18-2), that custodian establishes a dedicated pledge account at the Taiwan Depository & Clearing Corporation. The institution's eligibility and the account arrangements are separate parts of the transaction.

## What the book entry does

[Article 3 of the Regulations Governing Book-Entry Operations for Centrally Deposited Securities](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0400099&flno=3) permits delivery for a pledge through book entry. [Article 30-1](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0400099&flno=30-1) describes the mechanism: the relevant participant records the instruction and notifies the depository, which moves the securities from the pledgor's holdings into the pledge portion of the pledgee's participant account.

That recorded movement is more specific than a general instruction to keep shares with a custodian. For a financing team, confirmation needs to identify the securities, quantity and pledge entry that correspond to the proposed collateral. This is a practical consequence of the account mechanism; it is not a conclusion about either bank's undisclosed documentation or the enforceability of a particular offshore agreement.

## Release and default use their own procedures

When the pledge is extinguished, [Article 30-2](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0400099&flno=30-2) calls for the pledgee's participant to notify the depository so the securities can be moved back to the pledgor's relevant holdings. An email recording repayment and a completed transfer back are different records. The release arrangements need to account for who can give the instruction and how completion is evidenced.

For the collateral received under this framework, Article 18-1 limits the book-entry operations that the pledgee may request from its designated custodian. They concern pledge release or an auction conducted by the pledgee, including cancellation of that auction. When an offshore default leads to disposal, [TWSE Article 18-3](https://twse-regulation.twse.com.tw/TW/law/DOC01.aspx?FLCODE=FL100919&FLNO=18-3) requires reporting before disposal, subsequent book-entry processing and reporting of the sale. Proceeds first repay the agreed obligations, with any surplus returned to the pledgor's designated account.

An offshore default clause consequently needs to be read alongside the Taiwan custodian's permitted instructions and procedures. The documents may define when a contractual default occurs, but that definition does not itself complete a Taiwan disposal or release. Pricing, collateral valuation and the effect of foreign insolvency law require their own analysis; the announcement supplies no answer to those questions.
