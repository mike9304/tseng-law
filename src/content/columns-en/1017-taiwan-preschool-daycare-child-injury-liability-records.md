# Hurt at a Taiwan Daycare, Kindergarten or Nanny's Home: Footage, Insurance and Who Pays

Can you see the camera footage from the day your child came home hurt? It depends on where the child was. For infant daycare centres (托嬰中心), which take children under two, the regulations set a deadline. It is 14 days.

## Three kinds of care, three sets of rules

Infant daycare centres are child welfare institutions under [art. 75](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0050001&flno=75) of the Protection of Children and Youths Welfare and Rights Act (the Children's Rights Act). The health ministry's mandatory and prohibited terms for their standard-form contracts (托嬰中心定型化契約應記載及不得記載事項, from September 1, 2022; the mandatory terms) define them as serving children under two.

Kindergartens (幼兒園) fall under the Early Childhood Education and Care Act (the ECEC Act), which covers children from two until primary school ([art. 3](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=H0070031&flno=3)). Paid home-based nannies who are not relatives within the third degree must register locally ([Children's Rights Act art. 25](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0050001&flno=25)(2), [art. 26](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0050001&flno=26)(1)).

| | Infant daycare centre | Kindergarten | Registered nanny |
|---|---|---|---|
| CCTV | Required; parents may apply to view (art. 77-1) | No rule in the ECEC Act or its enforcement rules | No rule in the nanny regulations |
| Insurance | Group insurance, set locally (art. 77) | Group insurance (ECEC Act art. 34(1)); the competent authority insures the premises (art. 34(3)) | Liability insurance before care starts (nanny regulations art. 4(6)) |
| Records | Daily condition record, given to parents on request (mandatory terms, point 7) | Health file (ECEC Act art. 32(2)) | Daily life and growth record (nanny regulations art. 3(4)) |

## Daycare footage: kept 30 days, request within 14

[Art. 77-1](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0050001&flno=77-1) of the Children's Rights Act requires cameras, and the ministry's Regulations on CCTV at infant daycare centres fill in the detail. Cameras must cover entrances and play, sleep and meal areas ([art. 3](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0050208&flno=3)). Footage is kept at least 30 days ([art. 5](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0050208&flno=5)(3)).

Under [art. 8](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0050208&flno=8), in a care dispute, a written application stating specific reasons goes to the centre within 14 days of the incident or of learning about it. Footage deleted after the retention period is not provided. You see only the incident period, with a supervisor present. Filming the screen is prohibited.

Need a copy? Ask the city or county authority to help preserve it; the centre must cooperate (art. 8(3)). The authority, and courts and prosecutors in child protection cases, can also demand it ([art. 7](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0050208&flno=7)). Breaching the rules, obstructing inquiries or hiding evidence exposes the centre to action under art. 108 of the Children's Rights Act ([art. 9](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0050208&flno=9)).

A new statute is waiting. The Child Care Services Act (兒童托育服務法), promulgated May 8, 2026, will move daycare and nanny regulation to itself, ending the Children's Rights Act provisions on them ([art. 92](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0050217&flno=92)). Facilities must keep footage at least 30 days and send it to a local authority system ([art. 38](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0050217&flno=38)(1)–(2)). The Executive Yuan has yet to set its start date ([art. 94](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0050217&flno=94); national law database as of October 22, 2026). Until then, the 14-day and 30-day rules apply.

## Kindergarten footage and the personal data law

For kindergartens, the Personal Data Protection Act partly fills the gap. Identifiable footage is personal data ([art. 2](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=I0050021&flno=2)(1)), and public and private bodies must answer a data subject's request for access or a copy ([art. 10](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=I0050021&flno=10)). The subject is the child; the Act has no agency rule, so the parent asks as legal representative under Civil Code art. 1086(1). Requests harming a significant interest of the collector or a third party may be refused, as with other children on screen. Decisions are due within 15 days, extendable once by up to 15 ([art. 13](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=I0050021&flno=13)(1)). Where footage risks being lost, a court can order evidence preserved ([Code of Civil Procedure art. 368](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=368)(1)).

## What the centre must do on the day

Point 12 of the daycare mandatory terms requires immediate first aid and notice to the parents or their named emergency contact. Urgent cases go to a nearby hospital the parents chose, otherwise a suitable nearby one, then to one that hospital recommends if it cannot treat the child. Ambulance crews instead follow [art. 29 of the Emergency Medical Services Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=L0020045&flno=29), taking the child to a suitable nearby hospital as dispatch directs. A centre that breaches point 12 and causes the parents loss must compensate them.

These terms bind even when the contract leaves them out ([Consumer Protection Act art. 17](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0170001&flno=17)(5)). The prohibited terms bar clauses saying an emergency has nothing to do with the centre (point 5) or excluding liability for intent or negligence (point 6), and a clause breaching them is void (art. 17(4)). An April 2025 draft amendment would make parents bear necessary emergency treatment costs; it leaves the prohibited terms untouched.

Statutory duties apply to kindergartens. [ECEC Act art. 33](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=H0070031&flno=33)(1) requires emergency procedures, and [art. 30](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=H0070031&flno=30)(6) requires safety and emergency rules, actually carried out. Parents may require an explanation, refusable only for good reason ([art. 38](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=H0070031&flno=38)), and may complain to the local authority within 30 days of learning the kindergarten's response ([art. 40](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=H0070031&flno=40)(1)).

## Two years to claim group insurance

Kindergartens must carry group insurance under the Student and Preschool Child Group Insurance Act and actively help with claims ([ECEC Act art. 34](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=H0070031&flno=34)(1)–(2)); failing to help is a ground for a fine ([art. 59](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=H0070031&flno=59)(7)). It covers illness and accidents ([art. 13](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=H0070078&flno=13)(1)), with death, medical, disability, living-support and mass-poisoning benefits ([art. 14](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=H0070078&flno=14)). The child is the beneficiary, except that death benefits go to the heirs ([art. 4](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=H0070078&flno=4)(8)).

Claims lapse two years from the day they could be made ([art. 18](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=H0070078&flno=18)(1)). Daycare group insurance is set by each city or county ([Children's Rights Act art. 77](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0050001&flno=77)(2)). Registered nannies must hold liability insurance before taking a child ([nanny regulations art. 4](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0050195&flno=4)(6)).

## Claims under the Civil Code

In contract, the centre answers for staff fault as for its own ([Civil Code art. 224](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=224)), care falling short for reasons attributable to the centre is incomplete performance ([art. 227](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=227)), and injury to the child's personality rights brings in arts. 192 to 195 and 197 ([art. 227-1](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=227-1)).

In tort, the carer is liable under [art. 184](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=184), and the employer jointly under [art. 188](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=188) unless it shows proper care in hiring and supervising. If the victim then goes uncompensated, a court may, on application, order it to pay all or part, weighing both sides' finances (art. 188(2)).

Recoverable: actual loss and lost profits ([art. 216](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=216)), lost or reduced working capacity and increased needs ([art. 193](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=193)(1)), and non-pecuniary damages for bodily harm ([art. 195](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=195)(1)). Parents' own claim for harm to the parent-child relationship requires serious circumstances (art. 195(3)). Contributory fault can reduce or bar damages ([art. 217](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=217)).

If another child caused the injury, [art. 187](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=187) makes that child (if able to understand the act) and the legal representative jointly liable, or the representative alone; diligent supervision, or harm supervision could not prevent, is a defence. Tort claims lapse two years after learning of the damage and the liable person, and ten years after the act ([art. 197](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=197)(1)).

## Public kindergartens and state compensation

At a public kindergarten, the State Compensation Act may apply: [art. 2](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=I0020004&flno=2)(2) makes the state liable when a public official exercising public authority unlawfully injures someone by intent or negligence; the statute does not say whether a teacher's daily care counts. Defective playground equipment or facilities fall under [art. 3](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=I0020004&flno=3)(1).

A written claim to the liable agency comes first ([art. 9](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=I0020004&flno=9), [art. 10](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=I0020004&flno=10)(1)). Suit is possible if the agency refuses, does not start talks within 30 days, or talks fail after 60 days ([art. 11](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=I0020004&flno=11)(1)). Claims lapse two years after learning of the damage or five years after it occurred ([art. 8](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=I0020004&flno=8)(1)). A foreign child faces the reciprocity condition in [art. 15](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=I0020004&flno=15), discussed in [our article on a sidewalk injury claim](/en/columns/taiwan-pedestrian-public-facility-injury-state-compensation-abroad).

## When abuse is suspected

Anyone is barred from physically or mentally abusing a child ([Children's Rights Act art. 49](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0050001&flno=49)(1)), and ECEC Act art. 30(1) bans corporal punishment, bullying and improper discipline by kindergarten staff. Medical, social work, education and childcare professionals who learn of abuse at work must report it to the local authority immediately, within 24 hours at most; anyone may report, and reporters stay confidential ([art. 53](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0050001&flno=53)(1), (2), (5)). Kindergarten cases go to a committee within two working days, with the result sent in writing to the child's legal representative (ECEC Act art. 30(3)).
