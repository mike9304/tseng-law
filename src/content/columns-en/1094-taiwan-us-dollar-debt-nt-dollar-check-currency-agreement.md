After the supplier cashed four NT-dollar checks, [Shilin District Court](https://judgment.judicial.gov.tw/FJUD/printData.aspx?id=SLDV%2C112%2C%E9%87%8D%E8%A8%B4%2C374%2C20250619%2C1) still allowed a US$117,988 shortfall in a chip-supply dispute. The agreed payment currency was US dollars.

This analysis assumes a Taiwan-law payment obligation.

## A foreign-currency price and a promise to pay that currency

[Civil Code Article 202](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=202) generally permits a debtor to pay a foreign-currency amount in Taiwan currency, using the market value at the time and place of payment, except where the parties specifically agreed that payment must be made in the foreign currency.

A document that states a US-dollar price needs to be examined with the agreed method of paying it, including subsequent amendments. Article 202 itself does not designate one bank's historical buying or selling rate as the answer for every contract.

When considering an NT-dollar settlement, identify whether the proposed figure fixes the conversion rate, replaces the currency obligation, or leaves a remaining US-dollar balance to be paid.

## Replacing the checks did not prove an agreed currency change

In a [June 19, 2025 first-instance judgment of Shilin District Court, 112年度重訴字第374號](https://judgment.judicial.gov.tw/FJUD/printData.aspx?id=SLDV%2C112%2C%E9%87%8D%E8%A8%B4%2C374%2C20250619%2C1), two Taiwan companies disputed chip purchases and payment, including a US-dollar obligation for goods already collected.

The buyer provided four NT-dollar checks bearing a notation restricting them to security for the goods payment. The supplier requested replacements without that notation, while its email also recorded the expectation of a US-dollar remittance before maturity.

The buyer argued that replacing and cashing the checks showed agreement to switch to NT-dollar payment. The court rejected that argument. It read the replacement request as addressing the checks' validity, and found that the correspondence did not establish the claimed change of currency.

Separately, the same judgment awarded resale-loss damages. Finality and any appeal remain unverified.

Its reasoning does not establish that cashing a check can never accompany an agreed settlement, or that every supplier can recover whatever exchange-rate difference it calculates.

## Receiving a check and accepting substituted performance

[Civil Code Article 319](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=319) extinguishes a debt when the creditor accepts different performance in place of that originally agreed.

[Article 320](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=320) addresses a new obligation undertaken toward the creditor to discharge an existing debt, providing that, unless the parties expressed a different intention, non-performance of the new obligation leaves the old debt in place.

Delivery, clearing and acceptance as full settlement are events to identify separately in the file. A receipt or release describing the balance as fully settled needs to be read alongside the check and the communications that led to it.

## Currency terms, credited payments and the remaining claim

For reconciliation, compare the amount contractually due with the payment credited against it. The working list can identify each invoice, its currency, the agreed payment terms, the check's face amount and currency, and the date and amount of the credit.

Both check versions show which wording changed. The accompanying email thread can show whether the request also addressed the currency and amount to be paid.

If the parties fixed a conversion method, identify the relevant clause and any later agreement changing it. If reliance is placed on Article 202's general rule, the time and place of payment remain part of the statutory test. An invoice-date rate or an internal accounting rate cannot simply be substituted without explaining its basis.
