# The Elvis lawsuit and the training copies made in Taiwan

Authentic Brands Group sued Anthropic on Friday, October 23, [BBC News reported](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/c6rmyl0kxyxjo) and [CNA reported on October 26, 2026](https://www.cna.com.tw/news/ait/202610260141.aspx), accusing the company of using 274 Elvis songs to train its model without paying for a licence. CNA gave the same count and described the group as holding both the music catalog and the likeness rights.

For a developer in Taipei fine-tuning a model on song lyrics or recordings, the US case settles nothing yet. Are those training copies reproduction under the Copyright Act, and can fair use cover them?

## What the complaint asks for

BBC News said the songs named include All Shook Up, Can't Help Falling in Love, A Little Less Conversation, Don't Be Cruel and Return To Sender. The complaint, as BBC reported it, says those songs were used to create unlicensed derivative works.

Payments run up to $150,000 per infringed song, BBC News said, adding up to $41 million. CNA gave its own approximate conversion: about NT$4.77 million a song, and about NT$1,305,850,000 for the total it reported as about $41 million.

Anthropic's spokesperson told BBC News: "We disagree with the claims and intend to defend this case fully."

BBC News also quoted the complaint as saying the company "has built its entire business model based on copyright infringement at a scale never seen before."

The same story quoted the complaint as saying that guardrails the company "supposedly put up" after being sued did not stop it, and that when asked to recreate specific songs "for a modern audience," the model described its own output as landing "very close to the originals."

Reservoir accused the company of taking lyrics the previous week. Universal Music Group sued in January. BBC News said both remain ongoing. Last year, BBC News reported, Anthropic agreed to pay $1.5 billion to settle a class action by authors. Lawyers for the authors called that the largest publicly reported copyright recovery.

## A file on a Taipei server

Authors hold the exclusive right to reproduce the work, unless the act says otherwise. [Article 22 of the Copyright Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0070017&flno=22) is that grant. A performer has a separate exclusive right to reproduce the performance by sound recording, video recording or photography.

Reproduction, in [Article 3](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0070017&flno=3), includes direct or indirect, permanent or temporary, repeated making by printing, copying, sound recording, video recording, photography, transcription or another method. Recording a musical work when it is performed or broadcast counts as well.

Paragraph 3 of Article 22 withholds that exclusive right only in a narrow case. The copy has to be temporary. It has to be solely for lawful intermediary transmission on a network, or for lawful use of the work. The reproduction has to be a necessary, transitional and incidental part of a technical process, and it has to lack independent economic significance. Computer program works are left outside that withholding. A lyrics file that is stored and used again has to be set against those conditions. The paragraph does not, by itself, bless or condemn every training copy.

## When the rightsholder is not in Taiwan

Foreign works enter the act through [Article 4](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0070017&flno=4). A treaty or agreement the Legislative Yuan has approved prevails where it says otherwise. Otherwise a foreigner's work may be protected in one of the listed cases. One is first publication inside the Republic of China, or publication here within 30 days after a first publication abroad, and only where that foreigner's own country protects works of Republic of China nationals in the same situation, after that protection has been verified. That is the first listed case. Another is where, under a treaty, an agreement, or that country's laws or practice, works of Republic of China nationals enjoy copyright there.

## Two office letters about pictures

On August 29, 2025, the Intellectual Property Office answered an email about training on historical photographs. The [reply](https://www.tipo.gov.tw/tw/copyright/692-57511.html) says that using a work still inside its term to train a model differs from adaptation, even when the training result is mathematical parameters and weights. Adaptation, which [Article 3](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0070017&flno=3) defines as creating anew from the original by translation, arrangement, rewriting, filming or another method, is a different act. The training process is the part the letter flags. Collection, preprocessing, model training and optimization may each involve reproduction of the source. Absent fair use under Articles 44 to 65, the letter says a license is still required, and civil and criminal liability may follow.

Output is a separate step in that letter. If there is no new creation based on the original, the office said that output is not adaptation. On the facts the writer of the email had described, no substantial similarity, or similarity only in an unprotected fact such as how a building looked, the output would not infringe the training image. Those were the facts in the email.

Where a user treats the system only as a tool and adds new creativity to an existing work, to the point of creating something new from it, the letter says that can still be adaptation and needs the owner's consent or license. Copyright is a private right, the letter adds. Whether a given case is adaptation or infringement is for a court, on the evidence.

An earlier reply, [dated December 12, 2022](https://www.tipo.gov.tw/tw/copyright/692-16842.html), answered a question about artworks run through a generative picture tool. Learning from those works may be reproduction. Absent a fair-use case under Articles 44 to 65, the letter says a license is required. The office said that use was not a situation covered by Articles 44 to 63. It then pointed at Article 65, paragraph 2. Purpose and character of the use, the nature of the work, the amount and the share of the whole, and the effect on the potential market and present value, are weighed together. There is no fixed standard. A court decides both infringement and fair use.

Protection stops at expression. [Article 10-1](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0070017&flno=10-1) reaches the expression of the work. It does not reach the idea, procedure, process, system, method of operation, concept, principle or discovery that the expression carries. The December reply used that line for the picture that comes out, and gave style as an example of what falls outside. An output that differs from the original and only shows style was, in that letter, not an infringement. A line of lyric that stays close to the song is not the example the letter gave.

Both letters are about pictures. Neither mentions this complaint, Elvis, or a music catalog. They do state how this office reads reproduction, adaptation and fair use when someone trains on works that are still protected.

## Four factors, not a switch

Fair use of a work is not an infringement of economic rights. [Article 65](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0070017&flno=65) opens with that short rule.

Paragraph 2 then tells a court how to decide whether a use fits the fair range in Articles 44 to 63, or some other fair use. All the circumstances count. Particular attention goes to four matters: the purpose and character of the use, including whether it is commercial or for nonprofit teaching; the nature of the work; the amount used and its proportion of the whole work; and the effect of the use on the work's potential market and present value. An agreement between a rightsholder group and a user group on the range of fair use may be taken as a reference. While that agreement is being reached, the copyright authority may be consulted.

A commercial training set built from whole lyrics, and an existing market for licensing those songs, are the kind of facts a court weighs under the first, third and fourth factors. The article does not rank the factors, and the December 2022 letter said there is no fixed standard.
