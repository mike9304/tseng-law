# Visitor Activities That Can Affect Your Next Entry to Taiwan

For an American who has already left Taiwan after an immigration decision, arranging the next visit starts with the restriction recorded against that person. An invitation for a new trip does not reveal the ground or duration of an existing entry ban. The documents from the earlier departure do. They also show which decision may still be open to challenge.

## Reading the restriction before planning a return

The NIA's [Operation Directions for the Entry Bans on Foreign Nationals](https://www.immigration.gov.tw/5475/5478/141478/141482/148796/cp) distinguish the reasons for a ban. Paragraph II(h) generally prescribes three to five years when activity inconsistent with the authorized purpose results in deportation or an order to leave. Illegal work has a three-year period under paragraph IV(b). Both run from the day after departure.

The ground matters. [Immigration Act Article 18](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0080132&flno=18) includes prior deportation or an order to leave and illegal work among the grounds for refusing entry. For illegal work or overstaying, it sets a statutory range of one to seven years, also measured from the day after departure. The directions supply the more specific ordinary periods described above; the individual decision still needs to be read.

One activity can fit more than one provision. Paragraph VII of the directions uses the heavier restriction for the same act, rather than adding those periods together. Separate acts need their own calculation. Where family ties are relevant, paragraph X provides conditional routes to exemption or reduction. Marriage alone does not establish entitlement: the applicable route may depend on registration, duration, children and the family member's status.

A recent announcement cannot supply a missing end date. In its [October 22, 2026 announcement](https://www.immigration.gov.tw/5385/7229/7238/420118/), the NIA said that two people admitted for tourism were removed that evening and restricted from returning for a period it did not specify. Their legal status categories differed. This column concerns foreign nationals under the Immigration Act; it does not assign either person's undisclosed ban period to another visitor.

## Challenging a decision while living abroad

Travel plans may be months away. An appeal deadline can be much closer. Under [Administrative Appeal Act Article 14](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=A0030020&flno=14), the ordinary period for an administrative appeal, 訴願, is 30 days from the day after the disposition reaches the recipient. Filing is measured by receipt at the issuing agency or the competent appeal agency. A mailing date in the United States does not establish timely receipt in Taiwan.

Counsel therefore needs the decision and service information at the outset, even if the immediate request is simply to ask when another visit will be possible. Where an administrative appeal decision has already been served, the next deadline may instead be the ordinary two-month period for a revocation action under [Administrative Litigation Act Article 106](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=A0030154&flno=106), subject to special provisions.

Filing alone changes no enforcement date. An administrative stay under [Article 93](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=A0030020&flno=93) requires the statutory grounds: manifest doubt about legality, or difficult-to-reverse harm and urgency, with the applicable public-interest conditions. Judicial relief under [Article 116](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=A0030154&flno=116) likewise requires difficult-to-reverse harm and urgency. Public-interest limits apply; during litigation, a claim manifestly without legal merit also prevents a stay. The application and the decision granting a stay are separate steps.

## Explaining the earlier visit

What work was the visitor actually asked to do? For someone returning to the United States, a Taiwan invitation, contract and event schedule may be held by different organizations. Comparing them with payment records can reveal the activity that needs a legal explanation: a meeting, a demonstration or commercial promotion. The label on the invitation is only part of that evidence.

[Immigration Act Article 29](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0080132&flno=29) prohibits activity inconsistent with the permitted purpose of a visit or residence and prohibits anyone from causing a foreign national to engage in it. The provision excepts petitions and lawful assemblies or demonstrations by lawful residents. It does not declare every promotional appearance unlawful. The actual arrangement and the permission held must support the assessment.

If the disputed activity was work, [Employment Service Act Article 43](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0090001&flno=43) generally requires permission obtained through an employer's application, unless the Act provides otherwise. Visa-free admission supplies no work authorization of its own. For someone already employed in Taiwan, [changing employers](/en/columns/taiwan-foreign-worker-change-employer-without-consent-separation-document) raises an additional permission question.

## If departure has not yet taken place

There may still be a chance to present that explanation before removal. [Article 36](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0080132&flno=36) requires an opportunity to state a position before forced removal. A lawyer and interpreter may attend, subject to the restrictions concerning national security or inappropriate conduct affecting order or the proceeding. Residence and permanent-residence permit holders generally have an additional review-committee procedure, with statutory exceptions. Tourist status alone does not provide it.

Timing can be immediate. For an Article 29 violation, Article 36(2)(4) permits deportation or an order to leave within ten days; failure to obey the latter can lead to deportation. It does not guarantee every visitor ten days before removal. Presenting evidence may contest the characterization of the visit, but it does not promise permission to remain.
