# Finding a Late Parent's Taiwan Accounts From Abroad: The Tax Office's Single-Window Search

Taiwan's tax authorities will search banks, insurers, brokers and card issuers for a deceased Taiwanese national's accounts, as of the day of death, if an heir asks. For a child living in Seattle or Manchester who has a key ring, two passbooks and no idea what else is out there, that one application can save months of letters to individual banks.

## What one request covers

The rules sit in a Ministry of Finance directive on single-window inquiries into a decedent's financial estate, amended on December 26, 2023 and in force from January 1, 2024. It lists twelve categories: deposits; investment funds; listed, OTC and emerging-board securities; short-term bills; life insurance; futures; safe deposit boxes; bank loans and credit card debt; electronic payment accounts; registered stored-value cards; investment accounts; and credit cooperative member shares. Each is reported as of the date of death.

Debts show up too. That matters because an heir who wants to renounce the inheritance has three months from learning that they are an heir, and must file in writing with a court ([Civil Code Article 1174](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=1174)). The steps for doing that from overseas are in [our column on renouncing a Taiwanese parent's debts from Japan](/en/columns/taiwan-parent-debt-renunciation-heir-in-japan).

## Applying from overseas

Heirs who have not renounced, executors and estate administrators may apply. The person who died must be a Taiwan national with a national ID number.

Online applications need a Taiwanese digital certificate, a registered National Health Insurance card, mobile phone authentication or another credential the Ministry of Finance accepts. Without one, the counter is the practical route. Without a Taiwan ID card, the applicant shows an original passport. Documents issued abroad must be authenticated by a Taiwan overseas mission, such as a Taipei Economic and Cultural Office, and an authorization signed abroad for an agent in Taiwan needs the same treatment. Add a death certificate or the deregistered household record, and proof of the relationship; if the death has already been registered with a Taiwan household registration office, the death certificate can be skipped.

## How the answer arrives

Every Wednesday, the Ministry's Fiscal Information Agency collects the week's applications and passes them to the financial industry bodies and institutions, which reply within ten working days. The results come by registered mail from the tax office for the decedent's household registration, or can be downloaded from the Ministry's e-filing site between 30 and 90 days after the application. Through the estate tax filing software, they are available from 30 days after the application until nine months after the death.

It is not the whole estate. The directive calls the list a reference for the estate tax return, says other property must still be reported, and warns that under-reporting can be penalized. It does save paperwork: items the taxpayer confirms as matching the position on the date of death need no separate proof.

What about the parent's account back home? A Taiwan national who habitually lived in Taiwan is taxed on property inside and outside Taiwan ([Estate and Gift Tax Act Article 1](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0340072&flno=1)). A U.S. or UK account in the parent's name will not appear in this search, but it may still belong on the return.

## Six months, then the bank

The estate tax return is due within six months starting from the day after death, at the tax office for the decedent's household registration ([Article 23](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0340072&flno=23)). For a Taiwan national who habitually lived abroad, it goes to the tax office where the central government sits. The taxpayers are the heirs and legatees, including those living overseas, and their liability is limited to the estate ([Article 6(1)](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0340072&flno=6)). With good reason, a written request made before the deadline can extend it, normally by up to three months; where force majeure or other special circumstances apply, the tax office may set the period case by case ([Article 26](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0340072&flno=26)).

Then comes the bank. Until the estate tax is paid, the estate may not be divided or transferred (Article 8), and enterprises handling a transfer must see one of the certificates listed in Articles 41 and 42 before they act. A passbook in hand does not unlock the account.

Six months is not long from abroad. Counting the time an overseas mission needs to authenticate documents, the search request belongs near the top of the list once the funeral is over.
