# Hiring a household helper or caregiver in Taiwan: eligibility and employer duties

Since April 13, 2026, a family with a child under 12 has been able to apply for a foreign household helper. That change did not relax household-caregiver eligibility.

Both occupations appear in Article 46(1)(9) of the [Employment Service Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0090001&flno=46). Their duties differ under Article 4 of the [employment qualification and review standards](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0090029&flno=4). A helper cleans the home, cooks, attends to family members' daily household needs and performs related domestic work. A caregiver handles the care recipient's daily care.

The [Executive Yuan's description of the April measure](https://www.post.gov.tw/post/FileCenter/post_ww2/ad/ad_linkpage/1150409.htm) offers another form of household support to parents. It supplies auxiliary domestic labor, not a caregiver permit or a professional care worker.

## Household-helper eligibility has several branches

For a Taiwanese household, Article 12 of the [standards](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0090029&flno=12) requires a child under 12 or a specified relative aged 75 or older, together with at least four points under Table 1. Eligible older relatives are lineal ascendants or stepparents, and a spouse's parents or stepparents. A guardian may act as employer where the child meets the Civil Code guardianship provision cited in that article.

Household registration matters. Points or people cannot be counted when the family member is registered in a different household from the employer, has already supported an application for household care or technical household care, or has already been counted for a household-helper application.

Foreign employers follow Article 13 of the [same standards](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0090029&flno=13). Its routes combine company foreign investment or revenue with the applicant's corporate rank, cover specified salary-income or managerial tracks, and recognize official startup and venture-capital achievements. A separate provision covers certain higher-income foreign employees who employed the same helper abroad before entering Taiwan. Being a foreign manager alone is insufficient.

One household may employ only one helper under [Article 14](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0090029&flno=14). Pending initial recruitment, available or issued recruitment permits, current workers and other circumstances listed in the article all enter the count.

## Caregiver applications do not begin and end with the Barthel Index

Multiple care-recipient routes appear in Article 18 of the [standards](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0090029&flno=18). These cover designated disabilities, a qualifying team assessment by a medical institution, at least six consecutive months of government-subsidized use of specified long-term-care services, and a physician's diagnosis of an officially announced disease or condition.

Assessment levels vary by age. A person under 80 must need full-day care. Severe dependency is the test at 80 or older, while mild dependency suffices at 85 or older. The Barthel Index is one assessment tool; it does not cover the entire eligibility system.

Two further paths deserve attention. An employer may apply where a foreign household caregiver or technical household caregiver cared for the recipient within the previous year. Other recipients aged 80 or older may use their identity documents for an application. A person already counted for household-helper points under Article 12 cannot use this identity-document route under Article 18(3). That bar does not extend to the disability, team-assessment, subsidized-care, diagnosis or prior-caregiver routes.

A separate waiver in Article 46(4) of the [Employment Service Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0090001&flno=46) covers recipients aged 80 or older and those aged 70 to 79 with stage II or more advanced cancer. The waiver leaves kinship, residence and other permit requirements intact.

## Kinship, residence and the number of caregivers are separate tests

After the care-recipient assessment, Article 21 of the [standards](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0090029&flno=21) addresses the employer. Spouses, lineal relatives, collateral relatives within the third degree, and specified step and in-law relationships qualify. Where the employer or care recipient is a foreign national, that person must have permission to reside in Taiwan.

The care recipient or a nonrelative may act as employer only when the recipient has no relatives in Taiwan or the Ministry of Labor approves a special case. A care recipient acting as employer must designate a person with legal capacity to perform employer duties if unable to do so.

[Article 22](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0090029&flno=22) counts household-caregiver and technical household-caregiver permits together, with one person per care recipient as the default. One additional caregiver is allowed when a disability certificate records a vegetative state, or when a medical diagnosis gives a Barthel Index score of zero and no improvement is possible within six months. This narrow increase does not alter the one-helper-per-household rule.

## Employer duties begin before recruitment

First-time Taiwanese employers must attend pre-employment instruction before hiring a household helper or caregiver and attach proof to the permit application. Article 48-1 of the [Employment Service Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0090001&flno=48-1) imposes that requirement. Using an agent does not transfer the employer's legal role to the agent.

[Article 57](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0090001&flno=57) prohibits hiring without permission and assigning work outside the permit. A household need for care does not turn a helper permit into a caregiver permit.

Article 33 of the [Regulations on the Permission and Administration of the Employment of Foreign Workers](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0090027&flno=33) requires the employer to implement the worker's living-care plan. Safe and hygienic food and accommodation, personal safety and health, the accommodation location and living-care personnel are included. Household employers are exempt from planning information about recreational facilities and religious activities and from planning life-counselling services, but the other items remain. Changes to the accommodation or care personnel carry a seven-day notice requirement.

Passports and Alien Resident Certificates stay with the worker. Article 57(8) prohibits unlawfully retaining or taking possession of those documents or the worker's property. Calling the arrangement safekeeping does not cure unlawful retention.

Notice at the end of employment has its own deadline. Under [Article 56](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0090001&flno=56), the employer must give written notice within three days to the local authority, immigration authority and police when employment ends or a worker has been absent and out of contact for three consecutive days. The shared three-day period does not make every other administrative deadline three days.

## Contracts, wage statements and leave need different documents

A fixed-term written employment contract must be in Chinese and accompanied by a translation in the worker's native language. Article 65 of the [permission regulations](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0090027&flno=65) says so directly. A Chinese-only document or an oral explanation does not meet that form requirement.

Wage statements must also be bilingual under [Article 66](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0090027&flno=66). They show take-home pay, calculation items, gross wages, payment method and lawful deductions. The worker keeps the statement, and the employer retains it for five years. Wages are generally paid in full and directly in cash; another method requires supporting documents for both sides.

Domestic service is excluded from the Labor Standards Act. The Workforce Development Agency's [direct-hiring guidance](https://dhsc.wda.gov.tw/PreHireNotice?MenuId=176397ea-c6c8-49e2-83b2-107d1d7d3b80&ChildMenuId=2f140b2b-eb02-424b-9d9a-ca126c560972) therefore bases household-helper and caregiver leave on the employment contract. General statutory working time, rest-day and annual-leave rules for other employees should not be imported.

The contract still needs actual rest terms. In a [June 10, 2018 statement](https://www.mol.gov.tw/announcement/27179/36979/), the Ministry of Labor explained that source-country-verified contracts must specify sufficient rest and one day off every seven days, and called on employers to perform those obligations. That guidance does not convert household work into the Act's general working-time regime.

## The dependant rule for Category 2 workers has a narrow exception

Household helpers and caregivers are Category 2 foreign nationals. Article 67 of the [permission regulations](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0090027&flno=67) generally bars them from bringing dependants to reside in Taiwan. The exception applies when a worker gives birth in Taiwan during employment and can support the child.

Initial residence for a professional's spouse, minor child or adult child unable to live independently because of a disability falls under Article 16(2) of the [Act for the Recruitment and Employment of Foreign Professionals](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=A0030295&flno=16). [Article 17](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=A0030295&flno=17) separately covers extensions for professionals and dependants already holding residence permission, capped at one year in total. These professional-family provisions do not displace Article 67 for Category 2 migrant workers.

A document file should follow the selected route: occupation first, then family-member or care-recipient eligibility, employer kinship and residence, and finally instruction, the living-care plan and bilingual contract. A young child answers only one part of the new household-helper test.
