## Verdict
naturalness (1 = machine, 5 = native professional): 3/5 for guidance pack, 2/5 for columns
variety used: Bokmål intended; guidance + 001–003 are stiff, comma-heavy Bokmål (German *Sie* → capital **De**). Columns 004–007 mix Danish into the Norwegian surface (`Hvornår`, `at` + infinitive, relative `der`, `retsstilling`, `Ud over`, `sygdom`, `stk.`, `går ind`). 008–009 keep a Korean-blog first person. Not a Bokmål/Nynorsk mix.
systemic patterns (max 6, each one line, with 1 example quote):
- German pivot in the pack: "arbeidsgrupper", "farbar kommunikasjonsvei", "institutter i andre stater", "Vi følger klienter".
- Capital **De / Deres / Dem** throughout (German *Sie*); 2026 Norwegian sites use **du**.
- Danish leakage in columns 004–007: "Hvornår kan forberedelseskontoen", "ikke at vedlegges", "Historie og retsstilling".
- Korean-reader leftovers: Taiwan–Korea 10 % as the working rate, "hovedbank i Korea", "I motsetning til Korea".
- English/Hangul left on the Norwegian surface: `OUR TEAM`, `OFFICES`, `(English)`, Hangul slugs in 005/008/009.
- Legal-term calques: 拘役 → "hefte" (same word as limited liability), 大法官 → "de store dommere", 贍養費 → "nødunderhold".
Gender: Wei Tseng is correctly **hun** / first-person **jeg**. No masculine form for her. "han" in 008 is employee B, not the attorney. Consultation-language lock and the negative no-Norwegian / no-interpreter / no-free-first-meeting lines are intact (wording only), except the inquiry notice, which drops "bare". Internal `/nb/…` CTAs in 001–004, 008–009 are correctly localized; 005–009 still carry Hangul or Korea-default copy. 001–002 are legally careful despite the style; 006 is the least Norwegian.
