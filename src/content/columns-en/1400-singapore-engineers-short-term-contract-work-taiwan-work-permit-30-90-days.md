# Sending engineers from Singapore to install equipment in Taiwan: do they need a work permit?

For a two-week installation, an entry visa or entry permit (入國簽證或入國許可) is treated as a work permit (工作許可) if the assignment meets Taiwan's contract-work conditions and the stay is no more than 30 days. [Article 5 of the foreign-worker permit regulations](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0090027&flno=5) (雇主聘僱外國人許可及管理辦法) links this route to a specific statutory category of contract work; a two-week booking alone does not establish it.

As of November 23, 2026, the Bureau of Consular Affairs' [table updated October 7, 2026](https://www.boca.gov.tw/dl-4750-9804fc4d943a4d75ae534cbe285210fc.html) lists 30-day visa-free treatment for Singapore nationals entering Taiwan. An engineer employed in Singapore may hold another nationality. Check each passport and the actual admission conditions before matching the trip to the work-permit rule.

## Which contract assignments qualify?

Taiwan's starting point is [Employment Service Act Article 43](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0090001&flno=43) (就業服務法): unless the Act provides otherwise, a foreigner must not work in Taiwan without permission obtained through an employer. Payment from Singapore does not establish an exception to that wording.

A Singapore company with no Taiwan branch or representative office (分公司或代表人辦事處) can use [Article 51, paragraph 3](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0090001&flno=51) for the contract-performance route (履約). The Singapore supplier must need to send the engineers to Taiwan to fulfill its agreement with the customer for contracted work, a sale, technical cooperation or a similar transaction. The tasks must fall within that contract and [Article 46, paragraph 1, item 1 or 2](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0090001&flno=46): specialized or technical work (專門性或技術性工作), or a manager position in a business whose investment or establishment by overseas Chinese or foreigners has government approval.

Equipment projects need a closer match than the title “engineer.” [Article 4 of the qualification standards](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0090031&flno=4) (外國人從事就業服務法第四十六條第一項第一款至第六款工作資格及審查標準) requires work involving specialized knowledge, expertise or technical skill, and lists manufacturing among the fields covered. [Article 34](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0090031&flno=34) includes machine installation, testing, training, maintenance and technical guidance within manufacturing work.

Would calling the visit a customer meeting settle the issue? These provisions do not define a general boundary between meetings and work. Compare the agreed tasks with the statutory categories: in a fictional installation project, the delivery agreement might require the supplier's technicians to install a machine and train its operators. Those obligations give HR concrete activities to assess.

## Up to 30 days: the entry visa or entry permit is treated as a work permit

On an Article 51 contract assignment, Article 5 of the permit regulations treats the entry visa or entry permit as a work permit where the stay is no more than 30 days. Count this entry's whole stay toward 30 days. Counting only the days spent at the factory would miss that condition.

The statutory words are “entry visa or entry permit.” Before relying on them, confirm that each engineer's actual admission qualifies and that the assignment meets Article 51. Visa-free eligibility alone does not answer both questions. Compare each engineer's entry document and travel dates with the tasks in the supply contract.

A separate paragraph of Article 5 treats an entry visa or entry permit as a work permit for a stay of up to 90 days for holders of an immigration-issued academic and business travel card (學術及商務旅行卡) who give lectures or technical guidance under Employment Service Act Article 46, paragraph 1, item 1. A project involving technical guidance does not acquire that longer period merely because an engineer is traveling for business.

## Who files for an installation stay of 31 to 90 days?

When a qualifying assignment involves a stay of 31 to 90 days, [Article 12 of the permit regulations](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0090027&flno=12) allows an application under Article 9 within 30 days after entry. That provision specifies a filing window; it does not determine whether the engineers may start or continue work while approval is pending. Nor does it extend an immigration stay.

Responsibility sits with the Taiwan contracting business or an authorized agent (訂約之事業機構或授權之代理人), as Article 51, paragraph 3 requires. In the customer-installation arrangement, that means coordinating the filing with the Taiwan customer or the authorized agent.

Coordinate documents with the customer for the Taiwan applicant's filing under [Article 9, paragraph 2](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0090027&flno=9): a copy of the contract and registration documents for the Singapore company. Also provide a letter from the foreign company confirming it has assigned the engineers to perform the contract, the assigned engineers' roster, copies of their passports or Alien Resident Certificates (外僑居留證), and copies of their graduation certificates (畢業證書). No graduation-certificate copy is required when the engineer's contract work in the year before the application plus the work period now requested totals 90 days or less. Under paragraph 3, the authority may require documents made abroad to be authenticated by a Taiwan overseas mission. Authentication is not automatic in every case.

## Earlier assignments count toward the qualification threshold

[Article 7 of the qualification standards](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0090031&flno=7) removes Article 5's qualification restrictions for qualifying contract work when both the current work period and the cumulative total with contract work in the year before the application are no more than 90 days. It covers employees assigned by their foreign corporate employer to perform contract work within Article 4's fields in Taiwan. For the 90-day threshold, add the current contract-work period to all contract work in Taiwan in the year before the application. It is not a general permission to work without an application.

When the current assignment is no more than 90 days, add the contract-work periods in the year before the application to the period now requested. If that total exceeds 90 days, the engineer must meet one of [Article 5's items 1, 2 or 4](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0090031&flno=5): a certificate or qualification to practice obtained under Taiwan's law governing examinations for professional and technical personnel (專門職業及技術人員考試法); a master's degree or higher in a relevant field from a university in Taiwan or abroad, or a bachelor's degree in a relevant field plus at least two years of related work experience; or professional training or self-study with at least five years' related experience, innovation and exceptional performance.

Once the current work period exceeds 90 days, Article 7's relaxation does not apply and the ordinary Article 5 rule requires one of its four qualifications, including its item 3 route for an employee assigned to Taiwan after at least one year of service with a multinational enterprise. Qualification relief concerns Article 5; it does not remove the need to meet the other applicable standards. Record previous assignments for each engineer before choosing the requested period or relying on the graduation-certificate exception.

Engineers employed to perform specialized or technical work under Employment Service Act Article 46(1), item 1 are exempt from fixed work-experience requirements under [Article 6 of the Act for the Recruitment and Employment of Foreign Professionals](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=A0030295&flno=6) (外國專業人才延攬及僱用法) if they hold a master's or higher degree from a Taiwan or overseas university, or a bachelor's or higher degree from a university on the Ministry of Education's (教育部) published global top-1,500 list, subject to applicable practice qualifications, practice methods and conditions, and sector-specific legal requirements.

## Unauthorized work can affect the engineer and the customer

As of November 23, 2026, [Employment Service Act Article 68](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0090001&flno=68) sets an administrative fine (罰鍰) of NT$30,000 to NT$150,000 for violating Article 43. The authorities must immediately order a foreigner who violates Article 43 to leave, and that person must not work in Taiwan again under this provision.

[Article 44](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0090001&flno=44) prohibits anyone, including the customer, from unlawfully allowing a foreigner to work. If the customer is the engineer's employer, [Article 57, item 1](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0090001&flno=57) prohibits it from employing a foreigner without permission, with permission no longer valid, or whom another employer has applied to employ.

For either violation, [Article 63](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0090001&flno=63) sets fines of NT$150,000 to NT$750,000. A repeat violation within five years is punishable by up to three years in prison, short-term detention, or a criminal fine of up to NT$1,200,000, which may also be imposed alongside imprisonment or detention. Where a legal entity's representative, or a legal entity's or individual's agent, employee or other worker, commits the violation in the course of business, that legal entity or individual is also fined the applicable administrative or criminal fine, alongside punishment of the person responsible.

Installation work inconsistent with the permitted purpose of stay or residence violates [Immigration Act Article 29](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0080132&flno=29) (入出國及移民法); under [Article 36, paragraph 2, item 4](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0080132&flno=36), the National Immigration Agency (移民署) may deport the engineer or order departure within ten days. If the engineer misses that departure deadline, the agency may deport them.
