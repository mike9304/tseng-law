# Taiwan tells Grab no: a 13.1% stake and a 3.9% vote

Taiwan's Fair Trade Commission prohibited Grab's acquisition of foodpanda's Taiwan business on October 21, 2026. At the 1,825th commissioners' meeting, a majority voted to prohibit the deal, one commissioner favored letting it proceed with undertakings, and one commissioner recused.

Grab does not run a delivery service in Taiwan. [The commission's news release](https://www.ftc.gov.tw/internet/main/doc/docDetail.aspx?docid=18509&uid=126) says Grab agreed in March 2026 with Delivery Hero to acquire all the shares of foodpanda's Taiwan company and to control that company's operations and personnel decisions. The filing was formally accepted on June 16. The review period had been extended through October 27. The prohibition came six days before that date.

[Focus Taiwan's October 21 report](https://focustaiwan.tw/business/202610210018) describes the transaction as a proposed US$600 million acquisition. The commission's release does not state that price. Foodpanda and Uber Eats both said they had no comment when contacted by CNA, the news agency that publishes Focus Taiwan.

## Why a minority stake changed the competitive story

foodpanda and Uber Eats currently lead Taiwan's food-delivery market, which the commission called a duopoly. Grab and foodpanda do not compete with each other inside Taiwan today. After a completed deal, the commission said, Grab would step into foodpanda's place and become Uber Eats' main rival.

Uber, Uber Eats' parent, holds about 13.1% of Grab's shares. The commission said Grab uses a dual-class structure, so Uber's voting rights are only 3.9% and Uber cannot directly set Grab's competitive decisions. It also said Uber's chief executive had for a long period served as a Grab director, and that the companies have had close contact and commercial cooperation in other markets.

That combination, in the commission's words, would reduce Grab's incentive and ability to compete with Uber Eats in Taiwan. If Uber Eats cut prices or added promotions to win business from Grab, Uber Eats might gain revenue, but Grab's profit could fall, and Uber's return on the Grab holding could fall with it. Because the two platforms would be each other's closest substitutes in Taiwan, the commission said the equity link further weakens Uber Eats' reason to compete hard.

On Grab's side, the commission said the holding still changes how the two firms deal with each other and aligns their interests, even without voting control. Familiarity from other markets makes the other's next move easier to predict. The release also points to 2018, when Grab took on Uber's Southeast Asian business. The commission said the two went ahead in a form that was hard to unwind even though they knew, or at least should have known, that the transaction violated competition law. That is the commission's account of the history, given as a reason the coordination risk is higher now.

Entry, the release says, is expensive. A new platform needs capital and enough restaurants, couriers, and customers at the same time. Restaurants and customers, in the commission's view, do not have enough leverage to break up coordination between large platforms.

## The one-third line was not the finding

Before the decision, [our October 14 issue column](/en/columns/issues/grab-foodpanda-taiwan-merger-review-singapore-platforms) walked through Article 10's five routes and the open closing date. [Fair Trade Act Article 10](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0150002&flno=10) treats a holding or acquisition that reaches one third or more of another enterprise's voting shares or total capital as one type of merger. Shares held by enterprises in a control relationship with the acquirer, and by enterprises under the same control, are added in. Neither the share percentage nor the voting percentage reaches one third. The commission did not say that Uber's percentage, standing alone, was an Article 10 merger between Uber and Grab.

The merger it prohibited is the one Grab filed: taking all of the Taiwan company's shares and control of its business and personnel. [Article 13, paragraph 1](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0150002&flno=13), says the commission may not prohibit a filed merger when the overall economic benefit outweighs the disadvantage of restricting competition. The October 21 release says the disadvantage outweighed the overall economic benefit. Paragraph 2 allows conditions or undertakings on a decision in an extended case under Article 11, paragraph 8, so that the benefit still outweighs the disadvantage. The commission said Grab's promises could not secure that result, and it prohibited the merger under paragraph 1.

Asked whether blocking the buyer might leave Uber stronger if foodpanda later left Taiwan, Shin Chih-chung (辛志中), a commissioner who also spoke for the commission, said at the news conference, according to [Taipei Times](https://www.taipeitimes.com/News/front/archives/2026/10/22/2003866403) (which spells his name Hsin), that the commission does not decide on hypotheticals. The test, he said, is the effect on free-market mechanisms, not what might follow if the authority is used. Focus Taiwan reported the same answer.

## Remedies the commission would not accept

Grab offered to dilute Uber's holding, to restrict Uber personnel from serving as Grab directors, and to limit the exchange of competitively sensitive information. The commission said it had discussed possible fixes with the filing party more than once. It concluded that those measures could not be relied on to work in practice and still would not remove the concern.

What would reduce or remove the coordination risk, the release says, is a further cut in Uber's stake in Grab, or clearing the stake entirely. Shin declined to specify how far the stake had to fall, Focus Taiwan reported. The companies, he said, have to propose measures that meet the concern.

No safe percentage is written down. The statute is silent. So is the release. A US fund that holds shares in both the buyer and its main Taiwan rival has no published number to measure itself against, and 13.1% was not treated as safe.

[Article 39](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0150002&flno=39), in its first paragraph, covers three situations: combining without the required filing or before the waiting period ends (Article 11, paragraphs 1 and 7); combining after the commission has prohibited the merger; and failing to carry out an undertaking attached under Article 13, paragraph 2. The commission may prohibit the combination, order a split within a deadline, dispose of all or part of the shares, transfer part of the business, remove people from their posts, or make another necessary disposition. It may also fine the enterprises at least NT$200,000 and at most NT$50 million. A false filing that is followed by a merger has a different fine range, from NT$100,000 to NT$1 million, and the same kinds of orders are available. Defying those orders can bring dissolution, an order to cease business, or a suspension of business. Each suspension is limited to six months. For Grab, the second situation is the one that matters while the prohibition stands.

## Another filing, and a court route that skips the appeal board

Shin said at the news conference, Focus Taiwan reported, that Grab could file again, and that the law does not limit companies to one or two filings. Taipei Times reported him saying that Grab and Delivery Hero have a legal right to reapply after they propose changes to the deal or to their finances. Those are Shin's statements. They do not promise that a second set of promises will be accepted.

[Article 48](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0150002&flno=48) says that a person who objects to a disposition or decision the competent authority makes under the Fair Trade Act goes directly into the administrative litigation procedure. There is no administrative-appeal step first for dispositions under this Act. [Administrative Litigation Act Article 106, paragraph 3](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=A0030154&flno=106), says that where a revocation suit may be brought without an appeal, it has to be filed within an unchangeable period of two months after the administrative disposition reaches the person or is announced.

The release does not say when that period started for Grab or Delivery Hero. For a US shareholder, the two months do not run from the press conference. They run from the day the written disposition reaches the party or is announced, and whether a shareholder of the buyer may sue at all is a separate question of standing.

## What the share register and the board minutes show

For a US company in Uber's position, or for a US fund beside it on the cap table, the Taiwan file is concrete. The commission relied on the shareholding, the dual-class voting cap, the board seat Uber's chief executive once held, and cooperation in other markets. In a US investor's own files, those facts sit in the dual-class terms, any director-nomination right, information rights, and cooperation agreements. A voting cap did not end the inquiry. The commission said the cap meant Uber could not directly decide Grab's competitive strategy, and it still found an incentive to pull punches.

A later application would have to show a holding, a board arrangement, and an information barrier that the commission can believe will actually be carried out. The October 21 release says Grab's three offered remedies were not enough.
