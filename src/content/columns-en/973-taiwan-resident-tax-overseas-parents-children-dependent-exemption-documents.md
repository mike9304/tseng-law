# Supporting parents in the US: the Taiwan exemption depends on your records and tax year

[Focus Taiwan’s October 29, 2026 report](https://focustaiwan.tw/business/202610290013) describes an aging dependent population in Ministry of Finance statistics for the 2024 tax year. The return due in May 2027 concerns 2026 income and its rules.

For an American working in Taiwan and sending money home, the useful question is what the tax office can establish about the people supported. Residence abroad is not itself disqualifying.

## Whose residence, and which year?

The resident status at issue is yours. [Income Tax Act Article 7](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0340003&flno=7) includes a person domiciled and habitually resident in Taiwan, or a person without a Taiwan domicile who stays here for at least 183 days in the tax year. Holding an ARC alone does not settle that test. Someone taxed under [Article 73’s non-resident rules](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0340003&flno=73) does not receive these resident personal exemptions; the [residency column](/en/columns/taiwan-income-tax-residency) explains the underlying classification.

The [Ministry of Finance’s announcement for the 2026 tax year](https://www.mof.gov.tw/singlehtml/384fb3077bb349ea973e7fc6f13b6974?cntId=34b463dc8f1b49f29d440d92a6fd5139) sets the general exemption at NT$101,000 for the May 2027 filing. A qualifying supported parent aged 70 or above receives NT$151,500, as set out in the [Ministry’s annual measures](https://www.mof.gov.tw/singlehtml/384fb3077bb349ea973e7fc6f13b6974?cntId=c41c49023f51417a81b7ea3d0fc9a962). These amounts reduce the income base.

There is also a change for children. The [Taipei National Taxation Bureau’s October 2026 notice](https://www.ntbt.gov.tw/singlehtml/41ae3594197f4f69b47753ce08188516?cntId=87c980537570418490d94eddfaa830f3) confirms a 50% increase for qualifying minor children from the 2026 tax year, bringing their exemption to NT$151,500. An older filing guide can miss that change.

## Age does not replace support

[Income Tax Act Article 17(1)(1)](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0340003&flno=17) covers the taxpayer’s and spouse’s direct ascendants aged 60 or older, or unable to earn a livelihood, who are supported by the taxpayer. A parent under 60 needs the incapacity branch.

For children, the provision covers minors and adult children supported because they are studying, have a physical or mental disability, or cannot earn a livelihood. Majority is 18 under [Civil Code Article 12](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=12). A child remaining at a US college may therefore need evidence of study as well as evidence of the family relationship and support. The applicable test is Taiwan’s; this is not a conclusion about a US tax return.

## A bank transfer proves only part of the file

Document requirements appear in the [Taipei tax bureau’s foreign-taxpayer FAQ](https://www.ntbt.gov.tw/singlehtml/0868f69a0e16490f90558979b32c8e83?cntId=452e7619162c4a3fa971497ac44a3bcc): basic identification, relationship, support and proof that the dependent is alive. It expressly permits qualifying dependents abroad. For parents, its examples include passport or birth records, relationship documents, remittance or notarized evidence of support, and civil or other records sufficient to establish that the person is alive.

Those functions are distinct. A birth certificate may establish the relationship but say little about who paid the parent’s expenses in 2026. Conversely, a transfer to an account does not alone establish that the recipient is the claimant’s parent. A middle name shown in full in one record and as an initial in another may require an explanation linking the records.

When incapacity is the basis for claiming a parent under 60, the FAQ specifies a public-hospital certificate or a document authenticated by the local government. Adult students can use relevant enrollment evidence, a student ID, graduation certificate or tuition receipt among the listed options. The particular condition being claimed determines the added evidence.

Authentication is not universal. The FAQ includes locally issued relationship records and documents authenticated by local government or an overseas mission as examples; it does not require every remittance record to be authenticated by a Taiwan mission. Translation and authentication requests should be tied to the actual documents and any request for further evidence.

## Care payments need an additional legal basis

Sending money for a parent’s care does not automatically establish the long-term care special deduction. Article 17(1)(2)(3)-7 requires the taxpayer, spouse or dependent to meet the disability and long-term care criteria announced by Taiwan’s central health and welfare authority. The deduction is NT$180,000 per qualifying person each year. Overseas medical and care records must be assessed against those criteria; age 70 alone is insufficient.

Article 17(3) also excludes households in the specified higher-income situations: a tax rate of 20% or more for the taxpayer or spouse under the statutory calculation after the long-term care and rental deductions; election of the 28% separate dividend calculation; or basic income above the statutory exclusion. The [Ministry of Finance Q&A on the rent deduction (updated July 2026)](https://www.mof.gov.tw/singlehtml/979b54e408fb499eae3c1d9efe978868?cntId=98d2cf2a269b459aadd3e7cde33e1524) identifies that last threshold as NT$7.5 million.

## A family cannot multiply one Taiwan exemption

[Income Tax Act Article 15](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0340003&flno=15) generally requires joint reporting of the taxpayer’s, spouse’s and dependents’ reportable income, subject to statutory separation exceptions and alternative tax calculations. Separate calculation does not mean each spouse can claim the same parent again.

If siblings also file in Taiwan, the [Ministry’s duplicate-parent rules](https://law-out.mof.gov.tw/LawContent.aspx?id=GL011591) identify one claimant in a prescribed order, considering the parent’s written designation, agreement between claimants, guardianship and, where those routes do not resolve it, the actual or principal support provided. The rule concerns duplicate Taiwan claims for the same year.
