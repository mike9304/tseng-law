# Foreign employees can join Taiwan employee stock trusts from December. What happens to the shares when they leave Taiwan?

Until now, Taiwan's employee stock ownership trusts had no dedicated account structure for foreign employees. A foreign engineer at a listed chipmaker could watch colleagues build a holding through the company plan without a clear way in.

That changes this winter. [CNA reported on October 13](https://www.cna.com.tw/news/afe/202610130293.aspx) that the Financial Supervisory Commission would let foreign employees of listed companies and their affiliates join these trusts. Companies typically set a purchase allowance and add their own contribution; the report gave an example of employees paying 30 to 50 percent. [The Economic Daily News reported on October 26](https://money.udn.com/money/story/5607/9838115) that the Taiwan Stock Exchange had amended its rules and would start the new arrangement on December 21.

Many foreign engineers do not plan to stay in Taiwan for good. What happens to the shares, and to the tax on them, when the residence card is handed back?

## Onshore account, trust in the trustee's name

The legal basis is the Commission's [order of October 15, 2026 (Jin-Guan-Zheng-Jiao No. 1150383977)](https://www.sfb.gov.tw/ch/home.jsp?id=88&parentpath=0%2C3&mcustomize=lawnews_view.jsp&dataserno=202610150002), issued under Article 10, paragraph 1 of the [Regulations Governing Investment in Securities by Overseas Chinese and Foreign Nationals](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0400011&flno=10). A trust company registers with the exchange as a foreign institutional investor. It opens separate accounts for each employer and for onshore and offshore foreign employees.

Which side is yours? Article 3 of the same regulations treats a foreign national living in Taiwan with an Alien Resident Certificate as onshore ([Article 3](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0400011&flno=3)). The order also lets the trust place onshore foreign employees' orders through the Taiwanese employees' account. Purchased shares may then be transferred to the onshore foreign employees' account, once a month.

## Dividends while you live in Taiwan

Legally, the trustee holds the shares. Tax still follows the beneficiary: [Article 3-4 of the Income Tax Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0340003&flno=3-4) requires the trustee to compute each beneficiary's income by category, and the beneficiary includes it for that year.

A resident individual then chooses how dividends are taxed. One route adds them to consolidated income with a credit of 8.5 percent of the dividends, capped at NT$80,000 per household each year. The other taxes the household's dividends separately at 28 percent ([Article 15](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0340003&flno=15), paragraphs 4 and 5). Which costs less depends on the household's whole income.

Tax residence is not settled by a visa. [Article 7](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0340003&flno=7) treats as resident anyone with a domicile in Taiwan who habitually lives here. Someone without a domicile is a resident for a tax year if present for 183 days or more in total. The finer points are in [our guide to Taiwan tax residency](/en/columns/taiwan-income-tax-residency).

## The year you leave

A resident who gives up a domicile or residence in Taiwan and leaves during the year must file and pay tax for that year before departure ([Article 71-1](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0340003&flno=71-1), paragraph 2). There is one exception. If the spouse is a resident who stays in Taiwan, the spouse files a joint return under Article 71 instead. Appointing an agent to file instead is covered in [a separate article on departure filings](/en/columns/taiwan-departure-income-tax-agent-article-72).

Once you are a non-resident, dividends from the trust are paid to a non-resident beneficiary. Article 89-1, paragraph 3 makes the trustee the withholding agent for a non-resident beneficiary ([Article 89-1](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0340003&flno=89-1)). The rate on dividends is 21 percent ([Standards of Withholding Rates for Various Incomes, Article 3](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0340028&flno=3)). A tax agreement between Taiwan and your new country of residence may cap it lower, subject to the agreement's own conditions. Paperwork comes first. Under [Article 25 of the Regulations Governing Application of Agreements for the Avoidance of Double Taxation with Respect to Taxes on Income](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0340125&flno=25), paragraph 2, the recipient gives the withholding agent a residence certificate from the other country's tax authority and proof of beneficial ownership. Paragraph 5 adds a beneficiary register and individual residence certificates when a foreign institutional investor of the other contracting state invests through trust contracts for that state's residents. How paragraph 5 applies to a Taiwanese trust company registered as a foreign institutional investor under the October 15 order has not been publicly explained. The trust company is the one to ask which documents it will collect, and by when.

Selling the shares is simpler. Income tax on securities transaction gains has been suspended since 1990, and losses cannot be deducted either ([Article 4-1](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0340003&flno=4-1)).

## Where the shares can go

Three exits are open under the order, during the trust or on withdrawal. Shares can be moved into an investment account the employee registered under Article 10, paragraph 1 of the regulations. They can go into a sell-only account opened at a securities firm under the exchange's or TPEx's rules. Or the trust can sell them and pay out cash.

Insiders have fewer options. Directors, supervisors, managers and anyone holding more than 10 percent of the company's shares cannot take cash, and must report changes in their holdings under Article 25 of the Securities and Exchange Act.

One gap is worth asking about now. The order separates onshore and offshore foreign employees but does not say how a participant who moves abroad is switched from one account to the other. Will the trust keep you in, move your holding, or ask you to withdraw? The order does not answer that. The trust contract, the employer's plan documents and the trust company's procedures are the places to look, along with any exchange rules or later guidance. The same documents should say what happens to the company's matching contribution if you leave early.
