# Rescued on a Taiwan Mountain: Who Pays, and What Hiking Without a Permit Costs

NT$500,000. That is the minimum emergency-rescue cover Taichung City requires for routes such as the Xueshan West Ridge and Nanhudashan; on the Xuedong and Wuling Sixiu (武陵四秀) routes it is NT$300,000. The same figure caps what the city may bill a hiker it rescues. Without the permit or the insurance, the cap does not apply.

No single statute governs rescue bills. Zones closed during a disaster fall under the national Disaster Prevention and Protection Act; ordinary accidents, under local regulations where adopted. Permit fines sit in three other laws.

## Taichung's special control areas

The [Taichung Mountaineering Activities Management Autonomous Regulation](https://lawsearch.taichung.gov.tw/LawContent.aspx?id=GL003013) (臺中市登山活動管理自治條例) was enacted on November 24, 2016; as of November 3, 2026, the city database lists no later amendment. It covers announced areas within Taichung's boundaries only (art. 3(2)). "General control" areas need a police mountain-entry permit. "Special control" areas need a national park entry permit (art. 3).

Article 14(1) reaches only the special areas. If a hiker has an accident there and the city carries out the search and rescue, the city may order payment in writing. No breach is required. Paragraph 2 lists the costs: extra overtime, travel and insurance for city staff; civilian labour; dogs, equipment, vehicles, aircraft, land, buildings or structures the city requisitions, purchases, rents or requests, with feed, fuel, supplies and utilities; and other necessary rescue spending. Helicopter costs count.

## When the cap disappears

Article 14(3) limits the bill to the emergency-rescue amount of the mountaineering insurance required by article 6(4). A city [announcement of November 2, 2017](https://lawsearch.taichung.gov.tw/LawContent.aspx?id=GL003320) sets that amount by route, alongside NT$1 million death cover and NT$100,000 medical cover.

| Announced route (examples) | Minimum emergency-rescue cover | Entry permit from (announcement note) |
|---|---|---|
| Xuedong, Wuling Sixiu, Zhijiayang | NT$300,000 | Shei-Pa National Park |
| Xueshan West Ridge, Holy Ridge, Xuejian | NT$500,000 | Shei-Pa National Park |
| Nanhudashan, North Section 1 and 2 | NT$500,000 | Taroko National Park |

Any breach of article 4, article 6(1) or (4), or article 7(2) removes the cap:

- not obtaining a permit required under the National Park Act, mountain control rules or other law (art. 4(1));
- departing from the hiking plan in a special area, unless forced by natural disaster or force majeure (art. 4(2));
- entering an unopened trail or cutting a new path, unless to escape imminent danger (art. 4(3));
- no leader, or no mountaineering insurance (art. 6(1), (4));
- entering during, or not leaving after notice of, a closure the city announces once a sea typhoon warning is issued (art. 7(2)).

A hiker climbs Nanhudashan without an entry permit, twists an ankle and is lifted out by helicopter (an invented example). No NT$500,000 ceiling applies. Billing remains discretionary: the article says "may."

## Fines on top

Under the regulation, fines in NT dollars are 6,000 to 30,000 for leaving the plan or using unopened trails (art. 9); 3,000 to 15,000 for carrying no locating device or emergency communication equipment (art. 10); 10,000 to 50,000 for a leader's breach of article 6 (art. 11); 10,000 to 50,000 for breaching a typhoon closure, unless leaving at once would be clearly dangerous (art. 12); and 3,000 to 15,000 for refusing, without justification, an inspection or a demand for documents (art. 13(2)). Police, fire or area authorities may report a breach after an emergency ends (art. 8), so a fine can follow a rescue.

Entering without a permit (art. 4(1)) carries no fine under the regulation itself. The cost shows up elsewhere: the rescue cap is lost, and the permit laws apply. Acts of Indigenous persons under article 19 of the Indigenous Peoples Basic Act are exempt (art. 16).

## Typhoon closures: the national rule

In a disaster, [article 30](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0120014&flno=30) of the Disaster Prevention and Protection Act lets the commander of a disaster response center designate warning zones and restrict entry or order people out (para. 1(2)), and restrict vehicles, vessels or aircraft in set road sections, waters or airspace (para. 1(3)). Under paragraph 3, if a person breaches either order, falls into danger as a result and is rescued by the center, the rescued person, or a business responsible for the breach, may be ordered in writing to pay the costs. An ordinary accident with no response center in place does not meet these conditions.

The Ministry of the Interior's [rules on paying disaster search and rescue costs](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawAll.aspx?pcode=D0120022) (災害搜救費用繳納辦法) require the costs to be computed and a written decision issued within three months after the mission ends ([art. 3](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0120022&flno=3)(1)). Several people share equally; a responsible business pays half, the rescued the rest (art. 3(3)). Minor or special circumstances allow a reduction (art. 3(4)). [Article 4](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0120022&flno=4) prices staff costs at actual payments and other items at government rates, then trade association rates, then local market value. Entering a warning zone without a temporary pass, or refusing to leave, also brings a fine of NT$50,000 to NT$250,000 ([art. 55](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0120014&flno=55)(2)).

## No permit: which law applies

National park ecological protection areas require the park headquarters' permission ([National Park Act art. 19](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0070105&flno=19)). [Article 26](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0070105&flno=26) sets a fine of up to 1,000 yuan, which [article 2 of the currency conversion statute](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0380048&flno=2) triples to NT$3,000. Other park offences and appeals: see our [national park filming fine column](/en/columns/taiwan-national-park-filming-fine-administrative-appeal).

Forest nature reserves fall under the Forestry Act, whose [article 17-1](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=M0040001&flno=17-1) controls entry. [Article 56-3](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=M0040001&flno=56-3)(1)(4) fines unauthorised entry NT$1,000 to NT$60,000, with an exception for Indigenous customary needs (para. 2).

Mountain control areas carry a criminal sanction with a narrow trigger. [National Security Act art. 6](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=A0030028&flno=6)(2) requires a permit. Under the Enforcement Rules ([art. 31](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=A0030029&flno=31)), hikers apply to the National Police Agency or local police. [Article 15](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=A0030028&flno=15)(1) punishes only entry or exit without a permit and without good reason, followed by ignoring a notice to leave: up to six months' imprisonment, detention, and/or a fine of up to NT$15,000. Indigenous persons and those entering through force majeure or emergency, among others, need only show identification ([rules art. 32](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=A0030029&flno=32)).

## Visitors from abroad

Does travel insurance bought at home count? Article 6(4) and (5) of the Taichung regulation require the leader to buy mountaineering insurance for the group at the announced minimums. They say nothing about foreign policies, so the policy wording has to be checked against those amounts.

Reporting on September 8, 2026, the Economic Daily News quoted a Financial Supervisory Commission official saying that policy exclusions include emergency-rescue costs arising in permit areas entered without a permit, in warning zones, or in times, areas or ranges an agency has announced as prohibited or restricted; search costs within 24 hours after the planned descent (missing persons aside); and aircraft costs for search, care or transport. It also named Nantou, Hualien, Pingtung and Miaoli as having similar regulations; terms may differ.

Foreigners get no separate procedure under the Enforcement Rules on mountain control areas (arts. 29 to 32). Applications start at the government's [Taiwan Mountaineering One-Stop Service](https://hike.taiwan.gov.tw/), which brings national park entry, Forestry and Nature Conservation Agency areas and police mountain permits together.

## Working with a lawyer in Taiwan

Under the national cost rules, a decision has dates worth checking: whether it issued within three months of the mission, and whether it itemises the costs and states how to appeal ([art. 5](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0120022&flno=5)). An administrative appeal must be filed within 30 days from the day after the decision is served ([Administrative Appeal Act art. 14](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=A0030020&flno=14)(1)). A Taiwan lawyer can test whether the Taichung cap applies, request the calculations, handle the appeal and any lawsuit, and contest an insurer's exclusion.
