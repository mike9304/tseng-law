# In Taiwan, can a crash lead to negligent homicide charges if the victim dies months later?

Even after an agreement to withdraw a complaint in a negligent injury case, a driver may face investigation or trial for negligent homicide if the victim dies from a cause linked to the crash. Taiwan's [Criminal Code, Article 287](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0000001&flno=287), makes negligent injury a complaint-required offense. Negligent homicide is outside that category.

Chronology alone does not determine the offense. Courts examine what lasting impairments the injuries caused and how those impairments led to death ([New Taipei District Court judgment](https://judgment.judicial.gov.tw/FJUD/data.aspx?ty=JD&id=PCDM%2C114%2C%E4%BA%A4%E8%A8%B4%2C28%2C20260828%2C1); [Taiwan High Court judgment](https://judgment.judicial.gov.tw/FJUD/data.aspx?ty=JD&id=TPHM%2C115%2C%E4%BA%A4%E4%B8%8A%E8%A8%B4%2C20%2C20260528%2C1)).

## Brain damage persisted after the condition stabilized

On January 19, 2024, a motorcycle struck a person crossing a road in New Taipei City. Treatment followed for head trauma and a right-sided subdural hemorrhage. After discharge, the victim moved to a care facility and died on July 19 that year. Six months had passed since the crash. New Taipei District Court delivered its first-instance judgment in case 114年度交訴字第28號 on August 28, 2026.

Although the rider admitted negligently injuring the victim by failing to watch the road ahead, responsibility for the death was disputed. Defense counsel argued that treatment had controlled the symptoms and stabilized the victim's condition, leaving no adequate causal connection (相當因果關係) between the crash and death. To assess that argument, the court sought explanations from the Institute of Forensic Medicine and the treating hospital.

According to the institute's response quoted in the judgment, the brain damage found at autopsy explained the victim's prolonged bed confinement and impaired neurological, physiological and limb function. Those impairments made complications such as choking on food and pneumonia more likely. For that reason, the institute listed brain injury, pneumonia and asphyxiation from food aspiration together as causes of death. Death had not occurred immediately from the trauma at impact.

In its response, the treating hospital explained that the elderly victim had not regained consciousness after the severe brain injury. Prolonged bed confinement increased susceptibility to complications such as pneumonia. The hospital therefore considered the injury an indirect cause of death.

Did the stable condition break the causal connection? The court rejected that argument. It found that complications and death from these injuries were 「客觀上可預期」, or objectively foreseeable. Severe brain damage and bed confinement had persisted throughout the course of care, supporting a finding of adequate causation between the rider's negligence and death.

Read together, the initial brain injury, condition after discharge, autopsy findings and responses from both institutions explained the course of events. Where the dispute concerns whether crash injuries contributed to later complications, the records need to cover the impairments that remained at discharge and the care that followed.

## An injury prosecution can become a homicide case during trial

Another New Taipei District Court case concerned a pedestrian struck on a crosswalk by a passenger car proceeding straight through a red light on December 13, 2024. After the collision, the victim remained confined to bed and could not breathe adequately without a ventilator. Transfer to another hospital after acute treatment did not end the need for ventilation and continuing care. Ventilator-associated pneumonia and sepsis followed; the victim died on June 23, 2025 ([115年度交易字第20號](https://judgment.judicial.gov.tw/FJUD/data.aspx?ty=JD&id=PCDM%2C115%2C%E4%BA%A4%E6%98%93%2C20%2C20260629%2C1), first-instance judgment of June 29, 2026).

Despite the death during the investigation, the indictment still alleged negligent injury. Hospital responses and treatment records led the court to connect the crash to bed confinement, respiratory failure, ventilator dependence, pneumonia, sepsis and death. Citing Supreme Court decisions, the judgment explained that adequate causation can exist where a negligently inflicted injury causes an illness that leads to death. It also identified the limit: death from another illness wholly unrelated to the original injury would break the causal connection, leaving only negligent injury.

Records quoted from the receiving hospital stated that the victim had lived independently before the crash. Its response identified duration of ventilator use, advanced age and poor nutrition as risk factors for ventilator-associated pneumonia, while still finding a causal link between the crash injuries and death. Relying on that response, the court connected the post-crash bed confinement and respiratory failure to pneumonia and death.

[Article 300 of the Code of Criminal Procedure](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0010001&flno=300) permits a court to change the statutory provisions cited by the prosecutor in relation to the criminal facts charged. Here, the court explained that the underlying facts remained the same. It had also informed the defendant in court of the changed offense and provided an opportunity to respond.

The available penalties change too. For negligent injury under the first part of [Criminal Code Article 284](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0000001&flno=284), the alternatives are imprisonment for up to 1 year, short-term detention or a fine of up to NT$100,000. Negligent homicide under [Article 276](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0000001&flno=276) carries imprisonment for up to 5 years, short-term detention or a fine of up to NT$500,000.

For this driver, the court applied the aggravating provision for failing to yield to a pedestrian on a crosswalk in [Article 86, paragraph 1, subparagraph 5, of the Road Traffic Management and Penalty Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=K0040012&flno=86). It also reduced the sentence for voluntary surrender because the driver had identified themself to police at the scene as the person who caused the crash. The resulting sentence was 1 year and 3 months' imprisonment. It offers no benchmark for predicting other sentences.

These examples concern changes during an ongoing investigation or trial. They do not resolve how a case may be revisited when death occurs after a final judgment or a disposition closing the case. Both New Taipei judgments were at first instance. As of November 11, 2026, no higher-court judgment had been located in the judgment search system. Their finality cannot be established from these judgments alone.

## Why did the fracture case remain negligent injury on appeal?

During sewer construction in Taoyuan City on September 27, 2022, reversing construction machinery collided with a motorcycle. Surgery was performed for the victim's fractured right tibia. An acute myocardial infarction followed, requiring intensive care and transfer to another hospital for treatment of cardiac, respiratory and kidney conditions. Death occurred on February 11, 2023. Taiwan High Court issued its appellate judgment in case 115年度交上訴字第20號 on May 28, 2026.

Prosecutors argued that the crash injuries had led to death through the myocardial infarction, pneumonia during bed confinement and subsequent complications. They relied on a certificate issued by the district prosecutors' office following a death examination (相驗), which listed the crash and fracture among the antecedent causes. Hospital documentation gave a different account. Its death certificate recorded septic shock as the immediate cause, preceded by acute kidney failure with chronic kidney disease.

Asked about a connection to the tibial fracture, the hospital reported insufficient medical evidence linking that trauma to the cause of death. In assessing that certificate, the High Court noted that it reflected information available to the forensic physician at the death examination. There was no indication that the actual surgical and treatment records had been consulted. Merely listing the crash on that certificate did not establish a connection unsupported by the detailed medical records and hospital response.

Diabetes and hypertension also appeared in the hospital's response. Given the approximately 4 months between the crash and death, the court found it difficult to exclude the possible intervention of other causes, including the victim's own illnesses.

Negligence had caused the fracture. Evidence linking it to death was insufficient. Dismissing the prosecution's appeal, the High Court upheld the first-instance sentence of 4 months' imprisonment for negligent injury. Its judgment stated that the negligent injury portion was not subject to further appeal, while allowing the prosecutor a possible further appeal on the death-related portion on limited grounds. Whether the entire case had become final was not established in the judgment. As of November 11, 2026, no third-instance judgment had been located in the judgment search system.

What mattered in this case was why intensive care and transfer had become necessary after the fracture, and whether evidence connected the later treatment and death to that injury. Medical evidence supported the link between lasting impairments and complications in the two New Taipei cases. In the fracture case, that link remained unproven.

## Withdrawing a complaint and the role of mediation in sentencing

After settling a negligent injury case, what happens if the complainant actually withdraws the complaint? For a complaint-required offense, [Article 238 of the Code of Criminal Procedure](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0010001&flno=238) allows withdrawal before the close of arguments at first instance. A person who withdraws may not file the complaint again. For complaint-required cases, withdrawal is among the grounds on which [Article 303, subparagraph 3](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0010001&flno=303) requires the court to dismiss the case on procedural grounds (不受理). Both provisions apply to negligent injury as listed in Criminal Code Article 287.

Once death is found to be causally linked to the crash and negligent homicide is at issue, withdrawing a complaint alone does not entitle the defendant to the same procedural outcome. In the ventilator case, 115年度交易字第20號, the judgment recorded both a mediation record and a written withdrawal of the complaint. A negligent homicide conviction nevertheless followed. Sentencing reasons noted that the mediated amount consisted entirely of compulsory automobile liability insurance payments and that the complainant had requested a heavy sentence. Neither document was published in full in the judgment, so this case cannot establish every civil effect of the settlement.

Mediation still matters at sentencing. In the brain injury case, where death followed 6 months after the crash, the court considered the absence of prior convictions and the mediated settlement with the victim's heirs, among other circumstances. It applied a reduction for voluntary surrender under [Criminal Code Article 62](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0000001&flno=62), imposing 8 months' imprisonment, suspended for 2 years. Compliance with the compensation obligations in the mediation record was also imposed as a condition under [Article 74, paragraph 2, subparagraph 3](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0000001&flno=74). It remained a conviction.

Does settlement guarantee a suspended sentence? No. In the fracture case, the appellate court noted that the insurer treated the case as involving negligent injury only, that the parties remained far apart on compensation, and that mediation had failed several times. Failure to settle alone did not justify a finding of a poor attitude after the offense.

## Civil claims depend on the medical course too

Where the crash caused the death, civil claims may also cover losses resulting from that death. [Civil Code Article 192](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=192) imposes liability toward those who paid medical expenses, expenses for increased living needs or funeral expenses. Lost support carries an additional condition. The deceased must have owed the claimant a legal duty of support. Under [Article 194](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=194), the victim's parents, children and spouse may claim compensation for non-pecuniary harm. The criminal sentences discussed above did not themselves determine the family's compensation.

Putting the medical certificates in date order, from the crash to the death certificate, can reveal gaps in the documented course of care. Those records can be compared with pre-crash medical history, admission and discharge summaries, treatment after transfer, hospital responses and any available death-examination or autopsy materials. The judgments examined what impairments remained after the condition stabilized and whether they connected to the causes on the death certificate. Drivers can identify the precise link they dispute; families can check which records explain that part of the course of events.
