# The driver blamed the rental company for the missing insurance. A Taiwan court still sent the bill to the driver

"It was the car rental company that failed to buy compulsory insurance, which is why I have to pay so much." That, in the judgment's summary, was the driver's defence. On November 16, 2018, the New Taipei District Court rejected it and ordered the driver to pay the Special Compensation Fund NT$532,995 plus interest ([New Taipei District Court civil judgment 107 Su No. 2236](https://www.mvacf.org.tw/ckfinder/connector?command=Proxy&type=Attach&currentFolder=/tblResearches/&fileName=%E8%87%BA%E7%81%A3%E6%96%B0%E5%8C%97%E5%9C%B0%E6%96%B9%E6%B3%95%E9%99%A2%E6%B0%91%E4%BA%8B%E5%88%A4%E6%B1%BA107%E5%B9%B4%E5%BA%A6%E8%A8%B4%E5%AD%97%E7%AC%AC2236%E8%99%9F.pdf&c=7&p=f5eca9ba280545f59e1e33e59795ecd1&l=3)).

This was a first-instance ruling. It allowed an appeal within 20 days of service. Whether anyone appealed cannot be confirmed from the public copy.

## A left turn and a motorcycle

On November 19, 2017, the driver was turning left in a car at an intersection in Xinzhuang, New Taipei, and collided with a motorcycle travelling the other way on the same road. The police preliminary analysis suspected the driver had turned left on a green straight-ahead arrow and found no cause attributable to the rider. A preliminary analysis is not a final finding. Still, the driver did not dispute the medical certificate or the payment records.

Left with a disability, the rider had no insurer to turn to. The car carried no compulsory automobile liability insurance, a point the driver did not dispute, so the rider claimed from the Special Compensation Fund under [Article 40(1)(ii) of the Compulsory Automobile Liability Insurance Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0390060&flno=40). The Fund paid NT$532,995 for medical costs and disability. The driver said the car had been rented; the judgment did not separately establish who owned it or who was obliged to insure it.

## Two different people: the one who must insure, the one who must pay

The duty to insure falls in principle on the vehicle's owner ([Article 6](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0390060&flno=6)). For a rental car, that is usually the company. The driver's argument aimed at exactly this gap. Why should the person who rented the car carry the cost of the owner's failure?

The court's answer was short. The Fund's subrogation right is the victim's own damages claim, transferred to the Fund ([Article 42(2)](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0390060&flno=42)). That claim lay against the person who drove and caused the crash, under [Article 184(1), first sentence, and Article 184(2)](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=184) and [Article 191-2 of the Civil Code](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=191-2). The Fund's payment counts as part of the damages that driver owes ([Article 42(1)](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0390060&flno=42)). Even if the rental company had failed to insure, as the driver argued, that did not change the structure.

Would an insured rental car have made a difference? Yes. An insurer that pays the victim may recover from its own insured only in the cases listed in [Article 29](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0390060&flno=29), such as drink-driving, drug-driving, intentional acts or driving without a licence. Without insurance that limit disappears. One exception survives: if the person liable is the victim's spouse, head of household, household member, or a close blood relative or in-law within the degrees set out in [Article 42(4)](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0390060&flno=42), the Fund has no subrogated claim unless an Article 29(1) circumstance applies.

## What is left for the rental company

If the rental company owned the car and was the person obliged to insure it, the company would face its own administrative fines: NT$3,000 to NT$15,000 for a car, and NT$9,000 to NT$32,000 if an uninsured vehicle causes an accident ([Article 49](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0390060&flno=49)). Those fines run separately from what the driver owes the Fund.

Whether the driver could pass the bill on to the rental company was not an issue in this case. That would turn on what the rental contract said about insurance and what the company told the customer, and would need its own claim and its own proof.

## Before renting a car or scooter in Taiwan

Visitors and residents alike may want to check that the compulsory policy covers the whole rental period, and to photograph the contract and any insurance papers handed over at pickup. A related case, about a rider of an uninsured scooter who left Taiwan and was still pursued by the Fund, is [covered here](/en/columns/taiwan-uninsured-scooter-fund-recourse-after-leaving).

In this case the order was NT$532,995, with interest from September 12, 2018, the day after the complaint was served.
