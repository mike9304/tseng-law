# A Foreign Spouse Leaves a Taiwan Prison with an Expired ARC: Immigration Detention, Bond and the Road Back

Fifteen days. That is the longest Taiwan's National Immigration Agency (移民署) can hold a foreigner on its own decision under [Article 38 of the Immigration Act (入出國及移民法)](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0080132&flno=38). Anything longer goes to a judge. The agency must ask the court at least five days before the 15 days run out; a continued detention can last up to 45 days. A further extension of up to 40 days requires that removal cannot be carried out, either because a lost or invalid passport or travel document has not yet been renewed, replaced or extended, or because of a natural disaster, epidemic or other force majeure. The agency must also consider continued detention necessary and apply to the court at least five days before the continued detention ends ([Article 38-4](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0080132&flno=38-4)).

For an American married to a Taiwanese citizen, those numbers become real on the day a prison sentence ends. The criminal case may be over. The immigration file is still open.

## At the prison gate

Taiwan's statutes do not say every foreigner leaving prison is detained. Article 38, paragraph 1 sets three conditions together: a deportation order; at least one of three grounds (the person lacks the relevant travel documents and removal therefore cannot be carried out as prescribed, facts suggest the person may disappear, flee or refuse to leave voluntarily, or a foreign government has issued a wanted notice); and a finding that removal would be clearly difficult without detention. The agency must give the person a chance to state an opinion before ordering provisional detention.

Where does a Taiwanese spouse fit? Paragraph 2 of the same article. If, after hearing the person, the agency considers detention unnecessary, it may require a guarantor instead: a Taiwan national with household registration who lives in Taiwan, a charity, an NGO, or staff of the person's embassy or authorized office in Taiwan. It may require a cash deposit instead. Conditions can be attached, among them reporting regularly to a designated immigration station, living at a designated address, staying reachable by phone and cooperating in obtaining travel documents for the return home. For an American whose passport ran out during the sentence, the first ground in paragraph 1 is where the agency will look: the question is whether a missing document means removal cannot be carried out as prescribed, and a renewed passport or other travel document ready by release day answers it.

Some people may be spared detention altogether. [Article 38-1](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0080132&flno=38-1) lists, among others, serious illness where detention would harm treatment, pregnancy of five months or more, old age or disability that prevents self-care, and an exit ban from a court or other authority.

If detention is ordered, the person, a spouse, a lineal relative, a legal representative or a sibling may object, orally or in writing and stating reasons, once the person has received the written detention decision and while provisional detention lasts. The agency reviews it. If it finds the objection groundless, it must send the person and the file to court within 24 hours of receiving the objection ([Article 38-2](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0080132&flno=38-2)). The written decision must be in a language the person understands and state how to challenge it, and the agency must contact the person's embassy or authorized office, or a friend or relative in Taiwan whom the person names, within 24 hours ([Article 38-6](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0080132&flno=38-6)).

## Where the deportation order comes from

Detention presupposes a deportation order, and two routes lead to one for a spouse who has served time.

The first is the length of the sentence. Under [Article 32](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0080132&flno=32), subparagraph 3, a final sentence of one year or more of imprisonment means the agency revokes or annuls the residence permit and cancels the ARC. The wording is mandatory. It does not apply to negligent offences or to suspended sentences. Once the permit is gone on that ground, [Article 36](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0080132&flno=36), paragraph 2, subparagraph 8 allows the agency to deport the person or order departure within ten days.

The second route is the calendar. A sentence under a year does not trigger subparagraph 3 of Article 32. [Article 24](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0080132&flno=24), paragraph 1, subparagraph 3 still lets the agency revoke or annul a permit already granted to someone with a criminal record, though that power is discretionary ("may"). If the permit survives, the card can still expire while the holder is inside. Article 31, paragraph 1 requires an extension application before expiry, and [Article 9 of the Regulations Governing Visiting, Residency, and Permanent Residency of Aliens (外國人停留居留及永久居留辦法)](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0080129&flno=9) opens that window three months before the expiry date. Neither text says whether an inmate can file through a relative. That has to be asked of the agency directly.

Miss the window and the spouse is an overstayer. The fine is NT$10,000 to NT$50,000 ([Article 74-1](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0080132&flno=74-1), paragraph 2). Paragraphs 4 and 5 of that article let the competent authority reduce or waive it for special reasons under standards it sets; the statute does not say whether imprisonment counts. Article 36, paragraph 2, subparagraph 6 allows deportation or a ten-day departure order. One narrow exception exists in [Article 31](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0080132&flno=31), paragraph 3: if the overstay is under 30 days and the original reason for residence still exists, the person may apply for residence again after paying the fine. A marriage that is still intact is that reason. The 30 days run from the day the card expired, so the exception seldom helps someone whose ARC lapsed early in the sentence.

Even a timely extension can be refused. [Article 17 of the same Regulations](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0080129&flno=17) applies the grounds in Article 24, paragraph 1 of the Act to extension applications, and a criminal record is one of them.

A court can also order deportation as part of the criminal judgment itself, under Article 95 of the Criminal Code. That separate track, and the review committee that Article 36, paragraph 4 requires before a resident is deported unless one of its exceptions applies, are covered in [After a Taiwan Criminal Sentence Becomes Final](/en/columns/taiwan-sentence-fine-conversion-probation-deportation-foreigner).

## Coming back on the marriage

Deportation leaves the legal basis for spousal residence in place. [Article 23](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0080132&flno=23), paragraph 1, subparagraph 1 lets a foreigner whose spouse is a Taiwan national living in Taiwan with household registration apply for residence. To use that route, the applicant must have entered on a valid visa allowing a stay of 60 days or more, with no annotation barring extension or imposing another restriction. Entry comes first.

[Article 18](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0080132&flno=18), paragraph 1 allows the agency to bar entry for a criminal record in Taiwan or abroad (subparagraph 7), a prior refusal of entry, departure order or deportation (subparagraph 11), or a past overstay or illegal work (subparagraph 12). After entry, [Article 24](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0080132&flno=24), paragraph 1 allows the agency to refuse residence for a criminal record or past deportation (subparagraph 3) or a past overstay (subparagraph 11).

Only for the overstay and unauthorized-work grounds does the Act itself fix a period: at least one year and no more than seven, counted from the day after departure (Article 18, paragraph 3, for subparagraph 12; Article 24, paragraph 3, for subparagraphs 11 and 12). For a criminal record, the numbers live in administrative rules instead. The Interior Ministry's [guidelines on refusal periods for residence applications (外國人申請居留或變更居留原因不予許可期間處理原則)](https://gazette.nat.gov.tw/EG_FileManager/eguploadpub/eg030038/ch02/type2/gov10/num6/Eg.htm), in the version effective March 1, 2024, implement Article 24 and scale the bar to the sentence a Taiwan court imposed, counted from the day after departure. Point 3 sets six years for a sentence of one year to under three, and five years for a sentence under one year.

Two qualifications in that same point decide most spousal cases. A foreigner who already held a residence permit falls under those periods only for an intentional offence with a final, unsuspended sentence of one year or more. And for a sentence under one year, as for a suspended sentence, short-term detention or a fine, there is no bar if the foreigner's spouse or minor child is a Taiwan national living in Taiwan with household registration. Where the sentence for an intentional offence is at least one year but less than three years, the six-year period applies, but Point 9 halves it when the spouse or a parent is a Taiwan national living in Taiwan with household registration (or holds certain residence status), unless the final sentence was three years or more, and lets the agency lift the bar where it would leave a household-registered spouse, parent or child in extreme hardship. Overstays have their own scale and exceptions in Points 5 and 7, one of which covers a marriage of at least a year, registered in Taiwan, to a Taiwan national living there with household registration.

Entry runs on a parallel rulebook, the agency's [Operation Directions for the Entry Bans on Foreign Nationals](https://www.immigration.gov.tw/5475/5478/141478/141482/148796/), published in English on its website. They set six years for a sentence of at least one year but less than three years and five years for one under a year, also from the day after departure. Under Point X, a foreigner married to a household-registered Taiwan national, with the marriage registered in Taiwan, may be exempted or apply for exemption after a sentence of six months or less if the couple has a biological child or has been married at least two years; a spouse or parent who is a Taiwan national with household registration (or has certain other residence status) supports an application to halve the ban unless the final sentence was three years or more; and grave hardship to a household-registered spouse, parent or biological child supports an application for exemption. Point 10 of the residence guidelines ties the two together: when the entry ban for the same case was never imposed, has expired or has been waived, that case no longer restricts the residence application.

These are agency rules, not text of Articles 18 or 24, and the Act still says "may." A period that has run, or an exception that fits, removes one obstacle; it does not oblige the agency to approve if another ground applies. Subparagraphs 7 and 8 of Article 24, paragraph 1 also cover sham marriages and spouses who do not live together without good reason, so a new application is read with that in mind: the judgment, proof that the sentence or parole ended, and evidence of a real, shared household.

## If release comes on parole

Parole is possible once more than half of a fixed term has been served, or two-thirds for a repeat offender, with evidence of reform; the prison applies to the Ministry of Justice, and the Ministry decides ([Criminal Code, Article 77](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0000001&flno=77)). Paragraph 2 of that article rules parole out in three cases: less than six months of the fixed term served; a repeat offender whose offence carries a minimum of five years and who, during parole or within five years after the sentence was fully served or partly served and then pardoned, again intentionally commits an offence carrying a minimum of five years; and a person serving time for an offence listed in Article 91-1, mainly sexual offences, who received counseling or treatment during the sentence and whose risk of reoffending was then assessed as not clearly reduced. A parolee is placed under protective supervision for the parole period ([Article 93](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0000001&flno=93), paragraph 2). The supervised person may not leave the supervision area without the supervisor's permission, and an absence of ten days or more needs the prosecutor's approval ([Rehabilitative Disposition Execution Act (保安處分執行法), Article 74-2](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=I0030002&flno=74-2), subparagraph 5).

Foreigners face one more provision. [Article 74-1](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=I0030002&flno=74-1) of that Act says protective supervision of a foreigner may be replaced by deportation. It is an option on the criminal side, and it says "may."

How does the agency's deportation fit with supervision that is still running? The closest provision is Article 36, paragraph 3 of the Immigration Act: the agency must notify the judicial authorities ten days before deporting a foreigner whose criminal case has entered judicial proceedings, and unless that person is lawfully held in pretrial detention (羈押), compelled to appear (拘提), held in civil custody (管收) or barred from leaving Taiwan, the agency may deport the person or order departure. The provision does not, by itself, say how that applies to a parolee under supervision, or which step is carried out first.

## Working with a Taiwan lawyer

While the sentence is running, a Taiwan lawyer can ask the agency in writing how an extension can be filed for someone in custody, so the ARC does not lapse unnoticed. Near release, the lawyer can be appointed, with an interpreter, to attend the hearing, or the review committee where one is convened; the agency may limit or bar attendance that would endanger national security or disrupt the proceedings (Article 36, paragraph 5). The lawyer can also draft the spouse's detention objection or the papers for a guarantor or deposit. After a removal, the lawyer can assemble the marriage and household record for a new application and work out whether an entry or residence bar, under the Act or the agency rules, is still running and whether a spousal exception or halving applies.
