## Verdict
naturalness (1 = machine, 5 = native professional): 3/5 for guidance pack, 2/5 for columns
variety used: Hungarian as used in Hungary, consistent as a language; place-name romanization mixed (Tajpej vs Taicsung vs Kaohsiung/Pingtung). No dialect split.
systemic patterns (max 6, each one line, with 1 example quote):
- French «guillemets» instead of Hungarian „alsó-felső” quotes: "a «Kapcsolat» oldal elmagyarázza"
- Korean-audience leftovers not recast for a Hungary reader (treaty, “Korean-speaking lawyer”, Hangul URLs): "Még ha koreai anyavállalat lép is be Tajvanra"
- visz/kísér calques for running or representing a matter: "Az iroda a tajvani jog szerinti fő ügycsoportokat viszi."
- ISO dates with a Hungarian case ending: "2023-12-27-én lépett hatályba"
- Digit+period legalese (3. személy, 1. fok, 2. fok) where Hungarian legal prose spells the ordinal: "polgári mellékkeresetet … a 2. fok szóbeli tárgyalásának"
- Word-for-word Taiwanese family-law calque kept as the Hungarian term: "a kiskorú gyermekkel kapcsolatos jogok és kötelezettségek gyakorlását és viselését"

Gender (F): no finding. Hungarian ügyvéd is the professional title for a woman; verbs and possessives do not mark sex. No úr, masculine pronoun, or ügyvéd úr attached to Wei Tseng (曾雋崴).
Columns 001–002, 004 and 007 are dense, usable legal Hungarian once the Korea-audience holes are closed. 003 from Q16, 005, 006, 008 and 009 still read as a Korean blog run through a translator. Do not treat 002 as a problem file: aside from two grammar slips it is the most native stretch in this part.
