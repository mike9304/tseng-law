## Verdict
naturalness (1 = machine, 5 = native professional): 4/5 for guidance pack, 3/5 for columns
variety used: Filipino (Tagalog-based, Philippines), formal kayo/ninyo in the guidance pack and in 001–004 and 007. Not mixed with another national variety. 006 is conversational blog Filipino and reads native. 008–009 and 003 Q16–Q20 / 005 drop into chopped first-person lines. Spanish-legal residue (partido, empleo, Personalidad Legal, litigasyon) is word choice, not a second standard.
systemic patterns (max 6, each one line, with 1 example quote):
- "partido" for a party to a contract or case, which a reader hears as a political party: "o sa ibang partido"
- Coined or Spanish-phonetic spellings: "pribasiya", "akawntant", "konstruksiyon", "establisimyento"
- English legal labels left inside Filipino, often with a second gloss: "work permit (permit sa trabaho, 工作許可)"
- "paksa" for the person or entity that holds rights (主體): "nagiging paksa ng mga karapatan at obligasyon"
- U+200B-only blank lines chopping answers into one-liners: 003 Q16–Q20 (52), also 005 (8), 006 (4), 008 (10), 009 (5)
- US month-day order in running Filipino: "Disyembre 27, 2023"
Gender of attorney Wei Tseng: feminine in the guidance pack (`Abogada Wei Tseng`, `Punong abogada`, `abogadang kwalipikado`, `Sinuri ni Abogada Wei Tseng`) and in the bylines of 001–004 and 007 (`Abogada Wei Tseng`). 003 closes `abogada sa Taiwan`. 006 offers `isang abogada o abogado` for any lawyer the reader brings. 008 opens `abogada` but then tells her own case as `sa tulong ng abogado`. Filipino `siya/niya` is epicene. Consultation lock stays negative in the FAQ, contact page, and llms notice (`lamang` + no interpreter, no reply-time promise, no free first meeting). 002 and 007 are the strongest legal prose. 006’s barbershop narrative is genuinely native. The Korea-as-example caveats in 001, 004, 005, 008, and 009 are in place; 005 Q2 still widens one Korea-only banking waiver.
