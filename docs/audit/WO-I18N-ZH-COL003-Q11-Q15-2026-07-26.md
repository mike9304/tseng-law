## Content-fidelity contract

### Q11 — Temporary income loss

- Require proof that an accident-related injury made work wholly or partly
  impossible during a supported treatment or recovery period and caused an
  actual income reduction.
- A diagnosis certificate or rest recommendation is important evidence but is
  not conclusive by itself.
- Mention useful evidence: treatment records, attendance or leave records,
  payroll and tax material, employer confirmation, and business-specific
  records for a self-employed claimant.
- Continuing to work or receiving unchanged pay is relevant to temporary
  income loss, but does not by itself decide Q12's separate lasting
  earning-capacity issue.

### Q12 — Loss of earning capacity

- Distinguish it from Q11's actual income reduction during recovery.
- Under Civil Code Articles 193 and 216, cover accident causation, lasting
  functional impairment, work and ability, ordinarily expected income,
  supported working life, and proof.
- Unchanged current pay does not automatically eliminate the claim; neither an
  impairment percentage nor current pay mechanically determines the award.
- A medical appraisal may be useful when lasting impairment is genuinely
  disputed, but is not mandatory in every case.
- Mention Civil Code Article 217 comparative fault and other adjustments.
- If a lump sum is calculated, intermediate-interest discounting may be
  considered. The Judicial Yuan Hoffman calculator is only an aid, not a
  mandatory method or result guarantee.
- Under Article 193, on a party's application, a court may order secured
  periodic payments.
- Do not use fixed retirement ages, salaries, impairment percentages, monthly
  payments, appraisal fees, or lump-sum examples.

### Q13 — Non-pecuniary damage

- Explain that Civil Code Article 195 permits an appropriate amount for
  qualifying unlawful infringement of body or health.
- Cover individualized factors such as injury and treatment, lasting effects,
  pain and life impact, age and status, social and economic circumstances, and
  the parties' evidence.
- Do not state a usual range, predict a lower-than-expected result, or
  discourage a particular amount without case-specific review.

### Q14 — Possible employer civil liability

- Civil Code Article 188 addresses an employee who unlawfully injures another
  while performing duties; work hours alone do not automatically establish
  the required duty connection.
- Explain possible joint civil liability, the employer's statutory
  reasonable-selection/supervision or unavoidable-loss defense, paragraph 2
  relief for an otherwise uncompensated victim, and employer recourse against
  the employee after payment.
- Distinguish civil party selection from criminal liability. Criminal Code
  Article 284 turns on each natural person's own breach of duty and causation.
- Do not recommend adding an employer because it may have more assets or use
  criminal-complaint terminology for a civil claim.

### Q15 — Compulsory and voluntary motor insurance

- Under Compulsory Automobile Liability Insurance Act Article 6, the duty
  ordinarily belongs to the vehicle owner and in specified cases to its user
  or manager; do not say everyone must buy it.
- Explain the basic no-fault benefit structure for motor-vehicle injury or
  death while noting the statutory passenger/outside-third-person scope.
- A driver in a single-vehicle accident is generally outside that vehicle's
  compulsory cover; in a multi-vehicle accident, a driver may claim against
  another involved vehicle's compulsory insurer.
- State that the benefit standard amended on `2026-05-29` applies to accidents
  on or after `2026-07-01`; earlier accidents may use the earlier standard.
- Use the exact current limits:
  - necessary and reasonable injury medical expense: `新臺幣 TWD 200,000`;
  - disability benefit by 15 statutory grades: `TWD 80,000–3,000,000`;
  - death benefit: `TWD 3,000,000`;
  - combined death/disability/medical maximum per victim per accident:
    `TWD 3,200,000`.
- Do not treat disappearance as a separate current category.
- Describe third-party liability, driver injury, and own-damage insurance only
  as contractual voluntary products. Actual cover depends on the insured,
  limit, deductible, exclusions, fault, and other policy conditions.
