A shareholder register and the bank statements behind last quarter's expenses do not fall under the same access right in every Taiwan company. Before sending another demand for “all financial records,” identify the company's legal form and the particular documents being withheld. Those details determine which statutory or contractual right to use.

Check who holds the investment as well. A US fund may have subscribed for shares in an offshore parent that owns the Taiwan operating company. The fund's name on the parent's capitalization table does not establish that it is the shareholder entitled to make a statutory request to the subsidiary. Start with the Taiwan entity's registration, ownership records and the completed transfer or subscription documents.

## Match the request to the Taiwan company form

Taiwan's Company Act distinguishes a limited company, 有限公司, from a company limited by shares, 股份有限公司. A small number of owners does not make the two forms interchangeable. Their direct shareholder inspection routes differ.

In a limited company, [Article 109](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0080001&flno=109), applying [Article 48](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0080001&flno=48), gives nonexecuting shareholders the right to inquire into operations and inspect property documents, books and statements. The Ministry of Economic Affairs' [official explanation](https://serv.gcis.nat.gov.tw/crm/faqAction.do?id=922&method=faqDetl) identifies these shareholders as those who are not directors. It also says the company should cooperate with necessary copying or transcription when they exercise that right.

This direct right does not require a 1% holding or six months of ownership. Article 109 also permits the qualifying shareholder to engage a lawyer or accountant on the company's behalf for the examination. The scope and handling of that engagement should be arranged expressly; inspection does not mean taking away the original books.

For a company limited by shares, [Article 210](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0080001&flno=210) identifies a different set of documents: the articles of incorporation, shareholders' meeting minutes, financial statements, shareholder register and corporate-bond counterfoils. A shareholder requesting inspection, transcription or copies must provide evidence of an interest and specify the scope. That route has no 1% or six-month threshold either, but its document list does not amount to unrestricted access to every invoice, bank statement, board minute or customer contract.

The distinction matters when management sends annual financial statements in response to a request for the entries supporting a related-party payment. The documents supplied may answer one statutory request without answering the underlying question. Identify the missing record and the applicable route before treating the response as complete or unlawful.

## Reopen the information-rights agreement

Monthly management accounts, a quarterly budget comparison or access for the investor's auditors may have been negotiated at closing. Read those provisions beside the statutory rights. Check who promised delivery, which investor can demand it, the reporting periods, delivery dates and any conditions tied to the investor's holding.

The agreement may bind the Taiwan company directly, or it may require a parent or founder to obtain information from the company. Those are different obligations. An offshore agreement's information clause should not be assumed to bind every subsidiary merely because its accounts are consolidated into the group's results.

Compare the executed agreement with side letters, amendments and any waiver. Identify whether the disputed item was promised as a routine report or only as inspection access. A clause offering access on notice does not necessarily promise that management will prepare a new analysis in the investor's preferred format. Conversely, sending a year-end statement may not satisfy an express quarterly reporting promise.

After closing, those same entity details help determine who can enforce a reporting obligation and against whom. Investment approval and proof of payment do not replace the executed governance documents.

## A director's access has its own purpose

If the investor has appointed a director, establish whether the request is being made by that director or by the shareholder. The Ministry's [director-access guidance](https://serv.gcis.nat.gov.tw/crm/faqAction.do?id=970&method=faqDetl) recognizes access connected with the reasonable needs of performing the director's duties, while requiring proper use and confidentiality. A director's request can therefore require a different assessment from an ordinary shareholder's demand under Article 210.

Before forwarding board material to a US investment committee, check the director's duties, the information-sharing arrangements and confidentiality restrictions. The nomination arrangement alone should not be treated as unlimited permission to circulate company records throughout a fund group.

## Make the missing documents identifiable

A written request can distinguish existing records from explanations the investor wants management to provide. State the requesting entity and its capacity, the records and period, the purpose or interest where required, and whether inspection or copies are sought. Propose a practical arrangement for access and specify which contractual clause or statutory provision supports each group of items.

Keep a record of what comes back. If twelve months were requested and only nine supplied, identify the missing months. If the company permits viewing but refuses copies, preserve that response separately from any refusal to disclose a document at all. Where it relies on confidentiality, ask it to identify the particular restriction and discuss an arrangement that permits lawful review.

Do not describe a date proposed in the request as a statutory response deadline. Article 210 does not give every records request a fixed number of days for compliance. A contractual reporting date is a different matter and should be identified as such.

## When a court-appointed inspector may be available

For a company limited by shares, [Article 245](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0080001&flno=245) allows a shareholder who has continuously held at least 1% of the issued shares for at least six months to apply for a court-appointed inspector. The application must give reasons, supporting evidence and an explanation of necessity. The inspector's examination is limited to the necessary scope and can cover business accounts, assets, particular matters and specific transaction documents and records.

Meeting the holding threshold permits an application; it does not guarantee appointment or an unrestricted review of the company's files. A useful application connects the particular concern with the records needed to investigate it. Preserve the information already available, any inconsistency it reveals, and the company's response to a focused request.

[Article 110](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0080001&flno=110) also applies Article 245's first paragraph to limited companies, with the necessary adaptations. That court route should be distinguished from a limited-company non-director shareholder's direct Article 109 rights. A recent investor who has not met the six-month condition should still have the other applicable statutory and contractual routes assessed.

Articles 109 and 210 also provide administrative penalties for specified obstruction or unjustified refusal, with Article 210 providing correction orders. Whether an administrative complaint or a court proceeding is appropriate depends on the request and evidence. Neither a complaint nor an inspection order automatically returns the investment or establishes a director's liability.
