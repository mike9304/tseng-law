# Can a Taiwan business refuse a qualified assistance dog?

A restaurant cannot rely solely on a no-pets policy to exclude a qualified guide dog or impose an assistance-dog surcharge. [Article 60 of the People with Disabilities Rights Protection Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0050046&flno=60) protects entry to public places, business premises and public transport, while prohibiting extra charges, refusals and additional entry conditions.

It covers people with visual, hearing or physical disabilities accompanied by qualified guide dogs, hearing dogs or mobility assistance dogs. Professional trainers are also covered when bringing young dogs during training.

## Refusal and separate entry conditions are both prohibited

The locations in Article 60(1) include public places, public buildings, business premises, public transport and other public facilities. The reach is broad. Under paragraph 2, owners, managers and users of those places cannot refuse free entry to qualified dogs or young dogs in training.

An extra pet fee is prohibited. So is a condition that a person accompanied by an assistance dog may use only a separately assigned area. Paragraph 2 contains no blanket exception based on a shop's no-pets sign or another customer's allergy.

Other people have duties as well. Article 60(3) prohibits arbitrarily touching or feeding any of the three kinds of assistance dogs while they guide a person with a visual, hearing or physical disability. Interference through sounds or gestures is also forbidden.

## Qualification turns on training and credentials

[Article 2 of the assistance-dog regulations](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0050065&flno=2) defines a qualified dog as one trained by a professional training organization accredited by the central competent authority and issued a qualified-dog work certificate. A young dog must be undergoing training with an accredited organization and hold a young-dog training certificate. A professional trainer needs a qualification document issued by an accredited organization.

Credentials set the boundary. Calling a dog an emotional support animal does not satisfy that definition. Owner training by itself is also insufficient.

How are overseas organizations treated? Article 2(4) includes the International Guide Dog Federation, Assistance Dogs International and training organizations they accredit within the scope of accredited training organizations. That does not make every foreign-issued document automatically equivalent to a Taiwanese qualified-dog work certificate. The organization and credential still need to fit Article 2.

## Users and trainers carry different documents

Under [Article 11](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0050065&flno=11), a person with a visual, hearing or physical disability must carry a user certificate and the qualified dog's work certificate. A professional trainer must carry the trainer qualification document and either a work certificate or a young-dog training certificate. The trainer must present those documents proactively.

Equipment is specified too. Guide dogs wear guide-dog harnesses. Hearing dogs and mobility assistance dogs wear vests. Young guide dogs wear a training vest or guide-dog harness, while the other two kinds of young dogs wear training vests.

Access carries management responsibilities. [Article 12](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0050065&flno=12) requires users and trainers to exercise due care for the safety of other people and property. [Article 13](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0050065&flno=13) requires users to maintain the qualified dog's bodily cleanliness, comply with epidemic-prevention, public-health and related rules, and accept health guidance from the competent authority for the relevant business and qualified veterinarians.

## Air travel and long-term housing involve further procedures

Public access under Article 60 does not resolve every situation involving an accompanying dog. Air travel requires a separate check of aviation rules and airline procedures. Entry into Taiwan requires an animal-quarantine check. Long-term rental housing should not be decided simply by analogy to public access; housing rules and the lease also matter.

## The fine follows failure to comply with an improvement order

[Article 100](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0050046&flno=100) begins with an order to correct an Article 60(2) violation within a specified period. A fine is not immediate. If the violation remains uncorrected after that deadline, the penalty is NT$10,000 to NT$50,000 and an order to attend four hours of training.

Records of the place, date and time of a refusal, the credentials shown and the operator's response can support a later review of the improvement process. The sanction analysis likewise separates the initial refusal from noncompliance after an improvement order.
