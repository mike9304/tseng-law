## Content-fidelity contract

### Q1 — Leaving the scene

- For an accident involving injury or death, state the duties to render
  immediate aid, notify police, and preserve the vehicle and scene evidence.
- Informal consent or a recording does not replace required measures.
- Distinguish moving a vehicle—with both parties' agreement and after marking
  its position and traces—to avoid obstructing traffic from leaving the
  scene.
- For property-only damage where the vehicle can move, mark and record the
  scene and promptly move to safety. Leaving without necessary measures can
  carry administrative consequences.
- Explain that Criminal Code Article 185-4 concerns a driver leaving after an
  accident causing injury or death and requires fact-specific analysis.

### Q2 — Evidence preservation

- Put personal safety and warning measures first.
- Mention `119` for injury or rescue, `110` or `112` for crime or urgent
  police situations, and an appropriate traffic-accident police report.
- After safety is secured, cover wide and close photographs of vehicle
  positions, damage, road markings, signals, and weather.
- Cover witness contacts, requests to preserve CCTV and dashcam footage,
  party/vehicle/insurance information, and medical records.
- State that private photographs do not replace required police handling for
  an injury or death accident.
- Accurately state police-document timing:
  - party registration/contact form at the scene;
  - scene diagram and photographs after seven days from the accident;
  - preliminary analysis report after thirty days;
  - confirm current availability and application requirements with the
    competent police agency.

### Q3 — Claims and deadlines

- Criminal Code Article 284 negligent injury and serious injury are
  complaint-based under Article 287. Under Code of Criminal Procedure Article
  237, the complaint period is generally six months from learning the
  offender's identity.
- Civil Code Article 197 uses two periods: two years from learning the damage
  and liable person, and ten years from the tort.
- Explain the criminal-attached civil action under Articles 487 and 488 and
  its filing window through the end of second-instance oral argument while
  the criminal matter is pending.
- This route ordinarily avoids a separate advance court-fee payment, but not
  every outcome is cost-free. Mention the Articles 503 and 504 transfer/cost
  caveats.
- End with case-specific factors: interruption of limitation, defendants,
  evidence, insurance, and jurisdiction. Do not state that one route is
  always best.

### Q4 — Mutual fault

- Criminal responsibility requires each person's breach of a duty of care and
  causation of the other party's injury. Mutual fault alone does not
  automatically establish negligent injury.
- Under Civil Code Article 217, a court may reduce or exempt damages when the
  injured person's fault contributed to the occurrence or expansion of
  damage.
- Preserve the example: accepted damage of `TWD 1,000,000` and 50% claimant
  fault yields `TWD 500,000` before other adjustments.
- An appraisal or preliminary analysis can be important evidence but does not
  mechanically bind the court.

### Q5 — Settlement scope

- Cover accident time/place and parties, payment amount/timing, insurance
  handling, included and reserved claims, future treatment or
  later-discovered injury, document delivery, and the relationship between
  payment and complaint withdrawal.
- Civil Code Articles 736 and 737: settlement is a mutual-concession contract,
  and extinguished rights depend on the scope relinquished in the agreement.
- Do not state that every future claim automatically disappears.
- For complaint-based offenses, explain Code of Criminal Procedure Article 238
  withdrawal before the end of first-instance oral argument and the bar on
  filing the complaint again after withdrawal.
- Private settlement does not automatically end prosecution for a
  non-complaint-based offense and does not compel complaint withdrawal in
  every case.
