# Taiwan–US R&D platform: what an A+ grant contract must cover

[CNA reported on November 6](https://www.cna.com.tw/news/afe/202611060202.aspx) that Taiwan's Industrial Technology Research Institute, Northeastern University and MTM Venture Partners had signed a cooperation letter of intent. The platform aims to connect Taiwan companies and US startups for joint research and commercialization. An approved Taiwan applicant must sign a grant contract addressing payments and research results under [the existing subsidy rules](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0030115&flno=15), with the forthcoming call terms still to be confirmed.

The [Ministry of Economic Affairs announcement](https://www.moea.gov.tw/Mns/Populace/news/News.aspx?kind=1&menu_id=40&news_id=124492) schedules the first call for November 17, 2026 through March 17, 2027. Taiwan companies may seek support through the A+ international R&D cooperation program, with Taiwan funding capped at 50% of total R&D expenditure. Selected projects are expected to begin in the third quarter of 2027. Further application rules will be announced; the launch is not an individual funding award.

## Ownership needs its own agreement

The currently posted [A+ international-cooperation guidance, dated August 2025](https://service.moea.gov.tw/EE514/wSite/public/Attachment/00106/f1754385253273.pdf), says that ownership of research results follows the IP agreement in the partners' contract. That is the existing program framework. It should be read with the forthcoming Taiwan–US call terms, rather than treated as the complete rules for a call that has yet to open.

Taiwan's subsidy regulations also require a contract. [Article 15](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0030115&flno=15) requires the grant agreement to address ownership of R&D results, the project and its duration, progress milestones, payment conditions and proportions, expenditure accounting, and grounds for ending the agreement. The grant contract and the partners' collaboration contract therefore need to be considered together. A funding percentage does not supply an ownership percentage.

For a US startup bringing an existing design to a Taiwan manufacturer's development project, an agreement can distinguish that design from improvements created during the collaboration. It can also address whether each party receives ownership or permission to use particular results. The parties would need to negotiate those terms. A promise of commercial access needs enough detail to show what technology it covers and who may use it.

## Funding comes with continuing obligations

Even an approved Taiwan applicant still has a signing deadline. Article 15 provides that approval lapses if the grant contract is not signed within the period specified in the approval letter, unless an extension is approved. The extension cannot exceed one month. Approval and payment are separate steps.

Project accounting continues after signing. Under [Article 16](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0030115&flno=16), the recipient must use a dedicated subsidy account and separate records. The ministry or its authorized institution may inspect project progress and supporting expenditure documents. The recipient cannot refuse those inspections and must supply the required reports and explanations.

An agreed development schedule also affects funding. [Article 17](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0030115&flno=17) allows the agency, under the grant contract, to stop the next payment and recover amounts due where serious delay remains uncorrected after the specified period. It does not make every missed milestone an automatic demand for full repayment.

That distinction matters when a US startup promises prototype delivery or access to technology on the assumption that its Taiwan partner will receive a particular subsidy installment. The collaboration agreement can address how the parties handle a funding interruption, while the grant agreement determines the recipient's obligations to the funding authority. Ownership, permitted use and the payment schedule belong in that review before the parties rely on them to build a product together.
