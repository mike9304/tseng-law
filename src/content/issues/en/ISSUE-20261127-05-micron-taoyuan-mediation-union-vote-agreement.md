# Micron's Taiwan mediation continues. When does a union proposal become an agreement?

December 4 is the next scheduled session in Micron's Taoyuan labor mediation. The union agreed to send the company its own proposal by December 2 after a November 26 meeting, according to [Liberty Times' report of the city's Labor Department statement](https://ec.ltn.com.tw/amp/article/breakingnews/5654712). [United Daily News reported](https://udn.com/news/story/7240/9904382) that the parties left without agreement. No settlement was reported. For a US employer operating in Taiwan, the status of a proposed bonus or other labor term depends on the documents and approvals that follow.

Earlier talks involved the structure of a proposed collective agreement. At the November 17 meeting, the sides disagreed over terms including dispute restrictions and the agreement's duration, [Liberty Times reported](https://ec.ltn.com.tw/amp/article/breakingnews/5644163). The union was to put the company's proposal to its members. [The union later reported that 1,567 of the 1,852 members who voted rejected that proposal](https://www.civilmedia.tw/archives/142293). At the November 26 session, the union agreed to submit its own proposal on December 2, and the next mediation session was scheduled for December 4. Those reports still do not supply a signed agreement or a completed mediation record for the Taoyuan dispute.

## A proposal for the next meeting is still a proposal

[Article 19 of Taiwan's Act for Settlement of Labor-Management Disputes](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0020007&flno=19) specifies when a mediation committee's proposal becomes a settlement: both parties must consent and sign the mediation record. [When a union is a party, Article 23 treats that settlement as a collective agreement between the parties](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0020007&flno=23). Sending new text on December 2 says nothing about consent to that text by the company. The scheduled meeting does not establish a settlement.

What if members approve one version before the parties meet? Their vote may guide the union's negotiators. It does not substitute for the other party's acceptance or the statutory mediation record. The actual vote question, union authorization and the final text would have to be compared before describing any outcome as settled.

## A collective agreement has its own approval step

[Article 9 of the Collective Agreement Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0020006&flno=9) governs internal approval when a union signs a separately negotiated written agreement. It addresses a union or employer association signing a collective agreement. Unless its charter sets the procedure, more than half of the members or representatives must attend a meeting and at least two thirds of those attending must approve. Alternatively, after notifying all members, at least three quarters of all members must consent in writing. An agreement signed without the required procedure has no effect until it is ratified. A poll reported in the news cannot be assumed to satisfy Article 9 without knowing which approval route the union used and what text was approved.

Bonuses are one permitted subject. [Article 12](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0020006&flno=12) also permits collective agreements to cover wages, allowances and procedures for resolving disputes. The legal effect of any Micron proposal will therefore turn on its actual terms and the route by which the parties adopt them. A public description of “progress” cannot answer whether a particular worker's bonus has changed.

For an employer group coordinating decisions across borders, the immediate records to preserve are the version sent to the union, the union's response, any member-approval record, and the signed mediation or agreement text if one is eventually made. As of November 27, the cited reports establish a further meeting and an upcoming union proposal, not a final Taoyuan settlement. Rumors about closing the plant or dismissing staff are not a basis for this analysis; [the city said it had no confirmed company decision of that kind](https://newtalk.tw/news/view/2026-11-26/1073962).
