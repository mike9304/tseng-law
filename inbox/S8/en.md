# Severance and Retirement Pay When You Leave a Taiwan Job: The 2026 Tax-Free Amounts and the 183-Day Line

How much of a Taiwan severance check does the tax office see? Two numbers decide it. One is your years of service. The other is how many days you spent in Taiwan in the year you leave.

## What counts as retirement income

Class 9 of [Article 14(1) of the Income Tax Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0340003&flno=14) treats pensions, severance pay (資遣費), retirement pay, separation pay, lifetime annuities and non-insurance old-age payments as retirement income. Contributions you made from your own taxed salary are left out. A payment your employer labels a "goodwill" or "transition" sum may still fall within the list if you received it because you left.

The thresholds move with consumer prices, and the Ministry of Finance announces them each year (Article 14(4)). The 2026 figures were published on November 27, 2025.

## The 2026 amounts

Lump sums are split into three slices:

- up to NT$206,000 × years of service: zero income;
- above that, but below NT$414,000 × years of service: half counts as income;
- above NT$414,000 × years of service: all of it counts.

Part-years under six months count as half a year; six months or more counts as a full year. Payments by instalment are taxed on the year's total minus NT$894,000.

An illustration with invented figures: six years of service, and NT$2,000,000 of severance and retirement pay in one payment. The first NT$1,236,000 is zero income. The total sits below NT$2,484,000, so half of the remaining NT$764,000, or NT$382,000, is retirement income.

## Why the month you leave matters

Residence is a matter of days. A foreigner with no domicile in Taiwan is a resident for a tax year only after spending 183 days or more here in that year ([Article 7](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0340003&flno=7)). The same severance is handled differently on each side of that line.

For a resident, the employer withholds 6% of the payment after deducting the tax-free amount ([Article 2 of the Standards of Withholding Rates for Various Incomes](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0340028&flno=2)), unless the tax to be withheld on a payment is NT$2,000 or less, in which case nothing is withheld ([Article 13](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0340028&flno=13)). Skipping withholding does not make the income tax-free. Withholding is only a prepayment. The retirement income joins the year's other income and is taxed at progressive rates, after exemptions and deductions. In 2026 those rates run in five steps from 5% to 40%, with the 5% band covering net income up to NT$610,000. The same announcement sets the annual personal exemption at NT$101,000, the annual standard deduction for a single filer at NT$136,000 and the special salary deduction at NT$227,000. The exemption and the standard deduction come off net income as a whole. The salary deduction is taken only in calculating salary income and cannot push it below zero (Article 14(1), Class 3), so it does not reduce retirement income. A resident who gives up a domicile or residence in Taiwan and leaves during the year must file for that year before departure ([Article 71-1(2)](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0340003&flno=71-1)). On that departure return, the exemption and the standard deduction are prorated by the days resident in Taiwan ([Article 17-1](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0340003&flno=17-1)). If the spouse is a resident who stays on in Taiwan, the spouse files the joint return under Article 71 instead; Article 17-1 is written for returns filed under Article 71-1.

Non-residents get no such settlement. [Article 3 of the Standards](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0340028&flno=3) applies 18% to the payment after the tax-free amount, and under [Article 73 of the Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0340003&flno=73) that withholding replaces an annual return. Lower brackets and deductions are out of reach, because there is no annual progressive-rate settlement. [Article 3 of the Withholding Standards](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0340028&flno=3) also sets 18% for a non-resident's salary, except that a monthly salary of no more than 1.5 times the monthly basic wage approved by the Executive Yuan is withheld at 6%.

Leave in May, fly home in June, and you may well end the year short of 183 days. That is what decides whether your employer withholds 18% or 6%.

## When the withholding statement is issued

Severance and retirement pay are among the payments an employer must withhold on when paying ([Article 88(1)(2)](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0340003&flno=88)). For a non-resident, the employer must pay the tax within ten days of withholding and have the withholding statement (扣繳憑單) verified by the tax office before handing it to you ([Article 92(2)](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0340003&flno=92)). Three or more consecutive public holidays in that window add five days. For a resident, the employer files the statements with the tax office by the end of January and issues them by February 10 of the following year, or by February 5 and February 15 if January has three or more consecutive public holidays (Article 92(1)). An employer that filed on time may skip handing the statement to a resident when the data are in the tax office's filing-season income lookup, but must still issue it if the employee asks ([Article 94-1](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0340003&flno=94-1)). How your home country taxes the payment, and whether a tax treaty or agreement applies, is a separate question that this column does not answer. Either way you will want proof of the tax paid in Taiwan, and it is far easier to collect while you still have a desk and a badge.
