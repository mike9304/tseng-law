## Verdict
naturalness (1 = machine, 5 = native professional): n/a for guidance pack (part a: 3/5), 2/5 for columns 010–015
variety used: Bokmål intended; 010–013 and 015 are stiff comma-heavy Bokmål (German *Sie* → capital **De**). 014 mixes Danish into the Norwegian surface (`Efter`, `rimeligt`, `nøjere`, `Grænsen`, `stk.` in 011, relative *der*). Not a Bokmål/Nynorsk mix. Register splits: 013/015 are first-person Korean blog copy (emoji, `Eks.)`, `Q.`); 011/014 are German-pivoted statute commentary; 012 is the one file a native could almost publish.
systemic patterns (max 6, each one line, with 1 example quote):
- Capital **De / Deres / Dem** throughout (German *Sie*); 2026 Norwegian sites use **du**. "Har De pådratt Dem en skade i et treningssenter"
- Danish leakage starting in 011 (*stk.*) and flooding 014 (`Efter bekreftelse`, `rimeligt omfang`, `nøjere`).
- German thousands `.` (`1.579.589 TWD`, `TWD 40.000`) instead of space (`1 579 589 TWD`).
- FAQ answers pasted as body openings: sections start with "Nei." / "Det er ikke den samme prosedyren." with no question above them (011, 014).
- Digit-style legalese (`1. instans`, `tredjemann (3)`, `2 barn` later) instead of spelled forms.
- 013/015 still read as a Korean blog (😊, "legg igjen en kommentar", `Q.`), while 011/014 read as a German statute commentary.

Gender: Wei Tseng is correctly **jeg** (010, 013) / ungendered **advokat**. No masculine form, pronoun, or title for her. "han" in 012 is motorcyclist A, not the attorney.

012 (overtaking) is genuinely the strongest file in this slice: clear structure, working anti-guarantee close, no Korea-reader leftover, no `/ko/` links. 014 is already the weakest: *tegningspremie* and *erstatningsmulighet* would mislead a Norwegian labour lawyer.
