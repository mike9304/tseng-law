## Six exact FAQ answers

Each answer must occur exactly twice in the raw source: once in frontmatter and
once, character-for-character, as the first paragraph of its assigned H2.

### FAQ 1 — H2 2

Under Civil Code Article 1050, a Taiwan mutual-consent divorce is effective only when three requirements are met together: the divorce is in writing; at least two witnesses who personally perceived and confirmed both spouses’ genuine mutual intent to divorce sign the instrument; and the divorce is registered with the household-registration authority. A signed private agreement alone does not complete the divorce, because registration is constitutive for this path. Where foreign elements are involved, parties must also examine applicable law, authentication and translation of documents, and any registration or recognition steps required in another jurisdiction. Always check the current official household-registration guide and the competent office for filing details.

### FAQ 2 — H2 3

No. When a court orders a party or legal representative to appear in person and that person fails to appear without just cause, Family Act Article 13 applies Civil Procedure Code Article 303 mutatis mutandis. The first fine for unjustified nonappearance may be up to NTD 30,000, and repeated sanctions may follow after further lawful notice and another unjustified failure to appear. Compulsory appearance by arrest is not available. These rules do not mean both spouses must always sit together in the same room. Whether remote participation, separate or safety arrangements, or representation is permitted depends on the court’s decision under law and the circumstances of the case.

### FAQ 3 — H2 4

Civil Code Article 1052 paragraph 2 allows a petition where another serious cause makes continuation of the marriage difficult, but its proviso provides that if that cause is attributable to one spouse, only the other spouse may petition. That proviso remains in the current statutory text. Constitutional Court Judgment 112-Hsien-Pan-4 did not repeal it. The Court held the restriction is generally constitutional, yet unconstitutional to the extent that, without considering whether a serious cause arose or continued for a considerable period, it completely deprives the solely responsible spouse of any opportunity to divorce and is manifestly harsh. Outcome turns on the court’s application of that reasoning to the facts, not on an absolute bar or an automatic right to divorce.

### FAQ 4 — H2 6

No single factor is conclusive. Registered title, beneficial-ownership or nominee-registration theories, source-of-funds evidence such as premarital savings used for a down payment or loan installments, gifts, loans, reimbursement or other claims, and the separate residual-property calculation under Civil Code Article 1030-1 raise different legal questions. Title in one spouse’s name does not by itself defeat every ownership, contractual, or reimbursement claim, and premarital funding does not by itself transfer title or fix the residual-property result. Parties must examine the real agreement, acquisition timing and cause, fund flows, debts, gratuitous acquisitions, and supporting records. Ownership issues and the Article 1030-1 equal-difference calculation after exclusions and possible court adjustment remain distinct inquiries.

### FAQ 5 — H2 7

No. Residual-property distribution under Civil Code Article 1030-1, divorce damages under Article 1056, post-divorce support under Article 1057, ongoing child support, claims arising from unmarried cohabitation that are not divorce rights, and tort or other claims against a third party are separate bases with different elements, proof, and limitation analysis. Only the Article 1030-1 residual-property claim is extinguished if not exercised within two years from knowledge of the residual-property difference and, in any event, within five years from termination of the statutory regime. That two-year and five-year rule must not be treated as a universal period for damages, post-divorce support, child support, ownership, or third-party claims. Each right’s accrual, knowledge, and limitation rule must be checked on its own terms.

### FAQ 6 — H2 8

Under Civil Code Articles 1055 and 1055-1, arrangements for the exercise and assumption of rights and duties regarding a minor child, and related contact or visitation, are governed by the child’s best interests. Parents may agree, but the court may decide or change an arrangement when agreement is absent, fails, or is adverse to the child. The court considers all relevant circumstances, including continuity and safety, care history, each parent’s capacity and willingness, whether a parent facilitates the child’s relationship with the other parent, the child’s views where appropriate, and reports or information from competent authorities or child-welfare professionals as the law allows. Marital fault is not a basis for punishment, and no automatic income-based or fault-based rule decides the result.

### Divorce paths, foreign elements, and registration

- Explain mutual-consent divorce, divorce established through court mediation
  or settlement, and judicial divorce separately.
- For a foreign-element matter, examine Taiwan jurisdiction or authority,
  applicable law, the recognition or effect in Taiwan of a foreign status act
  or judgment, Taiwan household registration, and any procedure required in
  another jurisdiction as distinct questions.
- Under Article 1050, writing, signatures by at least two witnesses who
  personally perceived and confirmed both spouses' genuine mutual intent, and
  household registration are separate requirements. A private agreement alone
  does not make this divorce path effective.
- The Household Registration Act Article 48 general application period is
  thirty days from finality of the Taiwan divorce judgment or establishment of
  the court mediation or settlement. A late application must still be
  accepted. If no one applies after written demand, the office registers
  directly when Article 48-2 applies. The period for the online channel is not
  the general validity rule, and lateness does not undo an effective divorce.

### Court procedure and Article 1052

- Family matters ordinarily proceed through court mediation first, without
  claiming every matter follows one immutable process.
- Family Act Article 13 applies only when the court orders personal appearance.
  Civil Procedure Code Article 303 applies mutatis mutandis: the first fine is
  at most NTD 30,000, arrest is unavailable on that basis, and repeated lawful
  notice followed by unjustified nonappearance can lead to repeated sanctions.
- Distinguish the effects of court mediation or settlement, final judgment,
  and the available review or appeal route and period according to document
  type, service, finality, and procedural posture. Promise no fixed duration.
- List Civil Code Article 1052 paragraph 1's ten grounds accurately:
  1. bigamy;
  2. consensual sexual intercourse with a person other than the spouse;
  3. unbearable abuse by one spouse against the other;
  4. abuse by one spouse against the other spouse's lineal relative, or by one
     spouse's lineal relative against the other spouse, making common life
     unbearable;
  5. malicious desertion of the other spouse in a continuing state;
  6. an attempt by one spouse to kill the other;
  7. an incurable serious disease, while identifying the statute's archaic
     wording and avoiding any moral or character judgment;
  8. a serious incurable mental illness;
  9. life or death unknown for more than three years; and
  10. a final sentence of imprisonment for more than six months for an
      intentional offense.
- Separate the ten grounds from paragraph 2's other serious cause. A
  missing-person report and a prior action demanding cohabitation are not
  universal statutory prerequisites; several months away from home is not
  itself a divorce ground.
- Article 1052 paragraph 2's proviso remains in the statute. Judgment
  112-Hsien-Pan-4 neither deletes it nor creates an automatic yes or no answer
  for a responsible spouse.

### Property, damages, support, and children

- Separate title and ownership of a specific asset; gift, nominee-registration,
  loan, trust, unjust-enrichment, reimbursement, and related claims; and the
  residual-property difference calculation.
- Article 1017 classification and presumptions concerning premarital and
  marital property are not shortcuts to title or a complete case outcome.
- Under Article 1030-1, explain the net residual-property difference on
  termination of the statutory regime, the general equal division of the
  difference, exclusion of inherited and other gratuitously acquired property
  and solatium, and judicial adjustment or waiver where equal division would be
  manifestly unfair.
- Apply Article 1030-1's two years from knowledge and five years from
  termination only to that claim, not to Articles 1056 or 1057, child support,
  ownership, or another claim.
- Distinguish Article 1056 damages upon judicial divorce, Article 1057
  post-divorce support for a non-fault spouse placed in hardship by judicial
  divorce, and Article 1116-2 continuing parental support of a minor child.
  Average consumption expenditure is not a binding formula.
- Unmarried cohabitation does not itself create divorce rights. Analyze actual
  co-ownership, loans, contracts, nominee registration, trusts, unjust
  enrichment, and torts separately. In-laws and third parties do not
  automatically owe Article 1057 support or divorce damages.
- Apply Articles 1055 and 1055-1 to rights and duties concerning a minor child
  and contact or visitation under the child's best interests. `Custody` may be
  used only as an explained shorthand, not as a substitute for the complete
  legal concept.
- A child-support modification request requires the child's current needs, the
  parents' resources and circumstances, the existing agreement or order, and
  the child's best interests; unforeseeability is not the sole threshold.
- If contact is obstructed, the existing instrument and facts determine whether
  a determination, change, enforcement, or interim measure is available.
  Family Act Article 194 methods remain governed by the child's best interests;
  do not promise immediate handover, force, a change, or punishment.
- For cross-border relocation, separate authority over residence and travel,
  consent or court order, best interests, passport and immigration rules,
  recognition and enforcement, actual expenses and both parents' resources,
  and urgent protection. Do not state that the 1980 Hague Child Abduction
  Convention automatically governs Taiwan.
