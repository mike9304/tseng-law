# How Can You Recover Capital When You No Longer Want to Operate a Company Established in Taiwan?

![Featured image](../images/002-withdraw-capital-taiwan-company/featured-01.png)

Many investors ask me:

If I no longer want to operate a company established in Taiwan,

can I remit the capital I invested at the time directly back to Korea?

​

**The answer: No. Only after completing the company “dissolution” (解散) and “liquidation” (清算) procedures may shareholders recover the company’s remaining assets (capital).**

![](../images/002-withdraw-capital-taiwan-company/img-01.png)

If company funds are diverted directly, the person may be sentenced to imprisonment or detention for up to 5 years, or a fine of not less than TWD 500,000 and not more than TWD 2.5 million. (Company Act Article 9)

If the liquidator distributes company property to shareholders before first repaying company debts, the liquidator may be sentenced to imprisonment or detention for up to 1 year and a fine of up to TWD 60,000. (Company Act Article 90)

In addition, the criminal offense of breach of trust (背信罪) may also apply.

The National Taxation Bureau may also impose additional tax and fines.

Therefore, to recover company investment funds, you must go through “dissolution” and “liquidation” procedures.

Statutory procedures for dissolution and liquidation:

**​**

**1. Dissolution (解散)**
(1) Limited company: consent of at least 2/3 of shareholders’ voting rights (Company Act Article 113, Paragraph 1)

Company limited by shares: attendance by shareholders representing at least 2/3 of total issued shares, and consent by a majority of the voting rights of attending shareholders (Company Act Article 316)

(2) Within 15 days, submit the “minutes of the shareholders’ meeting resolution” (股東會決議議事錄) and the “company dissolution application” (公司解散申請書) to the competent authority to apply for dissolution and cancel business registration (Company Registration Act Article 4)

(3) After dissolution, complete various tax filings within the prescribed deadlines

**2. Liquidation (清算)**
(1) Report the liquidator’s assumption of office to the court

(2) Carry out liquidation procedures

(3) Report completion of liquidation to the court

Only after the above procedures are completed may shareholders recover the company’s remaining assets (capital)!

​

Additional Q&A

**Q1: If the company’s liabilities exceed its assets, do you still dissolve and then liquidate?**

Only when the company’s assets exceed its liabilities do you proceed with “dissolution” followed by “liquidation.”

If assets are less than liabilities, and two conditions are met—there is property to liquidate and there are multiple creditors—you must apply to the court for “bankruptcy” (破產).

​

**Q2: What if I do not want to dissolve and liquidate the company, but still want to recover my investment?**

You may carry out a “capital reduction” (減資) procedure.

**​**

**Q3: If I no longer want to operate the company and there is no capital left either, can I just leave it and skip dissolution and liquidation?**

Even if the company has no capital, you must still carry out dissolution and liquidation procedures under the Company Act and related regulations.

That is because, unless the company is dissolved and liquidated, it must still file tax returns as required by law (business tax, income tax, etc.).

If filing deadlines are missed, fines will be imposed, and continued non-payment may result in business suspension.

Therefore,
if you want to wait and observe for a while before deciding whether to continue operations later,

you may apply for “suspension of business” (停業) with the National Taxation Bureau and the Ministry of Economic Affairs. (One year per application; renewable upon expiry)

Then you will not need to file tax returns for the next period.

(However, if the company holds vehicles, real estate, or similar assets, related vehicle tax and building tax must still be paid.)

​

If it is certain that the company will never operate again,

you must still complete lawful dissolution and liquidation procedures.
