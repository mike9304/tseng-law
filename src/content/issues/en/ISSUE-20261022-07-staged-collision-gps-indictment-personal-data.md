# Five people indicted over an alleged staged scooter crash in New Taipei

Someone pretending to be an insurer, prosecutors say, obtained the medical certificate from the scooter crash, saying they would help with a claim. An electronic copy then went to the overseas contact through a messaging app. The balance stayed unpaid.

That sequence is what [Focus Taiwan reported on October 21, 2026](https://focustaiwan.tw/society/202610210019), from New Taipei prosecutors, about an alleged attack on a Taiwanese comedian. Prosecutors indicted five people the same day. They described the case as alleged cross-border repression meant to silence political views. None of that is a court's finding. [Code of Criminal Procedure Article 154](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0010001&flno=154) presumes a defendant innocent until a judgment proves guilt and that judgment becomes final. Criminal facts have to be proved by evidence.

## What the indictment account says happened

Around 4:54 p.m. on September 11, Focus Taiwan reported, a vehicle carrying two people hit the comedian as he rode his scooter home in Yonghe, New Taipei. He was 44. Prosecutors said he suffered a contusion to his right pelvis. CCTV, in the prosecutors' account, showed the two men in the vehicle and a third man waiting at an alley entrance from 7:31 that morning.

Prosecutors said the driver, a passenger who filmed from the front seat, and the man who signaled when the comedian approached had been hired for the attack. A fourth man planned the "accident" with the person whom an overseas contact, identified only as K, had engaged. That person, prosecutors said, tracked the comedian's movements and routine from July 25 through August with surveillance and a GPS tracker placed on the scooter.

K is a Chinese national, prosecutors said, and the rest of K's background is still under investigation. K had promised NT$450,000 to break the comedian's arms and legs in a traffic collision so he could not perform on September 20. Focus Taiwan converted that sum to about US$13,870. NT$250,000 was paid up front. The rest was not paid after the group sent K an electronic copy of the medical certificate. Prosecutors said the certificate was obtained by pretending to be an insurance company helping with a claim, and that K was unhappy the injuries were only minor.

Investigators from the New Taipei prosecutors office, the police, and the Taipei City Field Office of the Ministry of Justice Investigation Bureau searched eight places in three operations since October 6. Prosecutors said the five suspects have been detained with a court's approval. Detention is a court order about custody. It is not a conviction.

## The charge is an attempt, and the injury on the chart is a contusion

[Criminal Code Article 278](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0000001&flno=278) punishes causing serious injury with imprisonment of at least five years and at most twelve. If that offense causes death, the punishment is life imprisonment or at least ten years. Paragraph 3 punishes an attempt of the paragraph 1 offense.

[Article 25](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0000001&flno=25) defines an attempt as a person who has begun to carry out the offense and does not complete it. Attempt is punished only where a statute says so, and the court may reduce the punishment below the completed offense. Prosecutors are treating this as an attempt. The bodily result in the prosecutors' account, as Focus Taiwan reported it, is a pelvic contusion, while the sum they say was promised was for breaking arms and legs. A contusion and a completed serious injury are not the same thing on this record. Whether the evidence proves that anyone began a serious-injury offense, and with what intent, is for the trial.

The prison terms Focus Taiwan listed are requests, not judgments. Prosecutors are seeking more than five years and six months for the person they say K engaged to plan the attack. More than five years for the person who planned the collision with him. Four years for the driver. Three years and six months for the man who waited and signaled. Two years and seven months for the passenger who filmed. The person K engaged, who prosecutors say did the tracking, also faces the recording charge described below.

## A certificate is medical information

[Personal Data Protection Act Article 2](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=I0050021&flno=2) defines personal data to include a natural person's medical records, medical treatment, genetic data, health examinations, and other data that can directly or indirectly identify the person. Collection means obtaining personal data by any method.

Medical records, medical treatment, genetics, sexual life, health examinations, and criminal records may not be collected, processed, or used unless an exception applies. [Article 6](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=I0050021&flno=6) is the provision that says so. The exceptions are narrow: a statute, a statutory duty within what is necessary, lawfully public data, de-identified research, help with a statutory duty, and written consent within limits.

A real insurer, with a real claim and a real consent, can sit inside those exceptions. Someone who only claims to be the insurer does not fit any of them just by saying so. Focus Taiwan said prosecutors charged the five with attempted serious injury and with illegally collecting and using personal data. The English report does not quote the indictment's article number for the data charge. Whether the certificate was collected unlawfully is for the court to decide on the evidence.

## The tracker and the recording charge are both in the story

[Criminal Code Article 315-1](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0000001&flno=315-1) punishes two kinds of conduct. The ceiling is three years' imprisonment, short-term imprisonment, or a fine of up to NT$300,000. One is using a tool or device, without cause, to watch or eavesdrop on another person's non-public activity, speech, conversation, or a private part of the body. The other is secretly recording those same things, without cause, by audio, photograph, video, or an electromagnetic record.

Focus Taiwan said the person who tracked the comedian also faces a charge of illegally recording private activity with an electronic device. It separately said the tracking used surveillance and a GPS unit on the scooter. Article 315-1 is about non-public activity, speech, conversation, or bodily privacy, not about every device that reports a location. Taiwan's Supreme Court has addressed trackers directly. In [judgment 106 Tai-Shang 3788 of November 30, 2017](https://judgment.judicial.gov.tw/FJUD/data.aspx?ty=JD&id=TPSM%2c106%2c%e5%8f%b0%e4%b8%8a%2c3788%2c20171130&ot=in), it treated the position, direction, speed and stopping time a tracker collects as an electromagnetic record that can amount to secret recording under Article 315-1, item 2. That judgment dismissed a prosecutor's appeal in a different case, so it does not decide the September collision. Focus Taiwan does not say which item of Article 315-1 the indictment cites, or how prosecutors connect the tracker to that charge; that link is for the court to examine on the evidence. An Article 315-1 offense is prosecuted only on complaint ([Article 319](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0000001&flno=319)), and the complaint must be filed within six months of learning who the offender is ([Code of Criminal Procedure Article 237](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0010001&flno=237), paragraph 1).

Prosecutors also tied this indictment, in Focus Taiwan's account, to the way Taipei prosecutors had characterized an earlier alleged attack at a venue where the comedian was due to perform. Focus Taiwan describes that earlier case, against other people, only at the indictment stage, and it does not prove the September collision.

## Custody, trial, and a person who does not follow the questions

Five people are in detention by court order, prosecutors said. No judgment has been entered, so there is no appellate deadline running on a sentence. If a district court rules later, that judgment can be appealed, and Article 154 still treats the defendants as innocent until a guilty judgment is final. The terms prosecutors requested can be rejected, cut, or increased inside what the statutes allow. Article 25 allows a reduction for an attempt where attempt is punishable.

[Article 99 of the Code of Criminal Procedure](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0010001&flno=99) requires an interpreter when a defendant has a hearing impairment or a speech impairment, or cannot communicate in the language being used. When needed, questioning may be in writing, or the person may be told to answer in writing. The same rule applies to other people being questioned, unless another law says otherwise. A witness who cannot follow the language is covered by the second paragraph. A person questioned by police as a suspect is covered through [Article 100-2](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0010001&flno=100-2), which applies the same chapter, including Article 99, to police questioning. An American resident who cannot follow the questions is covered whether questioned as a defendant, a witness, or a police suspect.

Where could this touch an English-speaking resident? A person whose scooter picked up a device they did not install. A person asked to hand a clinic certificate to a caller. A person told to wait at an alley and send a message when someone rides past. The public story is an allegation about hired roles. Agreeing to one of those roles can be charged if the evidence supports it.
