# Traveling with one parent or relatives: consent documents for minors

Taiwan's airport inspection rules do not create a blanket requirement for every minor to produce notarized consent from an absent parent. The border officer checks travel documents and departure restrictions. A destination's parental-consent rules belong to another stage.

## Passport consent is not the journey's consent letter

The starting rule is Article 16 of the [Passport Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=E0030001&flno=16), which sets the application method by age. Written consent from a legal representative is required for minors aged seven or older. A representative applies for a child under seven. If two or more representatives exist, any one of them may consent or apply. The article does not require both parents' consent for every passport application.

Signature and identification rules come next. Article 7 of the [Enforcement Rules of the Passport Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=E0030004&flno=7) addresses who qualifies as a legal representative, identification and the application signature. Article 13 of the [passport application and issuance regulations](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=E0030016&flno=13) covers accompaniment when an applicant under 14 makes a first ordinary-passport application in Taiwan or completes identity verification. A legal representative, lineal blood ascendant or collateral blood relative within the third degree may accompany the applicant, with relationship records. Special agency arrangements exist when those people cannot accompany the applicant.

That accompaniment concerns the passport counter. It does not authorize a trip with grandparents or a school group. An existing passport does not answer the remaining travel-document questions either.

## Taiwan departure inspection has its own basic documents

Border inspection begins with Article 4 of the [Immigration Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0080132&flno=4), which requires inspection by the National Immigration Agency. Under [Article 5](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0080132&flno=5)(1), nationals with household registration in Taiwan generally do not apply for entry or departure permission, subject to the national-security exception.

A valid passport is central to Article 2 of the [entry and exit inspection regulations](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0080140&flno=2), which requires a registered national leaving Taiwan to pass inspection and be free of a departure prohibition. Those basic provisions do not establish a universal rule that every minor must submit notarized consent from the other, non-traveling parent.

It would be equally wrong to conclude that consent is never needed. Destination immigration or visa rules, transit requirements and the exit law of another nationality held by the child may apply. Completing check-in in Taiwan does not settle the later stages.

## Destination, transit and airline checks can differ

For a visa-required journey, ask the relevant overseas mission whether the application needs a parent's or legal representative's signature, birth or relationship evidence, a translation, notarization, or any particular form of consent for the adult who will actually accompany the child. Visa-free admission does not itself eliminate documentary rules for a traveling minor. One form may not cover the itinerary.

Include every transit point in the review. Even an airside connection may involve entry inspection, transit permission or minor-travel documents, depending on the airport and itinerary. A form accepted for travel with one parent may not fit a journey with relatives or a group leader.

Airlines inspect destination and transit documents before boarding. Tour operators and school groups may also collect consent or authorization forms. Those requests should not be confused with a Taiwan departure statute imposing one form on every minor.

For the actual itinerary, compare the companion's relationship evidence, travel schedule, parental contact details and whether consent needs an original, translation or notarization. This is practical document coordination, not a single statutory checklist for all children.

## Parental authority and court restrictions remain separate questions

Parental responsibility comes from Article 1089 of the [Civil Code](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=1089), which generally gives parents joint responsibility for the rights and duties concerning a minor child. If they disagree over a major matter, either may ask a court to decide in the child's best interests. That provision does not automatically become a uniform notarized-letter requirement at the airport.

A dispute or a need to stop a departure calls for more than a private consent letter. Under Article 85 of the [Family Proceedings Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010048&flno=85), a court that has accepted a family non-contentious case may, before the ruling becomes final, order a necessary provisional disposition, subject to the Act's conditions. In the parent-child proceedings specified in [Article 7(1)(4) of the implementing regulations](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010056&flno=7), this may include prohibiting a person from taking a minor child abroad. Check whether an order exists and what it says.

Two 12-year-old travelers may therefore need different papers for a trip with one parent and a trip with relatives or a group. After obtaining the passport, show the full itinerary to the destination mission and the operating airline. Taiwan's passport-issuance documents alone do not complete the transit and arrival review.
