### FAQ 1

- H2: `## 1. Choosing a Taiwan Market-Entry Structure and Importer`
- Q:
  `Does a foreign cosmetics brand have to establish a subsidiary or branch to sell products in Taiwan?`
- A:
  `No. A Taiwan importer—including one that also serves as the distributor—may handle importation and sales, so a foreign brand does not necessarily need its own Taiwan subsidiary or branch. If the brand operates directly in Taiwan, a Taiwan subsidiary and a Taiwan branch of a foreign company follow different formation or registration procedures and have different liability and tax structures. The time required for foreign-investment approval and company or branch registration depends on the matter and whether supplemental documents are requested. The brand should first choose its business model and identify the entity that will assume the statutory responsibilities of the cosmetics manufacturer or importer.`
- H3s, in order:
  - `### Using a Taiwan Importer`
  - `### Operating Directly in Taiwan`

### FAQ 2

- H2: `## 2. Product Registration and the PIF Are Separate Requirements`
- Q: `What is a PIF, and is it the same as TFDA product registration?`
- A:
  `No. Cosmetic product notification—referred to here as product registration—is a separate filing completed through TFDA’s cosmetics product registration platform. A Product Information File (PIF) compiles information on the product’s quality, safety, composition, claimed functions, manufacturing methods, test results, and safety assessment. The cosmetics manufacturer or importer must prepare, update, and retain the PIF; the PIF itself is not submitted to TFDA in advance. Beginning July 1, 2026, the remaining cosmetics categories are subject to the PIF requirements, so the rules generally cover all cosmetics. The exception is limited to solid handmade soap manufactured at a site exempt from factory registration.`
- H3s, in order:
  - `### When Product Registration Is Required and How Long It Remains Valid`
  - `### What the PIF Contains and When the Rules Apply`
  - `### Updating and Retaining the PIF`
  - `### Inspections, Corrective Orders, and Administrative Penalties`

### FAQ 3

- H2: `## 3. Labeling, Promotion, and Advertising Rules`
- Q:
  `What claims require particular care in Taiwan cosmetics advertising?`
- A:
  `Taiwan evaluates an advertisement from its overall presentation, including the product name, text, images, symbols, sound, and context—not from isolated words alone. False or exaggerated claims and claims of medical efficacy are prohibited. Claims that a cosmetic treats acne, has anti-inflammatory effects, or sterilizes are examples requiring particular care. The administrative fine is NT$40,000–NT$200,000 for false or exaggerated advertising and NT$600,000–NT$5,000,000 for claims of medical efficacy. An influencer or reviewer post should be reviewed under the same rules when its content and commercial context make it advertising; not every personal post is automatically an advertisement.`
- H3s, in order:
  - `### Evaluate the Advertisement as a Whole`
  - `### Influencers, Reviewers, and Sales Partners`
  - `### A Pre-Market Compliance Sequence`

## Market-entry structure

Explain:

- A Taiwan importer may also serve as distributor or sales agent.
- A contractual label does not determine statutory responsibility.
- The parties must identify who imports, registers the product, prepares and
  retains the PIF, checks labeling, keeps distribution records, receives
  complaints and safety reports, and answers agency requests.
- The contract should address intellectual-property use, manufacturer-record
  access, translation, PIF updates, advertising preclearance, adverse-event
  reporting, recall cooperation, costs, and post-termination handoff.
- Required records must remain accessible after the relationship ends.
- A Taiwan subsidiary is a separate Taiwan legal entity.
- A Taiwan branch is part of the foreign head office and is not separate.
- Formation or registration, liability, head-office responsibility,
  accounting, taxation, earnings remittance, representative authority, and
  internal controls differ.
- Review and registration timing varies with the investor, activities,
  documents, inquiries, and supplements. Never promise three months.
- Use `Department of Investment Review, Ministry of Economic Affairs (MOEA)`.
- The statutory actor is `cosmetics manufacturer or importer`.
- Outsourcing does not transfer the manufacturer’s or importer’s responsibility.

## Product registration

Define the official English term once as:
`cosmetic product notification—referred to here as product registration`.

Explain:

- it is a filing separate from the PIF;
- it is completed through TFDA’s cosmetics product registration platform;
- it must be completed before supply, sale, giveaway, public display, or
  consumer trial use;
- validity is three years;
- continued supply requires an extension during the three months before
  expiration;
- changes may require amendment or a new notification as applicable; and
- completion does not establish PIF completeness, TFDA review or approval of
  evidence, label compliance, advertising legality, or guaranteed eligibility
  for sale.

## PIF content, phase-in, updating, and retention

Explain that a PIF is product-specific quality, safety, manufacturing, testing,
and substantiation material. It is not registered with, routinely uploaded to,
approved by, or certified by TFDA.

State that the governing regulation organizes the required information into
exactly 16 categories. Cover representative product, manufacturer, notification,
composition, packaging, label, manufacturing, use, adverse-effect, physical
and chemical, toxicology, stability, microbiology, preservative-effectiveness,
claimed-function, contact-packaging, and safety-assessment records.

State:

- beginning July 1, 2026, the remaining categories enter the PIF regime;
- the rules then generally cover all cosmetics;
- the only contracted exception is solid handmade soap manufactured at a site
  exempt from factory registration;
- neither `handmade` nor `soap` alone creates the exception;
- a qualified third party may assist, but responsibility remains with the
  cosmetics manufacturer or importer;
- the PIF must be updated when ingredients, formula, manufacturing method or
  site, label, leaflet, claims, tests, complaints, adverse events, or safety
  information change.

Distinguish the two provisions:

- Article 7: retain the PIF for at least five years, beginning the day after
  the product was last supplied to the market.
- Article 8: retain it at the manufacturer’s or importer’s address stated under
  Article 7(1)(7) of the Cosmetic Hygiene and Safety Act.

Original-manufacturer, electronic, or cloud storage is acceptable only if the
responsible manufacturer or importer retains complete access and can promptly
retrieve and produce the file. Address access rights, backups, version control,
responsible personnel, file formats, completeness, and post-termination handoff.

## Inspection and enforcement

State:

- the competent authority ordinarily gives at least seven days’ advance notice;
- an emergency or public-interest need is an exception;
- false registration or PIF information may draw an administrative fine of
  `NT$10,000–NT$1,000,000`;
- an incomplete PIF ordinarily produces an order to correct within a specified
  period; the fine applies if the deficiency is not corrected by the deadline;
- withdrawal, recall, confiscation, or destruction is not automatic for every
  documentation defect and depends on the statutory conditions, violation,
  correction result, safety risk, findings, and order.

Do not describe an administrative fine as criminal.
