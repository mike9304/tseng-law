# Your Partner's Country Does Not Recognize Same-Sex Marriage. Can You Register in Taiwan?

Yes, if one of you is a Taiwanese citizen. A household registration office will no longer turn a couple away because the foreign partner's home law does not recognize same-sex marriage. That has been the position since January 19, 2023, when the Ministry of the Interior sent local governments a ruling (letter Tai-Nei-Hu No. 1120240466) on how to apply Taiwan's choice-of-law statute to these couples.

## Why international couples were stuck after 2019

Taiwan did not amend its Civil Code. It passed a separate statute, the [Act for the Implementation of J.Y. Interpretation No. 748](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawAll.aspx?pcode=B0000008), in force since May 24, 2019. Article 2 lets two people of the same sex form "a permanent union of intimate and exclusive nature" to live together. The Act calls it an "Article 2 relationship."

Choice of law was the obstacle. [Article 46](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000007&flno=46) of the Act Governing the Choice of Law in Civil Matters Involving Foreign Elements says the formation of a marriage is governed by each party's national law. In June 2019 the Judicial Yuan read that to mean the substantive requirements had to be met under Taiwan law and under the foreign partner's law. A partner from Japan or Korea failed the second test.

Courts opened the first gap. On May 6, 2021, the Taipei High Administrative Court (case 109-Su-14) ordered registration for a Macau resident by applying renvoi under Article 6: Macau law pointed to the law of habitual residence, and the partner lived in Taiwan. The ministry accepted the judgment on May 24, 2021, but warned that a partner habitually resident elsewhere would get a different answer. Outcomes depended on the file.

## The 2023 ruling: setting the foreign law aside

[Article 8](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000007&flno=8) of the same act says a foreign law is not applied if the result would offend Taiwan's public order or good morals. The 2023 ruling, reflecting a decision reached by the Executive Yuan after consulting the Judicial Yuan, treats the principle that two people of the same sex may not be denied an Article 2 relationship as part of Taiwan's public order.

So when a Taiwanese citizen marries a national of a country that does not recognize same-sex marriage, that country's rule is set aside. The couple can form an Article 2 relationship in Taiwan and register under [Article 4](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000008&flno=4) of the 2019 Act. The ruling's text is quoted in a Taipei City Government administrative appeal decision of June 21, 2023 (Fu-Su-Yi No. 1126081956).

Hong Kong and Macau residents are covered by the same reasoning. Mainland Chinese residents are not. The ruling says cross-strait same-sex marriages involve entry, interview and registration procedures and stay under the Act Governing Relations between the People of the Taiwan Area and the Mainland Area. The household registration department's current guidance lets such couples register after marrying in a third jurisdiction that recognizes same-sex marriage, with an authenticated certificate and an interview, as opposite-sex cross-strait couples do.

## What the registration office asks for

If either partner has, or once had, a household registration in Taiwan, a marriage in Taiwan can be registered at any household registration office. The department lists these documents:

| Whose | Documents |
|---|---|
| Both | A written marriage agreement with both names, dates of birth, ID, passport or residence permit numbers and addresses, signed or sealed by at least two witnesses |
| Taiwanese partner | National ID card, seal or signature, household registration book, a compliant photo taken within two years |
| Foreign partner | Passport or residence certificate issued by the National Immigration Agency, a declaration of Chinese name, and a certificate of marital status authenticated by a Taiwan overseas mission |

A marital-status certificate is valid for six months from issue. Documents made abroad need authentication by a Taiwan mission, and foreign-language documents need a Chinese translation authenticated by a mission or certified by a notary in Taiwan. A different track applies only where the partner's home country does not recognize same-sex marriage and is also on a list published by the Ministry of Foreign Affairs: the partner files the mission's interview result letter bound together with a home-country marital-status certificate, which then needs no authentication or translation.

What if you cannot both attend on the day? The guidance allows an application up to three working days in advance with a chosen registration date. Filing through someone else requires a good reason and the office's approval. Neither partner may be under 18 ([Article 3](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000008&flno=3)).

## Two foreign nationals

The 2023 ruling deals with a citizen and a foreigner. For two foreigners, the department's guidance allows registration in Taiwan only when both come from countries or regions that recognize same-sex marriage. Two Japanese nationals would not qualify on that guidance.

## What registration changes

Registered partners owe each other cohabitation (Article 11), act as each other's agents for daily household matters (Article 13), share living costs and are jointly liable for debts from them (Article 14), and follow the Civil Code's matrimonial property rules (Article 15).

Inheritance follows. [Article 23](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000008&flno=23) makes the partners each other's statutory heirs and applies the Civil Code's rules on spouses. Which law governs a particular estate is a separate question, answered by the deceased's national law at death ([Article 58](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000007&flno=58) of the choice-of-law act). If the Taiwanese partner dies, Taiwan law applies. If the foreign partner dies, that partner's national law applies, subject to a proviso: a Taiwanese citizen who is an heir under Taiwan law may inherit the estate located in Taiwan.

Residence is not automatic. [Article 24](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000008&flno=24)(2) applies spousal provisions in other statutes, so the foreign partner applies under [Article 23](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0080132&flno=23)(1)(1) of the Immigration Act as the spouse of a citizen who lives in Taiwan with a household registration. That route assumes entry on a valid visa permitting a stay of 60 days or more, with no bar on extension or other restriction. Visa-exempt entry does not meet it. Permit issue and renewal are covered in [residence for foreign spouses of Taiwanese citizens](/en/columns/taiwan-foreign-spouse-residence).

Children are addressed in [Article 20](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000008&flno=20), amended on June 9, 2023: the Civil Code's adoption rules apply when one partner adopts the other's child and when the two adopt jointly. A case involving a child born through overseas surrogacy is discussed in [overseas surrogacy and spousal adoption in Taiwan](/en/columns/taiwan-overseas-surrogacy-child-parentage-spouse-adoption-registration).

## Your home country's records

Whether your own country treats the Taiwan registration as a marriage is a question for its law. This column covers the Taiwan side only. Keep the household registration transcript and a copy of the marriage agreement; Taiwan inheritance, insurance and medical-consent procedures all start from them.

Ending the relationship by agreement takes a written agreement signed by at least two witnesses and registration of the termination ([Article 16](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000008&flno=16)). Without agreement, a partner asks the court to end it on the grounds in Article 17.
