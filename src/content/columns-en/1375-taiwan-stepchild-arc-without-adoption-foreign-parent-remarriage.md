# Can your child from a previous marriage get a Taiwan ARC without stepparent adoption?

Yes. A child under 18 may apply for Taiwan residence and an Alien Resident Certificate (ARC; 外僑居留證) through the child's foreign biological parent once that parent has approved residence or permanent residence. [Article 23(1)(2) of the Immigration Act (入出國及移民法)](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0080132&flno=23) covers a foreign national under 18 whose parent or other direct ancestor (直系尊親屬) is an ROC national who currently has household registration in the Taiwan area or has approved residence, a foreign national with approved residence or permanent residence, or a Hong Kong or Macao resident with approved residence. The law says the child may apply; it does not promise approval.

Article 23(1)(1) may support your own residence if your spouse is an ROC national who currently lives in Taiwan with household registration there, or an ROC national with approved residence. It does not make your new spouse the child's parent or direct ancestor in that sense. The child's route rests on your approved foreign-resident status, so adoption by the Taiwanese stepparent is not a precondition.

## The child applies through the foreign parent's residence

Two family relationships are doing different work. Your marriage supports your spouse-based residence application. Your parent-child relationship then supplies the lineal-ascendant link for the child's application.

Can the child apply while your own ARC is still pending? The statute identifies a parent with approved residence. The National Immigration Agency (NIA; 內政部移民署) should confirm whether it will coordinate the two filings in a particular case; do not assume that concurrent filing is available.

Adoption appears in the same provision, but only as a condition for a relationship created by adoption: the adopted child must live with the adopter in Taiwan. That sentence does not require a biological child to be adopted by a stepparent. Adoption would change the child's family-law status and should be evaluated separately from the immigration route.

The child's route also has limits. The applicant must be under 18. In addition, Article 23 excludes a child whose qualifying parent obtained residence under item 9 or 10 of that paragraph, or whose parent is permitted by the central labor authority to perform the work listed in Employment Service Act Article 46(1)(8) through (10). A spouse-based ARC under item 1 is not one of those two numbered exclusions, but the actual basis printed in the parent's residence record still matters.

For the parent's side of the process, see [residence as the foreign spouse of a Taiwanese national](/en/columns/taiwan-foreign-spouse-residence). The [first Taiwan ARC guide](/en/columns/taiwan-arc-first-application) explains how the entry record and residence basis shape a first-card filing.

## Entry status decides where the application begins

According to the Bureau of Consular Affairs (BOCA; 外交部領事事務局), a resident-visa applicant who is outside Taiwan must apply directly to a Taiwan overseas mission. BOCA adds that a person who enters on a resident visa, or obtains one inside Taiwan, must apply for the ARC and a re-entry permit within 30 days from the day after entry or from the visa's issue date. Under the Immigration Act, once a child enters and receives residence permission, [Article 22](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0080132&flno=22) requires an ARC application within 30 days, counted from the day after entry, unless the child holds a combined card or another document that already works as an ARC.

A direct application to the NIA is possible in a narrower in-country setting. Article 23 begins with three conditions: the child entered on a valid visa, the permitted stay is at least 60 days, and the issuing authority did not add a no-extension or other restrictive annotation. BOCA's [minor-child resident-visa guidance](https://www.boca.gov.tw/cp-402-182-eb0b7-1.html) likewise tells qualifying family-residence applicants with such a visitor visa to apply directly at an NIA service center. Article 6 of the foreign-residence regulations (外國人停留居留及永久居留辦法) says an Article 23(1)(2) application [may be filed starting 15 days before the permitted stay expires](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0080129&flno=6).

Visa-free entry is different. BOCA states that its in-Taiwan resident-visa procedure does not apply to a person who entered visa-free or with a landing visa. Its [separate dependent-resident page](https://www.boca.gov.tw/fp-402-181-b5735-1.html) repeats that restriction, although that page's title concerns another family category. Plan the overseas resident-visa route before the child travels instead of assuming that visa-free entry can be converted in Taiwan. Article 23, paragraph 3 separately lets the under-18 children of qualifying workers apply after visa-free or visitor-visa entry. That route requires the parent to meet paragraph 1, item 4 and to hold residence approved under paragraph 2 or after entry on a resident visa. Marriage-based residence alone does not establish those conditions.

One more annotation matters. BOCA says a nonextendable visitor visa coded “P” for tourism, family visit, or visit cannot be changed to a resident visa in Taiwan. The wording on the visa should therefore be checked before departure, not after the child arrives.

## Family records must connect the child to the resident parent

The closest BOCA minor-child checklists carry headings for children joining different categories of sponsors. Their document lists are useful preparation references, but they are not a substitute for confirmation from the Taiwan mission or NIA for a child joining a foreign parent on a spouse-based ARC.

Those checklists identify the family records at the center of the application:

- the child's birth certificate showing both parents' full names, authenticated by the responsible Taiwan overseas mission;
- proof of the child's parents' marriage relationship;
- the foreign parent's residence certificate, ARC or permanent-residence document, with at least six months of validity remaining, in original and copy; and
- if the biological parents are divorced, proof that the parent whom the child will join has custody.

BOCA also lists the application form, photographs, a passport valid for at least six months with blank pages, a health examination certificate issued within the last three months (children aged six or under are exempt, and other exemptions are narrow), and other documents requested for the individual case. Its [guidance for a minor child joining a household-registered national](https://www.boca.gov.tw/cp-9-180-d4b9d-2.html), which is a different sponsorship route, uses the same birth-certificate authentication and custody-proof language. A document made abroad must be authenticated by a Taiwan overseas mission, and one made in Mainland China must instead be certified by the Straits Exchange Foundation; a document that is neither Chinese nor English must also have a Chinese or English translation authenticated by the mission or certified by a Taiwan notary.

Custody proof and consent are not interchangeable. The BOCA material says that, after divorce, the joining parent must have custody and provide proof. It does not state that the other biological parent's consent is a separate ARC requirement. The scope of the custody order and the child's authority to travel still deserve a separate review before tickets are booked.

## The child's ARC remains tied to the parent's status

An approved ARC does not detach the child's status from the sponsoring parent. [Article 13 of the foreign-residence regulations](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0080129&flno=13) sets the validity of a family-dependent ARC by the residence period of the family member whom the applicant joined. For an extension, [Article 9](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0080129&flno=9) requires the application during the three months before expiration, with the application form, passport, ARC, other supporting records and one photograph.

Status changes can therefore affect both cards. [Article 31 of the Immigration Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0080132&flno=31) provides for revocation when the reason for residence disappears, while also listing circumstances in which NIA may permit continued residence. If the foreign parent's spouse-based residence ends, the child's basis through that parent's approved residence may end as well; NIA must assess the current basis and any applicable statutory exception.
