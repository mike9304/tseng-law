Two holders of the same US state license can face different exchange rules in Taiwan. One may be applying as a foreign national; the other may also hold Taiwan nationality. [Article 50 of the Road Traffic Safety Regulations](https://laws.gov.taipei/Law/LawSearch/LawChapterArticle/FL012455?chapterno=3) expressly separates those routes.

For Americans moving to Taiwan, the first question is therefore more precise than whether a US license is accepted: which state issued the full license, what vehicle class does it cover, and under which nationality and residence documents will the holder apply? An Alien Resident Certificate (ARC) answers only part of that question.

## Foreign applicants need the state-specific reciprocity route

Article 50 permits holders of valid full foreign licenses, with proof of permitted stay or residence for at least six months, to obtain an equivalent ordinary Taiwan license under reciprocity. Applications can begin the day after entry. This is a residence-document condition. It does not say applicants must first spend six months physically in Taiwan.

US licenses are issued by states, so the relevant entry is the issuing state's arrangement. The [Highway Bureau's exchange guidance](https://www.thb.gov.tw/en/cp.aspx?n=616) makes reciprocity a condition for foreign applicants and links to its [official jurisdiction tables](https://www.thb.gov.tw/en/News.aspx?n=878&sms=12831). A state's recognition of visitors' driving documents should not be read as a promise of license exchange. The linked English North America table is dated, so it is not sufficient by itself to confirm a state's current exchange conditions; obtain current confirmation from the receiving motor vehicles office.

Check the license category as well. Article 50 refers to an equivalent ordinary vehicle class. A car license is no basis for assuming a motorcycle entitlement. The motor vehicles office can identify the matching Taiwan class and any tests or further conditions attached to the applicable arrangement before an applicant pays for authentication.

## Taiwan nationality changes the legal starting point

Someone who also has Republic of China nationality falls under a separate sentence in Article 50 allowing exchange of a valid full foreign license for the equivalent ordinary vehicle class. That statutory exception is tied to nationality. Holding an ARC as a foreigner does not create it.

Household registration affects the identity documents. Nationals registered in Taiwan provide a national or military identity card. Nationals without household registration provide proof of permitted stay or residence for at least six months. A dual US–Taiwan national should establish which documentation branch applies, rather than relying on another American's experience at the counter. These distinctions do not remove the examination and authentication requirements below.

## Authentication is a separate step

Article 50 requires applicants to pass the physical examination and submit a license application with the relevant identity or residence documents. A US license must be authenticated through an authorized channel, such as a Taiwan overseas representative office or an appropriately authorized foreign institution in Taiwan. The rule requires a Chinese translation for foreign-language licenses other than English.

An English license still needs authentication. Language and authenticity answer different questions. The [Highway Bureau's application guidance](https://www.thb.gov.tw/en/cp.aspx?n=616) also identifies photographs, passport or entry records and medical documentation used in processing. Its English page uses slightly different wording about the residence-document period; the Chinese regulation says six months or more. Confirm the documents for the applicant's exact status with the receiving office.

## An IDP follows its own rules

Can you use an international driving permit (IDP) while preparing the exchange? [Article 55](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=K0040013&flno=55) allows holders of valid reciprocal-country IDPs making short stays of no more than 30 days to drive without local IDP registration. Longer stays require registration. The registration lasts no more than one year, shortened by an earlier expiry of the underlying license or permitted-stay/residence document. It is not a license-exchange approval.

Existing Taiwan traffic-law restrictions also apply. [Article 55-2](https://laws.gov.taipei/Law/LawSearch/LawChapterArticle/FL012455?chapterno=3) bars using a foreign license or IDP to drive or exchange for an ordinary license when a Taiwan traffic-law disposition has revoked or cancelled the license, prohibited taking a license test, or imposed a suspension that has not finished. A separate article discusses [an unlicensed accident victim's compensation claim](/en/columns/taiwan-unlicensed-victim-no-contributory-negligence), where accident causation also matters.

For a disputed refusal or a penalty involving foreign-license recognition, a Taiwan lawyer can examine the issuing-state arrangement, authentication record and documents relied on by the authority.
