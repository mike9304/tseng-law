# A pedestrian killed in Tainan, a driver who fled: what the victim's family can claim in Taiwan

Under an hour. That is how long it took Tainan police, working through roadside surveillance footage, to find the car that had struck a male university student walking in a lane in Yongkang District at about 11 p.m. on November 1, [CNA reported](https://www.cna.com.tw/news/asoc/202611020310.aspx). The driver had not stopped. The student was taken to hospital and died.

The 49-year-old driver had a previous drink-driving record, according to the report, and had lost his licence over it, so he was driving without one. A compulsory blood test came back above the legal limit; the report gave no figure. Prosecutors asked for detention, and the Tainan District Court granted it, CNA reported late on November 2. The case is still under investigation, and the report named no specific charge for the death.

## Why the court ordered detention

According to the report, the driver admitted the conduct after questioning. The court found strong grounds to suspect an offence, a risk that he would flee and a risk that he would again commit the offence of driving when unfit to do so. It also described the case as repeat drink-driving that had caused a death.

That last point matters for the procedure. [Article 101-1 of the Code of Criminal Procedure](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0010001&flno=101-1) allows detention to prevent repeat offending for a listed set of crimes, including driving while unfit under [Article 185-3 of the Criminal Code](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0000001&flno=185-3). That article treats a breath reading of 0.25 mg per litre or a blood level of 0.05 percent as an offence in itself. Where death results, the range is three to ten years. For someone with a final conviction or a final deferred-prosecution disposition for the same offence (or under Article 54 of the Armed Forces Criminal Act) who reoffends within ten years and causes a death, it is life imprisonment or at least five years. Leaving after a crash that kills or seriously injures someone is a separate offence carrying one to seven years ([Article 185-4](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0000001&flno=185-4)).

Which of these provisions the prosecutors will rely on was not in the report. Whether the repeat-offence range applies depends on the earlier record, which the report did not detail.

## Insurance pays first

Does drinking or a missing licence let the insurer off? No. [Article 29 of the Compulsory Automobile Liability Insurance Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0390060&flno=29) keeps the insurer liable to pay where the insured caused the accident while over the alcohol limit, while committing a crime or evading lawful arrest, or while driving without a licence in breach of [Article 21 of the Road Traffic Management and Penalty Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=K0040012&flno=21). The insurer can then pursue the insured for what it paid.

For a death, the benefit is NT$3 million for each person killed ([Article 6 of the payment standards](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0390067&flno=6)). [Article 11](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0390060&flno=11) sets who may claim: parents, children and spouse first, then grandparents, grandchildren and siblings. Family members in the same rank split the benefit equally, so the NT$3 million is a total, not an amount each.

Had the car been uninsured, or had it never been found, the family could have turned to the Special Compensation Fund instead, within the same statutory amounts ([Article 40](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0390060&flno=40)). Families living abroad, including those of foreign residents killed on Taiwan's roads, claim through the same rules.

## Beyond the insurance payment

A compulsory-insurance payment does not exhaust the family's possible claims. The payment counts as part of the damages, and the person liable may deduct it when sued ([Article 32](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0390060&flno=32)). Larger losses go through the Civil Code: funeral costs and the support the deceased owed to others under [Article 192](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=192), and damages for non-financial harm to parents, children and spouse under [Article 194](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=194).

The car was found within the hour. For the student's family, the questions now are about claims and timing, and the criminal case will set its own pace.
