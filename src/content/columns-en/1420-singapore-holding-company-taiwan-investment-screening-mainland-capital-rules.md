# Investing in Taiwan through a Singapore holding company: when do Mainland-capital rules apply?

One threshold—more than 30%—can move a Singapore holding company out of Taiwan's ordinary foreign-investment channel. Incorporation in Singapore does not settle the question by itself. As of November 24, 2026, [Article 3 of the Regulations Governing Permits for People of the Mainland Area Investing in Taiwan](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=Q0040015&flno=3) (大陸地區人民來臺投資許可辦法) reclassifies a third-area company, including a Singapore private limited company, as a Mainland investor if Mainland persons or entities directly or indirectly hold over 30% of its shares or capital, or have the ability to control it (具有控制能力). Once either applies, paragraph 3 removes the investment from the [Statute for Investment by Foreign Nationals](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawAll.aspx?pcode=J0040002) (外國人投資條例).

A Singapore venture fund, family office, or regional headquarters that pools investors from across Asia in one vehicle may have Mainland holders several layers up. Catching that before signing a term sheet avoids restructuring mid-deal.

## How Taiwan screens a Singapore holding company

Under [Article 3 of the Foreign Investment Statute](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0040002&flno=3), a foreign legal person's nationality follows its law of incorporation. Standing alone, that rule places a Singapore entity in the ordinary foreign-investment track administered by the Ministry of Economic Affairs (經濟部) under [Article 2](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0040002&flno=2).

Taiwan layers a look-through screen over incorporation. [Article 3, paragraph 2 of the Mainland permit regulations](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=Q0040015&flno=3) reclassifies a third-area company in either of two cases: Mainland individuals, legal persons, organizations, or other institutions directly or indirectly hold more than 30% of its shares or capital contributions, or have the ability to control it.

Exactly 30% does not exceed 30% ("逾百分之三十"). A Singapore vehicle with 25% Mainland equity still enters Mainland-capital review if a Mainland founder or entity has the ability to control it (an invented example). Under [Article 2](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=Q0040015&flno=2), the Ministry of Economic Affairs is the competent authority for these rules. The regulations print no formula for control, so confirm how the Ministry reads a given structure before filing.

What if a Mainland state-linked body holds an upstream stake? [Article 6](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=Q0040015&flno=6) adds a mandatory restriction. Where an investor—or its third-area invested company—is invested in by Mainland party, military, administrative, or political organs or organizations, the Ministry must restrict its investment in Taiwan. Cap-table reviews therefore need to trace every upstream backer.

## Negative list versus positive list and review speed

Reclassification inverts how Taiwan opens sectors to outside capital.

| Point | Ordinary foreign investment | Mainland-capital investment |
|---|---|---|
| Industry access | Negative list (Statute Art. 7) | Positive list: open items, caps, ratios (Rules Art. 8) |
| Decision window | 1 month; 2 months if another authority joins (Art. 8) | No fixed 1-to-2-month deadline in the regulations |
| Political links | General prohibited-sector rules (Art. 7) | Mandatory restriction on party, military, administrative, or political backers (Art. 6) |
| Filing agent | No CPA/lawyer mandate in Statute | Mandatory Taiwan CPA or lawyer if no local domicile or office (Art. 9) |
| Verification | Required after implementation (Art. 9) | Within 2 months after implementation (Art. 10) |
| Annual filing with the Ministry | No comparable filing duty in the Statute | Audited financials and shareholder register within 6 months after fiscal year-end at NT$30 million paid-in capital (Art. 11) |

For ordinary foreign investors, [Article 7 of the Foreign Investment Statute](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0040002&flno=7) bars activities harming national security, public order, good morals, or national health, plus legally prohibited sectors; restricted sectors require industry-regulator consent, with both lists set and reviewed by the Executive Yuan. Once formalities are complete, [Article 8, paragraph 3](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0040002&flno=8) requires a decision within one month, or two months if another regulator is involved.

Mainland-capital rules work from a positive list. Under [Article 8, paragraph 1 of the Mainland permit regulations](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=Q0040015&flno=8), open industries, caps, and ratios come from lists drafted with central regulators and approved by the Executive Yuan. Confirm exact open items before committing. Even inside an open item, paragraph 2 lets the Ministry restrict, prohibit, or revoke an investment for monopolistic, oligopolistic, or dominant position, political, social, cultural, or security sensitivity, or harm to economic development or financial stability.

## Deals beyond equity: contracts, asset buyouts and branches

[Article 4 of the Foreign Investment Statute](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0040002&flno=4) covers company shares or capital, branches, sole proprietorships, partnerships, and loans of one year or more to those invested businesses.

Reclassified Singapore vehicles face a wider scope under [Article 4 of the Mainland permit regulations](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=Q0040015&flno=4). Alongside shares, branches, partnerships, limited partnerships, and one-year-plus loans, paragraph 1 captures two alternative deal structures:

- Contractual control (item 4): having the ability to control a Taiwan sole proprietorship, partnership, limited partnership, or unlisted company—non-TWSE, non-TPEx, and non-Emerging Stock—through contracts or other means.
- Private M&A (item 5): a reclassified third-area company acquiring the business or assets of an unlisted Taiwan company.

Listed shares get one narrow carve-out under item 1: TWSE, TPEx, and Emerging Stock purchases stay outside this permit rule only when single and cumulative holdings both remain below 10%. Paragraph 2 removes that carve-out for Taiwan financial, insurance, securities, and futures institutions under specialized cross-strait rules.

Holding over one-third of a Taiwan business's shares or capital triggers downstream screening under both regimes. Once Mainland investors cross that mark, [Article 5](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=Q0040015&flno=5) designates a Mainland-invested enterprise (陸資投資事業) whose reinvestments follow these rules. Ordinary foreign investors face a matching one-third approval threshold under [Article 5 of the Foreign Investment Statute](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0040002&flno=5).

Can a Singapore headquarters sidestep subsidiary screening by opening a branch, or by doing business in Taiwan in its own name without registering one? Neither avoids Taiwan law. Branches require investment approval under both regimes. Operating in Taiwan under a foreign company's name without branch registration breaches [Company Act Article 371](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0080001&flno=371) (公司法). The individuals responsible face up to one year in prison, detention, or a fine up to NT$150,000 (or both), personal civil liability (joint and several if two or more act), and a name ban.

Registered branches carry strict capital locks under [Company Act Article 372](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0080001&flno=372), requiring dedicated operating funds and a Taiwan responsible person. After registration, a Taiwan responsible person who returns those funds to the foreign company, or lets it take them back, faces up to five years in prison, detention, or a fine of NT$500,000 to NT$2,500,000 (or both). That person is jointly and severally liable with the foreign company for third-party losses, and the central competent authority revokes or abolishes registration upon final conviction unless cured beforehand.

## Agents, capital verification, annual reporting and exits

Filing directly from Singapore is barred in the Mainland-capital track. [Article 9, paragraph 5 of the Mainland permit regulations](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=Q0040015&flno=9) mandates a Taiwan CPA or lawyer for filings, plan changes, and transfers when the investor has no Taiwan domicile or office. Under paragraph 3, the Ministry may order funding-source declarations within a deadline; changes must be reported within one month.

Two months is the verification deadline under [Article 10, paragraph 3](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=Q0040015&flno=10). Permitted capital must arrive within the approved period; unimplemented amounts expire unless extended beforehand for justified reasons. Ordinary foreign investors face parallel arrival, extension, and verification duties under [Article 9 of the Foreign Investment Statute](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0040002&flno=9). Both [Article 6 of the Statute](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0040002&flno=6) and [Article 7 of the Mainland regulations](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=Q0040015&flno=7) permit cash, own-use machinery or raw materials, IP, or approved property.

At NT$30 million or more in paid-in capital, annual reporting to the Ministry begins. Under [Article 11, paragraph 1](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=Q0040015&flno=11), a Mainland-invested enterprise at that level must file CPA-audited financials, its shareholder register, and designated materials within six months after fiscal year-end. Paragraph 2 lets the Ministry, when necessary, order an investor's invested business to file those same materials, and paragraph 3 authorizes periodic reviews and on-site inspections that the business must not evade, obstruct, or refuse.

Transferring shares out requires joint filing by transferor and transferee under [Article 10, paragraph 2 of the Foreign Investment Statute](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0040002&flno=10) and [Article 9, paragraph 4 of the Mainland permit regulations](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=Q0040015&flno=9). For Mainland investors, paragraph 4 allows refusal if a transfer affects national security or public interest. Foreign-exchange settlement for profits, verified capital on exit, and capital gains stays protected under [Article 12 of the Statute](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0040002&flno=12) and [Article 12 of the Mainland regulations](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=Q0040015&flno=12), while breaching the Foreign Investment Statute risks remittance suspension or revocation under [Article 18](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0040002&flno=18).

## Sorting ownership records before filing

Map every ownership layer above the Singapore vehicle before signing a binding Taiwan acquisition or lease. Review voting and veto rights in the constitution and shareholders' agreements, and check for Mainland party, military, administrative, or political backing. Corporate and tax duties in Singapore itself need separate confirmation under Singapore law.
