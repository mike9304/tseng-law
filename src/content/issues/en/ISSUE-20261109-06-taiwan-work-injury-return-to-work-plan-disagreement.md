# Returning to work after a Taiwan workplace injury when the plan is disputed

Can an injured employee obtain a return-to-work plan when the Taiwan employer will not join the discussion? [Article 66 of the Labor Occupational Accident Insurance and Protection Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0050031&flno=66) allows either party to request professional assistance. A joint application is unnecessary.

Taiwan's labor authorities described the occupational-injury support network at a November 6 event, [CNA reported](https://www.cna.com.tw/news/ahel/202611060212.aspx). The [Ministry of Labor's announcement](https://www.mol.gov.tw/1607/1632/1633/100053/post) describes links between occupational diagnosis, case management, rehabilitation and help returning to work. For an English-speaking employee or a Taiwan subsidiary's HR team, that professional process offers a way to examine a disputed return in terms of actual work capacity.

## A plan tied to the job

Under [Article 66](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0050031&flno=66), the application goes to an occupational-rehabilitation professional institution recognized by the central authority. Its assistance can include work analysis, functional-capacity assessment and training to restore or strengthen physical and psychological function. The employer and worker each have access to that application route.

Planning concerns the work itself. [Article 84 of the enforcement rules](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0050039&flno=84) calls for medical information, an assessment of work capacity, the proposed duties and abilities they require, workplace adjustments, supporting measures and an implementation timetable. A job title alone leaves much of that unexplained.

For an international company, headquarters' description of a role may be too general to explain the Taiwan workplace. The local tasks, equipment and working arrangements provide the practical context for the assessment. An employee can likewise describe the particular activity that remains difficult, instead of having the discussion turn only on whether they are “ready.” This is a way of making the disagreement specific, not a separate statutory form.

## Professional work can continue without consensus

Normally, Article 84 provides for discussion involving the employer, worker, an occupational-medicine specialist and other rehabilitation professionals. If the two sides do not participate jointly or cannot agree, the physician and other professionals may prepare the plan using the participating side's views and their professional assessment, and proceed on that basis.

Professional assessment remains part of the process. The rule does not turn one party's preferred schedule into a plan simply because the other party is absent. Recording who took part and where the views differed helps distinguish the evidence considered from the outcome one side wanted.

## Reassignment has an additional condition

After medical treatment ends, [Article 67 of the Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=N0050031&flno=67) requires the employer to follow the return-to-work plan and assist the worker in resuming the former work. If that cannot be resumed, placement in suitable work according to the worker's health and capacity requires agreement between employer and worker. Hospital discharge alone is not the condition stated in the provision.

That agreement condition remains. A plan prepared without consensus under the enforcement rules cannot simply be treated as proof that both sides accepted a different assignment.

A proposed move from equipment maintenance to a desk-based role could leave two disagreements: whether the worker can perform the new duties and whether the worker accepts the placement (an invented example). They need separate answers.

Article 67 also requires necessary assistive facilities, including devices and improvements to the workplace, equipment or machinery that restore, maintain or strengthen work ability. When a proposed return remains disputed, the documents can show whether the unresolved issue concerns capacity, adjustments or agreement to different work. That distinction gives the parties something more concrete to address than a bare demand to return on a particular day.
