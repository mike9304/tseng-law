# Entering Taiwan’s Cosmetics Market: Importer Selection, Product Registration, PIF Preparation and Retention, and Advertising Rules

![Cosmetics documentation and market-entry planning for Taiwan](../images/011-taiwan-cosmetics-market-entry-company-setup-pif-registration-legal-sales-guide/featured-01.jpg)

A foreign or Korean cosmetics brand planning to sell in Taiwan must make several related but separate decisions. It must determine who will import the products, which entity will be the statutory actor, when product registration must be completed, who will prepare and retain each Product Information File, and how labeling and advertising will be reviewed. It also needs workable procedures for inspections, consumer complaints, safety reports, and any required response. Forming a company, by itself, does not complete the regulatory work required to sell cosmetics.

The requirements depend on the product, its manufacturing site, the import structure, the distribution arrangement, and the content of the brand’s advertising. A structure that works for one product line may not fit another. The regulatory work therefore should be mapped to the actual supply chain, the records available from the original manufacturer, and the messages that consumers will see or hear.

![](../images/011-taiwan-cosmetics-market-entry-company-setup-pif-registration-legal-sales-guide/img-01.jpg)

## 1. Choosing a Taiwan Market-Entry Structure and Importer

No. A Taiwan importer—including one that also serves as the distributor—may handle importation and sales, so a foreign brand does not necessarily need its own Taiwan subsidiary or branch. If the brand operates directly in Taiwan, a Taiwan subsidiary and a Taiwan branch of a foreign company follow different formation or registration procedures and have different liability and tax structures. The time required for foreign-investment approval and company or branch registration depends on the matter and whether supplemental documents are requested. The brand should first choose its business model and identify the entity that will assume the statutory responsibilities of the cosmetics manufacturer or importer.

### Using a Taiwan Importer

A Taiwan importer may also serve as the distributor or sales agent. Alternatively, one business may import while another manages wholesale, retail, or online sales. Labels such as “agent,” “exclusive distributor,” and “sales partner” describe commercial relationships, but a contractual label does not determine statutory responsibility. The parties must compare the agreement with what each participant actually does.

That comparison should identify who imports the product and who completes product registration. It should also state who obtains source records from the original manufacturer, prepares and retains the PIF, reviews Chinese-language labeling, and maintains batch and distribution records. Operational responsibility must extend to receiving consumer complaints and safety reports, escalating adverse-event information, and answering requests from TFDA or another competent authority.

The written agreement should address use of trademarks, photographs, and other intellectual property; access to manufacturer records; translations; and the delivery of updated formulas, test reports, and safety information. It should assign advertising preclearance, adverse-event reporting, recall cooperation, and the costs of testing, translation, document management, and corrective work. A practical agreement also sets a post-termination handoff process for notification credentials, PIF materials, distribution data, complaint files, and advertising-review records.

Required records must remain accessible after the commercial relationship ends. A provision requiring a departing importer merely to “cooperate” may be insufficient if the brand or a replacement importer cannot retrieve a complete, current file. The agreement should define the material to be delivered, the file format, the deadline, continuing access rights, and responsibility for missing or unusable records.

Statutory obligations attach to the **cosmetics manufacturer or importer** identified under Taiwan law. The parties may use laboratories, regulatory consultants, safety assessors, storage providers, or other qualified contractors. Outsourcing can allocate tasks and expertise, but it does not transfer the manufacturer’s or importer’s responsibility to the contractor.

### Operating Directly in Taiwan

A Taiwan subsidiary is a separate Taiwan legal entity. A Taiwan branch of a foreign company is part of the foreign head office and is not a separate legal entity. Their formation or registration procedures, liability structures, and the foreign head office’s responsibility therefore differ. Accounting, taxation, earnings remittance, representative authority, and internal controls also require structure-specific analysis.

Foreign-investment matters are handled through the **Department of Investment Review, Ministry of Economic Affairs (MOEA)** when that department has jurisdiction. The steps and timing may vary with the investor, proposed activities, entity form, submitted documents, agency inquiries, and required supplements. The company or branch registration process must also be coordinated with tax, customs, banking, and any activity-specific requirements. A launch schedule should not be based on a fixed review period.

Direct operations do not collapse corporate and cosmetics compliance into one process. After forming or registering the Taiwan presence, the business must still identify the cosmetics manufacturer or importer, complete the product-specific filing, maintain each product’s supporting file, check labeling, and control advertising. Internal delegations should identify accountable personnel, escalation paths, access permissions, and substitutes for absences or personnel changes.

## 2. Product Registration and the PIF Are Separate Requirements

No. Cosmetic product notification—referred to here as product registration—is a separate filing completed through TFDA’s cosmetics product registration platform. A Product Information File (PIF) compiles information on the product’s quality, safety, composition, claimed functions, manufacturing methods, test results, and safety assessment. The cosmetics manufacturer or importer must prepare, update, and retain the PIF; the PIF itself is not submitted to TFDA in advance. Beginning July 1, 2026, the remaining cosmetics categories are subject to the PIF requirements, so the rules generally cover all cosmetics. The exception is limited to solid handmade soap manufactured at a site exempt from factory registration.

### When Product Registration Is Required and How Long It Remains Valid

The cosmetics manufacturer or importer completes the filing through TFDA’s cosmetics product registration platform. Product registration must be completed before the product is supplied, sold, given away, publicly displayed, or provided to a consumer for trial use. The rule is not limited to paid retail sales. Samples, launch displays, promotional gifts, and consumer testing must be included in the pre-supply plan.

Product registration remains valid for three years. If supply will continue, an extension must be sought during the three months before expiration. The responsible business should track the expiration date rather than rely on a distributor’s marketing calendar. Changes to a product name, purpose, dosage form, ingredients, manufacturing site, or other notified information may require an amendment or a new notification, as applicable to the change.

Completing product registration means only that the filing was made; it does not establish that the PIF is complete. Nor does it mean that TFDA reviewed or endorsed the supporting evidence, that the label satisfies every requirement, that the advertising is lawful, or that the product is guaranteed to be eligible for sale. Assigning responsible personnel, checklists, and completion criteria separately for notification, PIF work, labeling, and advertising reduces the risk that completion of one task will be mistaken for completion of the others.

### What the PIF Contains and When the Rules Apply

A PIF is a product-specific collection of quality, safety, manufacturing, testing, and substantiation records that documents the product throughout its life cycle. It is not routinely uploaded to TFDA, and TFDA does not review it in advance as a condition of product notification. The notification process neither certifies nor endorses the PIF. Instead, the responsible cosmetics manufacturer or importer must keep the complete file available for inspection and be able to trace each record to its source and applicable version.

The governing regulation organizes the required information into exactly 16 categories:

1. Basic information identifying the product and its manufacturer.
2. Documentary proof that product notification has been completed.
3. The full names and individual concentrations of all ingredients.
4. Packaging, container, label, and package-insert materials presented with the product.
5. Evidence or a declaration concerning the manufacturing site’s compliance with cosmetics good manufacturing practices.
6. Manufacturing methods and process records.
7. Directions covering use, application area, amount, frequency, and intended population.
8. Known adverse-effect information associated with product use.
9. Physical and chemical properties of the finished product and individual ingredients.
10. Toxicology information for the ingredients.
11. Product stability test reports.
12. Microbiological test reports.
13. Preservative-effectiveness test reports.
14. Evidence substantiating the product’s claimed functions.
15. Information on packaging materials that come into contact with the product.
16. Product safety information and the required safety-assessment conclusion and recommendations.

These categories cover representative product, manufacturer, notification, composition, packaging, label, manufacturing, use, adverse-effect, physical and chemical, toxicology, stability, microbiology, preservative-effectiveness, claimed-function, contact-packaging, and safety-assessment records. The required detail, signatures, and professional qualifications should be checked for the particular product rather than inferred from a generic template.

The PIF rules have been implemented in phases. Beginning July 1, 2026, the remaining cosmetics categories enter the regime, and the rules then generally cover all cosmetics. The limited exception applies only to solid handmade soap manufactured at a site exempt from factory registration. Neither the word “handmade” nor the word “soap,” standing alone, creates the exception. The product form and the manufacturing site’s legal status both matter.

A qualified third party may assist with testing, record compilation, or safety work. That assistance does not remove the cosmetics manufacturer’s or importer’s obligation to verify that the records apply to the correct product and version, can be traced to reliable sources, contain the required signatures, and can be produced promptly. Responsibility stays with the cosmetics manufacturer or importer.

### Updating and Retaining the PIF

A PIF is a controlled, living record rather than a one-time compilation. It must be reviewed and updated when ingredients, formula, manufacturing method or site, label, leaflet, claims, tests, complaints, adverse events, or safety information change. Change control should record what changed, when the change was evaluated, which sections were affected, who approved the revision, and whether product registration, labeling, distribution, or advertising also requires action.

Article 7 of the Regulations for Cosmetic Product Information File Management governs duration: the PIF must be retained for at least five years, beginning the day after the product was last supplied to the market. The starting point is not the first sale, the filing date, or the date the file was assembled. Distribution records therefore should be sufficient to establish the last date of supply for each product.

Article 8 governs location. It requires retention at the manufacturer’s or importer’s address stated under Article 7(1)(7) of the Cosmetic Hygiene and Safety Act. Article 7 and Article 8 perform different functions: the former fixes the retention period and its starting point, while the latter fixes the required address by reference to the Act.

The PIF may be stored by the original manufacturer or in an electronic or cloud system only if the responsible manufacturer or importer retains complete access and can promptly retrieve and produce the file. A link to a foreign manufacturer’s portal is not enough if access can be withdrawn or if no one can identify the operative version. The records program should address access rights, backups, version control, responsible personnel, usable file formats, completeness checks, and a tested retrieval process.

Post-termination handoff deserves the same attention. Contracts with an importer, testing provider, safety assessor, or document host should prevent records from becoming inaccessible when services end. The responsible business should receive the complete current file, prior versions needed to explain changes, credentials or exports, and an index showing what has been transferred. Retention duties continue even when the product is discontinued or the commercial relationship has ended.

### Inspections, Corrective Orders, and Administrative Penalties

The competent authority ordinarily gives the cosmetics manufacturer or importer at least seven days’ advance notice of an inspection. The notice requirement is subject to exceptions for emergencies and public-interest needs. Routine readiness remains important because an exception may apply and advance notice cannot remedy missing access, inadequate version control, or unavailable source materials.

False information in a product registration or PIF may result in an administrative fine of **NT$10,000–NT$1,000,000**. Incomplete PIF documentation is treated differently: the competent authority ordinarily orders correction within a specified period, and a fine applies if the deficiency is not corrected by the deadline. Accordingly, an incomplete PIF ordinarily triggers the correction-first process rather than an immediate fine and should not be characterized as a criminal offense.

Withdrawal, recall, confiscation, or destruction is not automatic for every documentation defect. The measures available depend on the statutory conditions, the nature of the violation, whether the deficiency was corrected, the product’s safety risk, the authority’s findings, and the terms of its order. A curable missing record, evidence of a health hazard, and a failure to comply with a formal order present different legal and operational questions. The appropriate response should be based on the facts and applicable legal authority.

## 3. Labeling, Promotion, and Advertising Rules

Taiwan evaluates an advertisement from its overall presentation, including the product name, text, images, symbols, sound, and context—not from isolated words alone. False or exaggerated claims and claims of medical efficacy are prohibited. Claims that a cosmetic treats acne, has anti-inflammatory effects, or sterilizes are examples requiring particular care. The administrative fine is NT$40,000–NT$200,000 for false or exaggerated advertising and NT$600,000–NT$5,000,000 for claims of medical efficacy. An influencer or reviewer post should be reviewed under the same rules when its content and commercial context make it advertising; not every personal post is automatically an advertisement.

### Evaluate the Advertisement as a Whole

The analysis considers the product name, written and spoken words, images, symbols, video, sound, surrounding context, relationships among messages, and the consumer’s overall impression. A reviewer should examine the advertisement as finally assembled, not merely a spreadsheet of isolated phrases. A small disclaimer does not necessarily cure a dominant headline, demonstration, before-and-after image, medical setting, or spoken claim that communicates a prohibited effect.

Claims that a cosmetic treats acne, has anti-inflammatory effects, or sterilizes illustrate language requiring particular care because it may convey medical efficacy. Ingredient research does not, by itself, permit a therapeutic claim for the finished product. The evidence, concentration, formulation, mode of use, and precise consumer-facing message all matter. Claims should be limited to permissible cosmetic functions and supported at the level at which they are presented.

The administrative fine for false or exaggerated advertising is **NT$40,000–NT$200,000**. The administrative fine for claims of medical efficacy is **NT$600,000–NT$5,000,000**. These are distinct administrative ranges. The review file should identify the proposed claim, its substantiation, the final version of the advertisement, the intended channel, and the approval decision so that the business can reconstruct what was reviewed.

### Influencers, Reviewers, and Sales Partners

Whether an influencer, reviewer, or sales-partner post is advertising depends on the facts and commercial context. Relevant factors include payment, free goods, affiliate links, posting instructions, editorial control, repeated collaboration, the content itself, the parties’ relationship, and the brand’s involvement. Using a personal account does not avoid the rules when the arrangement and message make the post advertising, but not every personal post is automatically advertising.

Agreements and campaign instructions should state the permissible claims and the substantiation available to support them. They should provide for advance review, retention of approval records, and prompt correction or deletion of noncompliant material. Controls should cover captions, images, product names, comments, livestream statements, affiliate pages, reposts, and later edits—not merely the first draft of a static post.

Cross-channel consistency also matters. A distributor’s product page, an influencer’s video, customer-service answers, physical displays, and the package label may combine to create the consumer’s overall impression. The parties should preserve drafts, comments, review notes, evidence supplied to creators, approved versions, correction requests, and the final published material. Those records help establish the content and the extent of brand involvement if questions arise.

### A Pre-Market Compliance Sequence

The following sequence separates the major workstreams while allowing document collection, label design, and review to proceed in parallel where appropriate:

1. **Choose the import structure.**
2. **Identify the entity bearing the statutory responsibilities of the cosmetics manufacturer or importer.**
3. **Complete product registration before supply, sale, giveaway, public display, or consumer trial use.**
4. **Prepare, update, and retain the PIF for the required period at the required address.**
5. **Review labeling, promotion, advertising, and commercial-partner content.**
6. **Maintain procedures for inspections, corrective orders, complaints, safety information, and required follow-up.**

The sequence is a compliance map, not a representation that every earlier task must finish before all later work begins. What matters is that each applicable requirement is separately satisfied before the relevant conduct occurs and that ongoing duties—especially updates, retention, complaint handling, and safety response—continue after launch.
