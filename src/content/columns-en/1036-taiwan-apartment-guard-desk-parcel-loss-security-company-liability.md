# A Parcel Vanished From Your Taiwan Building's Guard Desk: Who Answers for It?

The guard desk signed for your parcel, and now it is gone. Do you go after the management committee, or the company that put the guard there? It depends on who employs the guard, who signed the security contract, and why the desk holds parcels at all.

A tenant away on business hears the desk took in a box holding a laptop. Back home, the box is gone and the log shows another flat's resident signed for it (an invented example). The guard works for a security company the committee hired. Whom can the tenant sue, under what rule, and by when?

Burglary follows the same framework when a guard waves a stranger upstairs. Tenants count too: "residents" (住戶) include tenants and others using a unit with the owner's consent (Condominium Administration Act art. 3(viii)).

## Three parties behind the desk

Under [article 3](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0070118&flno=3)(ix) of the Condominium Administration Act (公寓大廈管理條例), the management committee is residents elected by the owners to carry out their resolutions and maintenance. Item (xi) defines "managers and service staff" (管理服務人) as staff or management companies hired or engaged by an owners' meeting resolution, the building manager or the committee. The committee may engage management companies or staff holding central-authority registration or certification ([art. 42](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0070118&flno=42)). Where the work falls under another industry's law, such as security, the building's management body and the management company must agree by contract to hand it to a firm licensed in that industry ([Regulations on Condominium Managers and Service Staff, art. 15](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0070132&flno=15)(2)). Security firms (保全業) are licensed companies limited by shares ([Security Industry Act, art. 3](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0080081&flno=3)).

The desk staff may work for the committee, a management company or a security firm. Security staff must wear a set uniform on duty ([art. 14](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0080081&flno=14)(1)), so the uniform and name badge are the first clue.

| Basis | Who claims | Against whom | Fault and defences |
|---|---|---|---|
| Civil Code art. 184(1) | Injured resident | The guard | Intent or negligence required |
| Civil Code art. 188(1) | Injured resident | Guard's employer (company or committee), jointly | Employer escapes by proving reasonable care in selection and supervision (subject to para. 2) |
| Security Industry Act art. 15(2) | The security contract's client | Security firm, jointly with guard | No fault needed |
| Civil Code arts. 589–590 (deposit) | Depositor | Depositary (identified on the facts) | Unpaid: care as in own affairs; paid: care of a prudent manager |
| Civil Code arts. 535, 544 (mandate) | Principal (usually the committee) | Management company | Paid: care of a prudent manager |

## Tort: articles 184 and 188

Tort needs no contract. [Article 184](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=184)(1) of the Civil Code makes anyone who intentionally or negligently infringes another's rights liable. If the guard handed your parcel over unchecked, the fight is over what he failed to do. Did he check ID? Record who collected? Let people rummage through the parcel area?

[Article 188](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=188)(1) makes the employer jointly liable for an employee's infringement in the course of duty. The employer escapes by proving it took reasonable care in selecting and supervising the employee, or that the damage would have happened anyway. If the victim then goes uncompensated, the court may, on application, order the employer to pay all or part, weighing both sides' finances (para. 2). If the guard took the parcel himself, expect a dispute over whether that was "in the course of duty"; Security Industry Act art. 15(2) uses the same requirement.

If the committee employs the guard directly, it is the employer. It can sue and be sued, and as plaintiff or defendant must promptly tell the owners the gist of the case ([art. 38](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0070118&flno=38)).

## Whom does the security firm's strict liability protect?

[Security Industry Act art. 15](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0080081&flno=15) goes further. Paragraph 1 requires firms to supervise their staff and prevent harm to the client's (委任人) interests. Under paragraph 2, when staff unlawfully infringe the client's interests in the course of duty, the firm is jointly liable with them regardless of fault. There is no reasonable-care defence.

The sticking point is "client." The committee usually signs. Is a resident not named in the contract also a client? The Act does not say. Treat the committee's claim under article 15 and a resident's claim under article 188 as separate routes.

Firms must carry liability insurance with an insurer licensed by the Financial Supervisory Commission from before opening, and may not cancel it mid-term without the central competent authority's consent ([art. 9](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0080081&flno=9)). The written contract must state the security duties, the insurance cover and compensation for loss caused by failing those duties ([Enforcement Rules, art. 9](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0080082&flno=9)). After a loss, get that contract first.

For a burglary, read the contract's scope: [article 4](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0080081&flno=4)(i) lists anti-theft, fire and disaster protection of residences as security work, and whether the contract covers access control or only desk duty shapes what the guard should have done.

## Is holding parcels a deposit?

Deposit (寄託) means one party hands over a thing and the other agrees to keep it ([Civil Code art. 589](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=589)(1)). That depends on the building rules (規約), any parcel policy and the log. The rules may govern building use and residents' relations ([Act art. 23](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0070118&flno=23)(1)), so check them for custody terms, pickup deadlines and ID checks.

An unpaid depositary must take the care it takes in its own affairs; a paid one, the care of a prudent manager ([art. 590](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=590)). Whether management fees pay for parcel-holding can be argued. Deposit starts out unpaid: no fee is due unless agreed or the circumstances show the keeper would not otherwise keep it (art. 589(2)). Unless otherwise agreed, a debtor answers for its agents' and assistants' fault as its own ([art. 224](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=224)).

One rule cuts the other way. A depositary may have a third party keep the item with the depositor's consent, by custom or out of necessity ([art. 592](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=592)), and is then liable only for choosing and instructing that third party ([art. 593](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=593)(2)). If the committee is the depositary and a contracted firm did the keeping, whether its staff count as the committee's assistants or as a substitute keeper changes the committee's exposure. The text does not settle which.

Deposit claims for damages lapse one year after the deposit relationship ends ([art. 601-2](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=601-2)). Tort claims run two years from learning of the damage and who must pay, ten from the act ([art. 197](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=197)(1)).

## The neighbour who signed

If another resident signed the log, that person is the most direct target. If the item already belongs to you, you may demand its return from anyone possessing or taking it without right ([art. 767](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=767)(1)). A mix-up with the item intact may end there. If it was used or damaged, damages remain, along with the question why the desk did not check identity.

Courier and seller liability is outside this column, but the delivery record of who received the parcel and when is evidence in any of these disputes.

## How much

Unless law or contract says otherwise, damages restore the prior state, or money is paid where that is impossible or seriously difficult ([arts. 213](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=213)(1), [215](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=215)). Receipts or orders prove value. For damaged goods, the reduction in value is recoverable ([art. 196](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=196)).

## Management companies and the committee

Management companies must use the care of a prudent manager, disclose serious defects to clients and keep written records ([Regulations art. 16](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0070132&flno=16)(i), (iv), (vii)), so logs and handover notes should exist; the duty runs to the committee, so ask through it. A paid mandatary owes the same care and answers to its principal, the committee, for negligent loss ([Civil Code arts. 535](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=535), [544](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=544)). The committee's listed duties ([Act art. 36](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0070118&flno=36)) do not by themselves say it must pay a resident for a lost item.

## On the day you notice

Report to the police. Security firms must report thefts at once ([art. 4-1](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0080081&flno=4-1)), with fines of NT$60,000 to NT$300,000 for failing to ([art. 17](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0080081&flno=17)(1)(i)), so ask whether the guard already has.

Then ask the committee and company in writing for the delivery record, desk log, CCTV and duty rosters. Interested parties may ask to inspect or copy the building rules, accounting records and committee minutes when necessary ([Act art. 35](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0070118&flno=35)); the security contract is not on that list, so start with the minutes approving it.

Your own conduct counts. Leaving a parcel uncollected for weeks invites a contributory-negligence argument, and the court may reduce or deny damages ([Civil Code art. 217](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=217)(1)). Smaller sums suit [small claims court](/en/columns/taiwan-small-claims-simplified-civil-procedure); see also [our column on committees and fees](/en/columns/taiwan-apartment-management-committee-fees-rules).
