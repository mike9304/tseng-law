# Goods sold in December, returned in January: Taiwan's tax year for sales returns

December 2024 was the sale month; January 2025 was when the goods came back. In an example in its [November 20 reminder](https://www.ntbt.gov.tw/singlehtml/41ae3594197f4f69b47753ce08188516?cntId=a5e09a22d770413d8a3d1c136edaf2fd), the National Taxation Bureau of Taipei described a customer returning goods after discovering damage that made them unusable. The sales return belonged in 2025. It could not be moved back to the 2024 sale year.

For a US-owned Taiwan subsidiary, the original sale, the customer's complaint and the actual return may have different dates. Which event does the January adjustment record? The reconciliation needs to connect the returned goods with the earlier invoice.

## The return year follows the actual event

The bureau's notice concerns merchandise returned for defects, a mismatch with specifications or other reasons. When sale and return fall in different accounting years, the sales return belongs to the year of the actual return. This is existing treatment. The Ministry of Finance's [Tax Portal explanation](https://www.etax.nat.gov.tw/etwmain/tax-info/understanding/tax-q-and-a/national/profit-seeking-enterprise-income-tax/operating-income-calculation/DrV2Gm2) also states that a return occurring in the following year is handled as that following year's sales return.

That timing question is narrower than deciding whether a customer has a contractual right to return goods. A complaint may explain the reason for a return, while records of the returned goods help establish that it occurred. The November notice uses an illustrative example; it reports no court decision or penalty against a named company.

## Recognition also depends on proof

[Article 19 of the Regulations Governing Assessment of Profit-Seeking Enterprise Income Tax](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0340051&flno=19) addresses recognition of sales returns. It provides a route involving reversal in the books and documents obtained under the Regulations Governing the Use of Uniform Invoices. Other conclusive evidence can also qualify. The evidence must prove the return. A missing standard document does not eliminate that alternative.

If the business cannot produce the relevant documents or evidence, Article 19 denies recognition of the sales return. The sale remains recognized. Taxable income attributable to it is calculated using the applicable profit standard for the same trade.

Export returns receive express treatment in paragraph 2. Customs return records and related supporting documents can establish the return, subject to the tax authority's verification. For a Taiwan subsidiary handling goods coming back from overseas, those records can connect the physical shipment with the original sale. The provision does not guarantee that an internal adjustment alone will suffice.

## Invoice records depend on how the sale was invoiced

Under [Article 20 of the Regulations Governing the Use of Uniform Invoices](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0340082&flno=20), the treatment of paper invoice documents depends on whether the buyer is a business and whether the original sale has already been declared. Those invoice mechanics support the transaction record; they do not supply a choice of income-tax year.

Electronic invoices have a separate provision. [Article 20-1](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0340082&flno=20-1) requires electronic issuance, transmission or receipt of the return or allowance certificate after buyer and seller agree to the return or allowance. The seller retains its accounting record and transmits a record to the platform, while the buyer receives its copy. For a business buyer, that copy also supports the corresponding input-tax adjustment.

The Taiwan finance team's reconciliation can link the original invoice, the records proving the return and the entry made in the return year. For the bureau's December-to-January example, that leaves the sale in the earlier year and the sales-return adjustment in the following year, supported by records of what actually came back.
