# Scalp Burns or Broken Hair After a Taiwan Salon Visit, and the Prepaid Card Left Over

You signed a form before the bleach promising not to dispute the result. That night your scalp blisters. Can you still claim? Taiwan's Civil Code art. 222 answers part of it: liability for intent or gross negligence cannot be waived in advance.

Someone pays NT$6,800 at a Taichung salon for bleach and color. By evening the crown burns and blisters; a week later the bleached hair starts snapping off. Their salon prepaid card: NT$10,000 loaded, NT$6,400 left (an invented example).

## Which rules fit a hair salon

Health ministry mandatory terms for beauty contracts (美容定型化契約應記載及不得記載事項, announced Jan. 6, 2023, No. 1110101594, in force July 1, 2023) detail pre-treatment questions, records and refunds. Yet [those terms](https://www.ey.gov.tw/Page/DFB720D019CCCB0A/7fbd4cbf-b00e-49ad-8718-b4e260f79f75) define beauty as makeup, facial care and similar acts other than slimming, and their service list has no hair treatments. The Food and Drug Administration's [amendment table for the slimming-beauty terms](https://www.fda.gov.tw/tc/includes/GetFile.ashx?id=f637418303356588154) places facial care and hairdressing (理髮美髮) in the gift-certificate annex's "beauty and hairdressing services" category.

So liability for the perm, color or bleach itself turns on the general Civil Code and Consumer Protection Act. The salon's prepaid card falls under the gift-certificate terms. Medical procedures at clinics are out of scope.

## When the color comes out wrong

[Civil Code art. 490](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=490) defines a contract for work (承攬): one side completes a piece of work and the other pays on completion; materials the contractor supplies are presumed part of the price. Dyeing hair to an agreed shade for a fee can be examined under it.

Contractors must deliver the agreed quality, free of defects that reduce value or fitness for use ([art. 492](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=492)). For a defect, the customer may set a reasonable deadline for repair ([art. 493](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=493)(1)); if it passes, the customer may repair elsewhere and recover necessary costs (493(2)), though the contractor may refuse a repair whose cost is excessive (493(3)). If the contractor misses that deadline, refuses under 493(3) (which makes 493(2) inapplicable, both self-repair and cost recovery), or the defect cannot be repaired, the customer may cancel or cut the price, but cannot cancel over a minor defect ([art. 494](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=494)). Where the contractor is to blame, damages are available too ([art. 495](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=495)(1)).

Start with a redo request, deadline in writing.

## Blisters are a bodily injury

Burns weigh more than a bad shade. When non-performance of a contract infringes personality rights, articles 192 to 195 and 197 apply ([art. 227-1](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=227-1)), so a contract claim can include compensation for pain and suffering. Incomplete performance also supports claims for further loss ([art. 227](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=227)).

Whoever intentionally or negligently injures another's rights must compensate ([art. 184](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=184)(1)), and an employer is jointly liable for an employee's acts in the course of work unless it proves due care in hiring and supervision, or that the harm would have occurred anyway; if the victim then cannot recover, the court may, on the victim's petition, order the employer to pay all or part, weighing both sides' finances ([art. 188](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=188)(1), (2)).

Injury to body or health that reduces working capacity or increases living needs must be compensated ([art. 193](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=193)(1)), and non-pecuniary loss can be claimed ([art. 195](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=195)(1)). What about days off work for treatment? Lost working capacity under art. 193(1) covers that; keep leave records, pay slips and the doctor's advice.

## Safety under Consumer Protection Act art. 7

Service businesses must ensure the safety reasonably expected under the technology or professional standards of the time ([CPA art. 7](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0170001&flno=7)(1)). Services that may endanger life, body or health require a conspicuous warning and emergency instructions (7(2)). Breach that causes harm brings joint liability, which a court may reduce if the business proves it was not at fault (7(3)).

Proving it met that standard is the business's burden ([art. 7-1](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0170001&flno=7-1)(1)), though a service is not unsafe merely because a better one appears later (7-1(2)). In suits under the Act, punitive damages of up to one, three or five times the loss are available for negligence, gross negligence or intent ([art. 51](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0170001&flno=51)). Advertising binds too: obligations to consumers may not fall below what the ad promised ([art. 22](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0170001&flno=22)(1)). Screenshot any "damage-free bleach" post.

## The "no complaints" form

Liability for intent or gross negligence cannot be waived in advance ([Civil Code art. 222](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=222)). A form the salon drafted for many customers is also tested under consumer law: terms that breach good faith and are clearly unfair to consumers are void ([CPA art. 12](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0170001&flno=12)(1)), and unclear terms are read in the consumer's favor ([art. 11](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0170001&flno=11)(2)).

Courts may reduce or cancel damages where the victim's fault contributed ([Civil Code art. 217](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=217)(1)), including failing to warn of a serious risk the other side could not know or failing to limit the harm (217(2)). Not mentioning a recent bleach elsewhere when asked, or skipping a doctor while the burn worsens, invites that argument.

## The product box and the doctor's note

Cosmetic packaging must clearly show the name, use, directions, full ingredients, precautions, maker or importer, dates and batch number ([Cosmetic Hygiene and Safety Act art. 7](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=L0030013&flno=7)(1)). Photograph the bleach and dye containers used on you.

The beauty terms' two-year record duty skips hair salons, so keep your own file: scalp photos, a dermatologist's diagnosis, receipts, messages.

## NT$6,400 left on the card

On April 10, 2020, the Ministry of Economic Affairs and eight other agencies announced the Mandatory and Prohibited Terms for Standard-Form Gift Certificates (商品(服務)禮券定型化契約應記載及不得記載事項) effective January 1, 2021. Their annex lists "beauty and hairdressing services (facial care and hairdressing)" under the Ministry of Health and Welfare ([Executive Yuan posting](https://www.ey.gov.tw/Page/DFB720D019CCCB0A/5486b053-67e1-47e8-90e4-a9a59529dc87)). Published mandatory terms form part of the contract even if left out ([CPA art. 17](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0170001&flno=17)(5)).

Gift certificates here mean a voucher, magnetic-stripe card, chip card or similar issued for a stated amount and redeemable for that value; free coupons and electronic stored value cards under their own Act are excluded. A card showing a loaded amount fits. Whether a "10 haircuts" pass with no amount fits is not clear from the text.

Required on the card: issuer details, face value, serial number, sale date, usage, complaint channel and performance guarantee (Point 1). A lost registered card can be reissued for no more than NT$100 per magnetic or chip card (Point 3). App-only balances may show fewer items, but the issuer must disclose the rest and offer anytime transaction checks (Point 6). Screenshot the balance after each visit.

Refunds: the card must state the procedure and amount; any fee may not exceed 3% of the refund, and no fee is allowed when the refund is for a reason not attributable to the consumer (Point 4). Here 3% is NT$192. Whether quitting the salon after an injury counts as such a reason may be disputed.

Prohibited: expiry dates, barring use of an unused balance, excusing the duty to serve or adding charges, unreasonable limits, unilateral cancellation by the issuer, terms worse than paying cash, advance waiver of the issuer's intent or gross negligence, and "ads for reference only." An issuer that does not itself provide the service must also state its paid-in capital or Taiwan operating funds and the salon's details (Point 1), needs at least NT$30 million of either unless adjusted or approved (Point 5), may use only a bank guarantee, trust or escrow (Point 5(3)), and may not disclaim liability for your disputes with the salon.

Issuers must keep a performance guarantee for at least one year from sale (bank guarantee, trust, escrow, peer guarantee and the like) and remain liable afterwards (Points 1, 2).

## Complaints, mediation and court

Complaints go to the business, a consumer group or a consumer service center, and the business must handle them within 15 days of filing ([CPA art. 43](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0170001&flno=43)); if it is not properly handled, the local consumer protection officer comes next (43(3)), then mediation if still unresolved ([art. 44](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0170001&flno=44)). Claims of NT$100,000 or less use small claims procedure ([Code of Civil Procedure art. 436-8](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=436-8)(1)); see the [small claims](/en/columns/taiwan-small-claims-simplified-civil-procedure) and [gym injury](/en/columns/taiwan-gym-injury-lawsuit) columns. Consumer suits may be filed where the consumer relationship arose ([CPA art. 47](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0170001&flno=47)).

Two clocks run. Defect claims and cancellation lapse one year after discovery ([Civil Code art. 514](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=514)(1)); tort claims two years after learning of the damage and who is liable, or ten years after the act ([art. 197](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=197)(1)).
