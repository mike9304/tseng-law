# Can You Get Your Money Back From a Taiwan Pre-Need Funeral Contract? Refunds, the 75% Trust and Extra Charges

Many people assume a pre-need funeral contract in Taiwan is money gone for good. The Ministry of the Interior's mandatory terms say otherwise. When a funeral business uses its standard-form contract, a written cancellation within 14 days of signing brings back every dollar paid.

Later exits still return money. A buyer who paid in full gets back the ratio fixed in the contract, and that ratio cannot be set below 80% of the total price.

## Which rules apply

The Mortuary Service Administration Act (殯葬管理條例) defines a pre-need funeral contract as an agreement that, after one party or a person it names dies, the other will provide funeral services ([art. 2](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0020040&flno=2)(16)).

There are two sets: [self-use](https://www.ey.gov.tw/Page/DFB720D019CCCB0A/fef0468e-473b-41ac-9764-14ae9b1beb22), for the buyer's own funeral, and [family-use](https://www.ey.gov.tw/Page/DFB720D019CCCB0A/6749aa4d-a2af-4d88-b5d8-4d094c44a820), for a relative's. Both were issued on June 27, 2006, took effect on January 1, 2007, and were partly amended on June 12, 2014.

Why do these terms beat the printed contract? A conflicting standard-form clause is void, and the mandatory terms form part of the contract even if omitted ([Consumer Protection Act art. 17](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0170001&flno=17)(4)–(5)).

## What comes back, and when

Counting starts on the signing date (self-use point 18, family-use point 16).

| Timing | Refund | Deadline |
|---|---|---|
| Written cancellation within 14 days | Everything paid | Days set in contract, max. 30 from cancellation |
| Termination after 14 days, paid in full | Total price × ratio in contract (at least 80%) | Days set in contract, max. 30 from termination |
| Termination after 14 days, instalments | Amount paid − total price × ratio in contract (max. 20%) | Same |
| Ratio left blank | Full price, or everything paid on instalments | Same |

After 14 days, the cost of any service already begun is deducted. Buyers who signed as multi-level marketing distributors are refunded under the Multi-Level Marketing Supervision Act ([arts. 20](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0150017&flno=20)–22, 24), and door-to-door sales follow the Consumer Protection Act's rules for them (same points).

Take a NT$300,000 contract paid in full with an 80% ratio. Termination returns NT$240,000. An instalment buyer who has paid NT$120,000 under a 20% deduction loses NT$60,000 and keeps NT$60,000 (an invented example).

If the business changes hands, buyers must be told and may terminate for a full refund of what they paid (self-use point 19, family-use point 17). Short of ending it, the buyer may review the contract once a year and swap items or specifications at the same total price (self-use point 17, family-use point 15).

Sellers can cancel too. Once missed payments add up to two months and a demand allowing at least 30 days goes unanswered, it may cancel in writing and keep payments as damages, but no more than 20% of the total price; any excess must be returned within 30 days (self-use point 20(1), family-use point 18(1)).

## Where the money sits

Only a company licensed locally as a funeral ceremony business may sell these contracts, and only after filing proof of size, its standard-form contract and its trust agreement for city or county approval ([art. 50](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0020040&flno=50)(1)–(2)). An unlicensed seller faces fines of NT$600,000 to NT$3 million, as do its agents and employees ([art. 89](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0020040&flno=89)(1)). Licensed sellers lacking the required size or approval face NT$60,000 to NT$300,000 and a deadline to correct; if they miss it, repeat fines follow, and serious cases can cost the licence (art. 89(2)).

Three quarters goes into trust. [Article 51](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0020040&flno=51) requires 75% of everything prepaid to be handed to a trust company by the end of the following month, untouchable except for performance, cancellation, termination or as the Act provides. The mandatory terms are narrower: absent another legal rule, withdrawals are allowed only for termination, cancellation or performance after the death of the buyer (self-use point 14(2)) or of the person to be served (family-use point 12(2)). The trust is settled each December 31, counting unrealized losses, and the business must top up any shortfall below 75% in cash ([art. 53](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0020040&flno=53)(1)–(2)).

What if the company goes bankrupt? Trust assets stay outside the bankruptcy estate ([art. 54](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0020040&flno=54)(2)). On bankruptcy, loss of licence or the other events in art. 54(3), the trust company returns the assets to consumers still waiting for service, with local approval. Each gets back, in principle, what they paid; shares go pro rata if the assets fall short (art. 54(4)).

## When the buyer was a parent

Self-use buyers name a contract executor (契約執行人) who acts in their own name after the death (point 9). If no executor exists and the buyer cannot name one, the heir order in [Civil Code art. 1138](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=1138) decides; that order is covered in a [column on intestate succession](/en/columns/taiwan-intestate-succession-order-shares-representation). Where the executor and the buyer's relatives and friends disagree, the contract and the executor prevail (self-use point 11), and family and funeral arrangers must respect funeral wishes an adult with legal capacity left in a will or written statement ([art. 61](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0020040&flno=61)).

When the buyer dies before paying in full, the business still performs, and the executor pays the balance (self-use point 16(3)). An executor who refuses the balance before service lets the business cancel and keep up to 20% of the total; the rest goes back within 30 days and passes under the succession part of the Civil Code (self-use point 20(2)). If the buyer dies in an air or sea disaster, war or other force majeure and the business cannot serve, it must end the contract and refund everything paid (self-use point 18(4)).

The family-use version works differently. If the parent who bought it dies first with money still owed, the heirs choose whether to keep paying. If no one does, the business may cancel and refund on the same 80% and 20% terms that govern a termination, with the refund passing under succession law (family-use point 16(5)). The force majeure refund applies if the person to be served dies that way (family-use point 16(4)). A fully paid family-use contract whose buyer dies first is not addressed. The starting point is then [Civil Code art. 1148](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=1148)(1): heirs take over the deceased's property rights and obligations, except those personal to the deceased.

## Charges that were not in the contract

[Article 49](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0020040&flno=49)(1) is blunt. Funeral businesses must sign written contracts, have no claim to fees not stated in them, and may not invent charges after signing. Price lists must be displayed and kept on hand ([art. 48](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0020040&flno=48)), and breaches lead to an order to correct, then fines of NT$30,000 to NT$150,000 ([art. 88](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=D0020040&flno=88)).

Some extras are legitimate: listed government fees outside the total price; changes to items or specifications, or resulting out-of-area service, requested by the executor (self-use) or the buyer (family-use) and priced separately (point 7 of each); and service beyond the agreed counties, priced by separate agreement of both sides (point 5). The contract may not use vague items such as "one funeral" or "one ceremony team", nor demand more money because of currency swings, inflation or trust losses (prohibited terms 2–3).

If the business fails to start within four hours of the death notice, or after a demand, the self-use executor, or the family-use buyer who reported the death, may cancel in writing, recover everything paid and claim punitive damages of at least twice the total price, unless the failure is not the business's fault. Under self-use point 20(3), refund and damages pass under succession law; the family-use rule is point 18(2).

## Disputes

A consumer may complain to the business, a consumer group or a consumer service centre; the business must deal with it within 15 days of the complaint, after which a local consumer protection officer and then a mediation committee can step in ([Consumer Protection Act arts. 43](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0170001&flno=43)–[44](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0170001&flno=44)).
