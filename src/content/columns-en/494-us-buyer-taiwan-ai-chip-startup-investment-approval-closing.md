Even a small stake in a private Taiwan AI-chip company can require a foreign-investment application before the investment proceeds. The Ministry of Economic Affairs’ [official guidance](https://investtaiwan.nat.gov.tw/faqQContent?lang=cht&search=19) applies that requirement regardless of the amount invested when the company is outside Taiwan’s listed, over-the-counter and emerging-stock markets.

A company incorporated in the United States may fall within Taiwan’s separate regime for Mainland Area investors because of its ownership or control.

## The buyer behind the acquisition vehicle

Under [Article 3 of the Measures Governing Investment Permit to the People of Mainland Area](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawAll.aspx?pcode=Q0040015), a third-area company falls within the regime if Mainland Area persons or entities directly or indirectly hold more than 30% of its shares or capital, or control it. The tests are alternatives. Exactly 30% does not meet the ownership threshold, while a lower holding still leaves the control question open.

A list of the acquisition vehicle’s immediate shareholders may therefore be incomplete for this purpose. Where companies or funds sit above it, the ownership chart needs the relevant upstream entities and percentages. Those holdings are assessed under the Ministry’s [interpretation on indirect ownership](https://www.moea.gov.tw/Mns/dir_e/investment/wHandDirApply_File.ashx?file_id=372).

The Ministry’s [control interpretation](https://www.moea.gov.tw/Mns/dir_e/investment/wHandDirApply_File.ashx?file_id=374) addresses voting agreements, contractual power over financial, operational or personnel policies, and rights concerning a majority of the board or its votes. Shareholder agreements, board appointment provisions and relevant side letters may reveal rights absent from a fully diluted cap table. A veto or protective right still needs to be read in context; its name does not establish control.

A third-area company within Article 3 does not make its Taiwan investment under the ordinary Act For Investment by Foreign Nationals. The applicable permission regime and permitted investment scope differ. Transaction documents that assume the ordinary approval route before resolving ownership and control may therefore rest on the wrong premise.

## Approval must cover the deal that actually closes

For an ordinary foreign investor, [Articles 4 and 8 of the Act For Investment by Foreign Nationals](https://law.moj.gov.tw/ENG/LawClass/LawAll.aspx?pcode=J0040002) cover shareholdings and require an application supported by an investment plan and relevant documents. Whether the buyer purchases existing shares or subscribes for new ones belongs in both the filing and the agreement. Legal names, share classes, quantities and investment amounts should describe the same transaction across those documents and the capitalization table.

A financing round or share conversion can change the proposed ownership before closing. Counsel then needs to assess whether the application or supporting records must change. If the seller is transferring an existing approved foreign investment, [Article 10](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0040002&flno=10) requires the transferor and transferee to apply jointly for approval. The buyer therefore needs the seller’s participation in the approval process to complete that transfer.

The target’s registration and location help identify where to file. The Ministry’s [agency-routing guidance](https://investtaiwan.nat.gov.tw/faqQContent?lang=cht&search=2) distinguishes the Department of Investment Review’s work from matters delegated to science-park or industrial-park authorities. This analysis concerns shares in a private Taiwan company; offshore holding-company acquisitions, asset purchases and public-company transactions need separate assessment.

An approval condition in the purchase agreement is easier to use when the parties have also agreed who supplies ownership records and answers the authority’s questions. They may need to decide which changes require the other party’s consent and how to handle an approval condition neither side anticipated. The outside date has to leave room for that work. [Article 8](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0040002&flno=8) measures its decision periods from completion of the application procedures, not from signing. It supplies no promise that the acquisition will close a fixed number of days after the agreement is signed.

If the vehicle, funding or control rights change, the earlier analysis and supporting documents need to be revisited. At closing, the relevant record is the authority’s approval, including its conditions and whether the approved investor and transaction match the deal being completed. A filing receipt cannot establish those points.

## The separate merger-control timetable

Foreign-investment approval does not resolve Taiwan’s merger-control requirements. The Fair Trade Commission uses a definition of merger that reaches beyond two companies combining into one. [Fair Trade Act Article 10](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0150002&flno=10) includes holding or acquiring at least one third of another enterprise’s voting shares or capital, acquiring all or a major part of its business or assets, and specified forms of control.

Whether a transaction within that definition requires notification is a further assessment. [Article 11](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0150002&flno=11) sets triggers including market-share and announced sales thresholds, and [Article 12](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawAll.aspx?pcode=J0150002) provides exemptions. The parties, relevant markets and current thresholds all matter. Neither a small target nor a minority stake settles the filing question on its own.

The more-than-30% test discussed earlier concerns Mainland Area ownership of a third-area investor. The at-least-one-third test here concerns an enterprise’s holding in another enterprise for merger-control purposes. They examine different relationships and can produce separate requirements within the same acquisition.

The closing file therefore needs a documented assessment of whether merger notification is required. Where it is, the FTC’s communications and the applicable statutory waiting restrictions determine when the parties may proceed. Article 11 allows the FTC to shorten or extend that period under the statute. Submission of a filing is not itself permission to complete the transaction, just as submitting the investment application is not investment approval.

For an initial [Taiwan investment consultation](https://tseng-law.com/en/contact), a nonconfidential outline of the proposed deal, the target’s location and the signing or closing deadline is enough to introduce the matter. Cap tables, chip designs, passports and banking details should wait until document arrangements are confirmed with the attorney. US regulatory and tax questions require separate advice from appropriately qualified US counsel.
