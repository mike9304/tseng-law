# Consigned a Luxury Bag in Taiwan and Never Got Paid? Your Rights Against the Shop

Many people assume that once a consignment shop (寄賣店) takes their bag, the shop has bought it. Under Taiwan's Civil Code, a consignment arrangement is usually a commission agency (行紀): the shop sells in its own name, but on your account. The proceeds, less the agreed commission, and any unsold item belong back with you.

A Chanel bag goes to a secondhand luxury shop in Taipei with its price set at NT$150,000 (an invented example). Two months later the shop says it sold for NT$120,000, delays settlement, then shuts.

## Commission agency, sale or storage?

[Civil Code Article 576](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=576) defines commission agency as a business that, in its own name and for another's account, buys or sells movables or carries out other commercial transactions for remuneration. Consignment shops fit that description. Rules on mandate (委任) apply where the commission-agency section is silent ([Article 577](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=577)).

Not every deal is one. If the shop pays on the spot and takes the item, it is a sale. If you only left it for safekeeping, it is a bailment ([Article 589](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=589), paragraph 1). The consignment contract and receipt (寄賣單) decide: who sets the price, and what you receive when it sells. If the shop was free to set the price, Article 580's shortfall rule below is harder to use.

The shop itself acquires rights and bears obligations toward buyers ([Article 578](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=578)). If the buyer fails to pay, the shop must perform directly to you unless the contract or custom provides otherwise ([Article 579](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=579)). "The buyer hasn't paid" is a weak excuse unless your contract makes settlement wait for the buyer.

## Sold below your price

Under [Article 580](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=580), an agent that sells below the consignor's designated price must make up the difference. In the example, that is NT$30,000, and the same applies if the shop cut the price without consent after months unsold. A sale above your price, say at NT$180,000, gives the extra NT$30,000 to you ([Article 581](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=581)).

The commission comes from the contract. The agent may claim remuneration, storage and transport fees by agreement or custom, plus expenses and interest spent for you ([Article 582](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=582)). Whether the cut comes off the sale price or your set price depends on the wording.

## When the money is due

Because mandate rules apply, the shop must hand over money received in handling the matter ([Article 541](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=541), paragraph 1), report on progress and give a full account when the relationship ends ([Article 540](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=540)). Money it used for itself carries interest from the day of use, plus damages ([Article 542](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=542)).

Settlement dates are usually in the contract. Absent one in law, contract or the nature of the debt, the creditor may demand payment at any time ([Article 315](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=315)). With a fixed date, delay starts when it passes; without one, it starts when a demand goes unanswered ([Article 229](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=229), paragraphs 1 and 2). Late money carries statutory interest of 5% a year unless a higher rate was agreed ([Article 233](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=233), paragraph 1; [Article 203](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=203)).

## When the item disappears

"Storage isn't our job" is a common answer. Displaying and keeping unsold goods is part of the agent's mandate (Article 577). A paid agent must follow instructions and use a good administrator's care ([Article 535](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=535)), and answers for losses from negligence or exceeding authority ([Article 544](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=544)). Staff using or lending your bag raises that question.

An agent must act personally unless you consent or custom or necessity allows a substitute ([Article 537](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=537)). Handing your item to another business, such as a warehouse, in breach of that makes the shop answer for the third party's acts as its own; a permitted handover limits liability to choosing and instructing the third party ([Article 538](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=538), paragraphs 1 and 2). Moving it to another branch of the same shop stays under Article 535.

Sold items not yet handed to the buyer are different. [Article 583](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=583), paragraph 1, applies bailment rules while the agent holds goods bought or sold for your account. If paid for custody, the bailee owes a good administrator's care ([Article 590](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=590)), may not use the item or let others use it without consent ([Article 591](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=591), paragraph 1), and in principle keeps it personally ([Article 592](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=592)).

Insurance is separate. For such sold goods, the agent need not insure unless you instructed otherwise (Article 583, paragraph 2). No provision covers insuring unsold goods; put any insurance instruction on the receipt.

Two routes lead to compensation. In contract, you can claim damages where the shop is responsible for performance becoming impossible ([Article 226](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=226), paragraph 1), and staff intent or negligence counts as the shop's own unless the parties agreed otherwise ([Article 224](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=224)). In tort, a person who wrongfully infringes another's rights by intent or negligence pays ([Article 184](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=184), paragraph 1), and an employer is jointly liable for staff acting on duty ([Article 188](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=188), paragraph 1). Proof of due care in selecting and supervising staff, or that the harm was unavoidable, frees the employer, though on the victim's application the court may still order full or partial compensation given both sides' finances (paragraph 2). A damaged item supports a claim for its lost value ([Article 196](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=196)).

Watch the clock. Tort claims lapse two years after you learn of the damage and who is liable, or ten years after the act ([Article 197](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=197), paragraph 1). Claims for remuneration, expenses or damages under a bailment contract lapse one year after the bailment ends ([Article 601-2](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=601-2)). How far that one-year rule reaches into commission agency is not settled by the text, so plan around the shortest period.

## Unsold items and items already sold

To take back unsold goods, rely on the mandate rules: either party may end a mandate at any time, but must pay damages for ending it at a time unfavourable to the other, unless forced by causes not attributable to it ([Article 549](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=549)). If an unsold or withdrawn item is not collected within a reasonable period, the agent may set a deadline, then auction it, pay itself what the consignment owes, and deposit any surplus ([Articles 586](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=586) and [585](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=585)). Leaving Taiwan soon? Fix a collection date in writing.

Once a buyer has the item, a sale within the shop's authority stands between shop and buyer, and your claim is for the money. Even a sale after you withdrew the item may leave the buyer as owner, if the buyer neither knew nor was grossly negligent in not knowing that the shop lacked authority ([Article 948](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=948), paragraph 1; [Article 801](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=801)). Either way, pursue the shop.

If the shop withholds an item as "fake", ask for the authentication basis in writing.

## Does consumer protection law apply?

[Consumer Protection Act Article 2](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0170001&flno=2), item 1, defines a consumer as someone who transacts, uses goods or receives services for consumption. Whether a person consigning their own goods for sale qualifies is not answered by the text. As of November 3, 2026, the Executive Yuan's lists of mandatory terms and model contracts include none for secondhand consignment.

[Civil Code Article 247-1](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=247-1) applies regardless: in a contract built on one party's pre-drafted terms, clauses that exempt or reduce that party's liability or restrict the other's rights are void where clearly unfair. A printed "no responsibility for loss or theft" line is a candidate.

## If the owner vanishes

Taking another's property one holds, with intent of unlawful ownership, is embezzlement, punishable by up to five years' imprisonment among other penalties ([Criminal Code Article 335](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0000001&flno=335), paragraph 1); for property held in the course of business, six months to five years ([Article 336](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0000001&flno=336), paragraph 2). Someone handling another's affairs who, intending unlawful gain or harm, breaches that duty and causes property loss commits breach of trust ([Article 342](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0000001&flno=342), paragraph 1). Unless the owner is your relative, these offences do not require a complaint for prosecution: [Article 324](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=C0000001&flno=324), paragraph 2, applied through Articles 338 and 343, requires one only between certain relatives. Late settlement alone does not show intent; a complaint needs facts on where the goods and money went, and whether other consignors suffered the same.

Money recovery is a separate civil claim; for modest sums, see [small claims and simplified procedure](/en/columns/taiwan-small-claims-simplified-civil-procedure). Keep the contract and receipt, photos showing serial numbers, the warranty card, price messages, settlement notices, dated screenshots of listings, and your written Article 540 request for an account.
