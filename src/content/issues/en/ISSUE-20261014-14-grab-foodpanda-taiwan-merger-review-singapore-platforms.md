# Grab–foodpanda in Taiwan: the closing date remains open

Taiwan's Fair Trade Commission (FTC) said on [July 22, 2026](https://www.ftc.gov.tw/internet/enterprise/doc/docDetail.aspx?docid=18469&uid=126) that it had extended its review of Grab's proposed acquisition of foodpanda's Taiwan business through October 27. In that notice the FTC said the post-combination market would still be a duopoly, and that it needed further analysis of whether Uber's shareholding would affect Uber Eats' and Grab's incentives and ability to compete, including unilateral or coordinated effects similar to a horizontal combination. It did not find that those effects exist.

[CTWANT reported on October 13](https://www.ctwant.com/article/501944/) that, in an October 12 statement, Taiwan's New Power Party asked the FTC to reject the acquisition and not to approve it subject to conditions. The party said Grab's equity tie with Uber could leave two delivery apps without real competition. Separately, it raised national-security concerns about map and user data, including Huawei links and possible access if data passed through Chinese systems or partners. Those are the party's allegations. CTWANT also said the FTC review was still open.

[Next Apple reported on October 13](https://news.nextapple.com/life/20261013/16F2EF93CFDE9302C98305A6118E5A0E) that Grab had put out six statements after legislators and civic groups called on the FTC to reject the deal. Grab described itself as Singapore-headquartered and independently managed, and the statements cover Uber's voting stake and Grab's account of Taiwan map and user data. CTWANT's account of the New Power Party and Next Apple's account of Grab are opposing public positions. Neither is an FTC finding. For a Singapore or other overseas buyer, the immediate question is commercial: what can the parties promise about a Taiwan closing while the regulator is still examining the transaction? October 27 does not say what the signing parties can promise each other.

## Article 10's five routes

Taiwan's [Fair Trade Act, Article 10](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0150002&flno=10) defines a combination through five routes. Article 10 treats the following as a combination: merging with another enterprise; holding or acquiring shares or capital contributions reaching one-third or more of the other enterprise's voting shares or total capital; taking a transfer or lease of all or a major part of another enterprise's business or property; regularly operating jointly with another enterprise or operating under another enterprise's entrustment; or directly or indirectly controlling another enterprise's business operations or the appointment or dismissal of its personnel. On the share route, the calculation adds shares or capital contributions held or acquired by enterprises in a control-and-subordination relationship with the enterprise, and by subordinate enterprises controlled by the same enterprise or enterprises. A buyer assessing a Taiwan platform deal therefore needs the proposed asset, share and control terms, not just the name given to the agreement.

[Article 11](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0150002&flno=11) requires a prior filing if the combination would bring an enterprise's market share to one-third or more, if one participating enterprise's market share reaches one-quarter or more, or if the preceding fiscal year's sales of the participating enterprises exceed the amount the FTC has announced. Those sales add in the sales of enterprises that have a control-and-subordination relationship with a participating enterprise, and of subordinate enterprises under the common control of the same enterprise or enterprises, by the method the FTC announces. The cited public reports do not provide the figures needed to test these filing thresholds for this transaction.

The signing parties need a corporate chart that shows voting rights and who controls whom, a description of the Taiwan business being acquired, and the sales and share figures used in the filing. A group chart that stops at the immediate signing entities may miss relationships Article 10 or 11 requires the parties to consider.

## October 27 ends the extended review period

Under [Article 11](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0150002&flno=11), the parties may not combine during the first 30 working days after the FTC accepts a complete filing. The commission may shorten or extend that period when necessary and must notify the filing businesses in writing. An extension cannot exceed 60 working days; for an extended case, the FTC must make a decision under Article 13.

In its [July 22 notice](https://www.ftc.gov.tw/internet/enterprise/doc/docDetail.aspx?docid=18469&uid=126), the FTC says Grab submitted a complete filing on June 16 and explains its 60-working-day extension through October 27. That is the agency's stated review deadline. It does not promise a decision on that day, still less a closing. On [August 17](https://www.ftc.gov.tw/internet/main/doc/docDetail.aspx?docid=18484&uid=126), the FTC said the review remained open and that it had not taken any particular position in advance.

[Article 11](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0150002&flno=11) allows parties to proceed if the FTC has failed to send an extension notice by the relevant deadline or, in an extended case, failed to make the required decision. But it expressly withholds that route if the filing businesses agreed to a further extension or the filing contains false or untrue information. A deal team should verify the actual notice, filing status and any agreed extension before treating the clock as permission to close.

## Uber's stake and the data claims are still contested

On [July 22, the FTC said](https://www.ftc.gov.tw/internet/enterprise/doc/docDetail.aspx?docid=18469&uid=126) that Uber then held 13% of Grab's equity and 3.7% of its voting rights. Those are different measures. [Next Apple's October 13 account](https://news.nextapple.com/life/20261013/16F2EF93CFDE9302C98305A6118E5A0E) reports Grab's description of Uber holding roughly under 4% non-controlling voting shares, which Grab says does not give Uber control over Grab's management or business decisions, and Grab's statement that an Uber representative left the board in July 2026. The FTC's July figures and Grab's later statement come from different dates and speakers.

[CTWANT's October 13 report](https://www.ctwant.com/article/501944/) attributes concern about map and user information, Huawei links and possible access from China to the New Power Party. [Next Apple on October 13](https://news.nextapple.com/life/20261013/16F2EF93CFDE9302C98305A6118E5A0E) reports Grab's statement that claims of access to Taiwan user or map data through the Huawei cooperation are false; that cooperation with Huawei Petal Maps is strictly limited to eight Southeast Asian markets, involves no user data, and does not include Taiwan; and that from the first day of Taiwan operations Grab will use its own map. That is Grab's account, not an FTC finding. [Newtalk reported on October 12](https://newtalk.tw/news/view/2026-10-12/1065094) that Grab said all Taiwan data would be stored in Taiwan and Singapore and would not be stored in mainland China, Hong Kong, or Macau. These are allegations and company assurances, not findings of a leak, a statutory data-transfer conclusion or FTC approval.

A buyer can set those statements against the voting records, board minutes, investor agreements, and the Taiwan data and map design, and see whether the filing and the contract say the same thing.

## What the purchase agreement can say about closing

For a filed combination, [Article 13](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0150002&flno=13) says the FTC may not prohibit it if its overall economic benefits outweigh the disadvantages of restricting competition. For a decision on an extended-review filing under Article 11, paragraph 8, the FTC may attach conditions or burdens so that overall economic benefit remains greater than the disadvantage of restricting competition. The [August 17 notice](https://www.ftc.gov.tw/internet/main/doc/docDetail.aspx?docid=18484&uid=126) lists prohibition, a decision not to prohibit, and a decision not to prohibit but with burdens or conditions. It said Grab's case was still under review and that the commission had not taken any particular position in advance. [CTWANT on October 13](https://www.ctwant.com/article/501944/) still described the review as open. Those sources do not report which type has been chosen.

A Taiwan acquisition agreement can make closing depend on the applicable FTC process permitting the combination. The parties can also decide how they will handle a regulator's conditions, who prepares supporting material, what happens if review lasts past a negotiated long-stop date, and when either side may end the agreement. These are contract choices. They should be written against the actual filing and review record, without assuming that the published October 27 date is an agreed completion date.

If the buyer expects to migrate merchants, couriers, app users or maps, the plan should identify what depends on a completed acquisition and what remains only preparatory while the statutory waiting restriction applies. For as long as Article 11 bars the combination, merchant, courier, user, and map moves that depend on a completed acquisition remain preparatory.
