# Divorcing a Taiwanese spouse by agreement: what to check before signing

Suppose you have agreed to end the marriage, and your spouse sends a Chinese-language agreement for you to sign before your flight home. The payment figure looks familiar, but a later paragraph says you will make no further claims. Agreeing to divorce does not tell you what that paragraph covers. Before you sign, you need to understand what you are promising and how the divorce will actually take effect.

## What does the release cover?

A release clause deserves more attention than its length suggests. Which claims does it waive? What happens to assets that neither spouse has produced records for? If your spouse's spoken explanation differs from the Chinese wording, get the wording changed before you sign. If the agreement is bilingual, compare the two versions and check which one controls if they differ.

Payment terms work best when the amount, currency, due date, receiving account and responsibility for transfer fees are all spelled out. If you will be paid after leaving Taiwan, set up an account you can use from abroad and agree how you will communicate about payment. If one spouse is moving out, the agreement can set a date for collecting belongings. Transferring a house or other property may require the other spouse to provide documents or take part in the transfer, so the agreement should say what cooperation is expected.

If you agree between yourselves who will repay a loan or carry a guarantee, check separately what each of you will still owe the lender. For children, the agreement should cover where they will live and who will care for them, contact with the other parent, expenses, and school and medical decisions. Its legal effect depends on the applicable law and any existing court orders, and a divorce agreement does not give permission to move a child abroad.

## The legal route determines the formalities

Which law governs your divorce depends on more than your spouse's Taiwanese nationality: each spouse's nationality and domicile, and where you have lived together, all matter. Marriage records and any pending court cases also help show which steps remain. The [Taiwan divorce Q&A](/en/columns/taiwan-divorce-lawsuit-qna) explains the wider framework.

Where Taiwan law applies, [Article 1050 of Taiwan's Civil Code](https://mojlaw.moj.gov.tw/LawContent.aspx?TypeSort=3&lawNumber=1049&lsid=fl001351&media=print) requires a divorce by agreement to be in writing, signed by at least two witnesses and registered at a household registration office. The divorce takes effect only on registration, so signing the agreement is not the last step.

According to the notes on the Ministry of the Interior's [reference form for divorce agreements](https://www.ris.gov.tw/documents/data/2/09e9582e-9b91-437a-b01a-3d033eed2bc5.pdf), the witnesses must have full legal capacity and must personally confirm, by seeing or hearing the spouses themselves, that both genuinely intend to divorce. Plan how the witnesses will do that, and when they will sign.

## Can registration wait until after departure?

The [Ministry of the Interior's divorce registration guidance](https://www.ris.gov.tw/documents/html/2/3/1/384.html) requires both spouses to apply together as a rule. One spouse may appoint a representative by power of attorney only for good reason and with the household registration office's approval. Living abroad does not automatically qualify.

If you will sign documents overseas, tell the office what you plan to submit and where it will be signed. Its guidance requires documents made abroad to be authenticated, and foreign-language documents to come with an authenticated Chinese translation. Work out which originals, authentications and translations the office will need before you plan the registration around your flight home.

Recording a divorce already finalized abroad is a different task from getting an Article 1050 divorce in Taiwan. Registration in Taiwan also leaves recognition, reporting and enforcement in another country to be handled there. Check what your own country requires while you are choosing the Taiwan route, not after registration.
