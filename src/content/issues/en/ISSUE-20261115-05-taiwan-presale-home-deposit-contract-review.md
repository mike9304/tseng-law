# Taiwan presale contracts: access to the terms before paying a deposit

Buyers at a Tainan presale development were told to pay a deposit before taking the sales contract away to read. Taiwan's Fair Trade Commission imposed fines on the developer and sales agency, according to its [November 10 announcement](https://www.ftc.gov.tw/internet/enterprise/doc/docDetail.aspx?docid=18529&uid=126). The developer was fined NT$400,000. The agency's fine was NT$200,000.

For an English-speaking household arranging an explanation of a Chinese-language contract, access to the complete document determines whether that review can happen before money changes hands. A showroom explanation may leave the buyer's adviser without the clauses needed to assess delivery obligations, payment stages or the consequences of withdrawing.

## Payment pressure and the right to read

The FTC found a breach of [Fair Trade Act Article 25](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0150002&flno=25). That provision prohibits deceptive or obviously unfair business conduct sufficient to affect trading order, except where the Act provides otherwise. The commission explained that withholding the contract until payment exploited the seller's information advantage and put financial pressure on prospective buyers. It also disadvantaged competitors that provided proper access to their contracts.

[Consumer Protection Act Article 11-1](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0170001&flno=11-1) requires a reasonable period, within 30 days, for consumers to review all standard-form terms before entering the contract. It permits authorities to specify review periods for particular industries. A standard term making the consumer waive this right is invalid.

Does that reference to 30 days create a month-long cancellation window after signing? It does not. The provision addresses review before the agreement is made. It cannot be treated as a general promise that a buyer may sign, change their mind and recover every payment afterward.

If the business fails to provide the required review opportunity, Article 11-1 says its terms do not become part of the contract. The consumer may nevertheless assert that they do. That qualification preserves the consumer's choice; the statute does not simply declare the entire transaction void.

## A usable copy and the payment record

Paper is not the only option. The FTC announcement says sample copies, photocopies and electronic files are among the ways a contract may be supplied. An electronic copy can make it easier for a buyer abroad or an adviser outside the showroom to read the same text. The announcement does not promise an English translation or require one particular delivery format.

Where money has already been paid, the fine does not settle the buyer's individual refund claim. A reservation document, receipt and subsequent sales agreement may record different stages of the transaction. Messages requesting the full contract, the response imposing a payment condition and the version eventually supplied can help establish the sequence.

A consumer dispute may be raised with the business, a consumer-protection organization or a consumer service center under [Article 43](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0170001&flno=43). Businesses must handle complaints appropriately. The deadline is 15 days from the complaint date. This is not an automatic refund deadline. An unresolved complaint may go to the local consumer protection officer; [Article 44](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0170001&flno=44) also permits an application to the local consumer-dispute mediation committee after a complaint has not been appropriately handled.

For the buyer seeking independent review, a dated copy of the complete contract answers a concrete question: were the terms available while the decision to pay was still open?
