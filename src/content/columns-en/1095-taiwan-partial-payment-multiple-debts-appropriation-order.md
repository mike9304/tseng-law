When a payer owes several debts to the same creditor, all requiring the same kind of performance, [Civil Code Article 321](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=321) lets the payer designate which debt an insufficient payment covers. The designation is made at payment.

This article assumes Taiwan law governs the allocation. A customer's location alone does not settle that choice of law.

## The payer's designation comes before the statutory fallback

Consolidating debts owed to different group companies in one ledger does not satisfy the single-creditor condition.

Where the remittance advice or correspondence identifies a particular contract, installment or invoice, preserve that message and evidence of transmission alongside any agreed allocation terms before treating the payment as an unallocated credit.

In [Taoyuan District Court case 113 Jian-Shang 42, decided July 16, 2024](https://judgment.judicial.gov.tw/FJUD/printData.aspx?id=TYDV%2C113%2C%E7%B0%A1%E4%B8%8A%2C42%2C20240716%2C1), the court, hearing an appeal, explained that “at payment” is not confined to the precise moment money is tendered.

A designation before or after payment may qualify if appropriately timed under ordinary commercial understanding. The court accepted a court filing specifying the installments on the same day as the transfer, and treated other transfers matching a distinctive agreed installment amount as implied designations.

The judgment set no fixed grace period. Its reasoning depended on the timing and the particular installment agreement, not merely the payer’s later preference.

## A bank entry and a message to the creditor can prove different things

Did the creditor receive that designation? In a [New Taipei District Court first-instance judgment of October 21, 2025](https://judgment.judicial.gov.tw/FJUD/printData.aspx?id=PCDV%2C113%2C%E9%87%8D%E8%A8%B4%2C469%2C20251021%2C2), the payer said additional transfers totaling NT$300,000 paid interest on a loan.

Its transaction entries contained wording suggesting partial interest. The court found those entries were records in the account holder's transaction details, rather than declarations or notifications to the transaction counterpart.

The payer's lawyer's letter identified the money as contractual compensation. Applying Article 321, the court treated it as that separate compensation payment and rejected its use to reduce the loan principal or interest. The payer did not prove its claim of a verbal mistake.

Both companies were in Taiwan. An overseas accounts team can make the same comparison between the saved bank memo, the message sent to the recipient and any later explanation of the transfer.

## No designation does not automatically mean oldest invoice first

Without a designation by the payer, [Article 322](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=322) provides this fallback:

| Comparison | Statutory priority |
|---|---|
| Some debts are due and others are not | Debts already due come first |
| All are due, or all are not yet due | The least secured debt comes first |
| Security is equal | The debt whose discharge benefits the debtor most comes first |
| That benefit is also equal | The debt with the earlier due date comes first |
| Benefit and due date are equal at the final stage | Apply the payment proportionally |

An older secured debt and a newer unsecured debt cannot be ranked solely by their invoice dates when both are already due.

Proportional allocation may reduce several debts together. Keep the balance of each debt in the calculation, together with its due date and security, rather than only a single account total.

## Selecting the debt leaves costs and interest to calculate

[Article 323](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=323) applies money first to costs, next to interest, and then to principal; it expressly includes allocation under the preceding two articles. Choosing an invoice does not itself authorize principal-first treatment.

Applying the interest-before-principal rule to loan repayments, the New Taipei court explained that a debtor cannot change that order unilaterally. A creditor's agreement to different treatment is a separate issue to verify; the payer's preferred bookkeeping entry does not establish it.

A reconciliation can place each payment’s date and amount beside the designated debt and supporting communication, followed by the costs, interest and principal calculation.
