# You sent USDT to the wrong Taiwan exchange account. Can you get it back?

One digit in the UID was wrong. The USDT landed in a stranger's account on a Taiwan exchange, and support would not say whose account it was (an invented example). Taiwan's Civil Code (民法) can require the recipient to return a benefit received without legal cause, as long as you can tie their gain to your loss; if it cannot be returned, its value is owed instead. A court can also order a provisional attachment (假扣押) for a monetary claim, or one convertible into money, when later enforcement would otherwise be impossible or extremely difficult. It cannot press an undo button on the exchange.

The harder problem is the missing name. Nothing in the unjust-enrichment (不當得利) rules makes an exchange identify its customer, and an anonymous UID or wallet address is not a named defendant. Evidence preservation (證據保全) lets an applicant explain why the other side cannot yet be named, but its text does not promise access to exchange records.

## Who has to give it back

Civil Code [Article 179](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=179) (民法) requires a person to return a benefit received without legal cause when that benefit causes another person a loss. It also applies when a legal cause existed at first and later disappeared. For a mistaken UID or address, the sender therefore needs evidence of the transfer, the unintended destination, the corresponding loss, and the absence of a debt or other legal basis for that recipient to keep it.

USDT may no longer remain in the same form or account. [Article 181](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=181) reaches the original benefit and anything further obtained from it. If the benefit cannot be returned because of its nature or other circumstances, the recipient must repay its value. That value route matters when in-kind return is unavailable, although the article does not prescribe how a particular token should be valued.

Knowledge changes the scope of repayment. Under [Article 182, paragraph 1](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=182), a recipient who did not know there was no legal cause is exempt from returning the benefit or paying its value only when the benefit no longer exists. Both conditions matter. Under paragraph 2, a recipient who knew at receipt must repay the benefit received then, with interest; one who learned later must repay the benefit still existing at that later time, with interest. Either must also cover damages if there are any.

When did the recipient learn of the error? A dated message routed through the exchange, if one exists, may bear on that question. The transfer record and support replies should be kept in their original sequence. A mistaken bank transfer raises the same Civil Code foundation but fewer of the exchange-identification issues; those rules are discussed in [the separate bank-transfer column](/en/columns/taiwan-mistaken-bank-transfer-unjust-enrichment).

## What a freeze takes

For the interim measure, [Article 522 of the Code of Civil Procedure](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=522) (民事訴訟法) allows provisional attachment (假扣押) to preserve compulsory execution for a monetary claim or a claim that can be converted into money. It also covers claims subject to a condition or time limit. When in-kind return is impossible, Article 181 requires repayment of value. Whether that requested relief fits Article 522 still depends on the actual facts.

A claim alone is insufficient. [Article 523](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=523) bars provisional attachment unless there is a risk that later compulsory execution will be impossible or extremely difficult. The fact that an account holder is unknown may create an identification problem; the statutory attachment reason remains the risk to later execution and must be supported as such.

An application must say who, what and why. [Article 525](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=525) requires the parties and any statutory agents (法定代理人), the claim and its underlying facts, the reason for attachment, and the court. A claim without a fixed amount needs a stated value. When jurisdiction rests on where the target asset is located, the application must also state the asset and its location.

Both the claim and the attachment reason need a prima facie showing. [Article 526](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=526) requires a prima facie showing (釋明) of the claim and the attachment reason. If that showing is insufficient, and the creditor offers to provide security or the court considers that course appropriate, the court may set the amount and order attachment after the security is provided. Even a sufficient showing may still lead to a security order. None of these provisions directs the exchange to undo the transfer, disclose the customer, or move the USDT back.

## Finding out who holds the USDT

Evidence-preservation rules address an unnamed opponent more directly. Under [Article 370](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=370), an application must state the opposing party or, if that party cannot be specified, explain why. It must also identify the evidence to preserve, the fact that evidence would prove, and the reason preservation is needed. The applicant must make a prima facie showing of both the inability to specify the opponent and the preservation reason.

That is a pleading rule with limits. Article 370 does not say that exchange account records must be disclosed, that a particular exchange is required to identify its customer, or that an evidence-preservation application can replace identification in the later civil claim. Whether a court can use this procedure for the particular records and exchange involved needs case-specific confirmation.

Venue depends on timing. [Article 369](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=369) sends a post-filing application to the court hearing the case. Before filing, it goes to the district court where the person to be examined lives or where the evidence is located. In an urgent situation, the same district-court route remains available after filing.

Keep what already exists: the UID or address entered, the transfer quantity and time, any transaction identifier, the intended destination, and every support ticket and response. These records do not themselves reveal the recipient. They let counsel state precisely what evidence is missing and what fact it would be offered to prove.

As of November 17, 2026, the Virtual Asset Service Act (虛擬資產服務法) had been promulgated on July 22, 2026, but remained not yet in force with no effective date set. [Article 56](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0400163&flno=56) leaves that date to the Executive Yuan (行政院). The Act therefore supplies no current shortcut for a sender seeking disclosure, attachment, or return.

## Can the exchange balance itself be seized?

Execution against a debtor's monetary claim against a third party is the subject of [Article 115 of the Compulsory Execution Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010004&flno=115) (強制執行法). The enforcement court issues a seizure order barring the debtor from collecting or otherwise disposing of that claim and barring the third party from paying the debtor. The court may ask the creditor's views and, by order, allow the creditor to collect the claim or transfer it to the creditor. If the court considers it appropriate, it may instead direct the third party to pay the enforcement court for payment on to the creditor.

Nothing in Article 115 classifies a USDT balance held through an exchange as a monetary claim against that exchange. It also does not decide whether a particular UID transfer left the recipient with a monetary claim, a right to delivery of an asset, or some other contractual position. Applying Article 115 to exchange-held USDT therefore requires analysis of the platform terms and the legal character of the recipient's right; the article alone cannot support a promise that the balance will be seized.

If your own account has instead been restricted under fraud-control rules, that separate reader situation is covered in [the column on Taiwan exchange account controls](/en/columns/taiwan-crypto-exchange-account-fraud-control-warning-foreign-residents).
