# A Taiwan power of attorney after the New York document breach

Taiwan's Bureau of Consular Affairs said on November 9 that hackers obtained scans of some powers of attorney authenticated by Taiwan’s New York representative office. Its [announcement](https://www.boca.gov.tw/fp-56-8323-50e8c-1.html) says their authentication remains effective. The affected documents were authenticated between October 26, 2011 and November 6, 2026. It excludes applications handled in Taiwan, other overseas offices and other document categories from this part of the reported breach.

For a US-based person who appointed someone to handle a Taiwan transaction, the next question concerns that person's authority to act. Does the appointment still cover the transaction now being proposed? Where Taiwan law governs the authorization, the Civil Code addresses the scope of that authority and the consequences of changing it. The bureau's announcement does not decide those individual questions.

## The authority behind the document

Under [Civil Code Article 103](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=103), an agent's declaration made in the principal's name and within the agent's authority takes effect directly for the principal, meaning the person represented. The agent’s identity matters. So does the appointment’s scope. Authentication alone does not supply those answers.

An authorization for one matter may leave another proposed action outside its terms. Reading the document alongside the underlying arrangement allows the parties to identify the authorized person, the work entrusted to that person and any limits on the appointment. This is a question about the grant of authority, not simply whether an image of the document exists somewhere.

## Revocation and the counterparty's knowledge

Revocation is generally possible. [Article 108](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=108) provides that the underlying legal relationship determines when the authority ends. It also permits authority to be revoked while that relationship continues, except where the nature of the relationship prevents revocation. A principal therefore cannot assume every appointment has identical termination terms.

An ongoing transaction’s underlying agreement may explain why the authority was granted and whether an exception matters. The power of attorney's wording is only part of that file. A decision to stop a representative from proceeding also raises a separate question about the person dealing with that representative.

[Article 107](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=107) protects third parties acting in good faith: a restriction or revocation generally cannot be asserted against them. The protection does not apply when their ignorance results from negligence. A private instruction to the agent may therefore leave a dispute about the counterparty's knowledge unresolved.

In a pending deal, dated communications identifying the authorization and the change can help establish what the counterparty knew. Delivery records matter for that factual question. They do not produce an automatic outcome under Article 107; the content of the communications and the circumstances in which the other party acted remain relevant.

## Recovering the authorization document

After authority ends or is revoked, [Article 109](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=109) requires the agent to return the power-of-attorney document to the person who granted it. The agent cannot retain it. That duty concerns the document held by the agent.

Leaked copies may still remain. BOCA's announcement asks affected applicants to remain alert to possible misuse of their information; it does not report that every affected authorization has been used improperly.

For an overseas principal reviewing an unfinished Taiwan matter, the original authorization, the underlying agreement and dated communications with the representative and counterparty address different parts of the problem. Together, they help show who was entitled to act, whether that authority changed and what the other party knew when it proceeded.
