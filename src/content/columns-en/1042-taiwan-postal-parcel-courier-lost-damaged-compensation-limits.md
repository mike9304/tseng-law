# Lost or Broken Parcels in Taiwan: The NT$575 Postal Cap, Insured Mail and Courier Liability Clauses

If Chunghwa Post loses a domestic parcel weighing 5 kg or less, the most it pays is NT$575. Pricier contents do not raise that cap, and cheaper ones are paid at market value. The mail type chosen at the counter changes the outcome.

A private courier would answer under the Civil Code instead. The post office answers under the Postal Act (郵政法) and the Postal Items Handling Rules (郵件處理規則).

## What the post office compensates

[Postal Act art. 29](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=K0050001&flno=29)(1) lists four cases in which the sender may claim. Parcels, express (快捷), declared-value and insured items lost, stolen or damaged in whole or part. Other registered mail lost or stolen in whole. Prompt or express mail delayed through the post office's fault. Registered mail not delivered under the announced handling rules. Ordinary mail is not on the list.

Domestic amounts under the rules as amended on October 30, 2019:

| Item | Amount | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Domestic parcel or express, up to 5 kg or 60 cm combined | Up to NT$575 | [Rules art. 65](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=K0050021&flno=65)(1) |
| Over 5 kg up to 10 kg, or over 60 cm up to 90 cm | Up to NT$865 | Same |
| Over 10 kg, or over 90 cm | Up to NT$1,155 | Same |
| Domestic registered 函件, total loss or theft | NT$575 flat per item | [Rules art. 64](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=K0050021&flno=64)(1) |
| Declared-value or insured item | Declared value; partial loss at actual value, capped at the declared value | Art. 64(2) |

International parcels and express are calculated at 40 IMF Special Drawing Rights per item plus 4.5 per kilogram (art. 65(2)). Parcels and express, domestic or international, worth less than these amounts are paid at market value at the time and place of mailing (art. 65(3)). Registered 函件 differ: the term covers letters, postcards, aerogrammes, printed matter, items for the blind and small packets ([Postal Act art. 4](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=K0050001&flno=4)(1)), meaning items of 1 kg or less (art. 4(7)), and domestic ones get a flat NT$575 (art. 64(1)(i)).

Delay through the post office's fault refunds the full fee for prompt and domestic express mail, half for international express (art. 64(3)). Registered mail not delivered under the announced handling rules earns the full fee back (art. 64(4)). Both exclude insurance, declared-value, cash-on-delivery and advice-of-delivery (回執) fees (art. 64(5)). When a parcel, express, declared-value or insured item is lost, stolen or wholly damaged, postage is refunded too, though not the insurance or declared-value fee, and a cash-on-delivery item compensated on the same grounds also gets its collection fee back ([art. 67](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=K0050021&flno=67)(1)–(2)).

## Insured mail raises the ceiling

For valuables, insured mail (保價) is nearly the only route. Registered mail sent with a declared insured amount and an extra fee becomes insured mail ([Rules art. 30](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=K0050021&flno=30)).

Take a 1 kg box holding a NT$20,000 tablet. Sent as an ordinary parcel, it is worth NT$575 at most if lost. Insured for NT$20,000, it is measured against that sum (an invented example).

Do not inflate the figure. The declared amount may not exceed the actual value, and over-declaring forfeits any compensation for a loss ([Rules art. 31](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=K0050021&flno=31)).

## At the door

Accepting an item without objection ends any claim ([Postal Act art. 31](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=K0050001&flno=31)(6)), and so does signing a receipt without stating a defect on the spot ([Rules art. 62](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=K0050021&flno=62)). If the wrapping shows no tear and the weight has not dropped, or dropped only because of the item's nature, the item is not treated as damaged ([Postal Act art. 32](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=K0050001&flno=32)(1)). So check the box before signing and have any problem recorded and photographed.

Who claims? The sender, as a rule. Under art. 29(2), the recipient may claim only with evidence that the sender assigned the claim, or after accepting the rest of a partly damaged or stolen item while reserving the claim.

## Six months from mailing

The claim lapses six months after the mailing date, but an inquiry within that period counts as a claim ([Postal Act art. 34](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=K0050001&flno=34)). The inquiry window for registered mail is six months from the day after mailing, four for international express; after that the post office need not answer or pay ([Rules art. 60](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=K0050021&flno=60)). Payment is made against the mailing receipt or wrapper, within three months from the day after the inquiry ([art. 63](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=K0050021&flno=63)(1)). Keep the receipt.

## When the post office pays nothing

Article 31 bars claims for loss caused by the item's nature or defects, by natural disaster or other force majeure (for insured mail, only war), loss abroad where that country's law imposes no liability, prohibited or otherwise legally unmailable items, poor packing left uncorrected after notice, and acceptance without objection. Loss of value from the passage of time or price changes does not count (art. 32(2)).

There is no fallback. Although [art. 1](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=K0050001&flno=1) sends gaps to other laws, art. 29(3) bars senders and recipients from claiming damages from Chunghwa Post under any other law, and other consequential losses are not paid ([Rules art. 69](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=K0050021&flno=69)).

## Private couriers: the Civil Code

Couriers are carriers: businesses that transport goods for freight ([Civil Code art. 622](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=622)). A carrier is liable for loss, damage or delay unless it proves force majeure, the nature of the goods, or fault of the shipper or consignee ([art. 634](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=634)).

Damages follow the value at the destination when delivery was due, less freight and other charges no longer payable; intent or gross negligence opens the door to further losses ([art. 638](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=638)). Cash, securities, jewellery and other valuables are not covered unless their nature and value were declared, and then only up to that value ([art. 639](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=639)).

Accepting goods and paying freight and other charges without reservation ends the carrier's liability. Hidden internal loss or damage is an exception if reported within ten days of receipt, and a carrier that concealed the loss by fraud, or caused it intentionally or by gross negligence, cannot rely on either rule ([art. 648](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=648)).

The shipper is the contracting party; when an online shop ships, that is the shop. The consignee acquires the shipper's rights once the goods arrive and delivery is requested ([art. 644](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=644)). Claims expire one year after carriage ended or should have ended ([art. 623](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=623)(1)).

## The cap printed on the waybill

Courier waybills often print a per-parcel cap. That line may not hold. A clause on a waybill or other document the carrier gives the shipper that excludes or limits the carrier's liability has no effect unless the carrier proves the shipper expressly agreed ([art. 649](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=649)).

Agreement is not the end of it. A pre-drafted standard clause that excludes or reduces the drafter's liability is void to the extent it is obviously unfair in the circumstances ([art. 247-1](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=247-1)(1)). In consumer contracts, standard clauses that breach good faith and are obviously unfair are void, with unfairness presumed in cases such as a clause clearly contradicting the purpose of the default rule it displaces ([Consumer Protection Act art. 12](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0170001&flno=12)); ambiguous clauses are read for the consumer ([art. 11](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0170001&flno=11)(2)). Whether a given cap is obviously unfair turns on the amount, whether a value could be declared, and the freight charged.

No official model exists. As of November 3, 2026, the Executive Yuan's lists of [mandatory standard-form terms](https://www.ey.gov.tw/Page/2285E9A14973DE75) (88 items) and [model contracts](https://www.ey.gov.tw/Page/37D1D3EDDE2438F8) (96 items) contain nothing on courier or postal carriage; the only goods-carriage entry is house moving.

## Disputes

Consumers may complain to the business, a consumer group or a consumer service centre; the business must respond properly within 15 days of the complaint, after which the local consumer protection officer and then a mediation committee can step in ([Consumer Protection Act arts. 43](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0170001&flno=43)–[44](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0170001&flno=44)).
