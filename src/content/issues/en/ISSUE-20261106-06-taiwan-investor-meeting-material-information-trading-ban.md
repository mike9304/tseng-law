# Taiwan investor meetings and the 18-hour trading restriction

For investors meeting a Taiwan-listed company, the statutory trading restriction can continue for [18 hours after material information is publicly disclosed](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0400001&flno=157-1).

On November 4, the Taiwan Stock Exchange and Bloomberg signed a cooperation agreement aimed at improving Taiwan companies’ international visibility and foreign investors’ understanding of their businesses, [CNA reported](https://focustaiwan.tw/business/202611040019). The planned program includes tools for investor-relations teams to identify and engage potential investors. Research coverage remains subject to Bloomberg Intelligence’s own criteria.

## What the investor actually learned

[Article 157-1 of the Securities and Exchange Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0400001&flno=157-1) covers specified recipients who actually know precise information with a material effect on the issuer’s share price. They must not buy or sell its exchange-listed or OTC shares, or other equity securities, before public disclosure or during the following 18 hours. Trading in another person’s name is also covered.

Covered recipients include people who learn the information through an occupational or controlling relationship, and people who receive it from the insiders and other persons specified in the provision. A foreign analyst receiving information from a company manager may fall within that scope. Attendance alone is insufficient. The [TWSE’s explanation](https://www.twse.com.tw/en/products/education/insider-trading/qa.html) identifies the recipient, material information, actual knowledge, trading time and securities involved as separate elements of the inquiry.

What makes a business comment material? [Article 2 of the material-information regulations](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0400129&flno=2) includes major acquisitions and the ending of some or all business dealings with a principal customer or supplier. It also addresses other financial or business information materially affecting the share price or a reasonably prudent investor’s decision. The actual content matters: a broad discussion of an industry cannot simply be treated as knowledge of a specific corporate event.

Timing can precede a board vote. Under [Article 5](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0400129&flno=5), the relevant information’s existence is dated to the earliest applicable event, such as an agreement, contract signature or board resolution, or another date made clear by concrete evidence. An approval scheduled for later does not by itself settle when the underlying information became precise. Meeting notes may therefore need to be read alongside the transaction’s actual chronology.

## Public disclosure has a specified form

For the corporate financial and business information described in Article 2, [Article 6](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=G0400129&flno=6) specifies public disclosure through the company’s entry into the Market Observation Post System, or MOPS. A private presentation is not that filing. Nor does an investor’s circulation of meeting notes substitute for the company’s MOPS entry.

Other categories have different disclosure rules. Article 6 allows additional methods for the market supply-and-demand information covered by Article 3, so MOPS should not be described as the only method for every kind of material information. The relevant category comes first. For a meeting about the issuer’s own financial or business developments, the company’s filing and its timestamp provide the reference for assessing the statutory waiting period.

Practical review starts with the meeting record: who supplied the information, what was said or delivered, and when it became known to the person proposing the trade. Those details can be compared with the company’s public filing and the intended transaction time. A scheduled investor meeting offers access to management; it does not itself establish that every statement made there is already public.
