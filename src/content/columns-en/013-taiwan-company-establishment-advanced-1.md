# Taiwan Company Formation — Advanced Part 1

![Featured image](../images/013-taiwan-company-establishment-advanced-1/featured-01.jpg)

Recently, many people have successfully formed companies in Taiwan and obtained work visas and Alien Resident Certificates (ARCs).

Below I organize the questions that many people ask during the formation process.

If you have already read the [**Company Formation Basics**](https://www.wei-wei-lawyer.com/post/%EB%8C%80%EB%A7%8C-%ED%9A%8C%EC%82%AC%EC%84%A4%EB%A6%BD-%EA%B8%B0%EC%B4%88%ED%8E%B8) article, please also refer to this more detailed advanced part.

I hope the Q&A below is helpful to those considering forming a company in Taiwan. 😊

![](../images/013-taiwan-company-establishment-advanced-1/img-01.jpg)

**1. I want to form a company, but I have not yet found a company registration address. Can I still form the company?**

When a foreigner forms a company in Taiwan, the investment plan must be submitted to Taiwan's Investment Commission for review.

The Investment Commission must confirm that the capital is actually being used for investment purposes and

that overseas parties are not bringing funds into Taiwan under another pretext.

When preparing the investment plan, you do not need to state an exact company address; stating only the region is sufficient (for example, Taipei City). ​

But later, when opening a company preparatory account at a bank, many banks review the lease agreement and conduct an on-site inspection of the company address.

A Taiwan bank employee told me directly that

there have recently been many cases of foreigners opening accounts and then absconding, so account opening is becoming stricter and stricter.

In the earlier basics article, a reader also mentioned that opening a bank account is the most difficult step, and I agree.

Taiwan has had a great many money-laundering cases, so bank account opening is becoming increasingly strict.

**Advice:**

Because investment-plan review also takes time,

prepare the investment plan and look for a company registration address at the same time.

The Investment Commission requires that capital be remitted within one year after investment approval, so

after you find a company address and sign a lease,

you still have enough time to open a company preparatory account at the bank and remit the capital.

​

**2. Can I open a company bank account even if I do not have a Taiwan Alien Resident Certificate?**

Yes, you can.

Banks generally require dual identity documents, and

if you do not have an ARC, you can apply at Taiwan's National Immigration Agency for a '**Basic Information Form of Uniform ID Number** (統一證號基本資料表)'.

It can be issued on the same day, but

immigration offices are very crowded, so go early and take a number ticket.

​

**3. I heard that for investment-plan review I have to fill in my education and work history, and I am worried that my education and experience may not match the industry of the company I want to form.**

The Investment Commission under the Ministry of Economic Affairs does review the investor's background, but

it is not extremely strict.

You may mention various work experiences, including part-time jobs, and

explain them in enough detail to persuade the reviewers.

Please consult fully with a Taiwan lawyer on this point.

​

**4. What should I watch out for when leasing a company registration address (for example, a restaurant storefront)?**

Company formation takes about **3 months**, and obtaining a work permit and ARC also takes about **1 month**, so

set the lease start date as late as possible.

And in Taiwan storefront leases, landlords often give tenants a "**fit-out period** (裝潢期)".

This is a rent-free period, so try to negotiate it.

It is not easy for foreigners to lease storefronts, and Taiwan security deposits are relatively low (usually 2 months), so landlords have limited protection and

are reluctant to lease to foreigners.

Therefore, I recommend starting to look for business premises early.

If negotiations with the landlord do not go smoothly,

you may, if necessary, propose notarizing the lease agreement or providing an additional security deposit.

​

**5. When forming a company, can I lease commercial office space?**

It depends on the company's business items.

Example: if the business item is food and beverage service but you try to lease ordinary commercial office space, opening a bank account will be very difficult.

As noted above, banks review the lease agreement and inspect the company address on site.

Also, at final company registration, the city government requires that the registered address be in a zone where restaurant operations are permitted, and

if it is ordinary commercial office space, company registration may not be possible.

Example: if the business items are advertising, wholesale, or other activities that can be conducted from commercial office space, you can form the company there. Before signing the lease

agreement, please confirm this in advance.
