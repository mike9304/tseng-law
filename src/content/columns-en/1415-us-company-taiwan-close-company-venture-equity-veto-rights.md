# Structuring a Taiwan subsidiary or joint venture: when a Close Company fits US equity terms

Can a Taiwan subsidiary put share transfer restrictions, founder veto rights, and sweat-equity grants directly inside its corporate charter? Yes, by forming a Close Company (閉鎖性股份有限公司). US startups and corporate joint-venture partners accustomed to flexible governance at home sometimes try applying those terms to a standard Taiwan Company Limited by Shares (股份有限公司), whose contribution rule in Article 156, paragraph 5 leaves out labor and which sits outside the charter transfer-restriction framework of Articles 356-1 and 356-5.

[Article 356-1 of Taiwan's Company Act](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0080001&flno=356-1) (公司法) defines a Close Company as a non-publicly-issued company limited by shares with no more than 50 shareholders whose Articles of Incorporation set share transfer restrictions. Paragraph 2 lets the central competent authority raise that number and set how shareholders are counted. [Article 356-2](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0080001&flno=356-2) requires stating close-company status in the Articles, published on the authority's website. Home-country corporate and tax effects of holding a Taiwan entity still require separate US review.

## Why standard Taiwan entities clash with venture and JV terms

Taiwan recognizes four domestic company types under [Company Act Article 2](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0080001&flno=2): unlimited companies, limited companies, unlimited companies with limited-liability shareholders (兩合公司), and companies limited by shares. Foreign corporations can also register a Taiwan branch under [Article 371](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0080001&flno=371), allocating operating capital under [Article 372](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0080001&flno=372). Branches cannot issue stock to co-founders or investors.

A Limited Company (有限公司) fits a single-owner subsidiary: [Article 98](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0080001&flno=98) allows one or more members, [Article 108](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0080001&flno=108) sets one to three directors, and [Article 111](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0080001&flno=111) requires majority consent of other members' voting rights (or two-thirds for a director's stake) to transfer contributions. Yet it issues no shares and cannot create tiered preferred classes. An ordinary Company Limited by Shares permits par-value or no-par shares and preferred stock under [Article 156](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0080001&flno=156), while omitting labor contributions and the close-company transfer-restriction framework. US LLC parents should also check entity-authority proof in [investing in Taiwan through a Delaware LLC](/en/columns/taiwan-investment-delaware-llc-identity-authority).

## Transfer restrictions, sweat equity, and no-par shares

Charter transfer restrictions—such as board consent or right-of-first-refusal rules—must appear in the Articles of Incorporation under [Article 356-5](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0080001&flno=356-5). Printed certificates must carry conspicuous legends. For uncertificated shares, the transferor must state the restrictions in written transfer documents, and buyers may request a copy of the Articles from the company.

Under [Article 156, paragraphs 1 and 2](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0080001&flno=156), any company limited by shares, including a Close Company, may choose par-value or no-par-value shares, allocating all no-par proceeds to capital. As of November 24, 2026, [Article 356-3](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0080001&flno=356-3) lists cash, property needed for the business, technology, and labor as promoter contributions (with promoters acting by unanimous consent). Credit is not on the list. Labor-based shares cannot exceed a proportion of total issued shares set by the central competent authority.

Sweat equity carries public disclosure. Article 356-3, paragraph 4 requires unanimous shareholder consent for technology or labor contributions, charter disclosure of contribution type, offset valuation, and share count, and publication on the central authority's website. For follow-on rounds, [Article 356-12](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0080001&flno=356-12) requires a board resolution with two-thirds attendance and majority approval for new shares unless the Articles provide otherwise, allows monetary claims against the company as payment, and disapplies Article 267. Public offerings remain barred under [Article 356-4](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0080001&flno=356-4), except via licensed securities firms' equity crowdfunding platforms, which stay bound by the 50-shareholder cap and transfer restrictions.

## Preferred shares, veto rights, and voting agreements

Venture investors and JV parents usually negotiate liquidation priority, board seats, protective vetoes, and voting discipline. [Article 356-7](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0080001&flno=356-7) places all four inside the Articles through special shares (特別股):

1. Dividend, bonus, and residual-asset liquidation priority, fixed amount, or fixed rate.
2. Voting order or limits, non-voting shares, multiple-voting shares, or veto rights over specified matters.
3. Guaranteed election to a fixed number of director or supervisor seats, or limits barring election.
4. Conversion ratios, methods, or formulas into common shares, tailored transfer restrictions, and other defined rights.

In a Close Company, Article 356-7, paragraph 2 provides that Article 157, paragraph 2 does not apply to holders of multiple-voting special shares. Under Article 356-3, paragraphs 5 and 7, Article 198 governs director and supervisor elections unless the Articles provide otherwise.

Written voting agreements and voting trusts are validated by [Article 356-9](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0080001&flno=356-9), with trustees limited to shareholders unless the Articles say otherwise. To assert a voting trust against the company, shareholders must register the trust contract, holder details, and trusted share counts at least 30 days before an annual meeting or 15 days before a special meeting.

Shareholders' meetings may be held by video conference if the Articles so provide under [Article 356-8](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0080001&flno=356-8), and video participants count as attending in person. The Articles may also provide that, when all shareholders agree to the method, they vote in writing on that meeting's proposals without convening. For bridge rounds, [Article 356-11](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0080001&flno=356-11) authorizes private straight bonds by a two-thirds-quorum, majority board vote; private convertible or warrant bonds add a shareholder vote unless waived in the Articles, and bondholders who convert or exercise warrants remain bound by the 50-shareholder cap and transfer restrictions.

| Feature | Close Company (Arts. 356-1 to 356-14) | Ordinary Company Limited by Shares |
|---|---|---|
| Charter transfer restrictions | Required (Arts. 356-1, 356-5) | Outside Arts. 356-1 and 356-5 framework |
| Shareholder cap | Maximum 50 shareholders (Art. 356-1) | No 50-shareholder ceiling |
| Labor contributions | Allowed up to authority cap with unanimous consent and public posting (Art. 356-3) | Omitted from Art. 156(5) |
| No-meeting written vote | Allowed via charter when all shareholders agree to the method (Art. 356-8(3)) | Art. 356-8(3) is a Close Company rule |
| New shares under Art. 267 | Art. 267 disapplied (Art. 356-12(3)) | Subject to Art. 267 unless separately exempt |

## The 50-shareholder ceiling and conversion thresholds

How quickly can a growing team reach the cap? A US robotics firm and a Hsinchu hardware partner launch a Taipei Close Company with four shareholders, then issue direct shares to 48 engineers (an invented example). Crossing 50 holders forces conversion.

Leaving close-company status follows [Article 356-13](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0080001&flno=356-13). Voluntary conversion needs a meeting with holders representing at least two-thirds of issued shares and approval by a majority of votes present, or higher charter thresholds. Breaching Article 356-1 makes conversion and change registration mandatory. If the company does not register the change, the competent authority may order correction within a set period and impose successive fines on the responsible person under [Article 387, paragraph 5](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0080001&flno=387): NT$10,000 to NT$50,000 at first, then NT$20,000 to NT$100,000 each time a correction deadline passes, until the company complies. In serious cases the authority may order dissolution on its own initiative.

What if an existing Taiwan company limited by shares that has not publicly issued its shares wants to become a Close Company later? [Article 356-14](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0080001&flno=356-14) allows the change with the consent of all shareholders, after which the company must immediately notify each creditor individually and make a public announcement. The article names only companies limited by shares, not a Limited Company (有限公司). One holdout blocks the change. Annual corporate filings remain separate obligations, covered in [annual shareholders' meetings and director terms in Taiwan](/en/columns/taiwan-subsidiary-annual-shareholders-meeting-director-term).

## Foreign investment approval before wiring capital or transferring shares

Close-company flexibility still runs through Taiwan's foreign investment gate. Under the [Statute for Investment by Foreign Nationals](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawAll.aspx?pcode=J0040002) (外國人投資條例), Articles 3 and 4 treat holding Taiwan company shares (or lending for one year or more to that invested company) by a foreign individual or foreign corporation as foreign investment. [Article 8](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=J0040002&flno=8) requires prior approval from the Ministry of Economic Affairs (經濟部) before investing and whenever the investment plan changes. Once the application is complete, the Ministry must decide within one month, or within two months where another regulator's authority is involved.

Two points in the Statute affect the filing. First, Article 6 lists cash, self-use machinery or raw materials, IP or know-how, and other authority-approved property as foreign-investor contributions, omitting labor. Where a US parent funds cash or IP while local founders contribute services under Company Act Article 356-3, map each contribution track before filing. Second, Article 9 requires bringing capital in on schedule and verifying investment amounts, while Article 10, paragraph 2 requires the transferor and transferee to apply jointly to the Ministry for approval of any later transfer of foreign-held shares. Approval unlocks repatriation of annual dividends and verified exit proceeds under Article 12; where foreign ownership reaches 45% or more, Article 15, paragraph 2 says the Company Act rules it names on public issuance and employee share reservation need not apply. Registration filings under Company Act Article 387, paragraph 3 may be handled by a Taiwan lawyer or certified public accountant.
