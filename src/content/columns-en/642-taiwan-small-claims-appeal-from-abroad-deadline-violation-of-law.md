# Appealing a Taiwan Small Claims Judgment From the United States: What the Court Will Hear, and How the 20-Day Clock Stretches

The judge believed your former landlord. In Taiwan's small claims procedure, that complaint alone will not open an appeal. Appeals there are allowed only for a violation of law. The clock has its own condition. If you live in the United States and have no litigation agent residing where the court sits who could file in time, the 20-day appeal period is extended by a 44-day travel allowance.

Our column [Owed NT$100,000 or Less in Taiwan](/en/columns/taiwan-small-claims-simplified-civil-procedure) covers which claims go to small claims, the fees and mediation.

## What the appeal court will look at, and what it won't

A first-instance small claims judgment can be appealed to the district court's three-judge panel, but only on the ground that it violates the law ([Code of Civil Procedure Article 436-24](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=436-24)). Violation of law means failing to apply a rule or applying it wrongly ([Article 468](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=468)). The notice of appeal must identify the provision, explain how it was violated, and point to facts in the record that show it ([Article 436-25](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=436-25)).

Missing reasons will not carry an appeal. A small claims judgment may set out only its operative orders, adding the gist of the reasoning on disputed points where needed ([Article 436-18](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=436-18)). Of the automatic grounds in [Article 469](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=469), only items 1 to 5 (court composition, recusal, jurisdiction, representation, public hearing) apply to small claims appeals ([Article 436-32(2)](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=436-32)). Item 6, missing or contradictory reasons, does not.

A Keelung District Court ruling from September 2025 shows the line in practice. The appellant described the traffic accident and why he had missed the hearing, without naming any provision. The hearing notice had been served by deposit with the local authorities at the same address he gave on appeal, effective ten days after deposit (Article 138(2)), so the court found the default judgment lawful and rejected the appeal ([ruling of September 23, 2025](https://judgment.judicial.gov.tw/FJUD/data.aspx?ty=JD&id=KLDV%2c114%2c%e5%b0%8f%e4%b8%8a%2c27%2c20250923%2c1)).

## The ground that does work: notice

Notice is the ground that fits most clearly. A court may not rule on one side's argument alone if the absent party was not lawfully notified in good time ([Article 386](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=386), item 1).

One Taoyuan District Court judgment of September 2025 shows it working. The first-instance court had sent the defendant's hearing notice to the plaintiff's own litigation agent, then ruled when only the plaintiff appeared. The appeal court held the appeal admissible. It then heard the merits itself, struck repair items that did not match where the cars collided, and cut the award from NT$52,089 to NT$33,887 ([judgment of September 30, 2025](https://judgment.judicial.gov.tw/FJUD/data.aspx?ty=JD&id=TYDV%2c114%2c%e5%b0%8f%e4%b8%8a%2c37%2c20250930%2c1)).

That second step cuts both ways. In December 2024 the New Taipei District Court agreed that the lower court had ruled without lawfully notifying a defendant who was in prison, yet dismissed his appeal because the loan claim against him was valid ([judgment of December 25, 2024](https://judgment.judicial.gov.tw/FJUD/data.aspx?ty=JD&id=PCDV%2c113%2c%e5%b0%8f%e4%b8%8a%2c231%2c20241225%2c1)). The provision that lets an ordinary appeal court send a case back for a serious procedural defect, [Article 451](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=451), does not apply to small claims appeals. Winning on notice gets you a hearing on the merits, not a fresh start below.

## Filing from abroad

An appeal must be filed within the fixed 20-day period after service ([Article 440](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=440), applied by Article 436-32(2)). When a party does not live where the court sits, travel time is deducted in counting statutory periods ([Article 162](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=162)). The Judicial Yuan's [standard on travel periods, Article 3](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010020&flno=3), sets 44 days for North America, Europe, South America and Oceania, 37 for Asia, and 72 for Africa and Antarctica. Twenty plus forty-four is sixty-four.

There is a catch. If you have a litigation agent living where the court sits who could file within the period, no travel time is deducted (Article 162(1), proviso). The day of service is not counted ([Civil Code Article 120(2)](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=120)), and a last day falling on a Sunday or other rest day rolls to the next day ([Article 122](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0000001&flno=122), with Article 161 of the Code of Civil Procedure).

Courts count strictly. In March 2026 the Shilin District Court threw out an appeal by a party living in New Taipei that came one day after the deadline of 20 days plus two days of domestic travel time ([ruling of March 25, 2026](https://judgment.judicial.gov.tw/FJUD/data.aspx?ty=JD&id=SLDV%2c115%2c%e5%b0%8f%e4%b8%8a%2c30%2c20260325%2c1)).

You also need someone in Taiwan to receive court papers. An appellant with no place for service in Taiwan must appoint an agent for service there ([Article 133(2)](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=133)); without one, the court may order service by public notice on its own motion ([Article 149(5)](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=149)).

## After the appeal is filed

You cannot change or add claims, or bring a counterclaim ([Article 436-27](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=436-27)). New arguments and evidence are barred unless the lower court's own violation of law kept you from presenting them ([Article 436-28](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=436-28)). An appeal that is plainly unfounded on its face may be decided without oral argument ([Article 436-29](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=436-29)). The decision is final ([Article 436-30](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=436-30)), and once an appeal is dismissed as unfounded, the same ground cannot be reused for a retrial ([Article 436-31](https://law.moj.gov.tw/LawClass/LawSingle.aspx?pcode=B0010001&flno=436-31)). For claims of NT$100,000 or less, the appeal court fee is NT$2,250 ([Judicial Yuan fee table](https://www.judicial.gov.tw/tw/np-167-1.html), checked October 21, 2026).

In the Keelung case, a notice that retold the accident without naming a provision ended the appeal there.
